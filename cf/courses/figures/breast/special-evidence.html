Level of evidence, not a trial list — five special situations in breast cancer
Situation Evidence available Where that leaves practice How NCCN reflects it
Special
presentations		 Inflammatory
breast cancer		 No randomised trial defines the three-part sequence — preoperative systemic therapy, mastectomy, radiation. Institutional series and registry data Outcomes are better in the group that receives all three parts; dropping one is worse IBC-2 writes the surgery and radiation down in detail — skin-sparing and nipple-sparing mastectomy contraindicated, level I/II axillary dissection, regional nodal irradiation covering any undissected axilla at risk — rather than leaving it to clinical judgement
Paget disease Mainly retrospective series; a single-arm phase II in Europe examined the breast-conserving pathway Central lumpectomy including the NAC, plus whole-breast radiation, is a workable breast-conserving option PAGET-2 lists central lumpectomy and total mastectomy side by side, and notes that mastectomy is always an option
Phyllodes tumour No randomised trials; surgical series Benign lesions need only complete excision; borderline and malignant behave like soft-tissue sarcoma PHYLL-1 moves borderline and malignant entirely to the Soft Tissue Sarcoma guideline, and states that adjuvant RT is not indicated for positive margins
Special
populations		 Breast cancer
during pregnancy		 Prospective registries and series. The NCCN page itself cites three reviews — Kesireddy 2025 JCO Oncol Pract, Poggio 2020 Cancers, Boere 2022 Best Pract Res Clin Obstet Gynaecol Fetal outcomes with anthracyclines, cyclophosphamide and taxanes in the second and third trimesters are acceptable PREG-2 uses qualified language — “appear to be safe” — rather than a category of evidence
Breast cancer
in males		 Very rarely enrolled in trials; real-world data and registry analyses Extrapolating from data in females is reasonable — endocrine therapy is the exception BINV-J opens with “recommendations … are generally extrapolated from findings of clinical trials focusing on breast cancer in females”, then singles out single-agent AI as inferior and not recommended
The two middle columns are editorial annotation and are not on the NCCN pages; the right-hand column is NCCN's own wording. NCCN lists regimens and categories, and does not comment on the type of evidence behind them. The three reviews named for pregnancy are the ones listed in the PREG-2 page footer.
