What to check, and how often — HER2-positive metastatic disease
Agent or setting What to monitor When / how often What NCCN says to do Source
Fam-trastuzumab deruxtecan Interstitial lung disease / pneumonitis Regular monitoring for this serious side effect is recommended With a history of ILD/pneumonitis there are no data on managing safety or toxicity. The same warning is repeated on every page that lists the drug BINV-Q 4
Any HER2-targeted therapy Cardiac function Regular monitoring for current or prior anthracycline or HER2-targeted therapy See NCCN Guidelines for Survivorship: Cardiovascular Disease Risk Assessment BINV-Q 1
Overlap with an anthracycline At regimen selection Concurrent trastuzumab and pertuzumab with an anthracycline should be avoided; trastuzumab is safe with the non-anthracycline single agents on BINV-Q 5 of 15 BINV-Q 4
Subcutaneous substitution Which product is actually being given Whenever a substitution is made Trastuzumab and hyaluronidase-oysk may replace IV trastuzumab, but never for or with T-DM1 or fam-trastuzumab deruxtecan BINV-Q 1
Capecitabine-containing regimens DPYD status Not stated “For information regarding DPYD testing, see the NCCN Guidelines for Colon Cancer” BINV-Q 1
Capecitabine / neratinib Tolerance at the start Cycle 1 steps up: 120 mg days 1–7, 160 mg days 8–14, then 240 mg days 15–21 The flat 240 mg schedule is the other listed option — both appear as alternatives BINV-Q 11
Taxane-containing regimens Peripheral neuropathy; alopecia Not stated Consider cryotherapy of hands and feet; glove constriction may be considered. Consider scalp cooling for alopecia BINV-Q 1
Disease assessment Symptoms, examination, performance status, weight, LFTs, CBC; imaging Prior to each cycle; CT chest/abdomen/pelvis every 2–4 cycles; bone scan every 4–6; brain MRI as indicated Reassess on new or worsening signs or symptoms regardless of the interval. For brain metastases see NCCN Guidelines for CNS Cancers BINV-R 3
