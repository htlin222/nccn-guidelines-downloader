Randomised evidence behind follow-up and survivorship care — invasive breast cancer
Trial Population and comparison Key result What it changed
Surveillance Two Italian randomised trials of intensive follow-up, 1990s — GIVIO and Rosselli Del Turco Asymptomatic patients after surgery for early breast cancer
Routine imaging and laboratory follow-up vs clinical follow-up with mammography		 No difference in overall survival, and no better quality of life The evidence behind “in the absence of clinical signs and symptoms … there is no indication for laboratory or imaging studies for metastases screening”. That one sentence on BINV-17 rests on these two trials
Recurrence CALOR Completely resected isolated locoregional recurrence
Surgery ± chemotherapy after the recurrence		 Disease-free survival improved; the benefit was mainly in ER-negative recurrence Supports “consider appropriate systemic therapy” on BINV-19 and BINV-20 as a step with curative intent, not palliation
Fertility POSITIVE Premenopausal, HR-positive, 18–30 months of adjuvant endocrine therapy completed, wanting to conceive
Single arm: endocrine therapy interrupted for up to 2 years to attempt pregnancy		 No increase in recurrence risk on short-term follow-up BINV-C makes pausing endocrine therapy to conceive an explicit option, while noting that long-term safety is unknown
Ovarian suppression with a GnRH agonist during chemotherapy — POEMS/SWOG S0230, PROMISE-GIM6 and others Premenopausal patients receiving adjuvant chemotherapy
Chemotherapy ± GnRH agonist		 Better preservation of ovarian function; less chemotherapy-induced menopause The source of BINV-C's “randomized trials have shown … regardless of HR status”
