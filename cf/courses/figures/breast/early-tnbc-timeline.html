What happens when — and what each step decides for the next one
Step 1Diagnosis ER-negative, PR-negative and HER2-negative on the original report. Check the histology is not a favourable one — those go to a different page
Step 2The fork — before surgery is booked ≥cT2 or ≥cN1 → preoperative systemic therapy is preferred. cT1c, cN0 can be considered. Inoperable disease (inflammatory, bulky or matted cN2, cN3, cT4) always. Otherwise, upfront surgery
Step 3 — route APreoperative systemic therapy In TNBC the response is the product: prognostic information at an individual patient level, and it identifies the residual-disease group whose recurrence risk is high enough to escalate
Step 4Surgery — and the pathology report Route A yields ypT/ypN, route B yields pT/pN. Two different tables, and they are never mixed. Route A’s recommendations do not apply to residual DCIS (ypTis)
Step 5 — route AEscalation by response pCR → for high risk, adjuvant pembrolizumab, if a pembrolizumab-containing regimen was given preoperatively.
Residual → that pembrolizumab (category 1) and/or capecitabine 6–8 cycles and/or 1 year of olaparib for a germline BRCA1/2 PV (category 1). No data on sequencing or combining
Step 5 — route BAdjuvant therapy by pT and pN pT1a and pN0 → no adjuvant therapy. pT1a with pN1mi, or pT1b → consider chemotherapy. pT1c–pT3, or pN+ → chemotherapy (category 1). Each of the treated boxes adds olaparib for a germline BRCA1/2 PV
Step 6Surveillance History and exam 1–4× a year for 5 years, then annually. Mammography every 12 months. No labs or imaging for metastasis screening in the absence of signs or symptoms
Running alongside, from step 2 onwards
Germline BRCA1/2 testing — olaparib hangs off it on both routes
And the order is written down: olaparib should be given after completion of RT
Was a pembrolizumab-containing regimen given preoperatively?
Both of route A’s boxes carry that same condition. If it was not, the box is empty — it cannot be started after surgery
Favourable histologies are a different page
Non-high-grade, pure (>90% on the excision, not the core biopsy), HER2-negative → local therapy alone unless pN+
On an immune checkpoint inhibitor
Screen for and manage immune-related toxicity and endocrinopathies such as hypothyroidism and adrenal insufficiency
Anthracycline and taxane
Regular cardiac monitoring on an anthracycline. Docetaxel as the preferred taxane at higher TIPN risk (African descent, diabetes); consider cryotherapy of hands and feet, and glove constriction
Alopecia, and substitutions
Consider scalp cooling, possibly less effective with anthracycline-containing regimens. FDA-approved biosimilars may be substituted for any recommended biologic
