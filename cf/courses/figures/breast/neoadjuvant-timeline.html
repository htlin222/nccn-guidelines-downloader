What happens when — and what each step decides for the next one
Step 1Who is a candidate Inoperable disease always: inflammatory, bulky or matted cN2, cN3, cT4. When operable, preferred for HER2-positive disease and TNBC if ≥cT2 or ≥cN1; cT1c, cN0 can be considered
Step 2Workup, before the first dose Axillary exam and ultrasound; biopsy the most suspicious node and place a marker in that node only. Core biopsy with an image-detectable clip in the tumour bed. CBC; metabolic panel with LFTs and alkaline phosphatase
Step 3Preoperative systemic therapy Response is hard to assess: exam plus the imaging that was abnormal at initial staging. MRI is more accurate than mammography. No response and/or still inoperable → consider additional systemic therapy and/or preoperative RT
Step 4Surgery — and the pathology report Mastectomy or BCS with surgical axillary staging. After cN+, the false-negative rate of SLNB is >10%: dual tracers, ≥3 SLNs, and remove the clipped node. This is the branch point
Step 5Escalation — pathology × subtype First complete whatever the preoperative course did not finish. Then read the table: ypT0N0 or pCR against ypT1–4,N0 or ypN≥1, over four subtypes. Recommendations do not apply to residual DCIS (ypTis)
Step 6Radiation — read cN and ypN together cN2–3, any ypN+, or cT4 → comprehensive RNI. cN0 or cN1 with ypN0 → not routinely indicated — but the cN1 → ypN0 box carries the NSABP B-51 caveat and must be discussed
Step 7Surveillance History and exam 1–4× a year for 5 years, then annually. Mammography every 12 months. Cardiotoxicity monitoring after left-sided RT, anthracyclines or HER2-targeted therapy
Running alongside, from step 3 onwards
Chemotherapy and endocrine therapy sequentially, endocrine after chemotherapy
Adjuvant olaparib is the exception — it may be given concurrently with endocrine therapy
Up to 1 year of HER2-targeted therapy in total
The preoperative portion counts towards the same year
Cardiac monitoring on an anthracycline or HER2-targeted therapy
LVEF before and during trastuzumab: the FDA label says every 3 months, NCCN notes the optimal frequency is not known
Taxane substitution caps at 125 mg/m² weekly
Albumin-bound paclitaxel replacing weekly docetaxel or paclitaxel
Neuropathy and alopecia
Docetaxel as the preferred taxane at higher TIPN risk (African descent, diabetes); consider cryotherapy and glove constriction; scalp cooling, possibly less effective with anthracyclines
On an immune checkpoint inhibitor
Screen for and manage immune-related toxicity and endocrinopathies such as hypothyroidism and adrenal insufficiency
