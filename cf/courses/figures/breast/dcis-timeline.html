What happens when — and what each step decides for the next one
Step 1Diagnosis and workup History and exam, bilateral diagnostic mammography, pathology review, ER status — the only biomarker here. Genetic counselling if indicated, MRI if indicated, distress screening. No PR, no HER2
Step 2Choose the operation Lumpectomy without lymph node surgery, or total mastectomy with sentinel lymph node biopsy ± reconstruction. No surgical axillary staging for pure DCIS unless invasion or nodal metastasis is suggested
Step 3The specimen — margins At least 2 mm with whole breast RT; routinely wider is not supported by evidence. Re-excise for a negative margin; if an adequate margin still cannot be obtained, mastectomy. Invasion found → treat as stage I or II
Step 4 — after breast conservationRadiation: three ways Whole breast RT (category 1) ± boost to the tumour bed. Or APBI/PBI. Or omit RT (category 2B) — considered only with all four RTOG 9804 criteria: screen-detected, grade 1–2, ≤2.5 cm, margins ≥3 mm
Step 5 — if ER-positiveEndocrine risk reduction, 5 years Consider it after BCS with RT (category 1), or after excision alone. Premenopausal: tamoxifen. Postmenopausal: tamoxifen or an aromatase inhibitor. It is risk reduction, not adjuvant therapy
Step 6Surveillance Interval history and exam every 6–12 months for 5 years, then annually. First mammogram 6–12 months after the end of RT, or after BCS (category 2B) if no RT was given, and annually thereafter
