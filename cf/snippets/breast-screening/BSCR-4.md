# Which increased risk category applies

- 5-year risk of invasive breast cancer 1.7% or higher in individuals 35 y or older, per Gail Model
    - See Comparison of Risk Assessment Models (NCCN Guidelines for Breast Cancer Risk Reduction)
    - Periodic reassessment of risk is recommended, particularly when risk factors change
- 5-year risk of invasive breast cancer 1.7% or higher as calculated by an imaging-based risk assessment model
    - Periodic reassessment of risk is recommended, particularly when risk factors change
- ADH, or lobular neoplasia (LCIS/ALH)
    - Risk depends on age at diagnosis
- Dense breast tissue on mammography in patients who do not meet any other increased risk category
    - Heterogeneously dense breasts
    - Extremely dense breasts

# Clinical encounter

- Clinical encounter every 6-12 mo
    - Increased risk by risk model: to begin when identified as being at increased risk
    - ADH or lobular neoplasia (LCIS/ALH): to begin at diagnosis
    - Heterogeneously dense or extremely dense breasts: to begin when identified on mammography
- At minimum, review medical and family history
- Ongoing risk assessment, by age 25 years
- Risk reduction counseling
- Preferably a CBE even in individuals who are asymptomatic, when feasible
- Factor known breast density into risk assessment, if available from a prior mammogram
- Rationale is to maximize earliest detection of breast cancers and assure ongoing risk assessment, particularly in regions where mammographic screening may not be accessible
    - Randomized trials comparing incremental CBE versus mammographic screening have not been performed

# Annual screening mammogram with tomosynthesis

- Increased risk by risk model: to begin when identified as being at increased risk
- ADH or lobular neoplasia (LCIS/ALH): to begin at diagnosis, but not prior to age 30 y
- Heterogeneously dense or extremely dense breasts: to begin no later than age 40 y, but not prior to age 30 y
- Tomosynthesis can decrease call-back rates and improve cancer detection compared with 2D mammography alone
- Medicare and insurers allow the individual direct access to scheduling for screening mammography
- See Mammographic and/or ultrasound evaluation (BSCR-18)

# Supplemental imaging, by category

- Increased risk by risk model: consider annual breast MRI with and without contrast
    - Consider CEM or MBI for those who qualify for but cannot undergo MRI
    - Whole breast ultrasound may be done if CEM or MBI is not available
- ADH or lobular neoplasia (LCIS/ALH): consider annual breast MRI with and without contrast
    - To begin at diagnosis of ADH or lobular neoplasia (LCIS/ALH), but not prior to age 25 y
    - Consider CEM or MBI for those who qualify for but cannot undergo MRI
    - Whole breast ultrasound may be done if CEM or MBI is not available
- Heterogeneously dense breasts: consider supplemental screening (BSCR-A)
- Extremely dense breasts: breast MRI with and without contrast
    - To begin at age 50, but can consider starting at age 40 y
    - In patients with extremely dense breasts and normal mammograms, supplemental screening with breast MRI decreases interval cancers
    - In a prospective study, those randomized to breast MRI after a negative screening mammogram had significantly fewer interval breast cancers than those who did not have supplemental screening (category 1 for ages 50-75 y)
    - The ideal frequency of supplemental screening MRI in this patient population is not yet known
    - Consider CEM or MBI for those who qualify for but cannot undergo MRI
    - Whole breast ultrasound may be done if CEM or MBI is not available

# If breast MRI is ordered

- High-quality breast MRI requires a dedicated breast coil, access to biopsy under MRI guidance, experienced radiologists in breast MRI, and regional availability
- MRI should be correlated with other breast imaging modalities
- Many experts recommend alternating the mammogram and breast MRI with and without contrast every 6 months
    - Limited data support this approach; the presumption is that it may lead to earlier identification of cancer

# Risk reduction and breast awareness

- Consider risk reduction strategies (see NCCN Guidelines for Breast Cancer Risk Reduction)
- Breast awareness
    - Individuals should be familiar with their breasts and promptly report changes to their health care provider
