# What the clinical encounter must cover

- At minimum, review medical and family history
- Ongoing risk assessment, by age 25 years
- Risk reduction counseling
- Preferably a clinical breast examination (CBE), even in individuals who are asymptomatic, when feasible
- Factor known breast density into risk assessment, if available from a prior mammogram
- Breast Screening Considerations (BSCR-A)
- Rationale for recommending the clinical encounter
    - Maximize earliest detection of breast cancers
    - Assure ongoing risk assessment, particularly in regions where mammographic screening may not be accessible
    - Randomized trials comparing incremental CBE versus mammographic screening have not been performed

# Breast awareness

- Individuals should be familiar with their breasts
- Promptly report changes to their health care provider
- See Signs/Symptoms During Clinical Encounter (BSCR-5)

# Populations that take a different path

- Prior history of breast cancer
    - Refer to Surveillance/Follow-up in the NCCN Guidelines for Breast Cancer
- Pregnant and lactating individuals
    - See BSCR-B
- Individuals assigned male at birth (AMAB) with increased risk for breast cancer
    - There are limited data on screening in this group
    - Consider screening for individuals AMAB on feminizing hormones
- Veterans
    - Some veterans may now qualify for additional breast cancer risk assessment and/or mammography screening
    - Per the Dr. Kate Hendricks Thomas Supporting Expanded Review for Veterans in Combat Environments Act, also called the Dr. Kate Hendricks Thomas SERVICE Act 2022
    - https://www.congress.gov/117/plaws/publ133/PLAW-117publ133.pdf

# Risk assessment and risk models

- For individuals who meet criteria for multiple increased-risk categories, follow the most comprehensive set of screening recommendations
- Use risk models that include a comprehensive family history
    - First-, second-, and, when included in the model, third-degree relatives
    - BRCAPRO
    - Tyrer-Cuzick
    - BOADICEA/CanRisk
    - Breast Cancer Surveillance Consortium (BCSC) Invasive Breast Cancer Risk Calculator
    - See NCCN Guidelines for Breast Cancer Risk Reduction
    - See NCCN Guidelines for Genetic/Familial High-Risk Assessment: Breast, Ovarian, Pancreatic, and Prostate
- See Comparison of Risk Assessment Models (NCCN Guidelines for Breast Cancer Risk Reduction)
- Periodic reassessment of risk is recommended, particularly when risk factors change
- Residual lifetime risk of 15%–20%
    - May be considered for supplemental screening on an individual basis, depending on risk factors
- Genetic syndromes
    - There is variation in recommendations for initiation of screening for different genetic syndromes
    - See NCCN Guidelines for Genetic/Familial High-Risk Assessment: Breast, Ovarian, Pancreatic, and Prostate

# Mammographic screening

- Medicare and insurers allow the individual direct access to scheduling for screening mammography
- Shared decision-making is encouraged based on individuals' values and preferences
- Tomosynthesis can decrease call-back rates and improve cancer detection compared with two-dimensional (2D) mammography alone
- See Mammographic and/or ultrasound evaluation (BSCR-18)

# Supplemental MRI in extremely dense breasts

- In patients with extremely dense breasts and normal mammograms, supplemental screening with breast MRI decreases interval cancers
- In a prospective study, those randomized to breast MRI after a negative screening mammogram had significantly fewer interval breast cancers compared to those who did not have supplemental screening (category 1 for ages 50–75 y)
- The ideal frequency of supplemental screening MRI in this patient population is not yet known
- See Discussion
