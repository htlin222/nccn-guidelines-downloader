# Source

- NCCN Breast Cancer Screening and Diagnosis v1.2026, BSCR-3, p10
- Screening or symptom category: increased risk from RT with exposure to breast tissue between ages 10 and 30 y
- For individuals with a prior history of breast cancer, refer to Surveillance/Follow-up in the NCCN Guidelines for Breast Cancer instead
- RT exposure outside this age range: consider screening on a case-by-case basis
    - Screening mammography would not be done prior to age 25 years
    - Breast MRI may be considered
- Breast Screening Considerations apply to the clinical encounter and to each imaging modality below (BSCR-A)
- All recommendations category 2A unless otherwise indicated

# Clinical encounter

- Current age <25 y: annual clinical encounter
    - Begin 8 y after RT
- Current age 25 y or older: clinical encounter every 6-12 mo
    - Begin 8 y after RT
- At minimum, review medical and family history
- Encounter should encompass ongoing risk assessment (by age 25 years)
- Risk reduction counseling
- Preferably a CBE even in individuals who are asymptomatic, when feasible
- Factor known breast density into risk assessment if available from a prior mammogram
- Rationale is to maximize earliest detection of breast cancers and assure ongoing risk assessment, particularly in regions where mammographic screening may not be accessible
    - Randomized trials comparing incremental CBE versus mammographic screening have not been performed

# Imaging, current age 25 y or older

- Annual screening mammogram with tomosynthesis
    - Begin 8 y after RT but not prior to age 25 y
    - Tomosynthesis can decrease call-back rates and improve cancer detection compared with 2D mammography alone
    - Medicare and insurers allow the individual direct access to scheduling for screening mammography
    - Mammographic and/or ultrasound evaluation (BSCR-18)
- Annual breast MRI with and without contrast
    - Begin 8 y after RT but not prior to age 25 y
    - Consider CEM or MBI for those who qualify for but cannot undergo MRI
    - Whole breast ultrasound may be done if CEM or MBI is not available/accessible
    - High-quality breast MRI requires a dedicated breast coil, access to biopsy under MRI guidance, experienced radiologists in breast MRI, and regional availability
    - MRI should be correlated with other breast imaging modalities
    - Many experts recommend alternating the mammogram and breast MRI with and without contrast every 6 months
        - Limited data support this approach; the presumption is that this may lead to earlier identification of cancer
- No screening mammogram or breast MRI prior to age 25 y

# Risk reduction and breast awareness

- Current age 25 y or older: consider risk reduction strategies (see NCCN Guidelines for Breast Cancer Risk Reduction)
- Breast awareness, all ages
    - Individual should be familiar with their breasts
    - Promptly report changes to their health care provider
