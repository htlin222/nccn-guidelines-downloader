# Initial evaluation

- Empathetic discussion about the natural history of these types of cancer and their prognosis
    - For many patients the apparent uncertainties surrounding the diagnosis of an unknown primary cancer may result in significant psychosocial distress and increased difficulty in accepting treatment options
    - Provision of support and counseling both by the primary oncology team and by specialized services may help to alleviate this distress
    - See NCCN Guidelines for Distress Management
    - Refer to the NCCN Distress Thermometer and Problem List, which includes social determinants of health (DIS-A)
- Complete history and physical (H&P)
    - Breast, genitourinary, pelvic, rectal, skin, and/or oral cavity exam as appropriate
    - Review past biopsies or malignancies
    - Review removed lesions
    - Review spontaneously regressing lesions
    - Review existing imaging studies
- Complete blood count (CBC)
- Comprehensive metabolic panel (CMP)
- Lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) as indicated
- Chest/abdomen/pelvis CT scan
    - CT should be performed with contrast, and MRI with and without IV contrast, unless contraindicated
    - Fluorodeoxyglucose (FDG)-PET/CT is an alternative in patients with a contraindication to contrast enhancement
- Clinically directed endoscopy, as indicated
- Testing for some tumor markers such as serum CA-125, CA 19-9, and CA 15-3 may be useful in certain circumstances
    - They are not diagnostic, and caution must be exercised in their interpretation

# Workup of the biopsy specimen

- Core needle biopsy (preferred) and/or fine-needle aspiration (FNA) with cell block of most accessible site
- Consult pathologist for adequacy of specimen and additional studies including immunohistochemical (IHC) stains
    - Immunohistochemistry/In Situ Hybridization Markers for Unknown Primary Cancers (OCC-A)
- Biomarker testing using next-generation sequencing (NGS)
    - Or other technique to identify gene alterations or tumor mutational burden (TMB)
    - Testing on tumor tissue and/or cell-free DNA testing is appropriate
- Microsatellite instability (MSI) or mismatch repair (MMR) testing
    - Use IHC for MMR or polymerase chain reaction (PCR) for MSI, which are different assays measuring the same biological effect
    - The population of patients with MSI-high/MMR-deficient (MSI-H/dMMR) occult primary tumors is low
- HER2 IHC
- Tissue of origin studies are not recommended
