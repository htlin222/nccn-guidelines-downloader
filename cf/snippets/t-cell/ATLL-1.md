# Diagnosis, essential

- CBC with differential
- Peripheral blood smear for atypical cells
    - Lymphocytosis (ALC >4000/µL in adults) in acute and chronic subtypes (ATLL-A)
    - Typical ATLL cells ("flower cells") have distinctly polylobated nuclei with homogeneous and condensed chromatin, small or absent nucleoli, and agranular and basophilic cytoplasm
    - Multiple morphologic variations can be encountered
    - Presence of ≥5% atypical cells by morphology in peripheral blood is required for diagnosis of blood involvement in the absence of other criteria
- Peripheral blood immunophenotyping to establish diagnosis
    - Flow cytometry panel may include: CD2, CD3, CD4, CD5, CD7, CD8, CD25, CD30, TCR alpha/beta, TRBC1
    - Typical immunophenotype: CD2+, CD3+, CD4+, CD5+, CD7-, CD8-, CD25+, CD30-/+, TCR alpha/beta+
    - Presence of ≥5% T lymphocytes with an abnormal immunophenotype in peripheral blood is required for diagnosis of blood involvement
- Assessment of HTLV-1/2 by serology or other methods
    - See map for prevalence of HTLV-1/2 by geographic region
    - HTLV-1/2 has been described in patients in non-endemic areas

# Diagnosis, useful in certain circumstances

- Biopsy is required if the diagnosis is not established on peripheral blood, or to rule out an underlying infection (eg, tuberculosis, histoplasmosis, toxoplasmosis)
    - Biopsy of lymph nodes (excisional)
    - Skin biopsy
    - GI tract biopsy
    - Bone marrow biopsy
        - Bone marrow involvement is an independent poor prognostic factor
- If biopsy performed, the recommended panel for paraffin section IHC is: CD3, CD4, CD5, CD7, CD8, CD25, CD30
    - Use of Immunophenotyping/Genetic Testing in Differential Diagnosis of NK/T-Cell Neoplasms (TCLYM-E)
    - Usually CD4+ T cells with expression of CD2, CD5, CD25, CD45RO, CD29, TCR alpha/beta, and HLA-DR
    - Most cases are CD7- and CD26- with low CD3 expression
    - Rare cases are CD8+ or CD4/CD8 double positive or double negative
- Flow cytometry for CCR4
- Consider MGPT

# Workup, essential

- H&P examination, including complete skin examination
- Comprehensive metabolic panel
- LDH
- Serology for strongyloides
- FDG-PET/CT scan ± C/A/P/neck CT with contrast
    - Patients with T-cell lymphomas often have extranodal disease, which may be inadequately imaged by CT, and PET scan is often preferred in these instances
- Pregnancy testing in those of childbearing potential, if chemotherapy or RT is planned
- Assess for distress
    - Refer to the NCCN Distress Thermometer and Problem List, which includes social determinants of health (DIS-A)

# Workup, useful in certain circumstances

- HIV testing
- Hepatitis B and C testing
- CRP
- Soluble interleukin-2 receptor (sIL-2R)
- Serum albumin
- Blood urea nitrogen (BUN)
- Upper GI endoscopy
- Echocardiogram or MUGA scan, if anthracycline-based regimen is indicated
- CNS evaluation, in all patients with acute or lymphoma subtypes or in patients with neurologic manifestations
    - Head CT or MRI with contrast
    - And/or lumbar puncture (LP)
- Uric acid
- HLA typing
- Discuss fertility preservation
    - Sperm banking
    - IVF
    - Ovarian tissue or oocyte cryopreservation
