# Definition and clinical presentation

- Indolent T-cell lymphoma of the GI tract, or indolent clonal T-cell LPD of the GI tract
    - Rare condition with an indolent course
    - Characterized by infiltration of the intestinal mucosa with clonal abnormal T cells
- Indolent NK-cell LPD of the GI tract
    - Rare noninvasive EBV-negative NK-cell proliferation localized to the GI tract
- Typical presenting symptoms
    - Diarrhea
    - Abdominal pain
    - Weight loss
    - Lesions can also be asymptomatic and identified on endoscopy
- Most common sites of involvement
    - Small intestine
    - Colon
    - Stomach
    - Involvement of the oral cavity and esophagus has also been reported
    - Patients can present with involvement of single or multiple sites
- Endoscopic findings vary
    - Polyps
    - Ulcerations
    - Mucosal nodularity
    - Erosions
    - Scalloping
    - Ulcerated lesions are more common in NK-LPD
- Clonal T-LPD localized to the GI tract arising after chimeric antigen receptor (CAR) T-cell therapy
    - Emerging data, including cases with an indolent clinical course resembling T-LPD
    - More data are needed to characterize the natural history of these rare entities

# Differential diagnosis

- EATL
- MEITL
- ENKTL
- Type II refractory celiac disease

# Recommended staging evaluation

- Endoscopy with biopsy of any abnormalities
- FDG-PET/CT scan (preferred), or CT scan of C/A/P with contrast
- Bone marrow aspirate and biopsy
- Peripheral blood flow cytometry
- When there is presence of disease outside of the GI tract, other subtypes of T-cell lymphoma should be considered

# Treatment principles

- There are no prospective studies to guide management of these rare, emerging entities
    - Recommendations are based on case series and expert opinion
- T-LPD and NK-LPD have an indolent clinical course in the vast majority of cases, with excellent overall survival
    - Remission after resection is not uncommon
    - Spontaneous remission has also been reported
    - Though rare, patients with subsequent diagnosis of aggressive T-cell lymphoma have been reported
- Consider conservative treatment
    - Observation
    - Steroids
- Avoid aggressive treatment options, including combination chemotherapy

# Surveillance

- There are no established recommendations for surveillance for patients with T-LPD and NK-LPD
- Follow-up should be individualized
- Consider surveillance endoscopies or imaging if there are clinical concerns regarding changes in symptoms
