# Workup

- History and physical
- Contrast-enhanced MRI, with or without CT, of primary site
- Chest CT
    - Can be performed with or without contrast as clinically indicated
- FDG-PET/CT (preferred) (head-to-toe) and/or bone scan
- Consider bone marrow biopsy and/or screening MRI (with and without contrast) of spine and pelvis
    - Campbell KM, et al. Pediatr Blood Cancer 2021;68:e28807
- Cytogenetics and/or biomarker testing (may require re-biopsy)
    - Ninety percent of Ewing sarcomas will have one of four specific chromosome translocations
    - Consider MGPT or other gene fusion panel for Ewing sarcoma to identify translocations if pathologic workup of targeted polymerase chain reaction (PCR), fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH), or cytogenetics is negative
    - For patients with other primary round cell sarcomas of bone (eg, CIC::DUX4, BCOR::CCNB3), treating as Ewing sarcoma can be considered
    - For those who are negative, additional biomarker testing is recommended
    - Other primary round cell sarcoma of bone was previously referred to as Ewing-like sarcoma, terminology that is no longer included in the WHO classification
- Lactate dehydrogenase (LDH)
- Fertility consultation should be offered

# Primary treatment

- Multiagent chemotherapy (category 1) for at least 9 weeks prior to local therapy
    - Bone Cancer Systemic Therapy Agents (BONE-B)
    - Longer treatment prior to local control therapy can be considered in patients with metastatic disease based on response
    - Select patients with present, or impending, pathologic fracture or fungation may benefit from immediate surgery
- For patients with mesenchymal chondrosarcoma, single-agent doxorubicin and doxorubicin/ifosfamide can be considered as primary systemic therapy treatment options
    - See BONE-B 1 of 8

# Restage

- Chest CT
    - Low-dose, non-contrast CT is recommended for restaging
- Contrast-enhanced MRI, with or without CT (without contrast), of primary site
- X-rays of primary site
- Consider FDG-PET/CT (head-to-toe) or bone scan
    - Use the same imaging technique that was performed in the initial workup
- Repeat other abnormal studies
