# Local control therapy, stable/improved disease following primary treatment

- Wide excision (BONE-A)
    - Consider preoperative RT for marginally resectable lesions
- or Definitive RT (BONE-C) and chemotherapy (BONE-B)
    - There is category 1 evidence for between 28 and 49 weeks of chemotherapy depending on the chemotherapy and dosing schedule used
- or Amputation (BONE-A) in selected cases

# Local control therapy, progressive disease following primary treatment

- Consider RT (BONE-C) and/or surgery and/or ablative therapies to primary site for local control or palliation

# Adjuvant treatment / additional therapy after wide excision

- Positive margins
    - Continue chemotherapy (BONE-B) (category 1), followed by RT (BONE-C)
    - or RT (BONE-C) and chemotherapy (BONE-B) (category 1, for chemotherapy)
    - There is category 1 evidence for between 28 and 49 weeks of chemotherapy depending on the chemotherapy and dosing schedule used
- Negative margins
    - Chemotherapy (BONE-B) (category 1)
    - Consider RT (BONE-C) for pelvic tumors
    - RT may be considered for close margins
    - There is category 1 evidence for between 28 and 49 weeks of chemotherapy depending on the chemotherapy and dosing schedule used
- Postoperative chemotherapy (BONE-B) (category 1), consider RT (BONE-C) depending on margin status

# Surveillance

- Physical exam
- Contrast-enhanced MRI ± CT of primary site
- Chest imaging every 3 mo
    - X-ray
    - or CT, which can be performed with or without contrast as clinically indicated
    - Low-dose, non-contrast CT is recommended for restaging
- X-rays of primary site
- Complete blood count (CBC) and other laboratory studies as indicated
- Increase intervals for physical exam, imaging of primary site and chest after 24 mo, and annually after 5 y, as clinically indicated (indefinitely) (category 2B)
- Consider FDG-PET/CT (head-to-toe) or bone scan
