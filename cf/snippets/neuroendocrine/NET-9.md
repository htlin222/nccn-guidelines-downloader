# Evaluation

- Multiphasic abdomen/pelvis CT or MRI (NE-B)
    - Multiphasic imaging studies are performed with IV contrast in arterial and portal venous phases
- Chest CT (± contrast) as clinically indicated
- SSTR-PET/CT or SSTR-PET/MRI (NE-B)
    - Of skull vertex to mid-thigh, with multiphase IV contrast when possible
    - Data are limited on the optimal timing of scans following administration of SSAs
    - Tracers: 68Ga-DOTATATE, 64Cu-DOTATATE, and 68Ga-DOTATOC
- Routine biochemical evaluation with 5-HIAA (NE-A)
    - For ileal, jejunal, and cecal NETs
    - In patients with symptoms
    - Consider in asymptomatic patients with metastatic disease

# Treatment if surgical cytoreduction of metastases possible

- Surgical treatment of primary and distant disease (NE-D)

# Treatment if surgical cytoreduction of metastases not possible, low tumor burden

- Consider resection of primary tumor, particularly if symptomatic (NE-D)
    - Primary + regional lymph node resection should be considered to reduce future obstruction, mesenteric ischemia, bleeding, or perforation
- And/or observe with imaging, or octreotide acetate long-acting release (LAR) or lanreotide
    - For symptom and/or tumor control, octreotide acetate LAR 20–30 mg IM or lanreotide 120 mg SC every 4 weeks
    - Higher doses have been shown to be safe
    - For breakthrough symptoms, octreotide 100–250 mcg SC TID can be considered
    - If injection site-related complications occur, consider switching to another SSA
    - Likely of greatest benefit in patients with SSTR-positive tumors
- Observation imaging schedule
    - Multiphasic abdomen/pelvis CT or MRI every 12 wk–12 mo, with IV contrast in arterial and portal venous phases
    - Chest CT (± contrast) as clinically indicated
- If disease progression, octreotide acetate LAR or lanreotide, if not already receiving

# Treatment if surgical cytoreduction of metastases not possible, clinically significant tumor burden and/or clinically significant disease progression (NET-10)

- Consider resection of primary tumor, particularly if symptomatic (NE-D)
- And/or octreotide acetate LAR or lanreotide, and/or alternative front-line therapy
    - For symptom and/or tumor control, octreotide acetate LAR 20–30 mg IM or lanreotide 120 mg SC every 4 weeks
    - Higher doses have been shown to be safe
    - For breakthrough symptoms, octreotide 100–250 mcg SC TID can be considered
    - If injection site-related complications occur, consider switching to another SSA
    - Likely of greatest benefit in patients with SSTR-positive tumors
    - In select cases, it may be appropriate to proceed to front-line systemic therapy or locoregional therapy prior to or concurrently with octreotide acetate LAR or lanreotide
