# Source

- NCCN Distress Management v2.2026, DIS-25, p32
- Applies to implementation of the standards and distress management guidelines at an institution
- The Commission on Cancer's accreditation standards include screening all patients with cancer for psychosocial distress, and referral for psychosocial care as needed
- All recommendations category 2A unless otherwise indicated

# Work group and institutional support

- Recommend creation of a work group / cancer committee
    - Composed of multiple disciplines' representatives, including but not limited to:
        - Oncology physician champions
        - Nurses
        - Psychologists
        - Psychiatrists
        - Advanced practice clinicians
        - Information technology experts
        - Administrative leadership
        - Social workers
        - Chaplaincy
- Explicit support and backing of the institutional leadership is essential
- The committee should identify and organize advocates and institutional stakeholders who will support the implementation of the program
- A small-scale pilot program may be a preferable way to test the screening process before a larger scale implementation is put in place

# Consider before implementation

- Already existing resources
    - Screening tools already in place
    - Programs already in place
- Current workflows
- Current processes
- Available technologies

# Selecting the screening tool

- Various distress screening tools have been developed, including the NCCN Distress Thermometer and Problem List (DIS-A)
- The cancer committee should select the most appropriate screening tool to be administered in its setting
- Standardized, validated instruments are recommended
- Tools with established clinical cutoffs are recommended

# Building the screening process

- Determine the cutoff score, or the specific concerns, that will be used to identify patients who are distressed
- Determine the frequency of screening
- Develop a response algorithm
    - Who is alerted to screening results
    - How information is processed
    - Triggering of appropriate referrals
- Develop a process in which distress screening results are made known to critical members of the care team
    - Including such strategies as incorporating the results into the patient's medical record
- Develop results thresholds for generating respective referrals
    - These may vary based on types of services available and their capacity

# Quality improvement and tracking

- Consider incorporating distress screening into the institution's quality improvement and assessment process
    - For example, distress screening becomes a measurable quality metric
- Distress screening data should be tracked
- Response to results data should be tracked
- Tracked data can be used for further improvements
- Tracked data can be used for expansion of needed services
