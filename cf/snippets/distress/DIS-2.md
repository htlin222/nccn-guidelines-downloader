# Source

- NCCN Distress Management v2.2026, DIS-2, p7
- Applies at all stages of disease and in all settings
- All recommendations category 2A unless otherwise indicated

# Overarching principles

- Distress is recognized
- Distress is monitored
- Distress is documented
- Distress is treated promptly
    - At all stages of disease
    - In all settings
- Health care system delivers evidence-based distress management strategies to patients identified as being distressed
    - Through a population-based approach

# Detection

- Screening identifies the level of the distress
- Screening identifies the nature of the distress
- Screen at every medical visit, ideally, as a hallmark of patient-centered care
- At a minimum, screen for distress
    - At the initial visit
    - At appropriate intervals
    - As clinically indicated, especially with changes in disease status
        - Remission
        - Recurrence
        - Progression
        - Treatment-related complications
- Social determinants of health (SDoH) can affect cancer-related distress and impact health outcomes
    - The NCCN Distress Thermometer and Problem List can facilitate evaluation of these factors by highlighting practical concerns
    - Use these factors to inform care

# Management and treatment

- Distress is assessed and managed according to clinical practice guidelines
- Inform patients, families, and the treatment team that distress management is an integral part of total medical care
    - Provide appropriate information about psychosocial services in the treatment center
    - Provide appropriate information about psychosocial services in the community
- Health care system ensures a culture of diversity, equity, and inclusiveness in the management of cancer-related distress
    - Because experiences of prejudice and discrimination may contribute significantly to distress
    - Particularly among individuals with mental illness and other groups that have been marginalized or made vulnerable
- Clinical health outcomes measurement includes assessment of the psychosocial domain
    - Symptoms
    - Quality of life
    - Patient and family satisfaction
- Distress management is integrated into medical care
    - With interdisciplinary psychosocial collaboration
    - With provision of longitudinal measurement-based care
    - The team-based Collaborative Care Model is an example of such a delivery system

# Institutional resources

- Interdisciplinary institutional committee formed to implement standards for distress management
- Educational and training programs developed so that health care professionals and certified chaplains have knowledge and skills in the assessment and management of distress
- Institutional resources may be provided to address caregiver distress, which impacts health care systems
- Licensed mental health professionals and certified chaplains experienced in psychosocial aspects of cancer readily available
    - As staff members, or by referral
- Medical care contracts include adequate reimbursement for services provided by mental health professionals
- Quality of distress management programs and services included in institutional continuous quality improvement (CQI) projects
