- Differential diagnoses considered
    - MCD, including TAFRO syndrome, see NCCN Guidelines for Castleman Disease
    - KS-IRIS (see KS-C)
    - Lymphoma, especially PEL
    - Sepsis
    - Tuberculosis (TB) / mycobacterial infection
    - Hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis (HLH)
    - Immunotherapy-related cytokine release syndrome

# Additional workup - essential

- Lymph node biopsy with KSHV specific evaluations such as LANA-1
    - See KS-1 and NCCN Guidelines for Castleman Disease
- Cytology of effusions and flow cytometry to rule out PEL
    - Including KSHV specific evaluations such as LANA

# Additional workup - useful in certain circumstances

- Bone marrow biopsy, if MCD (TAFRO syndrome) or HLH or lymphoma is suspected
- CT scan or FDG-PET/CT scan and lab workup, if lymphoma suspected (see KS-1)

# Treatment

- Steroids should generally be avoided, because they may exacerbate symptoms of KICS
- An FDA-approved biosimilar is an appropriate substitute for any recommended systemic biologic therapy
- Systemic therapy
    - Preferred: Liposomal Doxorubicin + Rituximab
    - Other recommended: alternative KS-directed systemic therapies + Rituximab may be considered (see KS-F)
    - Rituximab and hyaluronidase human injection for subcutaneous use may be substituted for rituximab, after the patient has received the first full dose of rituximab by intravenous infusion
    - In mild cases, KS-directed therapy alone may be adequate
        - Rituximab may be added later if inadequate response
    - For transplant recipients, the Sirolimus component of immunosuppression should be maximized and other components should be minimized
- Early consultation of KS experts is encouraged

# Monitoring

- Surveillance (see KS-4)
- Additional monitoring for improvement / normalization of symptoms, to determine duration of KICS therapy
    - Lymphadenopathy
    - Splenomegaly
    - Cytopenias
    - Inflammatory markers
- CBC, differential
- CMP and CRP
- KSHV serum viral load, IL-6, IL-10, if feasible
- Imaging as indicated, eg, as follow-up for lymphadenopathy / organomegaly
- Consider repeating cytology of effusions and biopsy of lymphadenopathy in refractory / progressive disease

# Next

- Refractory / progressive disease
    - Continue current KS-directed therapy
    - Consider adding anti-IL-6-directed therapy, Tocilizumab or Siltuximab
        - Tocilizumab blocks the IL-6 receptor, but not the portion bound by viral IL-6
        - Siltuximab binds to human IL-6, preventing it from activating the IL-6 receptor
- Inflammatory syndrome and lymphadenopathy / organomegaly improving
    - Rituximab can be withheld while KS-directed therapy is continued for 3 months or longer as needed for adequate / optimal KS response
        - Because flares of KS may occur due to prolonged Rituximab effect, KS-directed therapy should be continued for at least 3 months after cessation of rituximab
        - Systemic therapy should be continued until there is an adequate KS response
    - If inflammatory symptoms / lab markers flare after Rituximab is stopped, Rituximab should be resumed
