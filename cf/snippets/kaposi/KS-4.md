# Patient education

- Educate the patient on the signs of disease progression
- Encourage the patient to promptly report any concerns

# Follow-up schedule, for patients not requiring active therapy and with no signs of progression

- Periodic, life-long follow-up
    - Patient-centered, with timing based on response to therapy
    - Timing also based on presence of immunosuppressive therapy or condition, if applicable
        - Degree of HIV viremia
        - Degree of immune reconstitution
    - Include as appropriate: dermatology, medical oncology, hematology, and primary HIV care
- Less frequent (every 6-12 mo) oncology monitoring may be appropriate for selected patients
    - Undetectable HIV viral load
    - Normal T-cell subsets
    - Stable KS for 2 or more years
    - As long as the patient has regular follow-up with an HIV provider

# Follow-up should include

- History and physical examination
    - History of additional immunosuppression such as transplant/glucocorticoids
    - Complete skin examination
    - Complete oral examination
    - Evaluation and monitoring of edema
- CBC, differential
- Comprehensive metabolic panel
- For PWH
    - T-cell subsets (CD4+ T-cell count) and HIV viral load
    - Assess ART compliance

# Photography

- Photography of oral, conjunctival, and cutaneous lesions, for evaluation of extent of disease if change in disease is noted
    - Include a reference unit of measure in the picture

# Re-evaluation, indicated depending on the clinical circumstances

- Indicated if signs and symptoms concerning for visceral involvement
- Indicated prior to new therapy if progression or refractory disease
- Indicated if change in disease is noted
- Imaging should be directed by symptoms or findings concerning for visceral or bone involvement, as well as coexisting KICS, MCD, or KSHV+ lymphoma, and can include whole body FDG-PET/CT
- Evaluation for suspected OIs
- Stool hemoccult and/or chest x-ray as clinically indicated (eg, for advanced disease)
- If unexplained pulmonary symptoms or abnormalities on chest x-ray
    - Chest CT with contrast
    - Bronchoscopy
- If GI symptoms or positive hemoccult
    - Abdomen/pelvis CT with contrast, or MRI with and without contrast
    - EGD/colonoscopy
- If concerns for coexisting KICS, MCD, or KSHV+ lymphoma, or other KSHV-associated disease
    - FDG-PET/CT
    - And/or lab workup, useful in patients with clinical features (ie, fever, dyspnea, effusions) concerning for KICS or KSHV-associated MCD
        - C-reactive protein
        - KSHV serum viral load
        - SPEP
        - IL-6
        - IL-10
    - When effusions are present, encourage cytology and flow cytometry to evaluate for PEL
    - The diagnosis of KICS ideally requires excisional biopsy of lymphadenopathy to exclude MCD, if feasible
    - See NCCN Guidelines for Castleman Disease

# Risk of future KS after treatment

- KSHV is not eradicated with treatment of KS, so the risk for future KS persists even after complete remission
- For PWH, optimization and monitoring of HIV control and immune function is important to minimize this risk
    - This risk depends on immune function and generally decreases with immune reconstitution
    - KS can persist, relapse, or present even in the setting of normal values of T-cell subsets
