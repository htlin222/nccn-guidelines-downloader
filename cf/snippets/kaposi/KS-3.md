# Determine eligibility for systemic therapy

- Systemic therapy is preferred over RT as first-line therapy and as relapsed/refractory therapy for disseminated disease, whenever systemic therapy is feasible
- Performance status and medical comorbidities may preclude the use of upfront chemotherapy in some patients, although this is uncommon
- If poor performance status is because of KS, systemic therapy should be considered, because it may lead to an improvement in performance status

# Review before initiating therapy

- Reviewers: oncology and HIV clinicians, along with both an oncology pharmacist and an HIV pharmacist, if available
- Review for possible DDIs and overlapping toxicities, prior to initiation
    - Proposed cancer therapy
    - Supportive care medications
    - ART
- Co-management by oncologist and HIV clinician for the duration of therapy
    - See NCCN Guidelines for Cancer in People with HIV

# First-line therapy, eligible for systemic therapy

- Systemic therapy (KS-F)
- or Clinical trial, at clinicaltrials.gov
- and Continue or initiate ART for PWH
    - Initiation of ART may result in IRIS within 3-6 months
    - IRIS is characterized by marked lesional swelling, increased tenderness, and peripheral edema
    - Initiate systemic KS-directed therapy as soon as possible in all PWH who have symptomatic KS, eg, edema, oral KS, or other manifestation that interferes with normal functions or activities
    - In patients with limited cutaneous disease, KS-associated IRIS is an indication for urgent initiation of systemic KS therapy
    - Signs of past IRIS may also be an indication for earlier initiation of systemic KS therapy
    - Do not delay or discontinue ART unless life-threatening IRIS develops
    - Reconstitution of immune function is important for obtaining and maintaining control or remission of KS (KS-C)
    - Avoid glucocorticoids in any formulation, due to their association with KS progression
        - In cases of life-threatening conditions, their use may be considered
    - Reassess patients who are only on ART within 4 weeks, particularly to monitor for KS-IRIS

# First-line therapy, not immediately eligible for systemic therapy

- Medical optimization
    - Address reversible medical issues so that systemic therapy can be delivered as quickly as possible
    - eg, active infection requiring antibiotic or antiviral treatment
    - eg, high-level HIV viremia requiring initiation or modification of ART
- and Consider RT (KS-E)
- and Continue or initiate ART for PWH
    - Do not delay or discontinue ART unless life-threatening IRIS develops
    - Avoid glucocorticoids in any formulation, due to their association with KS progression
        - In cases of life-threatening conditions, their use may be considered
    - Reassess patients who are only on ART within 4 weeks, particularly to monitor for KS-IRIS
