# Source

- NCCN Pediatric Hodgkin Lymphoma v1.2026, PHL-8, p23
- Applies to pediatric CHL disease surveillance and follow-up after completion of treatment
- Also covers monitoring for late effects, starting 2 years after completion of systemic therapy
- All recommendations category 2A unless otherwise indicated

# Interim H&P

- Every 3–4 mo for 1–2 y
- Then every 6–12 mo until year 3
- Then annually until 5 y

# Laboratory studies

- CBC with differential
- ESR or CRP
- Chemistry profile as clinically indicated
- Thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH) at least annually if radiation therapy (RT) to neck
- Consider PFTs
    - If bleomycin
    - Or pulmonary RT
    - Or significant pulmonary involvement
    - Or other clinical concerns

# Imaging

- Consider end-of-therapy ECHO
- Imaging studies are only recommended when relapse is suspected
    - Most patients will clinically declare themselves
    - There is no survival advantage in pre-emptive imaging
- If clinical concern, may perform chest x-ray PA and lateral views, CT with contrast, or MRI of original sites of disease
    - Follow at 3- to 6-mo intervals up to 2 y following completion of therapy
    - MRI is acceptable in place of CT scan for neck/abdomen/pelvis, but not for chest
    - Diagnostic CT of chest is needed to evaluate lung parenchyma
- FDG-PET/CT or FDG-PET/MRI if previous FDG-PET was positive (Deauville 4–5)
    - To confirm CR at the end of all prescribed therapy including RT
    - Once negative, repeat FDG-PET should not be done unless evaluating suspicious findings on H&P or CT or MRI
    - Wait at least 8–12 wk after end of RT to perform FDG-PET, to minimize false-positive results
    - Surveillance FDG-PET is discouraged due to risk for false positives
- If concern for relapse, management decisions should not be based on FDG-PET scan alone
    - Clinical and pathologic correlation is needed
    - See Principles of Pathology (PHL-B) and Therapy for relapsed or refractory disease (PHL-9)

# Immunizations

- Annual influenza vaccine is recommended, even during therapy
- Other vaccines as per CDC Guidelines, typically starting 6 mo after completion of therapy
    - See Children's Oncology Group Survivorship Guidelines

# Counseling and survivorship documentation

- Counsel regarding reproduction, health habits, and psychosocial issues
    - For AYA, see NCCN Guidelines for AYA Oncology
- Screen for delayed immune-mediated adverse events (imAEs), such as hypothyroidism
    - May present within the first 2 years following completion of treatment
    - May be needed for those who received a CPI (Durbin SM, et al. JAMA Netw Open 2025;8:e252668)
- Provide the patient with a treatment summary at the completion of therapy
    - Include details of RT and chemotherapy
    - Include cumulative anthracycline and alkylator dosages given

# Monitoring for late effects, 2 years or more after completion of systemic therapy

- Appropriate screening and counseling related to
    - Thyroid
    - Cardiac
    - Pulmonary
    - Bone
    - Fertility and reproductive health
    - Subsequent cancers, with special attention to thyroid and breast cancer
    - Other treatment-associated late effects
- See Children's Oncology Group Survivorship Guidelines
