# Biopsy of the site of concern

- Biopsy site of concern
    - A biopsy must be obtained to confirm relapse and pathology
    - See Principles of Pathology (PHL-B)
- Before biopsy, after a regimen that includes a CPI
    - False-positive findings may be observed on FDG-PET/CT or FDG-PET/MRI, given their potential for delayed response or pseudo-progression
    - Clinical judgment is often necessary in such settings
    - Repeat imaging at short intervals, per LYRIC recommendations, may be considered prior to obtaining a biopsy
    - Cheson BD, et al. Blood 2016;128:2489-2496
    - See Principles of Imaging (PHL-C)
- If negative
    - Observe with short-interval follow-up
    - or additional workup (imaging or rebiopsy) if high index of suspicion
        - Consider consultation with radiation oncology depending on level of clinical suspicion
- If positive, restage (same as initial workup)

# Re-induction therapy

- There are no data to support a superior outcome with any of the treatment modalities
- Individualized treatment is recommended
- Clinical trial, if available
- and refer to or consult with a center of expertise
- Re-induction therapy
    - See Principles of Systemic Therapy for Relapsed or Refractory Disease (PHL-E, 3 of 6)
    - It is reasonable to try multiple different re-induction regimens as needed prior to ASCR, to minimize disease burden, with a goal of achieving a metabolic CR prior to transplant
    - If less than a metabolic CR, proceed to subsequent therapy

# Response assessment after re-induction

- Reimage with FDG-PET/CT or FDG-PET/MRI
    - See Principles of Imaging (PHL-C)
    - After a regimen that includes a CPI, false-positive findings may be observed, given the potential for delayed response or pseudo-progression
    - Clinical judgment is often necessary in such settings
    - Repeat imaging at short intervals, per LYRIC recommendations, may be considered prior to obtaining a biopsy
- Deauville 1–3
- Deauville 4–5
    - Therapy following a Deauville 5 response to re-induction for relapsed/refractory disease is not well established
    - Individualized treatment is necessary
    - Referral to or consultation with a center of expertise is strongly encouraged

# Treatment options after re-induction

- High-dose therapy and autologous stem cell rescue (HDT/ASCR) ± ISRT, for Deauville 1–3
- HDT/ASCR ± ISRT ± maintenance chemotherapy
    - Maintenance chemotherapy per Principles of Systemic Therapy for Relapsed or Refractory Disease (PHL-E, 3 of 6)
- Only in highly favorable patients, consider re-induction therapy + ISRT
    - Recommendations for those who may avoid ASCR, all of the following
        - Initial stage other than IIIB or IVB
        - No prior exposure to RT
        - Duration of first CR >1 year
        - Absence of extranodal disease or B symptoms at relapse
        - Adequate response to <4 cycles of chemotherapy in those with relapse between 3 and 12 months
    - Re-induction therapy per Principles of Systemic Therapy for Relapsed or Refractory Disease (PHL-E, 3 of 6)

# Conditions attached to transplant and radiation on this page

- Allogeneic transplant is an option in select patients who experience relapse post-ASCT, as a category 3 recommendation
- RT is usually performed as consolidation after transplant
    - If unable to get to a metabolic CR, then RT can be used prior to transplant
- Strongly consider RT for selected sites that have not been previously irradiated
- See Principles of Radiation Therapy (PHL-F)
- A higher risk of engraftment syndrome may be observed in patients who have been treated with CPIs
