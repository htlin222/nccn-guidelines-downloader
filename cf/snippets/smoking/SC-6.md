# Treatment for return to smoking or sustained smoking

- Base the decision to continue or switch therapy on prior cessation success, patient preference, toxicity, and/or a change in clinical status, eg, upcoming surgery
- Continue/resume primary therapy
    - Reinforce proper use of medication
- or Switch to alternate primary therapy not previously used
    - Combination NRT and/or varenicline
    - Combination NRT or varenicline are the preferred pharmacotherapy options
    - Bupropion ± NRT may be appropriate for select patients, eg, for those with depression or fatigue (SC-F)
    - Nausea is a common side effect of varenicline and may need to be managed for patients with cancer, especially during chemotherapy
    - For those being treated with varenicline, the addition of NRT or bupropion can be considered as clinically indicated to maintain abstinence despite continued urges to smoke
    - Short-acting NRT can be added to varenicline to control/manage cravings
    - For patients who are heavily dependent and/or have unsuccessfully tried varenicline, varenicline + patch can be considered
- With either option, provide additional behavioral treatment
    - Four or more sessions of individual/group therapy is recommended, with additional sustained counseling as needed
    - Brief advice by health care professionals results in a small but important increase in quit rates
    - Longer, more frequent sessions with trained counselors are linked to higher success rates (SC-E)
    - Adjust behavioral treatment frequency as needed
- With either option, pharmacotherapy dose adjustments can be considered as clinically indicated
    - Doses may be reduced for adverse effects
    - Doses may be increased if risk of return to smoking is high (SC-F)

# Reassessment and follow-up

- After switching therapy or making dose adjustments, reassess smoking status and side effects periodically, in person or by phone/telehealth, at a minimum:
    - Within 3 wks, 1 wk preferred, and
    - Following 12 wks of therapy

# Additional treatment and follow-up

- Smoke free
    - Extend duration of pharmacotherapy as clinically indicated to maintain abstinence, ie, ≥6 mo
    - Assess smoking status at end of therapy
    - Assess smoking status 6 and 12 mo after therapy
- Return to smoking or sustained smoking
    - Additional behavioral treatment (SC-E)
    - Alternate pharmacotherapy (SC-F)
