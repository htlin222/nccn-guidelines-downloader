# Evaluation of return to smoking risk

- Screen for patient risk factors for return to smoking
    - Frequent/intense cravings
    - Elevated anxiety/stress/depression
    - Current or prior history of psychiatric disorders
    - Chronic/uncontrolled pain
    - Living/working with someone who uses tobacco or nicotine-containing products
    - Time since quitting (<1 year)
    - Recently initiated quit attempt and/or pharmacotherapy
    - Drug/alcohol use (eg, cannabis, opioids, stimulants)
    - Low self-confidence in ability to quit/maintain abstinence
    - Recent history of smoking 20 cigarettes per day or more
    - Time to first cigarette within 30 minutes of waking
    - Unstable living conditions

# Management if any risk factors for return to smoking

- Behavioral treatment, including counseling on risk factors and prevention for return-to-smoking
    - Health care professionals, including tobacco treatment specialists, should discuss risks of return to smoking and slips
    - Provide guidance and support to encourage continued abstinence from smoking
    - Principles of Behavioral Treatment of Smoking (SC-E)
- and Consider pharmacotherapy as clinically indicated to maintain abstinence
    - Health care professionals, including tobacco treatment specialists, should discuss risks of return to smoking and slips, and provide guidance and support to encourage continued abstinence from smoking (SC-E)
    - Principles of Pharmacotherapy for Smoking (SC-F)
- Review smoking-associated risks and the benefits of remaining abstinent from smoking (SC-B)
- Offer patient support resources (SC-C)
- Document management and counseling plans in patient health record
- Refer to specialist for management of psychiatric and substance use disorders (NCCN Guidelines for Distress Management)
- Reevaluate smoking status and risk of return to smoking at each visit or more often as indicated

# Management if no risk factors for return to smoking

- Reinforce success and importance of remaining abstinent
    - Health care professionals, including tobacco treatment specialists, should discuss risks of return to smoking and slips, and provide guidance and support to encourage continued abstinence from smoking (SC-E)
- Reevaluate smoking status and risk of return to smoking at each visit
