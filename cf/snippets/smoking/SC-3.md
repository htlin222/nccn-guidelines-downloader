# Source

- NCCN Smoking Cessation v1.2026, SC-3, p14
- Applies to individuals who have smoked within the last 30 days
- All recommendations category 2A unless otherwise indicated

# Assessment

- Quit date ___ (quit_date)
- Cancer surgery planned ___ (surgery_planned)
- High nicotine use and/or prior unsuccessful quit attempts ___ (nicotine_history)

# Quit within prior 30 days

- Discuss risk of return to smoking
    - Health care professionals, including tobacco treatment specialists, should discuss risks of return to smoking and slips
    - Provide guidance and support to encourage continued abstinence from smoking (SC-E)
- Provide counseling and encourage continued abstinence from smoking
    - Longer periods of abstinence from smoking confer better surgical outcomes
    - Abstinence should not affect the offer of, or delay appropriate timing of, cancer resection (SC-D)
- Continue behavioral treatment as indicated
- Continue pharmacotherapy as indicated (SC-F)

# Ready to set a quit date

- Establish or reinforce personalized quit plan based on the evaluation
    - Adjustments to therapy length, intensity, and surveillance may be considered, as clinically indicated, for patients with high nicotine use and/or prior unsuccessful quit attempts
    - Smoking treatment options (SC-5)
- Discuss risk of return to smoking
    - Discuss risks of return to smoking and slips, and provide guidance and support to encourage continued abstinence from smoking (SC-E)
- Encourage abstinence from smoking as soon as possible if cancer surgery is planned
    - Longer periods of abstinence from smoking confer better surgical outcomes
    - Abstinence should not affect the offer of, or delay appropriate timing of, cancer resection (SC-D)
- Discuss the impact of continued smoking on cancer treatment outcomes
- Discuss complications from chemotherapy, radiation, and surgery

# Not ready to quit

- Brief advice of about 3 minutes by health care professionals is associated with a small but important increase in smoking abstinence rates
- Assess and address barriers and concerns of patient
- Provide pharmacotherapy with a goal of smoking reduction (SC-F)
- Set a quit date in the near future
    - Making an immediate quit attempt is recommended
    - Smoking reduction with pharmacotherapy and counseling may be considered, with a goal of achieving and maintaining abstinence at a future quit date (ie, 1-3 mo)
- Discuss the impact of continued smoking on cancer treatment outcomes
- Discuss complications from chemotherapy, radiation, and surgery

# Next

- Quit within prior 30 days, go to evaluation of return to smoking risk (SC-4)
- Ready to set a quit date, begin treatment for smoking (SC-5)
- Not ready to quit, reassess readiness to quit at each visit and offer smoking cessation treatment services
    - Examples are local and national quitline services and Smokefree.gov texting programs
