# Source

- NCCN Palliative Care v3.2026, PAL-13, p22
- Applies to anorexia/cachexia at estimated life expectancy of years, months to year, or weeks to months
- Days to weeks (dying patient), go to interventions (PAL-14) instead
- See Palliative Care Drug Appendix (PAL-A) for specific recommendations for medical management of symptoms
- All recommendations category 2A unless otherwise indicated

# Assessment

- Estimated life expectancy ___ (life_expectancy)
- Rate/severity of weight loss ___ (weight_loss)
- Associated symptoms ___ (associated_symptoms)
- Conditions/symptoms that interfere with intake ___ (interfering_symptoms)
- Medications that interfere with intake ___ (interfering_medications)
- Complexities related to social determinants of health ___ (social_determinants)
    - Refer to the NCCN Distress Thermometer and Problem List, which includes social determinants of health
    - See NCCN Guidelines for Distress Management (DIS-A)

# Screen for conditions/symptoms that interfere with intake

- Dyspnea (PAL-11)
- Early satiety
    - If gastroparesis, try metoclopramide
- Fatigue, see NCCN Guidelines for Cancer-Related Fatigue
- Gastrointestinal
    - Constipation (PAL-17)
    - Nausea/vomiting (NV) (PAL-15)
- Intractable hiccups
- Oral health
    - Dental and/or jaw issues
    - Dysgeusia
    - Mucositis
    - Oropharyngeal candidiasis
    - Xerostomia
- Pain, see NCCN Guidelines for Adult Cancer Pain
- Pancreatic exocrine enzyme insufficiency
- Psychiatric
    - Anxiety
    - Depression
    - Eating disorders/distorted body image

# Interventions

- Evaluate rate/severity of weight loss and associated symptoms
- Educate patient/family/caregiver on patient's condition and risk/benefits of treatment options
- Review/modify medications that interfere with intake
- Consider evaluating for endocrine abnormalities (eg, thyroid dysfunction)
- Encourage, if able, high protein, high nutrition food
- Consider swallowing evaluation
- Consider psychiatric consultation if patient has an eating disorder or phobia/anxiety related to eating/swallowing challenges
- Consider nutrition support, enteral and parenteral feeding (as appropriate)
- Consider enrolling patient in a clinical trial with novel cachexia medication

# Reassessment outcomes

- Weight stabilization or gain
- Improvement in symptoms that interfere with intake
- Improved energy
- Resolution of metabolic or endocrine abnormalities

# Next

- Outcomes met, continue to treat and monitor symptoms and quality of life, with ongoing reassessment
- If ongoing needs
    - Re-evaluate palliative care interventions and intensify as possible
    - Provide nutritional consultation
    - Consider appetite stimulant
- Days to weeks (dying patient), go to PAL-14
