# Source

- NCCN Palliative Care v3.2026, PAL-25, p34
- Applies to malignant wounds at any estimated life expectancy: years, months to year, weeks to months, or days to weeks (dying patient)
- All recommendations category 2A unless otherwise indicated

# Interventions

- Assess wound comprehensively
- Educate patient/family/caregiver on patient's conditions and risk/benefit of treatment options
- Treat if potentially reversible
    - Surgical debridement; consideration of skin grafting
    - Radiation/chemotherapy/hormonal therapy
    - Vacuum-assisted wound closure
    - Wound cleansing and irrigation
    - Dressing changes
    - Treat infection
    - Optimize nutrition and mobilization
    - Reduce or prevent odor from wounds or exudates
    - Prevent wound spread
        - Keep patient dry and clean
        - Position to avoid pressure on bony prominences
        - Avoid shearing force on fragile skin when repositioning

# Reassessment outcomes

- Adequate symptom management
- Reduction of patient/family caregiver distress
- Acceptable sense of control
- Optimized quality of life
- Decreased caregiver burden

# Relieve symptoms if ongoing needs

- Odor
    - Wound cleansing and debridement
    - Topical antibiotic gel
    - Activated charcoal or silver sulfadiazine dressings
    - External odor absorbers
    - Avoid strong "cover" odors
- Infection
    - Topical or systemic antibiotics
    - Irradiated honey applications
- Exudate
    - Absorbent alginate, foam dressings
    - Drainage stoma appliance if high volume
- Bleeding
    - Hemostatic dressings: alginate, collagen, non-adherent gauze, gelfoams, sucralfate paste
    - Oral antifibrinolytics
    - Consider radiotherapy or embolization if brisk bleeding
    - Dark-colored linens
    - Anxiolytics
    - Pain management
- Pain
    - Treat according to NCCN Guidelines for Adult Cancer Pain, see PAL-8 and PAL-10
    - Use non-absorbent dressing, or reduce frequency of dressing changes, if pain is associated with dressing changes
    - Topical opioid and/or local anesthetic
    - Complementary therapies
- Pruritus
    - Topical doxepin cream
    - Cooled hydrogel sheet dressings
    - Transcutaneous electrical nerve stimulation trial
- Body image
    - Maintain symmetry in dressings
    - Manage wound odor and exudate
    - Screen for depression, anxiety
    - Address intimacy concerns

# Next

- Outcomes met, continue to treat and monitor symptoms and quality of life, with ongoing reassessment
- If ongoing needs
    - Re-evaluate palliative care interventions and intensify as possible
    - Consult or refer to specialized palliative care services or hospice
    - Relieve symptoms as above
