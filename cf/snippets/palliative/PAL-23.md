# Delirium interventions

- See Palliative Care Drug Appendix (PAL-A) for specific recommendations for medical management of symptoms
- Educate patient/family/caregivers on patient's condition and treatment options
- Screen for and treat underlying reversible causes, such as
    - Metabolic causes
    - Dehydration
    - Unrelieved pain
    - Hypoxia
    - Bowel obstruction/obstipation
    - Infection
    - Brain metastases or other CNS events
        - Consider palliative RT for brain metastases
    - Urinary retention
    - Medication or substance effect or withdrawal
        - Benzodiazepines
        - Opioids
        - Anticholinergics
        - Non-prescribed substances

# Severe hyperactive delirium (agitation)

- Reduce or eliminate delirium-inducing medications as possible
    - Steroids
    - Anticholinergics
    - Benzodiazepines
- Maximize nonpharmacologic interventions
    - Optimize sleep-wake cycle
    - Address sensory impairment
    - Provide glasses, hearing aids
    - Consider role of exercise and activity
- Consider addition of low-dose benzodiazepines with or without antipsychotic medications, if agitation is severe and either disrupting care or causing the patient distress
    - Titrate starting dose to optimal effect with lowest possible dose
- Consider opioid dose reduction or rotation
- Encourage supportive caregiver presence at bedside
- Support caregivers

# Severe hypoactive delirium

- Reduce or eliminate delirium-inducing medications as possible
- Maximize nonpharmacologic interventions
- Orient patient with family/caregiver(s) presence
- Support caregivers

# Reassessment outcomes

- Adequate symptom management
- Reduction of patient/family/caregiver distress
- Decreased caregiver burden
