# Definition of palliative care

- An approach to patient-/family-/caregiver-centered health care
    - Focuses on optimal management of distressing symptoms
    - Incorporates psychosocial and spiritual support, according to patient/family/caregiver needs, values, beliefs, and cultures
- Involve each patient's support network, as determined by the patient, in all aspects of care as needed and appropriate
    - Applies unless otherwise stated in these guidelines
- Goals
    - Anticipate, prevent, and reduce suffering
    - Promote adaptive coping
    - Support the best possible quality of life for patients/families/caregivers, regardless of the stage of the disease or the need for other therapies
- Timing
    - Can begin at diagnosis
    - Can be delivered concurrently with disease-directed, life-prolonging therapies
    - Facilitates patient autonomy, access to information, and choice
    - Becomes the main focus of care only when disease-directed, life-prolonging therapies are no longer effective, appropriate, or desired
- This definition follows Hui D, et al. J Pain Symptom Manage 2012;43:582-592, and Seaman JB, et al. J Palliat Med 2020;23:1157-1158

# Standards of palliative care

- Palliative care should be provided by the primary oncology team
    - Augmented as needed by collaboration with an interprofessional team of palliative care experts
    - This specialty palliative care team includes, but is not limited to
        - Board-certified palliative care physicians
        - Advanced practice providers
        - Nurses
        - Social workers
        - Spiritual care providers
        - Mental health professionals
        - Pharmacists
    - The specialty palliative care team should be available to provide consultative or direct care
        - To patients/families/caregivers who request or require their expertise
        - To health care professionals who request or require their expertise
- Institutions should develop processes for integrating palliative care into cancer care
    - As part of usual oncology care
    - For patients with specialty palliative care needs
- All patients with cancer should be screened for palliative care needs
    - At their initial visit
    - At appropriate intervals
    - As clinically indicated (PAL-2, PAL-3)
- Patients/families/caregivers should be informed that palliative care is an integral part of their comprehensive cancer care
- Educational programs should be provided to all health care professionals and trainees, so that they can develop effective palliative care knowledge, skills, and attitudes
- Quality of palliative care should be monitored by institutional quality improvement programs
- These standards follow Ferris FD, et al. J Clin Oncol 2009;27:3052-3058, and IOM (Institute of Medicine). 2014 Dying in America: Improving quality and honoring individual preferences near the end of life
