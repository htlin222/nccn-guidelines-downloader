# Source

- NCCN Palliative Care v3.2026, PAL-2, p10
- Palliative care general overview: assessment by oncology team, interventions, reassessment, outcomes
- Oncologists should integrate palliative care into general oncology care
    - Early consultation/collaboration with a specialty palliative care/hospice team should be considered to improve quality of life and survival
- Patients with one or more positive indicators may benefit from a care plan developed by an interprofessional specialty palliative care team
- All recommendations are category 2A unless otherwise indicated

# Interventions

- Advance care planning
- Appropriate treatment of comorbid physical and psychosocial, spiritual conditions
- Cancer therapy
- Cancer risk assessment and modification
- Coordination of care with other health care providers
- Consultation with specialty palliative care team
- Culturally congruent care
- General support (ie, education and debriefing)
- Promote adaptive coping
- Proportional sedation
- Psychosocial, spiritual support
- Resource management/social support
- Response to request to withdraw or withhold life-sustaining treatment
- Response to expressions of distress, including requests for hastened death
    - According to local regulations, culture, and/or practices (medical aid in dying [MAID])
- Symptom management
- Transition to end-of-life care including hospice referral as appropriate

# Inform, anticipate, and rescreen

- Inform patients/families/caregivers about palliative care services
- Anticipate symptoms and discuss preventive measures
- Discuss advance care planning
- Rescreen at next visit

# Reassessment

- Ongoing reassessment
- If ongoing needs
    - Re-evaluate intervention options and intensify as possible
    - Consult with other clinicians and refer to specialist if available
    - Ongoing reassessment

# Outcomes

- Acceptable sense of control
- Adequate symptom management
- Decision-making capacity
- Decreased caregiver stress and burden
- Optimized quality of life
- Patient satisfied with response to cancer therapy
- Personal growth and enhanced meaning
- Reduction of patient/family/caregiver distress
- Strengthened relationships
