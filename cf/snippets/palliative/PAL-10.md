# Source

- NCCN Palliative Care v3.2026, PAL-10, p19
- Applies to the patient with pain
- Interventions are chosen by estimated life expectancy
- All recommendations are category 2A unless otherwise indicated

# Assessment

- Pain ___ (pain)
- Estimated life expectancy ___ (life_expectancy)
- Current analgesic therapy ___ (analgesic_therapy)
- Current route of administration ___ (route)
- Level of consciousness ___ (consciousness)

# Interventions, estimated life expectancy months to years

- Treat according to NCCN Guidelines for Adult Cancer Pain

# Interventions, estimated life expectancy weeks to months, or days to weeks (dying patient)

- Treat according to NCCN Guidelines for Adult Cancer Pain
- In addition
    - Educate patient/family/caregiver on the importance of pain control at the end of life
    - Optimize psychosocial support for patient and family/caregivers
    - Optimize spiritual support for patient and family/caregivers
    - Consider integrative interventions, see NCCN Guidelines for Adult Cancer Pain (PAIN-D)
    - Consider interventional strategies, see NCCN Guidelines for Adult Cancer Pain (PAIN-M)
    - Consider palliative RT as indicated
        - eg, single-fraction palliative RT for painful bone metastases, for patients with a life expectancy of multiple weeks
    - Maintain analgesic therapy; titrate to optimal comfort
        - Balance analgesia against reduced level of consciousness as possible or desired
        - If opioid reduction is indicated, consider 10% daily or 25% every 3 days as per national tapering recommendations, to avoid acute opioid withdrawal or pain crisis
        - Modify routes of administration as needed (PO, IV, rectal, subcutaneous, sublingual, transmucosal, and transdermal), applying equianalgesic dose conversions
            - Transdermally administered medications have longer times to onset than other routes
        - IV bolus dosing combined with drip titration may be the effective method of rapid titration; drip titration alone is slower
        - Recognize and treat opioid-induced neurotoxicity, including myoclonus and hyperalgesia
            - Consider opioid rotation if needed

# Outcomes to check at reassessment

- Adequate symptom management
- Patient/family/caregiver distress mitigated
- Acceptable sense of control
- Decreased caregiver burden
- Strengthened relationships
- Optimized quality of life
- Personal growth and enhanced meaning

# Next

- Ongoing reassessment
- If ongoing needs
    - Continue to treat and monitor symptoms and quality of life
    - Continue to treat according to NCCN Guidelines for Adult Cancer Pain
    - Consider consultation with a pain management or specialty palliative care team
    - Consider hospice referral
    - Optimize communication
