# Source

- NCCN Palliative Care v3.2026, PAL-33, p42
- Applies to patients with severe and refractory symptoms being considered for proportional sedation
- Proportional sedation is the intentional lowering of awareness towards, and including, unconsciousness
    - Primary goal is symptom relief
    - Formerly known as palliative sedation
- All recommendations are category 2A unless otherwise indicated

# Assessment

- Severe symptom being targeted ___ (refractory_symptom)
- Symptom is refractory
    - Cannot be adequately managed despite comprehensive, interprofessional palliative care
    - That palliative care does not compromise consciousness
- Palliative care already delivered for this symptom ___ (prior_palliative_care)
- Current level of awareness ___ (awareness_level)
- Institutional guidelines/policy reviewed ___ (institutional_policy)
    - Refer to institutional guidelines/policy for additional guidance

# Before starting sedation

- Consider interdisciplinary discussion prior to inception of sedation
    - Purpose is to support the care team
    - Care team includes the caregivers and family in support of the patient
    - Care team includes the medical staff taking care of the patient

# During sedation

- Consider interdisciplinary discussion throughout the process
- Consider periodic reassessment of symptoms
    - Ensure level of sedation is proportional to need for symptom relief

# Terminology and legal context

- The NCCN Guidelines committee has replaced the term palliative sedation with proportional sedation
    - The change is intended to emphasize that the level of sedation should be proportionate to the patient's symptom burden
- A patient who is suffering from a terminal illness and who is experiencing great pain has no legal barriers to obtaining medication, from qualified physicians, to alleviate suffering, even to the point of unconsciousness and hastening death
    - Justice O'Connor, see Vacco v Quill and Washington v Glucksberg, 1997
