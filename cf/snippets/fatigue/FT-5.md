# Education and counseling

- Information about known pattern and dimensions of fatigue during and following treatment
    - Cognitive
    - Physical
    - Emotional
- Reassurance that treatment-related fatigue is not necessarily an indicator of disease progression
- At end of life, fatigue is an expected end-of-life symptom
    - May vary in intensity

# Active treatment

- Interventions should be culturally specific and tailored to the needs of patients and families along the illness trajectory, because not all patients may be able to integrate these options due to variances in individual circumstances and resources
- There is limited scientific evidence for children
- Consider initiation and/or encourage maintenance of a physical activity/exercise program
- Self-monitor fatigue levels
- Energy conservation, an area in which there is a lack of scientific evidence
    - Set priorities and realistic expectations
    - Pace
    - Delegate
    - Schedule activities at times of peak energy
    - Use labor-saving and assistive devices
        - Reachers for grasping items beyond arm's length
        - Sock aids for pulling on socks
        - Rolling carts for transporting items
        - Wheelchairs
        - Walkers
        - Commodes
        - Escalators and elevators for traveling between building floors
        - Electrical appliances for performing common household tasks, eg, opening cans
    - Postpone nonessential activities
    - Limit naps to not interfere with night-time sleep quality
    - Have a structured daily routine
    - Attend to one activity at a time
- Consider referral to appropriate specialist or supportive care provider
- Review list of medications and other agents that may be contributing to fatigue, and adjust and advise accordingly
    - Comprehensively assess prescription and OTC medications, supplements, and other substances
    - Consider drug/substance interactions

# Post-treatment

- Same strategies as active treatment above

# End of life

- Same strategies as active treatment above
- Eliminate nonessential activities
- Conserve energy for valued activities
- See NCCN Guidelines for Palliative Care
