# Source

- NCCN Cancer-Related Fatigue v2.2026, FT-7, p11
- Applies to patients post-treatment
- See Discussion for information on differences between active treatment, post-treatment, and end-of-life treatment
- Interventions should be culturally specific and tailored to the needs of patients and families along the illness trajectory, because not all patients may be able to integrate these options due to variances in individual circumstances and resources
- There is limited scientific evidence for children
- Consider referral to appropriate specialist or supportive care provider
- All recommendations category 2A unless otherwise indicated

# Nonpharmacologic: physical activity

- Physical activity (category 1)
    - Maintain optimal level of activity for daily living
    - Consider initiation and/or encourage maintenance of a physical activity/exercise program, as appropriate per health care provider
        - Cardiovascular endurance training (walking, jogging, or swimming)
        - Resistance (weights) training
        - See NCCN Guidelines for Survivorship: Physical Activity
    - Cautions in determining level of activity
        - Persistent and late effects of treatment (eg, cardiomyopathy)
        - Safety issues (ie, assessment of risk of falls)
    - Consider referral to specialty providers
        - Exercise oncology
        - Physical therapy
        - Occupational therapy
        - Physical medicine and rehabilitation
- Yoga (category 1)

# Nonpharmacologic: psychosocial interventions

- Psychosocial interventions (category 1)
    - CBT/BT (category 1)
        - CBT is a type of psychotherapy that focuses on recognizing and changing maladaptive thoughts and behaviors to reduce negative emotions and behaviors and to facilitate psychological adjustment
        - BT influences thoughts and promotes changes in behavior; it includes a variety of strategies (eg, cognitive restructuring, relaxation, mindfulness)
    - CBT-I (category 1)
        - Trial evidence shows CBT-I can improve fatigue among participants with insomnia symptoms
    - Mindfulness-based stress reduction (category 1)
    - Psycho-educational therapies/Educational therapies (category 1)
    - Supportive expressive therapies (category 1)
        - Eg, support groups, counseling, journal writing
        - Facilitate expression of emotion and foster support from one or more people

# Nonpharmacologic: other

- Bright white light therapy
    - 1250–10,000 lux, most frequently self-administered in the early morning for 30–40 minutes
    - Timing needs to be adjusted for those who sleep during the day (Xiao P, et al. J Pain Symptom Manage 2022;63:e188-e202)
- Acupuncture
- Nutrition consultation

# Pharmacologic

- Adjust current treatments for pain, sleep disturbances, and other symptoms and comorbidities, including drugs
    - Nonpharmacologic management of pain may be considered, such as palliative radiation, nerve blocks, or epidural management
- Consider methylphenidate, in consideration of other modifiable causes
    - Pharmacologic interventions remain investigational, but have been reported to improve symptoms of fatigue in some patients
    - Methylphenidate should be used cautiously, and should not be used until treatment- and disease-specific morbidities have been characterized or excluded
    - Optimal dosing and schedule have not been established for use of psychostimulants in older adults and patients with cancer
