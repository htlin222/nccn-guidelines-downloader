# Screening

- Screen all patients for fatigue using age-appropriate measures
    - At the initial visit
    - At regular intervals during cancer treatment
    - At regular intervals following cancer treatment
    - As clinically indicated
- Screen for multiple symptoms and signs, because fatigue is rarely an isolated symptom and most commonly occurs with other symptoms and signs in symptom clusters
    - Pain
    - Emotional distress
    - Anxiety
    - Depression
    - Anemia
    - Sleep disturbances
    - Changes in cognitive function
    - Symptoms and signs screened for may vary according to diagnosis, treatment, and stage of disease
- Assess fatigue systematically, as a subjective experience
    - Patient self-reports
    - Other sources of data
- Screen, assess, and manage fatigue according to clinical practice guidelines

# Ongoing management

- Recognize, evaluate, monitor, document, and treat fatigue promptly
    - For all age groups
    - At all stages of disease
    - In consideration of one's daily activities
    - Prior to, during, and following treatment

# Patient and family/caregiver education

- Inform patient and family/caregiver(s) that management of fatigue is an integral part of total health care
- Inform patient and family/caregiver(s) that fatigue can persist following treatment
- Inform patient to maintain an optimal level of activity
    - While considering the significant impact fatigue can have on the ability to perform in school, work, and daily life activities

# Interdisciplinary care and referral

- Implementation of guidelines for fatigue evaluation and management is best accomplished by interdisciplinary teams who are able to tailor interventions to the needs of the individual patient
- Consider referral to an appropriate specialist or supportive care provider, from diagnosis to end of life
    - Survivorship
    - Palliative care
    - Integrative medicine
    - Psychology
    - Psychiatry
    - Physical therapy
    - Exercise specialist
    - Occupational therapy
    - Physical medicine and rehabilitation
    - Where available, an oncology specialist within these fields is preferred

# Institutional and system standards

- Implement educational and training programs to ensure health care professionals have knowledge and skills in the assessment and management of fatigue
- Include cancer-related fatigue in clinical health outcome studies, as an independent variable and potential moderator of outcome
- Include quality of fatigue management in institutional continuous quality improvement projects
- Medical care contracts should include reimbursement for the management of fatigue
- Disability insurance should include coverage for the continuing effects of fatigue
