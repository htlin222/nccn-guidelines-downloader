# Source

- NCCN Cancer-Related Fatigue v2.2026, FT-4, p8
- Applies to a fatigue score of moderate or severe
    - Age >12 y, score 4–10
    - Age 7–12 y, score 3–5
    - Age 5–6 y, Tired
- All recommendations category 2A unless otherwise indicated

# Focused history

- Disease status, treatments, and recent hospitalizations
    - Cancer treatment (eg, radiation therapy, systemic therapy)
    - Consider recurrence and/or progression
    - Cancer-related sequelae
- Medications, including over-the-counter (OTC) medications, and supplements
    - Side effects
    - Drug interactions
    - Misuse
    - See NCCN Guidelines for Older Adult Oncology (OAO-H)
- Review of systems
- Functional status prior to cancer diagnosis
- In-depth fatigue history
    - Onset, pattern, and duration
    - Change over time
    - Associated or alleviating factors
    - Interference with function
    - Consider causes other than cancer and associated treatment
- Social support status / availability of caregivers
- Economic status and resources for obtaining tangible support

# Assessment of treatable contributing factors

- Pain
    - See NCCN Guidelines for Adult Cancer Pain
- Emotional distress
    - See NCCN Guidelines for Distress Management
- Psychiatric disorders
- Anemia
    - See Management of Cancer- and Chemotherapy-Induced Anemia in the NCCN Guidelines for Hematopoietic Growth Factors
- Sleep disturbance, including poor sleep hygiene
    - Insomnia
    - Hypersomnia / narcolepsy
    - Obstructive sleep apnea
    - Restless legs syndrome
    - Circadian rhythm sleep-wake disorders
    - See NCCN Guidelines for Survivorship
- Nutritional deficits / imbalance
    - Vitamin imbalance
    - Weight / caloric intake changes
    - Fluid electrolyte imbalance: sodium, potassium, calcium, and magnesium
- Decreased functional status
    - Physical activity level
    - Deconditioning
- Comorbidities
    - Alcohol and drug misuse and illicit substance use
    - Cardiac dysfunction
    - Endocrine dysfunction (eg, hot flashes, hypothyroidism, hypogonadism, adrenal insufficiency), with special concern for people receiving immunotherapy
    - Gastrointestinal dysfunction
    - Hepatic dysfunction
    - Infection
    - Neurologic dysfunction
    - Pulmonary dysfunction
    - Renal dysfunction
    - Rheumatologic or autoimmune disorders
    - Medications, including OTC medications, and supplements / side effects / drug interactions
    - See NCCN Guidelines for Management of Immunotherapy-Related Toxicities
