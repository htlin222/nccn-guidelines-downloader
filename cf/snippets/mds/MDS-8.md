# Clinical monitoring

- Clinical monitoring
- Psychosocial support, see NCCN Guidelines for Survivorship
- Quality-of-life assessment

# Transfusions

- RBC transfusions, CMV-safe, recommended for symptomatic anemia
    - Avoid transfusion for arbitrary hemoglobin thresholds in the absence of symptoms of active coronary disease, heart failure, or stroke
    - Where transfusion is necessary, transfuse the minimum units necessary to relieve symptoms of anemia or to return the patient to a safe hemoglobin level
- Platelet transfusions recommended for thrombocytopenic bleeding
    - Generally not used routinely in thrombocytopenia in the absence of bleeding, unless platelet count <10,000/mcL
- Irradiated products are suggested for transplant candidates

# Bleeding not controlled by platelet transfusion

- Aminocaproic acid or other antifibrinolytic agents may be considered
    - For bleeding refractory to platelet transfusions
    - For profound thrombocytopenia

# Iron chelation

- Consider daily chelation if >20 to 30 RBC transfusions have been received
    - Particularly for patients with lower-risk MDS (LOW/INT-1)
    - Particularly for potential transplant candidates
- Deferoxamine SC
- Deferasirox orally
    - Ophthalmic and auditory tests are recommended annually when using deferasirox
    - Monitor serum creatinine regularly upon starting deferasirox
- If serum ferritin >2500 ng/mL, aim to decrease ferritin to <1000 ng/mL
- Do not treat with deferasirox or deferoxamine if creatinine clearance is low (<40 mL/min)

# Cytokines

- ESAs, see Anemia Pathway (MDS-5)
    - ESAs refer to epoetin alfa and darbepoetin alfa
- G-CSF
    - G-CSF refers to filgrastim and tbofilgrastim
    - Not recommended for routine infection prophylaxis
    - Consider use in patients with neutropenia with recurrent or resistant infections
    - Combine with ESAs for anemia when indicated, see Anemia Pathway (MDS-5)
    - Platelet count should be monitored

# Clinically significant thrombocytopenia

- In patients with lower-risk MDS who have severe or life-threatening thrombocytopenia, consider treatment with a thrombopoietin receptor agonist

# Infection

- Antibiotics are recommended for bacterial infections
- Prophylaxis may be considered when starting patients on therapy, consistent with local hospital guidelines

# Post-transplant

- Antimicrobial prophylaxis at least as long as the patient is on IST
- Detailed recommendations are provided in the guidelines generated by the American Society for Transplantation and Cellular Therapy (ASTCT, formerly ASBMT)
- See NCCN Guidelines for Hematopoietic Cell Transplantation (HCT)
