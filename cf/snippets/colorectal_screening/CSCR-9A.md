# Prerequisites before any surveillance interval applies

- Surveillance intervals assume complete resection
- Surveillance intervals assume adequate bowel preparation
- Surveillance intervals assume complete examination
- Screening should be individualized
    - Include a discussion of the risks and benefits of each modality
    - See Screening Modality and Schedule (CSCR-A)

# Age limits for surveillance colonoscopy

- Surveillance colonoscopy is recommended in adults aged 45–75 years with a history of adenomas
- Ages 76–85 years, surveillance should be individualized
    - Include a discussion of risks and benefits of continued colonoscopy
    - Base it on comorbidity status
    - Base it on estimated life expectancy
    - Base it on findings on the last or the most recent colonoscopy

# Hyperplastic polyps 1 cm or larger

- There are limited data to support whether individuals with hyperplastic polyps ≥1 cm in size represent an increased risk group
- Several analyses suggest that many of the larger polyps classified as hyperplastic in the past were reclassified as SSPs/SSLs when reviewed by experts
- It is reasonable to follow patients with hyperplastic polyps ≥1 cm in size similarly to patients with SSPs/SSLs
    - Particularly if they have not been reviewed by an expert GI pathologist

# Low-risk adenomas and SSP/SSL(s)

- Available data suggest that individuals with low-risk adenomas or SSP/SSL(s) may not have an increased risk of metachronous advanced colorectal neoplasia compared to the general population
- Any recommendation for a shorter interval should include a discussion with the individual, based on an assessment of individual risk
    - Age
    - Family history
    - Comorbidity
    - The results of previous colonoscopies

# Cap on the surveillance interval after high-risk findings

- In patients who previously had any of the following, the surveillance interval should not be increased to >5 years
    - High-risk adenomas
    - High-risk SSP/SSLs
    - TSAs
    - Hyperplastic polyps ≥1 cm

# Germline testing for cumulative adenoma burden

- Consider germline testing for 10–19 cumulative adenomas if other factors suggest the possibility of a polyposis/CRC syndrome
    - Age of onset
    - Family or personal history of CRC
- Nine or fewer polyps may sometimes be associated with an inherited polyposis syndrome
    - In the setting of a strong family history
    - In the setting of younger age (<40 years)

# After germline testing

- If germline testing is negative, or if evaluation is not performed, repeat colonoscopy within 1–3 years
- Frequency of surveillance may be modified based on
    - Age at which the patient met the cumulative adenoma threshold, with more frequent surveillance favored for younger age at meeting threshold
    - Total number of adenomas at the most recent colonoscopy, with more frequent surveillance favored for higher adenoma burden at last colonoscopy
