# Age and life expectancy for screening

- CRC screening is recommended in adults aged 45-75 years who might have a life expectancy of ≥10 years
- Ages 76-85 years, the decision to screen should be individualized
    - Include a discussion of the risks and benefits
    - Base that discussion on comorbidity status and estimated life expectancy
    - Eligible individuals who have not been previously screened are most likely to benefit in this age group

# Serrated polyp terminology

- Sessile serrated polyp (SSP), sessile serrated lesion (SSL), and sessile serrated adenoma are synonymous
- SSP/SSL(s) are a type of serrated polyp that are not dysplastic
    - They can develop foci of dysplasia, and are then termed SSP/SSL with dysplasia (SSP/SSL-d)
- These guidelines use SSP/SSL for SSPs/SSLs without dysplasia
- These guidelines use SSP/SSL-d for SSP/SSL(s) with dysplasia
- Classification systems for serrated lesions are evolving
    - A recent WHO proposal suggests using the term sessile serrated lesion
- See CSCR-GLOS 3 of 7

# How serrated lesions are managed

- In general, SSP/SSL(s) are managed like tubular adenomas
- SSP/SSL-d with any grade dysplasia are managed like high-risk adenomas
    - But may need even more frequent surveillance
- Advanced SSP/SSL(s) are generally considered to have a comparable cancer risk to advanced adenomas
    - Managed similarly to advanced adenomas, rather than high-risk adenomas
    - That definition includes multiplicity

# Family history in second- and third-degree relatives

- Current risk estimates for a family history of CRC in only second- and third-degree relatives may not be sufficiently elevated to recommend increased screening
- There are some data showing that a second-degree relative, and to a lesser degree a third-degree relative, with early-onset (<50 years old) CRC increases risk of both CRC and early-onset CRC
- Some combinations of affected first-, second-, and third-degree relatives may increase risk sufficiently to alter screening guidelines
- If there are multiple distant relatives affected, consider evaluation for an inherited CRC syndrome in the family
