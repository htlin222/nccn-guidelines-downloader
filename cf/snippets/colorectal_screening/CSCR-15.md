# Why a stricture is not a benign finding

- There is risk of underlying cancer, particularly in patients with long-standing IBD
- Reported prevalence of dysplasia or cancer in colitis-associated colonic strictures
    - Up to 7% in Crohn's disease
    - Between 2% and 90% in ulcerative colitis
- Among strictures with negative surveillance biopsies, reported rates of dysplasia or cancer in follow-up
    - 2%-6% in Crohn's disease
    - 7.5%-27% in ulcerative colitis
- Consider surgery in patients with symptomatic or non-traversable strictures

# Traversable stricture

- Obtain extensive targeted biopsies from the stricture
- Consider assessment with chromoendoscopy if not already performed
- Referral to center with expertise in IBD
- Colonoscopy follow-up in 1 y if surgery not performed

# Non-traversable stricture

- Consider referral to an IBD or therapeutic endoscopy expert with expertise in endoscopic management of IBD-associated colonic strictures
- And/or consider referral to a surgeon with expertise in IBD for surgical resection
    - A surgical consult may include a discussion about surveillance and colectomy, based on multiple factors including
        - Other visible dysplastic lesions in the same segment
        - Histology
        - A discussion with the patient about risks and benefits of each approach
