# Source

- NCCN Colorectal Cancer Screening v2.2026, CSCR-12, p27
- Applies to increased risk based on a personal history of inflammatory bowel disease
    - Ulcerative colitis
    - Crohn's colitis
- If PSC is present, annual surveillance colonoscopies should be started independent of the individual's time since symptom onset or colonoscopic findings, and instead should be initiated at the time of PSC diagnosis
- Family history of CRC is another important risk factor for developing CRC in patients with IBD, and such individuals may benefit from earlier initiation of colonoscopic surveillance
- All recommendations are category 2A unless otherwise indicated

# Risk factors for dysplasia in either ulcerative colitis or Crohn's disease

- PSC
- Colonic stricture
- Family history of CRC, particularly when diagnosed age <50 years
- Personal history of dysplasia
- Severe long-standing inflammation
- Dense pseudopolyposis

# Who needs increased-risk surveillance

- Crohn's disease with colitis historically involving more than one third of the colon requires increased-risk surveillance
- Isolated small bowel Crohn's disease should follow average-risk screening and surveillance recommendations
- Isolated ulcerative proctitis without historical extension of inflammation proximal to the rectum carries little or no increased risk for CRC compared with the population at large
    - Consider 5-year follow-up or average-risk follow-up

# Initiation of surveillance

- 8 y after onset of symptoms
    - If PSC is present, start annual surveillance colonoscopies at the time of PSC diagnosis instead
- Surveillance endoscopy ideally should be performed during quiescent disease
    - To optimize endoscopic detection of subtle dysplastic lesions
    - To avoid false-positive pathology findings of dysplasia
- Targeted biopsies improve detection of dysplasia, and should be performed for surveillance colonoscopies in patients with ulcerative colitis
- High-definition colonoscopes are suggested
    - If using standard-definition colonoscopes, perform non-targeted biopsies in 4 quadrants every 10 cm and dye spray chromoendoscopy is recommended

# Surveillance modality and schedule

- Chromoendoscopy (dye spray or high-definition virtual)
    - Targeted biopsies of any mucosal abnormality
        - Consider 2 biopsies in every bowel segment
            - Place them in separate specimen jars, to document microscopic disease activity and extent of disease involvement
        - Non-targeted (random) biopsies as described above should be considered in addition to targeted biopsies in patients with a history of dysplasia or primary sclerosing cholangitis (PSC)
- OR
- Colonoscopy
    - High-definition white light endoscopy (HD-WLE)
        - Targeted biopsies of any mucosal abnormality
        - Random 4-quadrant biopsies every 10 cm, with ≥32 total samples
- If HD-WLE or chromoendoscopy is not available, refer to institutions with expertise in these modalities when practical
