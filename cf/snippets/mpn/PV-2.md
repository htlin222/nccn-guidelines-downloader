# Initial management

- Manage cardiovascular risk factors (MPN-H)
- Aspirin 81–100 mg/day
    - Aspirin twice daily may be considered for patients with refractory symptoms
- Phlebotomy, to maintain hematocrit <45%
    - The <45% target is based on the data from the CYTO-PV study
    - There may be situations in which a lower hematocrit cutoff may be appropriate, and it should be individualized (eg, progressive symptoms)
- Start cytoreductive therapy

# Cytoreductive therapy regimens

- Preferred
    - Hydroxyurea
    - Ropeginterferon alfa-2b-njft
- Other Recommended
    - Peginterferon alfa-2a
        - An option for younger patients, or in pregnant patients in need of cytoreductive therapy (MPN-G 2 of 2)
        - If peginterferon alfa-2a is unavailable, use of other available pegylated interferons (eg, ropeginterferon alfa-2b-njft) is appropriate (MPN-G 2 of 2)
- Useful in Certain Circumstances
    - Ruxolitinib

# Monitoring

- Monitor for new thrombosis or bleeding
- Monitor response and signs/symptoms of disease progression (MPN-E 1 of 2) as clinically indicated
    - Supportive care for patients with MPN, see MPN-F
    - Normalization of blood counts after initiation of treatment is usually a goal in clinical practice, but it is not associated with long-term clinical benefit, and there are no evidence-based data to recommend a target WBC or platelet count for patients receiving cytoreductive therapy
    - In selected patients with a severe thrombotic event or other disease-related symptoms, normalization of blood counts might be a goal of treatment
    - Response criteria were developed mainly for use in clinical trials; clinical benefit may not reach the threshold of the 2013 IWG-MRT and ELN Response Criteria for PV (PV-A)
    - Response assessment should be done based on the improvement of disease-related symptoms at the discretion of the clinician

# Potential indications for change of cytoreductive therapy

- Intolerance or resistance to prior cytoreductive treatment
    - Definition of intolerance/resistance to hydroxyurea, see MPN-I
- New thrombosis or disease-related major bleeding
- Frequent phlebotomy, or intolerant of phlebotomy
- Splenomegaly
- Progressive thrombocytosis and/or leukocytosis
- Disease-related symptoms (eg, pruritus, night sweats, fatigue)

# Treatment for inadequate response or loss of response

- Preferred
    - Clinical trial
    - Ruxolitinib (category 1 for high-risk PV for hydroxyurea resistance or intolerance)
        - Ruxolitinib is FDA approved for patients with PV who have had an inadequate response to or are intolerant of hydroxyurea
        - Ruxolitinib may have activity after inadequate response or loss of response to other agents besides hydroxyurea
- Other Recommended
    - Hydroxyurea, if not previously used
    - Peginterferon alfa-2a, if not previously used
    - Ropeginterferon alfa-2b-njft, if not previously used
