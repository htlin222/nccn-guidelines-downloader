# Treatment planning

- Multidisciplinary consultation at center with specialized expertise to discuss options
- Radiologic staging
    - MRI with and without contrast, or CT with contrast, and/or ultrasound
    - Extensive disease includes deep involvement such as bone, named nerves, and deep soft tissue
    - If disease of named nerve(s) is suspected, MRI with and without contrast is preferred
    - If bone disease is suspected, CT with contrast is preferred unless contraindicated
    - MRI with and without contrast of the brain may be considered to rule out subclinical cortical involvement in cases with bone invasion
    - Imaging modality and targeted area at the discretion of the treating team, based on the suspected extent of disease (ie, local, regional, metastatic)
    - Histologic confirmation is sufficient to diagnose local recurrence, but MRI with and without contrast can be considered to assess extent of local disease
    - For nodal or distant metastases, histologic analysis and/or other imaging modalities can be used for confirmation and to gauge extent of disease
    - If exam of the nodal basin is normal, discuss and consider radiologic imaging of the nodal basin
    - If abnormal lymph nodes are identified by imaging studies, go to SCC-8
- Consider SLNB in cases that are recurrent or with multiple high-risk features
    - Discuss and consider SLNB prior to or at time of PDEMA (category 2B)
    - Applies to patients with very-high-risk CSCC that is recurrent or has multiple risk factors placing them in the very-high-risk group, and normal exam of the draining nodal basin
    - For positive SLNB, recommend multidisciplinary discussion after obtaining radiologic staging of the neck, chest, abdomen, and pelvis if not yet completed
        - In the absence of metastatic disease, consider completion lymphadenectomy of the affected nodal basin
        - If surgery is not an option due to patient preference or poor performance status, then consider radiation therapy
        - Following neck dissection, refer to SCC-6 for additional recommendations
    - For tumors being considered for SLNB, delay reconstruction if not able to close primarily with minimal undermining
- Consider neoadjuvant therapy with Cemiplimab-rwlc, per Principles of Systemic Therapy, if any of:
    - Nonreactive nonkeratoacanthomatous rapid growth tumors
    - In-transit metastasis
    - Borderline resectable
    - Surgery alone may not be curative or may result in significant functional limitation

# Primary treatment

- Mohs or other forms of PDEMA, preferred for very high risk
    - When Mohs is being performed and the preoperative biopsy is considered insufficient for providing all the staging information required to properly treat the tumor, submission of the central specimen for vertical paraffin-embedded permanent sections or documentation of staging parameters in Mohs report is recommended
    - As per other appropriate use Guidelines
    - Mohs surgery should be performed by dermatologic surgeons who have specialized training and experience in this procedure
    - PDEMA with permanent section analysis or intraoperative frozen section analysis is an alternative to Mohs (SCC-F)
    - If invasion to parotid fascia, superficial parotidectomy may be indicated
    - For tumors being considered for SLNB, delay reconstruction if not able to close primarily with minimal undermining
- or Standard excision with wider surgical margins
    - Appropriate margins should be determined case by case based on tumor and patient-specific factors
    - Postoperative margin assessment
    - Second intention healing, linear repair, or skin graft
- or For nonsurgical candidates, consider definitive RT (SCC-D)
    - Determination of the appropriateness of RT should be performed together with a radiation oncologist

# Additional treatment, positive margins

- Re-resect, if feasible
- or RT (SCC-D)
    - Determination of the appropriateness of RT should be performed together with a radiation oncologist
- or if surgery and/or RT are not curative, treat as laCSCC disease (SCC-7)

# Additional treatment, negative margins

- If extensive perineural, large or named nerve involvement, or other poor prognostic features, consider adjuvant RT (SCC-D)
    - Large nerve involvement is defined by the AJCC Cancer Staging Manual, 8th Edition for CSCC of the head and neck as ≥0.1 mm or nerve involvement deeper than the dermis
        - Most nerves deep to the dermis are >0.1 mm
    - Determination of the appropriateness of RT should be performed together with a radiation oncologist
    - Adjuvant RT can be considered for CSCCs with gross clinical radiologic PNI
    - Adjuvant RT can be considered for multifocal histologic nerve invasion
    - Adjuvant RT can be considered for ≥6 cm tumor diameter
    - Adjuvant RT can be considered for recurrent tumors
    - Adjuvant RT can be considered for high risk for regional or distant metastasis
    - Adjuvant RT can be considered for close surgical margins where further surgery cannot be performed
    - Adjuvant RT can be considered for desmoplastic or infiltrative tumors in patients who are chronically immunosuppressed
- In patients with extremely high risk nodal and non-nodal features, consider Cemiplimab-rwlc following adjuvant RT (category 1 preferred)
    - Studied in CSCC with very-high-risk for recurrence due to nodal features
        - Extracapsular extension with largest node ≥20 mm in diameter
        - or ≥3 involved nodes
    - or due to non-nodal features
        - In-transit metastases
        - T4 lesion, with bone invasion
        - PNI
        - Locally recurrent tumor with ≥1 additional risk feature
