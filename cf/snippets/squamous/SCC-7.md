# Primary treatment

- Consider neoadjuvant therapy with Cemiplimab-rwlc and/or clinical trial, after multidisciplinary discussion
    - Consider if the tumor has very rapid growth
    - Consider if in-transit metastasis
    - Consider if lymphovascular invasion
    - Consider if borderline resectable
    - Consider if surgery alone may not be curative, or may result in significant functional limitation
- Mohs or other forms of PDEMA
    - Mohs surgery should be performed by dermatologic surgeons who have specialized training and experience in this procedure
    - As per other appropriate use Guidelines
    - When Mohs is being performed and the preoperative biopsy is considered insufficient for providing all the staging information required to properly treat the tumor, submit the central specimen for vertical paraffin-embedded permanent sections, or document staging parameters in the Mohs report
    - PDEMA with permanent section analysis or intraoperative frozen section analysis is an alternative to Mohs, see Principles of PDEMA Technique (SCC-F)
    - If invasion to parotid fascia, superficial parotidectomy may be indicated
    - For tumors being considered for SLNB, delay reconstruction if not able to close primarily with minimal undermining
- Standard excision with wider surgical margins, and postoperative margin assessment, and second intention healing, linear repair, or skin graft
    - Appropriate margins should be determined case by case based on tumor and patient-specific factors
    - For tumors being considered for SLNB, delay reconstruction if not able to close primarily with minimal undermining

# Additional treatment - positive margins

- Re-resect, if feasible
- Or RT with or without systemic therapy
    - Determination of the appropriateness of RT should be performed together with a radiation oncologist
- Or systemic therapy, if curative RT is not feasible

# Additional treatment - negative margins

- If extensive perineural involvement, large or named nerve involvement, or other poor prognostic features, consider adjuvant RT
    - Large nerve involvement is defined by the AJCC Cancer Staging Manual, 8th Edition for CSCC of the head and neck as 0.1 mm or greater, or nerve involvement deeper than the dermis
    - Most nerves deep to the dermis are greater than 0.1 mm
    - Determination of the appropriateness of RT should be performed together with a radiation oncologist
    - Adjuvant RT can be considered for gross clinical radiologic PNI
    - Adjuvant RT can be considered for multifocal histologic nerve invasion
    - Adjuvant RT can be considered for tumor diameter 6 cm or greater
    - Adjuvant RT can be considered for recurrent tumors
    - Adjuvant RT can be considered for high risk for regional or distant metastasis
    - Adjuvant RT can be considered for close surgical margins where further surgery cannot be performed
    - Adjuvant RT can be considered for desmoplastic or infiltrative tumors in patients who are chronically immunosuppressed

# Treatment for nonsurgical candidates

- RT with or without systemic therapy
    - Determination of the appropriateness of RT should be performed together with a radiation oncologist
- Systemic therapy, if curative RT is not feasible
