# Definition

- Previously classified as primary cutaneous lymphomas
- Re-classified as LPD by both the 2022 WHO classification and the ICC classifications
    - Because of their excellent prognosis
    - Because of the absence of extracutaneous progression
- Must be distinguished from malignant CTCL
- The primary cutaneous indolent LPDs include two entities, see Table 1 (INCTLD-2)
    - Primary cutaneous CD4+ small/medium T-cell LPD (PCSM-TLPD)
    - Primary cutaneous CD8+ acral T-cell LPD (PCA-TLPD)

# Clinical presentation

- Both PCSM-TLPD and PCA-TLPD commonly have a clonal T-cell population by TCR gene rearrangement studies
- PCSM-TLPD typically shows a CD4+ T-follicular helper (TFH) phenotype
- PCA-TLPD expresses CD8 and a cytotoxic phenotype
- Both present with isolated nodules or plaques
    - Frequent spontaneous regression after biopsy

# Differential diagnosis

- Other reactive and neoplastic lymphoid proliferations
- Most importantly, distinguish from
    - Solitary presentation of MF
    - A subset of primary cutaneous CD30+ T-cell LPDs
    - Primary or secondary involvement by PTCL-NOS
- Consider alternative diagnoses, such as cutaneous manifestations of PTCL, in cases that are not behaving indolently
    - Generalized skin lesions
    - Rapidly growing tumors
    - Loss of pan T-cell markers

# Treatment principles

- Aggressive treatment is not needed for confirmed cases of primary cutaneous indolent LPD
- Imaging can be considered as part of the diagnostic workup, to exclude systemic lymphoma
    - Only for cases in which cutaneous manifestation of a systemic lymphoma is considered clinically
    - Further surveillance imaging is not needed for confirmed cases of primary cutaneous indolent LPD
- For symptomatic patients, can be considered
    - Clinical observation
    - Topical or intralesional steroids
    - Low-dose RT
    - Surgical excision
- Systemic therapy is usually not needed and frequently can be avoided
