# Essential

- History and complete physical examination
    - Complete skin examination
        - Monitoring the size and number of lesions will assist with response assessment
    - Palpation of peripheral LN regions
    - Liver or spleen enlargement
- CBC with differential
- Comprehensive metabolic panel
- LDH
- C/A/P CT with contrast, or integrated whole body FDG-PET/CT
    - Arms and legs included when disease assessment of the entire body is needed
    - Patients with T-cell lymphomas often have extranodal disease, which may be inadequately imaged by CT; FDG-PET scan may be preferred in these instances
- Biopsy suspicious nodes
    - Biopsy of enlarged LNs or suspected extracutaneous sites
    - Excisional or adequate core needle biopsy is preferred
    - An FNA biopsy alone is not sufficient for the initial diagnosis of lymphoma
    - Rebiopsy if consult material is nondiagnostic
    - Due to overlapping immunophenotype and morphology, use caution to avoid diagnosing CD30+ T-cell in LNs as Hodgkin lymphoma (Eberle FC, et al. Am J Surg Pathol 2012;36:716-725)
    - Consider systemic ALCL, regional LN involvement with PC-ALCL, or LN involvement with transformed MF
    - Consider PC-ALCL if in draining LNs only
- Assess for distress
    - Refer to the NCCN Distress Thermometer and Problem List, which includes social determinants of health
    - See NCCN Guidelines for Distress Management (DIS-A)

# Useful in certain circumstances

- Bone marrow aspiration and biopsy
    - Optional for solitary cutaneous ALCL, or for cutaneous ALCL without extracutaneous involvement on imaging
- Pregnancy testing in patients of childbearing potential, if contemplating treatments that are contraindicated in pregnancy
    - Many skin-directed and systemic therapies are contraindicated or are of unknown safety in pregnancy
    - Refer to individual drug information
- Discuss fertility preservation
    - Options include sperm banking, IVF, or ovarian tissue or oocyte cryopreservation
