# Essential

- Complete skin examination for any sign of benign or malignant skin lesions
- Biopsy of suspicious skin sites
    - Multiple biopsies may be necessary to capture the pathologic variability of disease at diagnosis
    - Adequate biopsy, by punch, incisional, or excisional, of all types of clinical lesions present will aid in final diagnosis
    - Review of a sufficient number of slides with adequate material to perform a comprehensive workup, and/or at least one paraffin block representative of the tumor
        - Done by a pathologist with expertise in the diagnosis of CTCLs
    - Rebiopsy if pathologic findings are non-diagnostic and/or discordant with the clinical presentation
- Adequate immunophenotyping on skin biopsy to establish diagnosis
    - IHC panel may include: CD3, CD4, CD8, CD20, CD30, CD56, ALK
    - Typical immunophenotype: CD30+ (>75% cells), CD4+ with variable loss of CD2/CD5/CD3, CD8+ (<5%), cytotoxic granule proteins positive
    - ALK positivity and t(2;5) translocation is typically absent in PC-ALCL and LyP
    - For differential diagnosis of NK/T-cell neoplasms, see Use of Immunophenotyping/Genetic Testing in the NCCN Guidelines for T-Cell Lymphomas

# Useful in certain circumstances

- Expanded IHC panel on skin biopsy may include: CD2, CD5, CD7, CD25, TIA1, granzyme B, perforin, IRF4/MUM1, EMA, TCR beta, TCR delta
- EBER-ISH
- Molecular analysis to detect clonal TRB and TRG gene rearrangements, or other assessment of clonality (PCLYM-B)
    - Clonal TRB and TRG gene rearrangements alone are not sufficient for diagnosis, as these can also be seen in patients with non-malignant conditions
    - Interpret results in the context of the overall presentation; see Principles of Biomarker Testing in the NCCN Guidelines for T-Cell Lymphomas (TCLYM-A)
