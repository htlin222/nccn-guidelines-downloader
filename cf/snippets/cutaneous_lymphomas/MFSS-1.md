# Essential, skin biopsy

- Biopsy of suspicious skin sites
    - Multiple biopsies may be necessary to capture the pathologic variability of disease at diagnosis
- Review of a sufficient number of slides with adequate material to perform a comprehensive workup as described below
    - And/or at least one paraffin block representative of the lesion
    - Review should be done by a pathologist with expertise in the diagnosis of CTCLs
    - Rebiopsy if pathologic findings are non-diagnostic and/or discordant with the clinical presentation
    - Presence of LCT or areas of folliculotropism may have important implications for selection of therapy and outcome, and should be included in pathology reports

# Essential, immunophenotype and clonality

- IHC panel of skin biopsy may include: CD2, CD3, CD4, CD5, CD7, CD8, CD20, CD30
    - Reference: Pimpinelli N, et al. J Am Acad Dermatol 2005;53:1053-1063
    - For immunophenotyping/genetic testing in the differential diagnosis of NK/T-cell neoplasms, see NCCN Guidelines for T-Cell Lymphomas
    - Typical immunophenotype: CD2+, CD3+, CD5+, CD7-, CD4+, CD8- (rarely CD8+), CD30-/+, cytotoxic granule proteins negative
- Molecular analysis to detect clonal TRB and TRG gene rearrangements, or other assessment of clonality (PCLYM-B)
    - Clonal TRB and TRG gene rearrangements alone are not sufficient for diagnosis, as these can also be seen in patients with non-malignant conditions
    - Results should be interpreted in the context of overall presentation

# Useful in certain circumstances

- Assessment of peripheral blood for Sezary cells (PCLYM-B)
    - Indicated for extensive skin disease where skin biopsy is not diagnostic
    - Indicated for extensive patch or erythrodermic skin disease
    - Indicated when findings are strongly suggestive but not diagnostic of advanced-stage disease
    - Flow cytometry to assess and quantitate an expanded T-cell population with aberrant immunophenotype; see MFSS-3 for specifics
        - Flow cytometry panel may include: CD3, CD4, CD7, CD8, CD26, CD45, TRBC1
        - Assess for expanded CD4+ cells with increased CD4/CD8 ratio, or with abnormal immunophenotype including loss of CD7 or CD26
        - TRBC1 may contribute in detecting clonality and can improve the specificity of detecting Sezary cells when CD7 and CD26 are lost
    - Sezary cell preparation is less useful than flow cytometry due to the subjective nature of the process, but may still be useful in cases where an aberrant immunophenotype is not detected
- IHC panel of skin biopsy may include: CD25, CD56, TIA1, granzyme B, TCR beta, TCR delta, CXCL13, inducible T-cell co-stimulator (ICOS), and programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1)
    - CCR4; loss of CCR4 expression and emergence of CCR4 genomic alterations might be associated with resistance to mogamulizumab
    - Presence of LCT or areas of folliculotropism should be included in pathology reports
- Consider FISH or MGPT in selected cases (PCLYM-B)
- Assessment of human T-cell lymphotropic virus (HTLV)-1/2 by serology or other methods is encouraged, as results can impact therapy
    - See map for prevalence of HTLV-1/2 by geographic region
    - HTLV-1 has been described in patients in non-endemic areas
