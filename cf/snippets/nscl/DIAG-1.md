- NCCN Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer v7.2026, DIAG-1, p20
- Applies to a nodule suspicious for lung cancer found as an incidental finding
- Also applies to lung nodules in asymptomatic patients at high risk detected during lung cancer screening with LDCT
    - For the screening-detected setting, see NCCN Guidelines for Lung Cancer Screening
- Risk calculators can be used to quantify individual patient and radiologic factors, but do not replace evaluation by a multidisciplinary diagnostic team with substantial experience in the diagnosis of lung cancer

# Patient factors to assess

- Age
- Smoking history
- Previous cancer history
- Family history
- Occupational exposures
- Other lung disease
    - Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD)
    - Pulmonary fibrosis
- Exposure to infectious agents
    - Endemic areas of fungal infections
    - Tuberculosis
- Risk factors or history suggestive of infection
    - Immune suppression
    - Aspiration
    - Infectious respiratory symptoms

# Radiologic factors to assess

- Size of the pulmonary nodule
- Shape of the pulmonary nodule
- Density of the pulmonary nodule
- Associated parenchymal abnormalities
    - Scarring
    - Suspicion of inflammatory changes
- FDG avidity on FDG-PET/CT imaging
- Change or stability compared with a previous imaging study
    - This is the most important radiologic factor
- Principles of diagnostic evaluation (DIAG-A)

# Actions at this visit

- Multidisciplinary evaluation
    - Include thoracic surgeons, thoracic radiologists, and pulmonologists
    - Purpose is to determine the likelihood of a cancer diagnosis and the optimal diagnostic or follow-up strategy
- Smoking cessation counseling
