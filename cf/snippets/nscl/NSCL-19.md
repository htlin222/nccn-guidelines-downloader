# Clinical presentation: do for every patient

- Establish histologic subtype, Principles of Pathologic Review (NSCL-A)
    - With adequate tissue for biomarker testing
    - Consider rebiopsy if appropriate
    - Consider plasma testing if appropriate
        - Complete biomarker testing including molecular assessment of EGFR, KRAS, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, NTRK1/2/3, MET, RET, ERBB2 (HER2), and NRG1 via biopsy and/or plasma testing
        - Combinations of tissue and plasma testing, either concurrently or in sequence, are acceptable
        - Concurrent testing can improve time to test results and should be considered in the appropriate clinical situation
        - A negative result, meaning absence of definitive driver mutation, by one method suggests the use of a complementary method
        - Treatment is guided by available results; if unknown, treat as though the patient does not have driver oncogenes
        - If therapy must start urgently while biomarker testing is pending, consider holding immunotherapy for one cycle, unless it is confirmed that no driver mutations are present
- Smoking cessation counseling
- Integrate palliative care (NCCN Guidelines for Palliative Care)
    - Temel JS, et al. N Engl J Med 2010;363:733-742

# Biomarker testing: adenocarcinoma, large cell, or NSCLC NOS

- Biomarker testing, Principles of Biomarker Analysis (NSCL-H), including
    - EGFR mutation (category 1)
    - ALK (category 1)
    - KRAS
    - ROS1
    - BRAF
    - NTRK1/2/3
    - MET exon 14 skipping
    - RET (category 1)
    - ERBB2 (HER2)
    - NRG1
    - HER2 immunohistochemistry (IHC)
        - During initial evaluation the priority is PD-L1 testing and multigene panel testing (MGPT); if tissue allows, additional testing such as HER2 IHC can be performed
    - HGF receptor (c-Met) IHC
        - During initial evaluation the priority is PD-L1 testing and MGPT; if tissue allows, additional testing such as HGF receptor (c-Met) IHC can be performed
- Testing should be conducted as part of multigene panel testing (MGPT)
- PD-L1 testing (category 1)

# Biomarker testing: squamous cell carcinoma

- Consider biomarker testing, Principles of Biomarker Analysis (NSCL-H), including
    - EGFR mutation
    - ALK
    - KRAS
    - ROS1
    - BRAF
    - NTRK1/2/3
    - MET exon 14 skipping
    - RET
    - ERBB2 (HER2)
    - NRG1
    - HER2 IHC
        - During initial evaluation the priority is PD-L1 testing and MGPT; if tissue allows, additional testing such as HER2 IHC can be performed
    - Supporting references: Lam VK, et al. Clin Lung Cancer 2019;20:30-36.e3; Sands JM, et al. Lung Cancer 2020;140:35-41
- Testing should be conducted as part of MGPT
- PD-L1 testing (category 1)

# What MGPT has to cover

- The NCCN NSCLC Guidelines Panel strongly advises MGPT
    - Goal is to identify rare driver mutations for which effective drugs may already be available
    - Goal is also to appropriately counsel patients regarding the availability of clinical trials
- MGPT is molecular testing that identifies all biomarkers listed in NSCL-20, in a single assay or a combination of a limited number of assays
    - Optimally it also identifies emerging biomarkers, Emerging Biomarkers to Identify Patients for Therapies (NSCL-I)
- Tiered approaches based on low prevalence of co-occurring biomarkers are acceptable
- MGPT is a key component of the improvement of care of patients with NSCLC
