# What to tell every person

- Inform of the health consequences of tobacco consumption and exposure to tobacco smoke
- Inform of the addictive nature of tobacco
- Inform of the mortal threat posed by tobacco consumption and exposure to tobacco smoke
- Approximately 85% to 90% of lung cancer cases are caused by voluntary or involuntary (second-hand) cigarette smoking
- Surgeon General reports show that both active smoking and second-hand smoke cause lung cancer
    - Living with a person who smokes carries a 20% to 30% increase in the risk for lung cancer

# Risks of persistent smoking

- Second primary cancers
- Treatment complications
- Drug interactions
- Other tobacco-related medical conditions
- Diminished quality of life
- Reduced survival

# Nicotine habituation

- The delivery system of lung carcinogens also contains the highly addictive substance, nicotine
- Identify, counsel, and treat people with nicotine habituation, per AHRQ Guidelines
- See the NCCN Guidelines for Smoking Cessation

# Chemoprevention

- People who smoke or who formerly smoked have significant risk for the development of lung cancer
- Chemoprevention agents are not yet established for these individuals
- When possible, encourage enrollment in chemoprevention trials

# Screening

- Lung cancer screening using low-dose CT (LDCT) is recommended in select individuals at high risk for lung cancer who smoke or formerly smoked
    - See the NCCN Guidelines for Lung Cancer Screening
- Lung cancer in individuals who do not smoke is increasing worldwide
    - Risk factors to augment screening in the future are not yet established and need more research

# Population-level measures

- Effective public health policies to prevent initiation of smoking
- FDA oversight of tobacco products, and other tobacco control measures
- Effective legislative, executive, administrative, or other measures at the appropriate governmental level, to protect all persons from exposure to tobacco smoke
