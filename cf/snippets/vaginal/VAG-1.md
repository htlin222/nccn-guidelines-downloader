# History and physical

- History and physical (H&P)
    - Sexual history
    - Immunosuppression
    - Prior hysterectomy
    - Smoking history
    - Gynecologic and anorectal symptoms
- Pelvic exam
    - Bimanual and rectovaginal
    - Cervical evaluation and pap smear
    - Colposcopy
    - Vulvar evaluation

# Rule out a non-vaginal primary

- Rule out synchronous anorectal, cervical, endometrial, or vulvar primary with vaginal metastasis or extension
- Rule out recurrent disease from prior malignancy
- Only a minority of vaginal cancers originate in the vagina; the remaining are generally metastatic from other sites
- If vaginal lesion(s) involve the cervix or vulva, it is not considered vaginal cancer
    - Consult the appropriate treatment algorithm, see NCCN Guidelines for Cervical Cancer or NCCN Guidelines for Vulvar Cancer

# Procedures and imaging

- Consider examination under anesthesia (EUA) with biopsies, as clinically indicated
    - Consider cystoscopy/proctoscopy
    - Principles of Surgery (VAG-E)
- Imaging
    - Principles of Imaging (VAG-B)

# Laboratory and risk-factor workup

- Complete blood count (CBC)
- Comprehensive metabolic panel (CMP)
- Human papillomavirus (HPV) testing in select patients
- Human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) testing in select patients
    - Consider HIV testing, especially in younger patients
    - Patients with vaginal cancer and HIV should be referred to an HIV specialist, and should be treated for vaginal cancer as per these guidelines
    - Modifications to cancer treatment should not be made solely on the basis of HIV status
    - See NCCN Guidelines for Cancer in People with HIV
- Smoking cessation and counseling intervention, if indicated
    - See NCCN Guidelines for Smoking Cessation
