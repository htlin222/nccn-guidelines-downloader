# Treatment

- Excision with Mohs or other forms of peripheral and deep en face margin assessment (PDEMA)
    - The most commonly used form of PDEMA is Mohs
    - See Principles of PDEMA Technique in NCCN Guidelines for Squamous Cell Skin Cancer
    - When anatomic structures at the deep margin (eg, major vessels, nerves, bone) preclude complete histologic evaluation of the marginal surface, use Mohs or other forms of PDEMA to evaluate as much of the marginal surface as feasible
        - Treatment considerations for non-visualized areas may be the subject of multidisciplinary discussion
    - If PDEMA is unavailable, consider wide excision
    - Wide undermining is discouraged prior to confirmation of clear margins
        - Difficulty of interpreting subsequent re-excised margins
        - Risk of concealing residual tumor below mobilized tissue
    - Principles of Surgery (DFSP-B)
- Examine debulking specimens from all excisions to identify fibrosarcomatous transformation of dermatofibrosarcoma protuberans (FS-DFSP), when sufficient clinical tumor is available
- For unresectable or borderline resectable disease, consider tumor biomarker testing and neoadjuvant imatinib
    - Consider neoadjuvant imatinib for patients in whom resection with negative margins may result in unacceptable functional or cosmetic outcomes

# If transformation to fibrosarcoma or other sarcoma subtypes is identified

- Multidisciplinary consultation for consideration of further treatment and surveillance is recommended
- FS-DFSP is associated with a higher metastasis risk
- Refer the patient to a center with expertise in management of soft tissue sarcomas
- See NCCN Guidelines for Soft Tissue Sarcoma for multimodal therapy and surveillance considerations, including CT of chest

# Adjuvant treatment

- Negative surgical margins
    - Observation
- Positive surgical margins
    - Re-resection until margins clear or surgery is not possible
        - Re-resection is by Mohs or other forms of PDEMA; if PDEMA is unavailable, consider wide excision (DFSP-B)
    - Negative margins after re-resection
        - Observation
    - Positive margins after re-resection
        - Consider radiation therapy (RT) if margins are deemed narrow by treating physician and further resection would not be feasible upon recurrence
        - Principles of Radiation Therapy (DFSP-C)
