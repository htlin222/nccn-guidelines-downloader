# Source

- NCCN Cancer in People with HIV v2.2026, HIV-4, p10
- Applies to non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) in people with HIV (PWH)
- Risk of lung cancer is 2 to 5 times higher in PWH than in individuals who are HIV-negative
- Lung cancer accounts for approximately 11% of cancers diagnosed in this population
- All recommendations category 2A unless otherwise indicated

# Screening and risk reduction

- Screen for lung cancer with low-dose CT as per the NCCN Guidelines for Lung Cancer Screening
    - Note that PWH may develop lung cancer at a younger age compared to the general population
    - Investigations are ongoing to determine if modifications to screening guidelines for PWH are warranted
- Discuss smoking cessation, see NCCN Guidelines for Smoking Cessation

# Workup of lung nodules and lymphadenopathy

- PWH may be more likely to have benign lung nodules than patients without HIV
    - Perform an infectious disease workup when indicated
    - Treatment for possible non-malignant diagnoses can be considered before biopsy
    - If concurrent pulmonary Kaposi sarcoma is suspected, take precautions because increased bleeding may occur with biopsies
- Lung biopsies should be cultured and stained for bacteria, fungi, and mycobacteria
- Lymphadenopathy
    - Consider non-malignant causes for lymphadenopathy in PWH
    - Biopsy suspicious PET-avid lymphadenopathy to differentiate between nodal metastasis and infectious etiology
        - Consult with HIV specialist
        - If negative for cancer, refer for an infectious disease workup

# Workup of brain lesions with advanced HIV-related immunosuppression

- Consider consultation with HIV specialist
- Evaluate to rule out infectious processes (eg, toxoplasmosis)
- Evaluate to rule out other malignancies such as NHL
- Consider relative risks based on viral load and CD4+ T-cell count to further inform these assessments

# Treatment considerations

- Co-manage NSCLC in PWH with an oncologist and an HIV specialist
- Treat PWH for NSCLC as per the NCCN Guidelines for Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer
- Do not modify cancer treatment solely based on HIV status
- Poor performance status in PWH and NSCLC may be from HIV, cancer, or other causes
    - Consider the reason for poor performance status when making treatment decisions
    - ART is recommended for all PWH and may improve performance status
- Drug interactions can occur in patients with NSCLC and HIV
    - Consult both an HIV pharmacist and an oncology pharmacist
    - See Principles of Systemic Therapy and Drug-Drug Interactions (HIV-B)

# Immune checkpoint inhibitors

- Evolving data suggest that ICIs are generally safe and effective in PWH, with the following considerations
- Concurrent KSHV infection
    - There may be increased risk of KSHV-associated inflammatory syndromes such as MCD or KICS when ICIs are used
    - If the patient has a history of KSHV-associated diseases, consider more frequent monitoring of signs and symptoms of KICS or MCD
    - Unexplained fevers should prompt workup of MCD and KICS with
        - C-reactive protein
        - KSHV serum viral load
        - SPEP
        - IL-6
        - IL-10
    - Diagnosis of KICS requires excisional biopsy of lymphadenopathy to exclude MCD
- Opportunistic infections
    - Use of ICIs has been linked to opportunistic infections including TB reactivation and flare of mycobacterial disease
    - Screening for latent TB infection can be considered before administering an ICI
        - So that the patient can be monitored for TB symptoms
        - So that treatment of mycobacterial infection can be expedited
