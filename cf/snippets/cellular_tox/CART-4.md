# Source

- NCCN Management of CAR T-Cell-Related Toxicities v2.2026, CART-4, p10
- Applies to anti-BCMA CAR T-cell therapy: ciltacabtagene autoleucel and idecabtagene vicleucel
    - See prescribing information for each agent and institutional protocols
- Emerging data suggest that other neurotoxicity events, with symptoms that do not fit the current definition for ICANS, may occur with anti-BCMA CAR T-cell therapy
- Typical time to onset is 11–108 days, later than ICANS
- All recommendations category 2A unless otherwise indicated

# Non-ICANS neurotoxicity

- Two entities are described on this page

# IEC-Parkinsonism

- Manifestation is similar to Parkinson's disease and may include
    - Bradykinesia
    - Rigidity
    - Gait change
    - Asymmetric tremor
    - Postural instability
    - Hypophonia
    - Personality change
    - Impaired memory
- Other signs and symptoms may include
    - Micrographia
    - Flat affect
    - Reduced facial expression
    - Bradyphrenia
    - Hypomimia
    - Impaired balance
    - Bradykinesia
    - Cogwheel rigidity
    - Abnormal posture
    - Decreased stride length
    - Neurocognitive impairment
- Risk factors
    - High baseline tumor burden
    - Grade 2 or higher CRS
    - Prior ICANS
    - High CAR T-cell expansion/persistence
        - Absolute lymphocyte count (ALC), when very elevated, may be a surrogate for high CAR T-cell expansion in this setting
- Optimal management has not been determined
- The characterized cases of IEC-Parkinsonism are levodopa unresponsive

# Management of IEC-Parkinsonism

- For mild symptoms, consider steroids such as 10 mg dexamethasone 2–4 times a day for 3–5 days
- For persistent, severe, or refractory symptoms, and if high circulating CAR T-cell levels are detected, consider chemotherapy such as high dose (2 g/m2) cyclophosphamide to ablate the CAR T cells
    - High circulating CAR T-cell levels: a very elevated ALC may be the surrogate for high CAR T-cell expansion in this setting
    - Blumenberg V, et al. Blood 2025;145:2788-2793; Graham CE, et al. Blood 2023;142:1248-1252; Graham CE, et al. Lancet Oncol 2025;26:e203-e213
- Other alternative strategies may include
    - Intrathecal chemotherapy (Kelly K, et al. Blood Adv 2025;9:3613-3616)
    - Ruxolitinib (Wirk B, Lim J. J Hematol 2025;14:146-151)
- Use of these therapies is currently based on very limited experience and should be balanced against potential safety concerns, such as infection risk

# IEC-cranial nerve palsy and IEC-polyradiculoneuritis

- Types of neuropathies reported
    - Lower motor neuron facial paralysis
    - Other cranial nerve palsy
    - Peripheral sensory neuropathy
    - Peripheral motor neuropathy
- For mild symptoms, such as unilateral facial nerve palsy, a short course of steroids, such as prednisone 1 mg/kg for 7–10 days, is recommended
- Consider IVIG for
    - Acute inflammatory demyelinating polyneuropathy (AIDP)-type picture
    - Bilateral facial palsy
