# CRS workup

- Laboratory tests
    - CBC
    - CMP
    - Blood cultures
    - MRSA
    - CRP
    - BNP
    - Ferritin
    - Urinalysis
    - Urine culture
    - Respiratory virus polymerase chain reaction (PCR) panel
- Diagnostic chest x-ray

# All grades

- Risks associated with each agent vary widely
    - Refer to the prescribing information for product-specific dosing and management
- Fever
    - Oral acetaminophen 650 mg every 4 hours PRN
    - Cooling blanket
    - Hyperthermia management protocol
- Initiation of empiric broad spectrum antibiotics in patients with neutropenia
- Consider inpatient care

# Grade 1, fever (≥38°C)

- Continue treatment if blinatumomab or tebentafusp-tebn
- Otherwise hold treatment, for all other bispecific agents
    - For considerations for rechallenge of T-cell-engaging bispecific antibody therapy after CRS, see ENGAGE-7
- Consider oral or IV dexamethasone 8–12 mg x 1 dose
- For prolonged steroid-refractory CRS (>24–48 hours), or if high risk for CRS, consider IV tocilizumab
    - Administer over 1 hour within 2 hours of onset
    - Maximum dose 800 mg
    - 3 doses in 24 hours, maximum of 4 doses
    - If no clinical improvement in oxygenation, hypotension, fever, or other symptoms, dosing may be repeated every 8 hours
    - Median time to response after first dose is hours to days, range generally 1–12 days
    - An FDA-approved biosimilar is an appropriate substitute for any recommended systemic biologic therapy

# Grade 2, fever with hypotension not requiring vasopressors and/or hypoxia requiring low-flow nasal cannula or blow-by

- Grading of this presentation
    - CRS grade is determined by the more severe event, hypotension or hypoxia, not attributable to any other cause
        - Example: a patient with a temperature of 39.5°C, hypotension requiring 1 vasopressor, and hypoxia requiring low-flow nasal cannula is classified as grade 3 CRS
    - Low-flow nasal cannula is defined as oxygen delivered at ≤6 L/min
        - Low flow also includes blow-by oxygen delivery, sometimes used in pediatrics
        - High-flow nasal cannula is defined as oxygen delivered at >6 L/min
- Continue treatment if blinatumomab
- Otherwise hold treatment, for all other bispecific agents
    - For considerations for rechallenge of T-cell-engaging bispecific antibody therapy after CRS, see ENGAGE-7
- Oral or IV dexamethasone 8–12 mg x 1 dose
    - May repeat every 12 hours
- Consider IV tocilizumab 8 mg/kg x 1 dose
    - Administer over 1 hour within 2 hours of onset
    - Maximum dose 800 mg
    - 3 doses in 24 hours, maximum of 4 doses
    - If no clinical improvement in oxygenation, hypotension, fever, or other symptoms, dosing may be repeated every 8 hours
    - Median time to response after first dose is hours to days, range generally 1–12 days
    - An FDA-approved biosimilar is an appropriate substitute for any recommended systemic biologic therapy
- If symptoms persist for >24 hours, consider adding alternative cytokine blockade (ie, anakinra or siltuximab)
- Oxygen low flow nasal cannula ≤6 L/min
- IV fluid bolus
- Cardiac monitoring
