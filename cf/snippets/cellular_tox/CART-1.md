# Before and during CAR T-cell infusion

- Baseline cardiac assessment, such as echocardiogram
    - Consult with cardiology if previous cardiac history or concern from assessment
- Central venous access, preferably with double or triple lumen catheter
    - For IV fluid and possible vasopressors use
- Cardiac monitoring
    - At least at the onset of grade 2 CRS until resolution to grade 1 or less
    - Clinically significant arrhythmia
    - Additionally as clinically indicated
- Tumor lysis prophylaxis and monitoring, for patients with large tumor burden and aggressive histologies, as per standard institutional guidelines
- Seizure prophylaxis started on the day of infusion, when there is high risk of ICANS based on patient factors or product type
    - eg, oral levetiracetam 500-750 mg every 12 hours for 30 days
- Baseline neurologic evaluation
    - ICE scores, for adults
    - Cornell Assessment of Pediatric Delirium (CAPD) scores, for children <12 years or non-verbal adults
    - Consider baseline brain MRI
- Baseline C-reactive protein (CRP) and serum ferritin, prior to lymphodepleting chemotherapy
    - Allows calculation of the CAR-HEMATOTOX score, to predict the risk for immune effector cell-associated hematotoxicity (ICAHT) and infection
- Relevant serologic screening: HIV, HBV, HCV
    - Consider CMV and additional screening based on epidemiologic risk

# Post-CAR T-cell infusion setting

- Follow the prescribing information for each agent and institutional protocols
- Hospitalization, or extremely close outpatient monitoring at centers with CAR T-cell experience
    - Close monitoring in the hospital is preferable with current products used for adults
    - Extremely close outpatient monitoring may be possible at centers with outpatient transplant experience
    - Patient remains within proximity of a healthcare facility for at least 2 weeks after infusion
- Hospitalization at the first sign of neurotoxicity, including change in mental status
- Patients with CRS who experience a response to tocilizumab and/or dexamethasone can be monitored closely in outpatient settings

# Post-CAR T-cell infusion monitoring

- CBC, CMP (including magnesium and phosphorus), and coagulation profile daily
- CRP and serum ferritin rechecked at least 3 times per week for 2 weeks post-infusion
    - Consider daily checks during CRS
    - CRP can normalize prior to the onset of neurotoxicity
- Vital signs to allow clinical assessment for CRS, at least every 8 hours or when the patient status changes
    - During the peak window of CRS risk, typically the first 1-2 weeks post-infusion
- Neurotoxicity assessment at least twice daily until hospital discharge
    - Urgently thereafter if there is a change in the patient status, or routinely every 2-4 weeks, extending to 2 months
    - Consider a physical assessment and/or tests to check handwriting and general function/gait, eg, Timed Up and Go (TUG) test
    - If neurologic concern develops, more frequent assessments are recommended
- Monitor for CRS, neurotoxicity, and other toxicities for the duration recommended by the CAR product prescribing information
    - At least 4 weeks and up to 3-6 months post-infusion, depending on the product used, for most patients
- Patient refrains from driving or hazardous activities for at least 2 weeks following infusion
