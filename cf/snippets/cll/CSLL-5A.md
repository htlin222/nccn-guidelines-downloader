# Before starting subsequent therapy

- If progression with indication for subsequent therapy, re-evaluate prior to initiation of subsequent therapy
    - FISH for del(17p)/TP53 mutation status
    - CpG-stimulated metaphase karyotype
- Consider the possibility of histologic transformation in patients with progressive disease
    - Biopsy is recommended to confirm histologic transformation
    - If histologic transformation, see HT-1
- Consider testing for BTK and PLCG2 mutations in patients with disease progression or no response while on BTKi therapy
    - Including if poor adherence is considered as a possible cause
    - BTK and PLCG2 mutation status alone is not an indication to change treatment in the absence of disease progression
- Assess response by iwCLL response criteria, see Discussion
- Supportive care for patients with CLL/SLL (CSLL-C)

# Relapsed or refractory therapy

- Clinical trial
- Chimeric antigen receptor (CAR) T-cell therapy (CSLL-D 2 of 5)
- ncBTKi, if not previously given (CSLL-D 2 of 5)
    - and referral to evaluate eligibility for CAR T-cell therapy
- Other Recommended Regimens (CSLL-D 2 of 5; Therapy for relapsed or refractory disease after BTK inhibitor (BTKi)- and BCL2i-containing regimens)
    - and referral to evaluate eligibility for CAR T-cell therapy
- Consider allogeneic hematopoietic cell transplant (HCT) if without significant comorbidities

# If relapse after a period of remission

- Venetoclax with or without anti-CD20 mAb, obinutuzumab preferred
- Venetoclax plus cBTKi
- Single-agent venetoclax, continuous, can also be considered
- Time-limited treatment with BCL2i + cBTKi with or without CD20 mAb may be fixed-duration or MRD-guided
    - Based on undetectable MRD (uMRD)-stopping criteria as described in clinical trials
    - See MRD Assessment (CSLL-E)

# Continuing or switching a BTKi

- In patients with disease responding to therapy, continue the same BTKi until progression and/or intolerance
- In patients with no intolerance, cBTKi can be continued until disease progression, following recommended dose modification guidance as needed
- Manage adverse reactions by following the recommended dose modifications in the FDA-approved package insert
- In patients with disease progression on a BTKi, do not stop the BTKi abruptly
    - Abrupt discontinuation can be associated with explosive disease flare, with rapid rise in ALC, enlargement of lymph nodes, and accelerated clinical deterioration
    - The rapid growth of lymph nodes can have elevated standardized uptake value (SUV) on PET/CT, and biopsy can have a pseudo-transformation appearance
    - Transition to the next treatment should be as soon as possible
    - Overlapping BTKi during the venetoclax dose ramp-up is safe and helpful to maintain disease control until a therapeutic dose of venetoclax is reached
- Patients without del(17p)/TP53 mutation who discontinue a cBTKi for intolerance can remain off treatment, if there is no disease progression, for an extended period of time
    - Alternate BTKi can be reinitiated when symptomatic
- Alternate cBTKi, acalabrutinib, ibrutinib, or zanubrutinib, could be considered for intolerance in the absence of disease progression
- ncBTKi can be considered for resistance or intolerance to prior cBTKi-based regimens
