# Essential

- Hematopathology review of peripheral blood smear and all slides
    - With at least one paraffin block representative of the tumor, if the diagnosis was made on a lymph node or bone marrow biopsy
- Flow cytometry of blood is adequate for the diagnosis of CLL/SLL
- CLL diagnosis requires the presence of monoclonal B lymphocytes at or above 5 x 10^9/L in peripheral blood based on flow cytometry
    - An absolute monoclonal B lymphocyte count below 5000/mm3 that persists >3 months, in the absence of palpable adenopathy or other clinical features of lymphoproliferative disorder, is MBL
    - Adequate immunophenotyping to establish diagnosis by flow cytometry: kappa/lambda, CD19, CD20, CD5, CD23, CD10, CD200
        - Typical immunophenotype: CD5+, CD23+, CD43+/-, CD10-, CD19+, CD20 dim, sIg dim+, and cyclin D1-
        - Some cases may be sIg bright+ or CD23- or dim; some MCL may be CD23+
        - Cyclin D1 IHC or FISH for t(11;14) should be considered in all cases, especially for those with an atypical immunophenotype (ie, CD23 dim or negative, CD20 bright, sIg bright)
        - CD200 positivity may distinguish CLL from MCL, which is usually CD200-
    - If flow cytometry is used to establish diagnosis, also include FISH for t(11;14) to exclude mantle cell lymphoma (MCL)
- SLL diagnosis requires presence of lymphadenopathy and/or splenomegaly with monoclonal B lymphocytes at or below 5 x 10^9/L in peripheral blood
    - Cells of the same phenotype may be seen in reactive lymph nodes; diagnose SLL only when effacement of lymph node architecture is seen
    - Confirm SLL by histopathology evaluation of lymph node biopsy, or by evaluation of bone marrow or peripheral blood for monoclonal B cells of diagnostic phenotype
    - Excisional or incisional biopsy, if lymph node is accessible
        - FNA alone is inadequate for the diagnosis
        - When a lymph node is not easily accessible for excisional or incisional biopsy, a combination of core needle biopsy (multiple biopsies may be required) with appropriate ancillary techniques, such as FNA, may be sufficient for diagnosis
    - Bone marrow aspirate with biopsy can be pursued if peripheral blood and lymph node biopsy material are nondiagnostic
    - Adequate immunophenotyping to establish diagnosis by immunohistochemistry (IHC): CD3, CD5, CD10, CD20, CD23, cyclin D1, LEF1, SOX11
        - Typical immunophenotype as above
        - LEF1 and SOX11 may be helpful in suspected cases of MCL that are cyclin D1-negative
- Absolute monoclonal B lymphocyte count
    - Bone marrow examination is not helpful for the diagnosis of MBL

# Informative for prognostic and/or therapy determination

- See Prognostic Variables for CLL/SLL (CSLL-A)
- FISH to detect +12, del(11q), del(13q), del(17p)
- TP53 sequencing
- CpG-stimulated metaphase karyotype for complex karyotype (CK)
- Molecular analysis to detect IGHV mutation status
- Beta-2 microglobulin

# Monoclonal B-cell lymphocytosis (MBL)

- Absolute monoclonal B-lymphocyte count below 5 x 10^9/L
- All palpable lymph nodes <1.5 cm
- No anemia
- No thrombocytopenia
- No palpable organomegaly
- No constitutional symptoms
