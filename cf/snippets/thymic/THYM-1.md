# Initial evaluation

- Chest CT with contrast
    - Chest MRI discriminates thymic malignancy from thymic cyst or thymic hyperplasia better than chest CT, potentially avoiding an unnecessary thymectomy
- Serum beta-hCG, AFP, if appropriate
- CBC, platelets
- FDG-PET/CT scan (skull base to midthigh), as clinically indicated
- Pulmonary function tests, as clinically indicated
- Chest MRI with and without contrast, as clinically indicated
    - Better discriminates thymic malignancy versus thymic cyst or thymic hyperplasia than chest CT, potentially avoiding an unnecessary thymectomy
- Assess for distress
    - Refer to the NCCN Distress Thermometer and Problem List, which includes social determinants of health
    - See NCCN Guidelines for Distress Management (DIS-A)
- Evaluate clinically for signs of myasthenia gravis and other paraneoplastic syndromes, with appropriate workup and treatment, in patients with thymoma

# Thymic tumor likely, all four features

- Well-defined anterior mediastinal mass in the thymic bed
- Tumor markers negative
- Absence of other adenopathy
- Absence of continuity with the thyroid

# Next

- Consider tissue biopsy
