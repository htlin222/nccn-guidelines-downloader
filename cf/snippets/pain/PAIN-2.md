# Universal screening

- Routinely screen for pain
- If pain present
    - Quantify pain intensity
    - Characterize pain quality
    - Pain Intensity Rating (PAIN-A)
    - Severe uncontrolled pain is a medical emergency and should be addressed promptly (PAIN-6)
- If no pain, rescreen at each subsequent contact

# Comprehensive pain assessment (PAIN-B)

- Pain experience
- Pain etiology
- Pain pathophysiology
- Estimated trajectory of pain
- Specific cancer pain syndrome (PAIN-K)
- Patient-specific goals for comfort and function
- Risks for aberrant drug use and/or diversion (PAIN-G)

# Management of pain

- Pain related to an oncologic emergency
    - Bone fracture or impending fracture of weight-bearing bone
    - Neuroaxial metastases with threatened neural injury
    - Infection
    - Obstructed or perforated viscus (acute abdomen)
    - Thromboembolic emergency
    - Analgesics as specified in the pathway below, in addition to specific treatment for the oncologic emergency, as consistent with patient goals
        - Specific treatment eg, surgery, steroids, radiation therapy [RT], antibiotics
- Pain not related to an oncologic emergency
    - Analgesics as specified in the pathway below
- Pain not related to cancer or cancer treatment
    - Referral or coordination with appropriate pain provider or program
- Anticipated painful events and procedures
    - Procedure-Related Pain and Anxiety (PAIN-J)

# Next

- Opioid-naive patient, go to Management of Pain in Opioid-Naive Patients (PAIN-4)
    - Opioid-naive patients are those not chronically receiving opioid analgesic on a daily basis, and therefore have not developed significant tolerance
- Opioid-tolerant patient, go to Management of Pain in Opioid-Tolerant Patients (PAIN-5)
    - Opioid tolerant includes patients who are chronically receiving opioid analgesic on a daily basis
    - The FDA identifies tolerance as receiving, for a week or longer, at least 25 mcg/h fentanyl patch, at least 60 mg of morphine daily, at least 30 mg of oral (PO) oxycodone daily, at least 8 mg of PO hydromorphone daily, or an equianalgesic dose of another opioid
- Severe uncontrolled pain, address promptly (PAIN-6)
- No pain, rescreen at each subsequent contact
