# Source

- NCCN Adult Cancer Pain v2.2026, PAIN-5, p14
- Applies to opioid-tolerant patients
- All recommendations category 2A unless otherwise indicated

# Assessment

- Pain intensity rating ___ (pain_score), see PAIN-A
- Pain intensity band ___ (pain_intensity)
- Current opioid analgesic ___ (current_opioid)
- Total daily dose ___ (total_daily_dose)
- Confirm the patient is opioid tolerant, not opioid naive
    - Opioid naive means not chronically receiving an opioid analgesic on a daily basis, and therefore has not developed significant tolerance
    - Opioid tolerant means chronically receiving an opioid analgesic on a daily basis
    - FDA identifies tolerance as any of the following for a week or longer
        - At least 25 mcg/h fentanyl patch
        - At least 60 mg of morphine daily
        - At least 30 mg of PO oxycodone daily
        - At least 8 mg of PO hydromorphone daily
        - An equianalgesic dose of another opioid
- Dose-limiting adverse effects ___ (adverse_effects)

# Mild pain

- See General Principles on PAIN-3
- AND non-opioids and adjuvant therapies (PAIN-G)
    - Unless contraindicated due to adverse effects, potential drug interactions, or comorbid conditions
- Re-evaluate need for opioids and reduce if appropriate (PAIN-G 3 of 22)

# Moderate or severe pain

- See General Principles on PAIN-3
- AND non-opioids and adjuvant therapies as appropriate, with short-acting opioids as needed (PAIN-G)
- Titrate short-acting opioid (PAIN-6)
    - May require dose increase of 30%–100%
- If multiple doses of short-acting opioid are consistently needed per day, consider addition or increase in dose of a long-acting opioid
    - Based on the total daily dose
    - If appropriate based on expected trajectory of pain

# Severe pain or pain crisis

- For acute, severe pain or pain crisis, consider hospital or inpatient hospice admission (PAIN-6)
    - To rapidly titrate analgesic
    - To quickly achieve patient-specific goals for comfort and function

# After titration

- Titrate further as needed
- If pain is inadequately controlled, re-evaluate working diagnosis with a comprehensive pain assessment (PAIN-B)
- Consider radiation oncology, pain medicine, and/or palliative care consultation (PAIN-L)
- Consider opioid rotation if dose-limiting adverse effects are noted
