# Assessment

- Screened for pain at this contact (PAIN-2)
- Patient self-report is the primary source for pain assessment
- Pain intensity ___ (pain_intensity)
- Pain location ___ (pain_location)
- Pain quality ___ (pain_quality)
    - Quantify and document routinely, as characterized by the patient, whenever possible
- Acute or chronic ___ (pain_type)
- Breakthrough pain reported by the patient ___ (breakthrough_pain)
- Treatments used and their impact on pain
- Adverse effects of treatments
- Satisfaction with pain relief
- Pain interference
- Provider assessment of impact on function
- Any special issues for the patient relevant to pain treatment and access to care
- Additional information from caregiver regarding pain and impact on function, whenever possible
- Comprehensive pain assessment (PAIN-B)
    - If new or worsening pain is present
    - Regularly for persisting pain
- Evaluate for risk factors for analgesic misuse/diversion

# Pain definition

- IASP definition: an unpleasant sensory and emotional experience associated with, or resembling that associated with, actual or potential tissue damage
    - Raja SN, et al. Pain 2020;161:1976-1982
- Acute cancer pain in adults: sudden-onset pain directly related to diagnostic procedures, cancer treatments (eg, surgery, RT, chemotherapy), or rapid tumor growth affecting surrounding tissues or organs
    - Intense sensation, typically short-lived, resolving within 3 months as the underlying cause is treated or subsides
- Chronic cancer pain in adults: persistent or recurrent pain lasting more than 3 months
    - IASP: chronic pain caused by the primary cancer itself or metastases (chronic cancer pain), or by its treatment (chronic post-cancer treatment pain)
    - Bennett MI, et al. Pain 2019;160:38-44
    - The terms cancer pain and cancer-related pain may be used interchangeably

# General principles

- Optimal use of disease-specific therapies is essential to managing tumor-related pain
- Pain management is an essential part of oncologic management and contributes to overall function and quality of life
    - Survival has been linked to symptom control and pain management, which contribute to broad quality-of-life improvement
- Analgesic therapy is done in conjunction with management of multiple symptoms or symptom clusters
    - Consider the interaction of complex pharmacologic therapies
    - Consider the risk for analgesic misuse
- A multidisciplinary team is optimal (PAIN-L)
    - Consider early referral to palliative care, see NCCN Guidelines for Palliative Care
    - Consider early referral to radiation oncology
    - Consider early referral to interventional pain management teams (PAIN-M)
    - Consider early referral to psychological/social/spiritual services
    - Refer to the NCCN Distress Thermometer and Problem List in the NCCN Guidelines for Distress Management, which includes social determinants of health
- Provide or refer for psychosocial/spiritual support (PAIN-C) (PAIN-D)
    - Emotional and informational support
    - Coping skills training
    - Other nonpharmacologic therapies
- Provide accessible educational material based on the patient's identified needs (PAIN-I)
    - To improve pain assessment, pain management, and the safe use of analgesics
    - Identified needs per Bennett MI, et al. Pain 2019;160:38-44
- Involve patients in developing treatment plans
    - Set meaningful, realistic expectations
    - Set measurable functional goals that incorporate patient values and preferences
- Due to the biopsychosocial nature of pain, health inequities and disparities influence pain experience and access to pain evaluation and care
    - Take steps to improve disparities in access to effective pain evaluation and care
- Address the multidimensional impact of suffering on patients and caregivers in a culturally respectful manner

# Management and intervention

- Goals of pain management, the 5 A's of outcomes
    - Analgesia: optimize analgesia
    - Activities: optimize activities of daily living
    - Adverse effects: minimize adverse effects (PAIN-H)
    - Aberrant behavior: monitor for aberrant drug-use behavior (PAIN-G)
    - Affect: relationship between pain and mood, including psychosocial functioning
- Develop treatment plans with regard for medication availability and cost
- Prevention of analgesic side effects, especially constipation, is of paramount importance
- For acute, severe pain or pain crisis, consider urgent evaluation and hospital or inpatient hospice admission
- Treat persistent cancer pain with regularly scheduled analgesics or long-acting analgesics
- Treat breakthrough/procedural/incidental pain with supplemental doses of short-acting analgesics
- For chronic pain in cancer survivors, see NCCN Guidelines for Survivorship

# Reassessment

- Perform pain reassessment at regular intervals
    - Analgesic therapy is providing maximum benefit with minimal adverse effects
    - The treatment plan is followed
- Encourage patients to report ongoing pain assessments in between visits, as needed
