# Source

- NCCN Antiemesis v2.2026, AE-1, p13
- Applies to any patient with cancer receiving anticancer agents
- Refer to the NCCN Distress Thermometer and Problem List, which includes social determinants of health, see NCCN Guidelines for Distress Management (DIS-A)
- All recommendations category 2A unless stated otherwise

# Assessment

- Anticancer agents: ___ (anticancer_agents)
- Emetic risk of the therapy: ___ (emetic_risk)
- Day ___ (days_since_last_dose) after the last dose of anticancer agents
- Nausea/vomiting pattern: ___ (nv_timing), acute is within 24 hours and delayed is beyond 24 hours
- Prior experience with antiemetics: ___ (prior_antiemetic_experience)

# Goal and period of risk

- Prevention of nausea/vomiting is the goal
- Risk of nausea/vomiting after the last dose of anticancer agents
    - At least 3 days for agents of high emetic risk
    - At least 2 days for agents of moderate emetic risk
- Patient is protected throughout the full period of risk

# Choice of antiemetic

- Choice is based on the emetic risk of the therapy
- Choice is based on prior experience with antiemetics
- Choice is based on patient factors
- Toxicity of the specific antiemetic(s) considered, see Pharmacologic Considerations for Antiemetic Prescribing (AE-B)
- Oral (PO) and parenteral serotonin receptor antagonists (5-HT3 RAs) have equivalent efficacy when used at the appropriate doses and intervals
- Continuous infusion may make an agent less emetogenic
- Emetic risk is expected to be the same for biosimilars as for the parent compound, unless otherwise noted

# Patient risk factors for anticancer agent-induced nausea/vomiting

- Younger age
- Female sex
- Previous history of anticancer agent-induced nausea and vomiting (chemotherapy-induced nausea and vomiting [CINV])
- Little or no previous alcohol use
- Prone to motion sickness
- History of morning sickness during pregnancy
- Anxiety/high pretreatment expectation of nausea
- Less than 7 hours of sleep the night before chemotherapy

# Other potential causes of nausea or emesis in patients with cancer

- Partial or complete bowel obstruction
- Vestibular dysfunction
- Brain metastases
- Electrolyte imbalance
    - Hypercalcemia
    - Hyperglycemia
    - Hyponatremia
- Uremia
- Concomitant drug treatments, including opioids
- Supplements/herbal products
- Gastroparesis
    - Tumor induced
    - Chemotherapy induced (eg, vincristine)
    - Other causes (eg, diabetes)
- Excessive secretions (eg, seen in patients with head and neck cancers)
- Malignant ascites
- Psychophysiologic
    - Anxiety
    - Anticipatory nausea/vomiting
- Cannabinoid hyperemesis syndrome
- Rapid opioid withdrawal
- Pancreatitis
- Dysmotility
- Concomitant radiation therapy (RT), especially total body irradiation (TBI) and RT directed at the abdomen or brain
