# Source

- NCCN Pediatric Antiemesis v2.2026, PEDAE-3, p43
- Applies to pediatric parenteral anticancer agents of high emetic risk
    - Emetogenic potential of pediatric parenteral anticancer agents, see PEDAE-1
- Select treatment option A, B, C, or D
    - This page details treatment option A, for children able to receive an NK1 RA and dexamethasone
- All treatment options should be started before anticancer therapy
    - Choose the antiemetic regimen based on the drug with the highest emetic risk, as well as on patient-specific risk factors
- Footnotes on PEDAE-7A
- All recommendations category 2A unless otherwise indicated

# Treatment option A, step 1, 5-HT3 RA, choose one

- Palonosetron 0.02 mg/kg (max 1.5 mg) IV once if <17 y of age
- Palonosetron 0.25 mg IV once if ≥17 y of age (preferred)
    - Palonosetron is the preferred 5-HT3 RA in the acute phase if there is a high risk of delayed-phase CINV, and if unable to use an NK1 RA and/or dexamethasone
- Granisetron 0.01–0.04 mg/kg IV once, or 0.04 mg/kg PO every 12–24 hours (max 2 mg/day)
- Ondansetron 0.15 mg/kg or 5 mg/m2 (max 16 mg/dose or 0.45 mg/kg/day) PO/IV every 8–24 hours
    - Not to exceed 3 doses/day (max 24 mg/day)
- ± Ondansetron or granisetron on all subsequent chemotherapy days if used in the acute and delayed phase
    - If palonosetron is given on Day 1, ondansetron or granisetron is not needed on subsequent days

# Treatment option A, step 2, dexamethasone

- Dexamethasone PO/IV once
- ± Dexamethasone PO/IV daily if used in the acute phase, on all subsequent chemotherapy days and for the delayed phase
- No optimal pediatric dexamethasone dose has been identified
    - Doses may be individualized
    - Consider doses of 5–10 mg/m2/day once daily or divided
    - Reduce dexamethasone by 50% when given concomitantly with NK1 RAs
- Give dexamethasone in the delayed phase if ondansetron or granisetron is given in the acute phase
- Dexamethasone may be considered in the delayed phase if palonosetron is used in the acute phase
- Avoid corticosteroid premedications with cellular therapies
- Clinicians may wish to consider a dexamethasone-sparing approach with ICI therapy as well
- Pharmacologic considerations for antiemetic prescribing, see (AE-B)

# Treatment option A, step 3, NK1 RA, consider adding in children ≥6 mo of age, choose one

- Aprepitant
    - Age ≥6 mo and <12 y, weight <30 kg: 3 mg/kg PO once (max 125 mg)
    - Age ≥12 y or weight ≥30 kg: 125 mg PO once
    - 2 mg/kg PO daily on Days 2 and 3 (max 80 mg)
- Aprepitant injectable emulsion IV once (3-day regimen)
    - Aprepitant injectable emulsion is a unique formulation of aprepitant and is NOT interchangeable with the IV formulation of fosaprepitant
    - Age ≥6 y and <12 y: 3 mg/kg IV once (max 100 mg)
    - Age ≥12 y: 100 mg IV once
    - 2 mg/kg PO daily on Days 2 and 3 (max 80 mg)
- Fosaprepitant single-dose regimen
    - Age ≥6 mo and <2 y, weight ≥6 kg: 5 mg/kg IV once (max 150 mg)
    - Age ≥2 y and <12 y: 4 mg/kg IV once (max 150 mg)
    - Age ≥12 y: 150 mg IV once
- Fosaprepitant 3-day regimen
    - Age ≥6 mo and <2 y, weight ≥6 kg: 3 mg/kg IV once (max 115 mg)
    - Age ≥2 y and <12 y: 3 mg/kg IV once (max 115 mg)
    - Age ≥12 y: 115 mg IV once
    - 2 mg/kg IV daily on Days 2 and 3 (max 80 mg)

# Treatment option A, step 4, olanzapine, consider adding in children ≥3 y of age

- Olanzapine 0.05–0.2 mg/kg (2.5–10 mg) PO once at bedtime prior to the first dose of anticancer therapy
- ± Olanzapine 0.05–0.2 mg/kg (2.5–10 mg) PO daily (bedtime) on Days 2, 3, and 4
- Pediatric randomized controlled trials evaluating olanzapine excluded patients <5 years of age
    - Safety has been established among pediatric patients with mental health conditions
    - Caution is warranted in younger children
