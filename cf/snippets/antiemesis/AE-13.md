# General principle

- Add one agent from a different drug class to the current regimen (AE-B)

# Breakthrough agents, one from a drug class not already in use

- Atypical antipsychotic
    - Olanzapine 2.5-10 mg PO daily (bedtime) (category 1, preferred)
- Cannabinoid
    - Dronabinol capsules 5-10 mg PO 3-4 times daily
    - Dronabinol PO solution 2.1-4.2 mg/m2 PO 3-4 times daily
        - Dronabinol PO solution has greater PO bioavailability than dronabinol capsules
        - 2.1 mg PO solution = 2.5 mg capsules
- Anticholinergics
    - Scopolamine 1.5 mg transdermal patch, 1 patch every 72 h
- Dopamine-receptor antagonists
    - The Panel discourages combining multiple dopamine-receptor antagonists, as this could increase the risk of extrapyramidal reactions
    - When used for breakthrough CINV, a single drug from this class could be utilized
    - No clinically significant drug-drug interactions with short-term use of antiemetics have emerged to date in randomized clinical trials
    - Haloperidol 0.5-2 mg PO/IV every 4-6 h
    - Metoclopramide 10-20 mg PO/IV every 4-6 h
    - Phenothiazine
        - Prochlorperazine 25 mg suppository (supp) PR every 12 h, or 10 mg PO/IV every 6 h
        - Promethazine 25 mg supp PR every 6 h, or 12.5-25 mg PO every 4-6 h
- 5-HT3 RA
    - Granisetron 1-2 mg PO daily
    - Granisetron 1 mg PO twice a day (BID)
    - Granisetron 0.01 mg/kg (maximum 1 mg) IV daily
    - Granisetron 3.1 mg/24-h transdermal patch every 7 days
    - Ondansetron 8 mg PO/IV every 8-12 h (16-24 mg total daily dose)
- Corticosteroid
    - Dexamethasone 4-8 mg PO/IV daily
        - Use of corticosteroid premedications should be avoided with cellular therapies
        - Clinicians may wish to consider a dexamethasone-sparing approach with ICI therapy as well (AE-B)
- Benzodiazepine
    - Lorazepam 0.5-1 mg PO/sublingual (SL)/IV every 6 h
        - Especially for patients where anxiety may be contributing to breakthrough CINV (AE-1, AE-B)

# Next

- Nausea and vomiting controlled
    - Continue breakthrough medications, on a schedule, not PRN
- Nausea and/or vomiting uncontrolled
    - Re-evaluate and consider dose adjustments
    - And/or sequentially add one agent from a different drug class
    - Consider changing antiemetic therapy to higher level primary treatment for next cycle
