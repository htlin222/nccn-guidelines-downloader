# Source

- NCCN Adult Antiemesis v2.2026, AE-15, p28
- Applies to adult radiation-induced emesis, prevention and treatment
- Emetogenic potential of radiation therapy is radiation-induced nausea/vomiting
- Order of the listed regimens does not imply preference
- Pharmacologic Considerations for Antiemetic Prescribing (AE-B)
- All recommendations category 2A unless otherwise indicated

# RT to upper abdomen or localized sites

- Start pretreatment for each day of RT treatment
    - Order does not imply preference
    - See Pharmacologic Considerations for Antiemetic Prescribing (AE-B)
- 5-HT3 RA, choose one
    - Granisetron 2 mg PO daily
    - Ondansetron 8 mg PO BID
- ± Dexamethasone 4 mg PO daily
    - Avoid corticosteroid premedications with cellular therapies
    - Consider a dexamethasone-sparing approach with ICI therapy as well

# Total body irradiation (TBI)

- Start pretreatment for each day of RT treatment
    - Order does not imply preference
    - See Pharmacologic Considerations for Antiemetic Prescribing (AE-B)
- 5-HT3 RA, choose one
    - Granisetron 2 mg PO daily
    - Ondansetron 8 mg PO BID–TID
- ± Dexamethasone 4 mg PO daily
    - Avoid corticosteroid premedications with cellular therapies
    - Consider a dexamethasone-sparing approach with ICI therapy as well

# Anticancer therapy and RT, including TBI

- See emesis prevention for chemotherapy-induced nausea/vomiting
    - Parenteral emetic risk, high (AE-6)
    - Parenteral emetic risk, moderate (AE-7)
    - Parenteral emetic risk, low and minimal (AE-8)
    - Oral emetic risk, moderate to high, prophylaxis required (AE-9)
    - Oral emetic risk, moderate to high, PRN dosing initially appropriate (AE-10)
    - Oral emetic risk, minimal to low (AE-12)
- If radiation is combined with anticancer therapy, antiemetic prophylaxis is based on the modality, anticancer therapy or radiation, with the highest emetic risk (AE-1)
