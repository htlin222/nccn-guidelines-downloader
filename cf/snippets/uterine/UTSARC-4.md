# Surveillance

- H&P exam every 3-4 mo for 2-3 y, then every 6-12 mo
    - Consider every 6 months for low-grade, early-stage sarcomas
- Imaging (UTSARC-B)
- Clinical evaluation and management of potential long-term and late effects of treatment
    - Patient education on symptoms of potential recurrence
    - Lifestyle
    - Obesity
    - Exercise
    - Sexual health
        - Vaginal dilator use
        - Lubricants/moisturizers
        - Local estrogen and hormone therapy for menopause
    - Smoking cessation
    - Nutrition counseling
    - Also see Principles of Gynecologic Survivorship (UN-B)
    - Also see NCCN Guidelines for Survivorship
    - Also see NCCN Guidelines for Smoking Cessation

# Recurrence: local recurrence

- Vagina/pelvis
- Imaging negative for distant metastatic disease (UTSARC-B)

# Recurrence: isolated metastases

- Metastasectomy is the historical standard for patients with oligometastatic disease
    - The ultimate choice of local control modality may depend on performance status, patient preference, lesion location/accessibility, ability to preserve normal tissue function, and anticipated morbidity of a treatment modality
- Resectable
    - Surgical resection
        - Observation may be an option in select, completely resected cases with no evidence of disease on postoperative imaging
    - or other local ablative therapy (SARC-F pages 1-3)
    - Consider preoperative or postoperative systemic therapy (UTSARC-C)
    - Consider preoperative or postoperative EBRT/SBRT (UN-A)
- Unresectable
    - Systemic therapy (UTSARC-C)
    - and/or local therapy
        - EBRT/SBRT (UN-A)
        - or local ablative therapy (SARC-F pages 1-3)
    - If response, consider surgery with or without EBRT/SBRT (UN-A)

# Recurrence: disseminated disease

- Systemic therapy (UTSARC-C), with or without palliative EBRT (UN-A)
- or Best supportive care, see NCCN Guidelines for Palliative Care
