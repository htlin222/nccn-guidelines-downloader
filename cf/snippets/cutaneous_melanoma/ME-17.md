# Workup of distant metastatic disease

- Biopsy to confirm
    - Initial presentation with stage IV disease or clinical recurrence should be confirmed pathologically whenever possible, or if clinically indicated
    - Biopsy techniques may include
        - Core, preferred
        - FNA
        - Incisional/partial
        - Excisional
    - Tissue is always preferred over cytology for molecular testing
    - See Principles of Biopsy and Pathology (ME-B)
- LDH
- Imaging for baseline staging
- Imaging to evaluate specific signs and symptoms
    - See Principles of Imaging - Workup (ME-D)
- BRAF testing, if not previously performed on a metastatic lesion
    - Obtain tissue to ascertain alterations in BRAF, and in the appropriate clinical setting, KIT
    - From either biopsy of the metastasis, preferred, or archival material, if the patient is being considered for targeted therapy
    - Consider MGPT, if the test results might guide future treatment decisions or eligibility for participation in a clinical trial
    - See Principles of Biomarker Testing (ME-C)

# Which pathway this patient is on

- Oligometastatic
    - With oligometastatic, resectable disease, a neoadjuvant approach should be considered depending on the surgical morbidity and multidisciplinary discussion
- Widely disseminated and/or brain metastases
    - In the setting of brain metastases, multidisciplinary consultation is recommended
    - See Principles of Brain Metastases Management (ME-J)

# Oligometastatic pathway

- Multidisciplinary consultation
- Systemic therapy, preferred for therapy naive patients
    - See Principles of Metastatic or Unresectable Systemic Therapy (MELSYS-1 of 10)
    - Imaging to assess response or progression
        - See Principles of Imaging - Treatment Response Assessment (ME-D 3 of 5)
    - Negative for other disease, go on to metastasis-directed therapy options
    - Positive for other disease, treat as widely disseminated pathway
- Metastasis-directed therapy options
    - Resection
    - Stereotactic ablative RT (SBRT)
    - T-VEC/Intralesional therapy, for accessible lesions
- After metastasis-directed therapy
    - Resected or NED, go on to adjuvant treatment options
    - Residual disease, treat as widely disseminated pathway

# Adjuvant treatment options, after resection or NED

- The choice of adjuvant systemic treatment versus observation should take into consideration the patient's risk of melanoma recurrence and the risk of treatment toxicity
    - See Adjuvant Systemic Therapy Considerations (ADJSYS-2 of 2)
- Systemic therapy
    - See Principles of Adjuvant Systemic Therapy (ADJSYS-1 of 2)
    - For patients who have previously received systemic therapy for cutaneous melanoma, either as active treatment or adjuvant therapy, selection of adjuvant treatment regimen should be informed by response to prior systemic therapies
    - For patients who experienced progression of melanoma during or shortly after a prior therapy, consider adjuvant agents of a different class
    - For patients who experience disease control (CR, PR, or SD) on a prior systemic therapy, and have no residual toxicity, but subsequently experienced disease progression/relapse >3 months after treatment discontinuation, adjuvant treatment with the same agent or same class of agents may be considered
- Observation
    - Follow-up on ME-11

# Widely disseminated and/or brain metastases pathway

- Options include, assessed with imaging per Principles of Imaging - Treatment Response Assessment (ME-D 3 of 5)
- Systemic therapy, preferred
    - See Principles of Metastatic or Unresectable Systemic Therapy (MELSYS-1 of 10)
- Consider palliative resection for symptomatic extracranial disease
- Consider palliative RT for symptomatic extracranial disease
    - See Principles of Radiation Therapy (ME-H)
- Consider intralesional T-VEC for symptomatic extracranial disease
    - T-VEC has shown a response rate, lasting at least 6 months, of 16% in highly selected patients with AJCC 7th Edition stage IV-M1a disease, that is skin, subcutaneous, and/or remote nodes
    - Similar efficacy has been demonstrated in AJCC 8th Edition stage IV-M1a disease
- Best supportive/palliative care, see NCCN Guidelines for Palliative Care
- In the setting of brain metastases, multidisciplinary consultation is recommended
    - See Principles of Brain Metastases Management (ME-J)
