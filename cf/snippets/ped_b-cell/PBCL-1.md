# Source

- NCCN Pediatric Aggressive Mature B-Cell Lymphomas v1.2026, PBCL-1, p6
- "Pediatric" includes any patient aged 18 years or younger
- Also includes adolescent and young adult (AYA) patients older than 18 years and younger than 39 years of age, as defined by the National Cancer Institute, who are treated in a pediatric oncology setting
    - Practice patterns vary from center to center as to whether AYA patients with mature B-cell lymphoma are treated by pediatric or adult oncologists
    - These guidelines are intended to apply to AYA patients with good organ function treated in a pediatric oncology setting
    - AYA patients treated in an adult oncology setting should be treated as per the adult NCCN Guidelines for B-Cell Lymphomas
- See NCCN Guidelines for Adolescent and Young Adult (AYA) Oncology

- See also Use of Immunophenotyping/Genetic Testing in Differential Diagnosis of Mature B-Cell and NK/T-Cell Neoplasms (NHODG-A) in the NCCN Guidelines for B-Cell Lymphomas

# Biopsy

- Excisional or incisional biopsy is preferred
    - Take the most accessible and/or the most FDG-avid site
- Touch preparations of fresh lesional tissue, encouraged whenever possible
    - If done properly, they may reveal essential cytologic details that may be difficult to detect in small biopsies (eg, small needle core biopsy)
- Core needle biopsy is less optimal
    - Use only when a lymph node or tumor mass is not easily accessible for excisional or incisional biopsy
    - Cores must be of sufficient size and number to allow adequate evaluation of morphology
    - Cores must be of sufficient size and number to allow all necessary ancillary studies, as applicable
        - Immunohistochemistry (IHC)
        - Flow cytometry
        - Karyotype
        - Fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) for major translocations
- Fine-needle aspiration (FNA) biopsy alone is not suitable for the initial diagnosis of pediatric lymphoma
- Place fresh specimen in saline, not formalin
    - Ensures viable diagnostic tissue for the pathologist

# Pathology

- Hematopathology review of all slides as clinically indicated
- Touch preparation for cytologic examination is recommended
- Follow Principles of Diagnostic Pathology (PBCL-A)
