# Essential

- History
    - Transplant history (orthotopic transplant or SOT)
        - Timing since transplant
        - Current/past immunosuppressive therapies
        - Recurrent infections
        - Immunodeficiency
    - Infection history
        - EBV viral load status
        - Recurrent infections prior to transplant
- Assess for distress
    - Refer to the NCCN Distress Thermometer and Problem List, which includes social determinants of health
    - See NCCN Guidelines for Distress Management (DIS-A)
- Baseline re-evaluation of graft organ function and any episodes of graft rejection by a graft transplant specialist, as appropriate
- Physical examination, with attention to
    - Lymph nodes
    - Tonsils
    - Liver and spleen
    - Effusions, ascites
    - Neurologic signs/symptoms
- EBV PCR (whole blood or plasma)
    - An elevated EBV PCR is not diagnostic of PTLD
    - EBV viral load cannot be used for response assessment in PTLD
- Laboratory tests
    - CBC with differential
    - Electrolytes
    - Calcium, phosphorus
    - BUN, creatinine
    - Uric acid
    - LDH
    - AST, ALT, bilirubin, albumin
    - Immunosuppressive medication levels
    - Consider hepatitis B testing (HBcAb, HBsAb, HBsAg)
    - Consider HIV testing, if indicated
    - Consider G6PD testing for male patients, see Principles of Supportive Care (PBCL-D)
    - Pregnancy test for patients of childbearing age
- Bilateral bone marrow aspirate and biopsy (in select cases)
    - Useful in selected cases of M-PTLD
    - Not essential for hyperplastic ND-PTLD, P-PTLD, and in localized tonsillar disease
- Lumbar puncture (in select cases)
    - CSF cell count and differential
    - Cytology of CSF, including total nucleated cell count and morphologic review of cytospin
    - Useful in selected cases of M-PTLD; not essential for hyperplastic ND-PTLD, P-PTLD, and in localized tonsillar disease
- Imaging
    - Cross-sectional imaging of the neck, chest, abdomen, and pelvis are preferred
        - Neck CT with IV contrast, or MRI with and without contrast
        - Chest CT with IV contrast
        - Abdomen and pelvis CT with oral and IV contrast, or MRI with and without contrast
    - FDG-PET/CT or FDG-PET/MRI, if available (do not delay treatment to obtain)
        - Obtaining a PET/CT or PET/MRI does not exclude the need for full diagnostic quality high-resolution CT or MRI
    - For CNS PTLD
        - MRI of the brain with and without contrast
        - MRI of the spine with and without contrast
- Fertility counseling recommended for all patients; fertility preservation as clinically appropriate
    - Fertility preservation is an option for some patients
    - Options include sperm cryopreservation, oocyte cryopreservation, harvesting of ovarian or testicular tissue for cryopreservation, or embryo cryopreservation
    - Referral to a fertility preservation/reproductive health program should be considered for eligible patients prior to initiation of chemotherapy
    - See NCCN Guidelines for Adolescent and Young Adult (AYA) Oncology

# Useful under certain circumstances

- MRI of the head with and without contrast
- MRI of the spine with and without contrast
- Flow cytometry of CSF
    - Not routinely recommended, but may be useful at the pathologist's discretion
- EBV PCR of CSF
- Flow cytometry, FISH for MYC rearrangement, and IHC of bone marrow aspirate and biopsy
    - For low-level or morphologically indeterminate involvement
- Consider baseline immunoglobulin panel prior to using rituximab, or if there are any underlying concerns about immunodeficiency
