# Source

- NCCN Rectal Cancer v2.2026, REC-3, p13
- Applies to rectal cancer without suspected or proven distant metastases
- Principles of Surgery and Locoregional Therapies (REC-C)
- Principles of Imaging (REC-A)
- Principles of Radiation Therapy (REC-E)
- Principles of Nonoperative Management (REC-H)
- All recommendations category 2A unless otherwise indicated

# Assessment

- ___ (age) yo
- Clinical stage ___ (ctnm)
- Rectal cancer without suspected or proven distant metastases
    - The rectum lies below the peritoneal reflection from the sacral promontory to the upper edge of the symphysis as determined by MRI
- Counseled for family history
    - Suspected LS, FAP, or attenuated FAP, see the NCCN Guidelines for Genetic/Familial High-Risk Assessment: Colorectal, Endometrial, and Gastric
- Melanoma histology, see the NCCN Guidelines for Melanoma: Cutaneous
- For tools to aid optimal assessment and care of older adults with cancer, see the NCCN Guidelines for Older Adult Oncology

# T1 (favorable risk), N0

- Endoscopic submucosal dissection (ESD) (REC-C)
    - Then Adjuvant Treatment (REC-4)
- or Transanal local excision (REC-C)
    - Then Adjuvant Treatment (REC-4)
- or Transabdominal resection (REC-C)
    - Then Adjuvant Treatment (REC-5)

# T1 (high risk), T2, N0

- High-risk features
    - Positive margins
    - Lymphovascular invasion
    - Poorly differentiated tumors
    - sm3 invasion, that is submucosal invasion to the lower third of the submucosal level
- Transabdominal resection (preferred) (REC-C)
    - Then Adjuvant Treatment (REC-5)
- or Long-course chemo/radiation therapy (RT), with capecitabine or infusional fluorouracil (REC-E)
- or Short-course RT (REC-E)
    - Evaluation for short-course RT should be in a multidisciplinary setting, with a discussion of the need for downstaging and the possibility of long-term toxicity
    - Short-course RT can be considered for preoperative radiation, but for high-risk rectal cancer the 5-year follow-up of the RAPIDO trial now indicates a statistically higher locoregional recurrence rate with short-course RT
        - High-risk here means clinical tumor stage cT4a or cT4b
        - Extramural vascular invasion (EMVI)
        - Clinical nodal stage c-N2
        - Involved mesorectal fascia (MRF)
        - Enlarged lateral lymph nodes considered to be metastatic

# After long-course chemo/RT or short-course RT

- Consider FOLFOX or CAPEOX
- Restaging (REC-A)
- Transabdominal resection (REC-C)
- or Transanal local excision for select patients (REC-C)
- or If complete clinical response, consider surveillance (REC-10A)
    - A watch and wait, nonoperative management approach may be considered in centers with experienced multidisciplinary teams
    - The degree to which risk of local and/or distant recurrence may be increased relative to standard surgical resection has not yet been adequately characterized
    - Decisions for nonoperative management (NOM) should involve a careful discussion with the patient of their risk tolerance
    - Principles of Nonoperative Management (REC-H)
