# Source

- NCCN Rectal Cancer v2.2026, REC-1, p10
- Applies to a pedunculated or sessile polyp (adenoma) with invasive cancer
- All patients with rectal cancer should be counseled for family history
    - Suspected Lynch syndrome (LS), familial adenomatous polyposis (FAP), and attenuated FAP, see the NCCN Guidelines for Genetic/Familial High-Risk Assessment: Colorectal, Endometrial, and Gastric
- For melanoma histology, see the NCCN Guidelines for Melanoma: Cutaneous
- All recommendations are category 2A unless otherwise indicated

# Workup

- Refer to the NCCN Distress Thermometer and Problem List, which includes social determinants of health
    - See NCCN Guidelines for Distress Management (DIS-A)
- Pathology review
    - Confirm the presence of invasive cancer (pT1)
    - pTis has no biological potential to metastasize
    - It has not been established if molecular markers, other than MSI-high (MSI-H)/MMR deficient (dMMR), are useful in treatment determination (predictive markers) and prognosis
- Colonoscopy
- Marking of cancerous polyp site
    - At time of colonoscopy, or within 2 weeks if appropriate
- Mismatch repair (MMR)/microsatellite instability (MSI) testing
    - Principles of Pathologic Review (REC-B)

# Findings: single specimen, favorable histology, clear margins

- Single specimen
- Completely removed
- Favorable histologic features
    - Principles of Pathologic Review (REC-B)
- Clear margins
- pT1 only
- Endoscopic ultrasound (EUS) or pelvis MRI

# Primary treatment if single specimen, favorable histology, clear margins, one of

- Observe
    - Historical data have suggested that endoscopically resected sessile malignant polyps with submucosal invasion harbored higher risk of residual disease and/or lymph node metastases than similar lesions that were of pedunculated morphology
    - More recently, evidence suggests that margin status and the presence or absence of unfavorable histologic features may be the most important predictors of residual disease/lymph node metastases regardless of morphologic type
    - See Principles of Pathologic Review (REC-B) - Endoscopically Removed Malignant Polyps
- Transanal local excision, if appropriate
    - Principles of Surgery and Locoregional Therapies (REC-C)
- Transabdominal resection
    - Principles of Surgery and Locoregional Therapies (REC-C)

# Findings: fragmented specimen, margin not assessable, or unfavorable histology

- Fragmented specimen
- or Margin cannot be assessed
- or Unfavorable histologic features
    - Principles of Pathologic Review (REC-B)

# Workup if fragmented, margin not assessable, or unfavorable histology

- Consider proctoscopy
    - Principles of Surgery and Locoregional Therapies (REC-C)
- Chest CT
- Abdominal CT or MRI
    - Principles of Imaging (REC-A)
- Complete blood count (CBC)
- Chemistry profile
- Carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA)
- Pelvis MRI with or without contrast
    - Principles of Imaging (REC-A)
- EUS, if MRI is contraindicated, inconclusive, or for superficial lesions
    - Principles of Imaging (REC-A)
- Enterostomal therapist as indicated for preoperative marking of site, teaching
- FDG-PET/CT is not indicated
    - Principles of Imaging (REC-A)

# Primary treatment if fragmented, margin not assessable, or unfavorable histology, one of

- Transanal local excision, if appropriate
    - Principles of Surgery and Locoregional Therapies (REC-C)
- Transabdominal resection
    - Principles of Surgery and Locoregional Therapies (REC-C)

# Next

- Observe, go to Surveillance (REC-10)

- Fragmented specimen, margin cannot be assessed, or unfavorable histologic features, after transanal local excision or transabdominal resection, go to Adjuvant Treatment (REC-5)
