# Source

- NCCN Breast Cancer v6.2026, DCIS-1, p12
- Applies to DCIS Tis, N0, M0
- Also encapsulated papillary carcinoma (EPC) without associated conventional invasion, staged as pTis because behavior is similar to DCIS (per AJCC)
- Also solid papillary carcinoma (SPC), which should be specified as in situ or invasive based on WHO criteria; both forms have favorable outcomes
- All recommendations category 2A unless stated otherwise

# Workup

- History and physical exam
- Diagnostic bilateral mammogram
- Pathology review
    - The Panel endorses the College of American Pathologists Protocol for pathology reporting for all invasive and noninvasive carcinomas of the breast (http://www.cap.org)
- Determination of tumor estrogen receptor (ER) status
- Genetic counseling for patients at risk of hereditary breast cancer
    - For risk criteria, see NCCN Guidelines for Genetic/Familial High-Risk Assessment: Breast, Ovarian, Pancreatic, and Prostate
- Breast MRI as indicated
    - Principles of Dedicated Breast MRI Testing (BINV-B)
    - MRI has not been shown to increase likelihood of negative margins or decrease conversion to mastectomy
    - Data to support improved long-term outcomes are lacking
- Assess for distress
    - Refer to the NCCN Guidelines for Distress Management (DIS-A) for the NCCN Distress Thermometer and Problem List, which includes social determinants of health

# Primary treatment option 1: breast-conserving surgery

- Breast-conserving surgery (BCS) without lymph node surgery
    - Do not perform surgical axillary staging for preoperative (biopsy-determined) pure DCIS, unless there is some clinical-radiographic-pathologic suggestion of invasion or axillary metastasis
    - SLNB may be considered for concern of persistently positive margins
    - SLNB may be considered for excision in an anatomic location compromising the performance of a future SLNB procedure
    - SLNB may be considered for oncoplastic breast-conserving surgery (see BINV-D for considerations for SLNB)
    - Re-resection(s) may be performed in an effort to obtain negative margins in patients desiring breast-conservation therapy
    - Patients in whom adequate surgical margins cannot be achieved with BCS should undergo a total mastectomy
    - For definition of adequate surgical margins, see Margin Status Recommendations After BCS for Invasive Cancers and DCIS (BINV-F)
- Then one of the radiation options below

# Radiation therapy after BCS

- Check contraindications to breast-conserving approaches requiring RT (BINV-G) before choosing an RT option
- Principles of Radiation Therapy (BINV-I)
- Whole breast radiation therapy (RT) (category 1), with or without boost to tumor bed
    - Whole breast RT following BCS reduces ipsilateral breast tumor recurrence (IBTR) rates for all DCIS by 50%-70%
    - Approximately half of the recurrences are invasive and half are DCIS
- or Accelerated partial breast irradiation/partial breast radiation (APBI/PBI)
- or No RT (category 2B)
    - The risk of local relapse increases with larger DCIS, palpable mass, grade III disease, margins <=2 mm, ER-negative tumors, and age <50
    - Omission of RT and treatment with endocrine therapy alone may be considered if all RTOG 9804 criteria are met: screen-detected, grade 1-2, size <=2.5 cm, margins >=3 mm

# Primary treatment option 2: total mastectomy

- Total mastectomy with sentinel lymph node biopsy (SLNB), with or without reconstruction
    - Omission of SLNB in the setting of mastectomy may be considered in patients >50 years with non-palpable low-grade ER-positive DCIS with no abnormal axillary findings
    - Omission of SLNB in the setting of mastectomy may be considered in patients in whom the primary reason for mastectomy is patient preference
    - Principles of Breast Reconstruction Following Surgery (BINV-H)
