# Exam

- History and physical exam 1–4 times per year as clinically appropriate for 5 years, then annually

# Genetic screening

- Periodic screening for changes in family history
- Periodic screening for changes in genetic testing indications
- Referral to genetic counseling as indicated, see NCCN Guidelines for Genetic/Familial High-Risk Assessment: Breast, Ovarian, Pancreatic, and Prostate

# Postsurgical management

- Educate for lymphedema management, see NCCN Guidelines for Survivorship: Lymphedema
- Monitor for lymphedema
- Refer for lymphedema management

# Breast imaging

- Mammography every 12 months, beginning 6 months or more after completion of breast-conservation therapy
    - Annual mammograms are the appropriate frequency for surveillance of patients who have had BCS and RT, with no clear advantage to shorter interval imaging
    - Wait 6 to 12 months after completion of RT before beginning annual mammogram surveillance
    - Suspicious findings on physical examination or surveillance imaging warrant immediate follow-up/workup
- Routine imaging of reconstructed breast is not indicated after mastectomy
- For patients with germline mutations or family history of breast cancer, refer to NCCN Guidelines for Genetic/Familial High-Risk Assessment: Breast, Ovarian, Pancreatic, and Prostate

# Fertility, birth control, and sexual health

- See BINV-C

# Screening for metastases

- In the absence of clinical signs and symptoms suggestive of recurrent disease, there is no indication for laboratory studies for metastases screening
- In the absence of clinical signs and symptoms suggestive of recurrent disease, there is no indication for imaging studies for metastases screening

# Post-treatment monitoring

- Cardiotoxicity monitoring for patients who received left-sided RT, anthracyclines, or HER2-targeted therapy
    - For anthracycline-induced toxicity, see NCCN Guidelines for Survivorship
- Provide guidance on risk of comorbidities

# Endocrine therapy

- Assess and encourage adherence to adjuvant endocrine therapy
- Patients on tamoxifen
    - Age-appropriate gynecologic screening
    - Routine annual pelvis ultrasound is not recommended
- Patients on an aromatase inhibitor, or who experience ovarian failure secondary to treatment
    - Monitoring of bone health with a bone mineral density (BMD) determination at baseline and periodically thereafter
    - The use of estrogen, progesterone, or selective ER modulators to treat osteoporosis or osteopenia is discouraged
    - A bisphosphonate (oral/IV) or denosumab is acceptable to maintain or improve BMD and reduce risk of fractures in postmenopausal (natural or induced) patients receiving adjuvant aromatase inhibitor therapy
        - Optimal duration of either therapy has not been established, and benefit of duration beyond 3 years is not known
        - Factors to consider for duration: BMD, response to therapy, and risk factors for continued bone loss or fracture
        - There are case reports of spontaneous fractures after denosumab discontinuation
        - Dental examination with preventive dentistry prior to initiation of therapy
        - Supplemental calcium and vitamin D

# Lifestyle

- Evidence suggests the following may lead to optimal breast cancer outcomes, see NCCN Guidelines for Breast Cancer Risk Reduction
    - Active lifestyle
    - Healthy diet
    - Limited alcohol intake
    - Achieving and maintaining an ideal body weight (20–25 body mass index [BMI])
- Menopausal hormone therapy is relatively contraindicated in survivors of hormonally mediated cancers
    - Should be used with caution in those with increased genetic cancer risk, see NCCN Guidelines for Survivorship

# Psychosocial support

- Survivors are at elevated risk for fear of recurrence, distress, anxiety, and depression that may persist for many years after diagnosis
- Periodic screening is recommended
- Referral to mental health professionals if needed, see NCCN Guidelines for Survivorship

# Communication

- Coordination of care between the primary care physician (PCP) and specialists is encouraged
- Personalized survivorship treatment plan, see NCCN Guidelines for Survivorship
    - Personalized treatment summary of possible long-term toxicity
    - Clear follow-up recommendations

# Engagement

- Follow-up encouragement to improve adherence to ongoing screening and medication adherence, see NCCN Guidelines for Patients
