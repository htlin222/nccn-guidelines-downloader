# Workup

- History and physical exam
    - For tools to aid optimal assessment and comprehensive care of older adults, see NCCN Guidelines for Older Adult Oncology
- Discuss goals of therapy
- Adopt shared decision-making
- Document course of care
- CBC
- Comprehensive metabolic panel
    - LFTs
    - Alkaline phosphatase

# Imaging for systemic staging

- Chest diagnostic CT with or without contrast
- Abdomen, with or without pelvis, diagnostic CT with contrast or MRI with contrast
- Brain MRI with contrast, if suspicious CNS symptoms
    - For the treatment of brain metastases, see NCCN Guidelines for Central Nervous System Cancers
- Spine MRI with contrast, if back pain or symptoms of cord compression
- Bone scan or sodium fluoride PET/CT (category 2B)
- Useful in certain circumstances
    - FDG-PET/CT
        - Consider FES-PET/CT for ER-positive disease and lobular histology
    - X-rays of symptomatic bones, and of long and weight-bearing bones abnormal on bone scan

# Biomarker testing

- Biopsy of at least first recurrence of disease
    - Consider re-biopsy if progression
    - Where a biopsy cannot safely be obtained but the clinical evidence is strongly supportive of recurrence, treatment may commence based on the ER/PR/HER2 status of the primary tumor
    - ER/PR and HER2 status can change with treatment and metastatic progression, so consider repeat testing on new samples in these scenarios if management will change
- Evaluation of ER/PR and HER2 status
    - Principles of Biomarker Testing (BINV-A)
    - False-negative ER and/or PR determinations occur, and there may be discordance between the ER and/or PR determination between the primary and metastatic tumor(s)
    - Endocrine therapy, with its low attendant toxicity, may be considered in patients with non-visceral or asymptomatic visceral tumors
    - Especially in patients with clinical characteristics predicting for an HR-positive tumor: long disease-free interval, limited sites of recurrence, indolent disease, older age
- Multigene panel testing (MGPT), somatic and germline, to identify candidates for targeted therapies (BINV-Q)
    - Tumor tissue or plasma-based circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA) assays may be used, each with benefits and limitations for diagnosis and disease progression
    - Tissue-based assays have greater sensitivity for some alterations, but ctDNA may reflect tumor heterogeneity more accurately
    - If one specimen is negative for actionable biomarkers, testing on the alternative specimen can be considered

# Other assessments

- Genetic counseling if patient is at risk for hereditary breast cancer
    - For risk criteria, see NCCN Guidelines for Genetic/Familial High-Risk Assessment: Breast, Ovarian, Pancreatic, and Prostate
- Assess for distress
    - Refer to the NCCN Guidelines for Distress Management (DIS-A) for the NCCN Distress Thermometer and Problem List, which includes social determinants of health

# Next

- Local or regional recurrence, go to BINV-19, plus supportive care
    - See NCCN Guidelines for Palliative Care and NCCN Guidelines for Supportive Care
- Recurrent unresectable (local or regional) or stage IV (M1), go to BINV-21 for systemic treatment, plus supportive care
    - See NCCN Guidelines for Palliative Care and NCCN Guidelines for Supportive Care
    - Optimal management of oligometastatic stage IV breast cancers remains uncertain
    - Routine utilization of locoregional treatment of the primary tumor, and ablative treatment for metastatic sites, have not been demonstrated to improve survival outcomes in prospective trials, however may reduce the risk of local progression and palliate symptoms
    - Patients with de novo oligometastatic cancer have better outcomes than those with recurrent oligometastatic disease
    - Therapeutic decisions should be individualized and discussed in a multidisciplinary context
    - For de novo oligometastatic disease with favorable features, an aggressive multimodality approach may offer improved long-term PFS and may be considered
        - Favorable features: HER2-positive or ER-positive, induced NED status after receipt of optimal multi-agent systemic regimens, limited sites of metastases, bone-only metastases, and young age/longevity
        - Locoregional therapy of the primary tumor
        - Ablative treatment to all sites of metastases
        - Adjuvant-like systemic therapy
