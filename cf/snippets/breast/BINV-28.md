- NCCN Breast Cancer v6.2026, BINV-28, p41
- Principles of survivorship for those living with advanced or metastatic breast cancer
- Applies to unresectable, stage IV (M1) or metastatic disease
- Management requires a multifaceted approach, encompassing treatment and a comprehensive survivorship plan
- Principles of monitoring metastatic disease: BINV-R
- All recommendations category 2A unless otherwise indicated

# General principles

- Encourage the patient to promptly report any new or worsening symptoms
    - A comprehensive evaluation is essential
    - Symptoms could indicate disease progression, treatment effects, or other unrelated medical issues
- Additional health monitoring, management, and immunizations, as appropriate to the prognosis, under the care of a PCP
- Encourage the patient to maintain a therapeutic relationship with a PCP throughout their lifetime

# Treatment management and supportive care

- Regular follow-up discussions about goals of care and care coordination
    - May be necessary to improve both survival and quality of life
    - Include therapy cessation, so that care aligns with the patient values and preferences
- Individualize surveillance and follow-up plans for all patients, particularly older patients
    - Comorbidities
    - Functional status
    - Life expectancy
- Multidisciplinary and interprofessional care that seeks to prevent and/or manage morbidities associated with breast cancer or its treatment
- Coordination between primary care providers and specialists, for continuity across transitions between care settings and providers
- Address all treatment options at each progression, including the option of supportive care alone or hospice care
- Consider early involvement of a palliative care team
    - Advance care planning
    - Goals of care discussions
    - Care transitions
    - Psychosocial support
    - Management of cancer and therapy-related symptoms

# Routine screening, evaluation, and assessment for supportive care interventions and referrals

- Hormone-related symptoms (NCCN Guidelines for Survivorship)
- Cognitive dysfunction (NCCN Guidelines for Survivorship)
- Peripheral neuropathy
- Cancer-related pain (NCCN Guidelines for Adult Cancer Pain)
- Cancer-related fatigue (NCCN Guidelines for Cancer-Related Fatigue)
- Cancer-related infections (NCCN Guidelines for Prevention and Treatment of Cancer-Related Infections)
- Chemotherapy-induced anemia, neutropenia, and thrombocytopenia (NCCN Guidelines for Hematopoietic Growth Factors)
- Immunotherapy-related toxicities (NCCN Guidelines for Management of Immune Checkpoint Inhibitor-Related Toxicities)
- Cardiac monitoring, if treated with anthracyclines or HER2-targeted therapy (NCCN Guidelines for Survivorship)
- Nausea/vomiting, anorexia, cachexia, diarrhea, dyspnea, malignant wounds, sleep-wake disturbances (NCCN Guidelines for Antiemesis and other NCCN Guidelines for Supportive Care)

# Lifestyle, wellness, and psychosocial support

- Encourage a physically active lifestyle and a healthy diet whenever possible (NCCN Guidelines for Survivorship)
- Consider appropriate cancer and preventive health screenings per patient age and sex assigned-at-birth
    - Individualize these decisions according to the patient prognosis
- Screen for anxiety, fear of progression, depression, and post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) related to cancer and its treatment
    - NCCN Guidelines for Distress Management
    - NCCN Guidelines for Survivorship
- Social determinants of health monitoring and referrals
    - Provide resources and assistance through financial navigators, social workers, or other specified personnel
        - Financial concerns
        - Employment issues
        - Family support
    - NCCN Guidelines for Palliative Care: Social Support/Resource Management
    - Financial Advocacy Services Guidelines (Association of Cancer Care Centers)
    - Financial Toxicity and Cancer Treatment (National Cancer Institute)
    - NCCN Guidelines for Distress Management
- Facilitate connections with consumer groups, support networks, and organizations that advocate for accessible and equitable care
- Sexual health concerns, or fertility and birth control in premenopausal patients, see BINV-C

# Communication and engagement

- Consistent and clear communication, while acknowledging uncertainties of disease trajectory and prognosis
- Communicate treatment goals, survivorship issues, and the specific roles of each provider to the patient
- NCCN Resources for Patients and Caregivers and Guidelines for Patients
- Address long-term survivorship issues specific to younger patients, who may face unique challenges that require targeted interventions
    - Psychosocial development
    - Educational or vocational support
- Discuss living will and end-of-life planning
