# Workup, acute GVHD suspected

- Additional tests as clinically indicated to rule out non-GVHD causes
    - Diarrhea: stool testing may be used to rule out an infectious etiology
    - Liver-associated enzyme abnormalities: consider imaging as clinically indicated to evaluate the etiology (eg, ultrasound and/or CT scan of the abdomen)
    - Liver dysfunction: liver biopsy and/or viral reactivation testing may be used to rule out non-GVHD causes (ie, VOD/SOS, infection, effects of preparatory regimen, drug toxicity)
        - Transjugular approach may be preferred, especially if thrombocytopenia or coagulopathy is present
- Organ-directed biopsy, as clinically indicated, to support acute GVHD diagnosis
    - Skin rash: consider biopsy of suspicious skin sites
    - Diarrhea: lower gastrointestinal (GI) biopsy
        - GI biopsy (esophagogastroduodenoscopy, colonoscopy, and/or flexible sigmoidoscopy) as clinically indicated to support the diagnosis of GI acute GVHD
    - Nausea/vomiting: consider upper GI biopsy
        - Same GI biopsy modalities as above, as clinically indicated to support the diagnosis of GI acute GVHD
    - Liver abnormalities: consider liver biopsy if elevated liver-associated enzymes or total/direct bilirubin and no evidence of acute GVHD elsewhere
        - Transjugular approach may be preferred, especially if thrombocytopenia or coagulopathy is present
- Determine acute GVHD grade, see Acute GVHD Grading Criteria (GVHD-A)

# Workup, chronic GVHD suspected

- Confirm chronic GVHD
    - A biopsy may be done to confirm chronic GVHD
    - A biopsy is not always feasible and is not mandatory if the patient has at least one of the diagnostic findings of chronic GVHD
- See Signs and Symptoms of Chronic GVHD (GVHD-B)
- Determine chronic GVHD grade, see Chronic GVHD: Grading (GVHD-C)
