# Grade I acute GVHD

- Skin 1–2
- Less than 50% BSA
- Non-bullous rash only

# First-line therapy for Grade I acute GVHD

- Continue or consider restarting original immunosuppressive agent(s)
- Topical steroids until resolution of rash
    - Triamcinolone or clobetasol, and/or topical tacrolimus
    - Medium to high potency formulations are recommended, except on the face or intertriginous areas
    - Low potency hydrocortisone on the face or intertriginous areas
    - Antihistamines may be used for symptoms, ie, itching, as needed
- For antibiotic prophylaxis during immunosuppressive therapy, see NCCN Guidelines for Prevention and Treatment of Cancer-Related Infections

# Response to first-line therapy, by the criteria in GVHD-D

- Response, ie, resolution of symptoms/rash
    - Taper immunosuppressive agent(s) as clinically feasible
- No response
    - Clinical trial, or
    - Continue topical steroids
        - Same agents, potency and antihistamine rules as above
    - Observe if asymptomatic or if rash is stable
- Progression and/or symptomatic, ie, pruritus, pain, sloughing, increasing BSA involvement
    - Treat as acute GVHD Grade II–IV

# Clinical trial enrollment

- Enrollment in well-designed clinical trials should be encouraged, since no standard, effective therapy for steroid-refractory GVHD has been identified
- Selection of therapy for steroid-refractory GVHD should be based on
    - Physician experience
    - Agent's toxicity profile
    - The effect of prior treatment
    - Drug interactions
    - Convenience/accessibility
    - Patient tolerability
