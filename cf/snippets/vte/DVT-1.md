# Clinical suspicion of acute DVT

- Swelling of unilateral extremity
- Heaviness in extremity
- Pain in extremity
- Unexplained persistent calf cramping
- Swelling in face, neck, or supraclavicular space
- Catheter dysfunction

# Workup / imaging

- Comprehensive medical H&P
- CBC with platelet count
- PT, aPTT, with or without fibrinogen
- Liver and kidney function tests
- Venous US
    - In cases with high suspicion of DVT and no contraindications, consider initiating early anticoagulation while awaiting imaging results
- Consider CT venogram (CTV) or magnetic resonance venogram (MRV)

# Imaging findings

- Positive for DVT, go to treatment (DVT-2)
- Negative or indeterminate, ask whether clinical suspicion of DVT continues
    - No continued clinical suspicion
        - Reassurance
        - Evaluate for other causes
    - Continued clinical suspicion, repeat venous imaging

# Additional imaging, if clinical suspicion continues

- Repeat venous US
- CTV with contrast
- MRV with contrast
- Positive for DVT, go to treatment (DVT-2)
- Negative
    - Reassurance
    - Evaluate for other causes

# Incidental acute DVT

- If not already performed
    - Comprehensive medical H&P
    - CBC with platelet count
    - PT, aPTT, with or without fibrinogen
    - Liver and kidney function tests
    - Venous US
        - If initial imaging results are inconclusive, consider venous US to confirm diagnosis
