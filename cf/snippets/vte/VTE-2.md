# Surgical oncology setting

- Out-of-hospital primary VTE prophylaxis is recommended for up to 4 weeks postoperative following high-risk abdominal or pelvic cancer surgery
    - High risk following abdominal/pelvic cancer surgery includes any of:
        - Surgery for gastrointestinal (GI) malignancies
        - Previous history of VTE
        - Anesthesia time >2 hours
        - Bed rest 4 days or more
        - Advanced-stage disease
        - Patient age >60 years
    - Patients with gastric and gastroesophageal tumors are at increased risk for hemorrhage with direct oral anticoagulants (DOACs)
    - Prophylactic anticoagulation options (VTE-B 4)

# Medical oncology setting

- Patients with multiple myeloma starting or receiving systemic therapy treatment
    - See NCCN Guidelines for Multiple Myeloma
- Patients with cancer receiving or starting systemic therapy for their cancer: VTE risk evaluation based on Khorana score (VTE-C)
    - Excludes multiple myeloma, acute leukemia, myeloproliferative neoplasms (MPNs), and patients with primary/metastatic brain tumors
        - Patients receiving hormonal therapy were excluded from the AVERT but not the CASSINI trial
    - Khorana score ___ (khorana)
    - Intermediate or high risk for VTE (Khorana score 2 or more)
        - Consider anticoagulant prophylaxis for up to 6 months or longer, if risk persists (see VTE-B 2 for dosing)
        - Check contraindications to VTE prophylaxis first (VTE-A)
        - Patients with gastric and gastroesophageal tumors are at increased risk for hemorrhage with direct oral anticoagulants (DOACs)
    - Low risk for VTE (Khorana score below 2)
        - No routine VTE prophylaxis
    - Prophylactic anticoagulation options (VTE-B 4)
