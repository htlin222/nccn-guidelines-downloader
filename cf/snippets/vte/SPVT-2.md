# Acute hepatic vein thrombosis, symptoms/signs 8 weeks or less

- No contraindication to anticoagulation
    - Anticoagulation
        - Weigh risks/benefits of anticoagulation, particularly for chronic thromboses
        - Duration should be at least 6 months for triggered events, eg, postsurgical
        - Duration indefinite if active cancer, persistent thrombophilic state, or unprovoked thrombotic event
        - Therapeutic Anticoagulation for VTE (VTE-D)
    - Hepatology/hematology evaluation
    - Consider catheter-directed pharmacomechanical thrombectomy, with or without transjugular intrahepatic portosystemic shunt (TIPS)
        - Consider TIPS as one of the management options for patients with SPVT and portal hypertension
        - If thrombectomy expertise is not available, consider consultation with a tertiary medical center
        - Providers can consult with interventional radiology or vascular surgery colleagues to determine the appropriate use of mechanical embolectomy, suction embolectomy, and US-facilitated catheter-directed thrombolysis devices at their institutions
        - Decision to offer thrombolysis should be based on local availability/expertise, location of thrombus, and risk of bleeding
        - Choice of regimen should be made based on institutional expertise/preferences in conjunction with interventional radiology or vascular surgery colleagues; see Thrombolytic Agents (VTE-H)
        - Contraindications to Thrombolysis and Indications for Thrombolysis (VTE-I)
- Contraindication to anticoagulation
    - Hepatology/hematology evaluation
    - Reassess contraindications to anticoagulation regularly
    - Consider TIPS or surgical shunt
        - Consider TIPS as one of the management options for patients with SPVT and portal hypertension

# Chronic hepatic vein thrombosis, symptoms more than 8 weeks

- Hepatology/hematology evaluation
- Consider TIPS or surgical shunt
    - Consider TIPS as one of the management options for patients with SPVT and portal hypertension
- Consider anticoagulation
    - Weigh risks/benefits of anticoagulation, particularly for chronic thromboses
    - Duration should be at least 6 months for triggered events, eg, postsurgical
    - Duration indefinite if active cancer, persistent thrombophilic state, or unprovoked thrombotic event
    - Therapeutic Anticoagulation for VTE (VTE-D)

# Acute portal, mesenteric, and/or splenic vein thrombosis

- This arm requires all three
    - Symptoms/signs 8 weeks or less
    - No cavernous transformation/collaterals
    - No signs of portal hypertension
- No contraindication to anticoagulation
    - Anticoagulation
        - Weigh risks/benefits of anticoagulation, particularly for chronic thromboses
        - Duration should be at least 6 months for triggered events, eg, postsurgical
        - Duration indefinite if active cancer, persistent thrombophilic state, or unprovoked thrombotic event
        - Therapeutic Anticoagulation for VTE (VTE-D)
    - Surgery, if bowel infarction
    - Consider catheter-directed pharmacomechanical thrombectomy, with or without TIPS
        - Therapeutic Anticoagulation for VTE (VTE-D)
        - Consider TIPS as one of the management options for patients with SPVT and portal hypertension
        - Providers can consult with interventional radiology or vascular surgery colleagues to determine the appropriate use of mechanical embolectomy, suction embolectomy, and US-facilitated catheter-directed thrombolysis devices at their institutions
        - Decision to offer thrombolysis should be based on local availability/expertise, location of thrombus, and risk of bleeding
        - Choice of regimen should be made based on institutional expertise/preferences in conjunction with interventional radiology or vascular surgery colleagues; see Thrombolytic Agents (VTE-H)
        - Contraindications to Thrombolysis and Indications for Thrombolysis (VTE-I)
- Contraindication to anticoagulation
    - GI/surgery evaluation
    - Surgery, if bowel infarction
    - Reassess contraindications to anticoagulation regularly

# Chronic portal, mesenteric, and/or splenic vein thrombosis

- This arm applies if any one is present
    - Symptoms more than 8 weeks
    - Cavernous transformation/collaterals noted
    - Signs of portal hypertension
- GI evaluation
- Beta blockade
- Consider variceal banding or sclerosis
- Consider anticoagulation
    - Weigh risks/benefits of anticoagulation, particularly for chronic thromboses
    - Duration should be at least 6 months for triggered events, eg, postsurgical
    - Duration indefinite if active cancer, persistent thrombophilic state, or unprovoked thrombotic event
    - Therapeutic Anticoagulation for VTE (VTE-D)
- Consider TIPS or surgical shunt
    - Consider TIPS as one of the management options for patients with SPVT and portal hypertension
