# Source

- NCCN Anal Carcinoma v2.2026, ANAL-2, p8
- Applies to perianal cancer with biopsy showing squamous cell carcinoma
    - The perianal region starts at the anal verge and includes the perianal skin over a 5-cm radius from the squamous mucocutaneous junction
    - For melanoma histology, see the NCCN Guidelines for Melanoma: Cutaneous
    - For adenocarcinoma, see the NCCN Guidelines for Rectal Cancer
- All recommendations are category 2A unless otherwise indicated

# Workup

- DRE
- Inguinal lymph node evaluation
    - Consider biopsy or FNA if suspicious nodes
- Chest CT
    - Intravenous contrast is not required for the chest CT
- Abdomen CT
    - CT should be with IV and oral contrast
- Pelvis CT or MRI
    - Pelvis MRI with contrast
    - If intravenous iodinated contrast material is contraindicated due to significant contrast allergy or renal failure, MRI of the abdomen and pelvis with IV GBCA can be obtained in select patients
    - See American College of Radiology contrast manual
- Consider FDG-PET/CT or FDG-PET/MRI
    - Does not replace a diagnostic CT
    - FDG-PET/CT performed skull base to mid-thigh
- Anoscopy
- HIV testing, if HIV status unknown
- Gynecologic exam, including screening for cervical cancer
- Consider fertility risk discussion/counseling in appropriate patients
- For information on DPYD testing and fluoropyrimidine-associated toxicity, see the NCCN Guidelines for Colon Cancer
- Refer to the NCCN Distress Thermometer and Problem List, which includes social determinants of health, see NCCN Guidelines for Distress Management (DIS-A)

# Primary treatment

- Modifications to cancer treatment should not be made solely based on HIV status, see NCCN Guidelines for Cancer in People with HIV
- T1, N0 well or moderately differentiated, or select T2, N0 that does not involve sphincter
    - Local excision, Principles of Surgery (ANAL-A)
    - Adequate margins
        - Observe
    - Inadequate margins
        - Re-excision (preferred), Principles of Surgery (ANAL-A)
        - or consider local RT, Principles of Radiation Therapy (ANAL-C), with or without
            - Fluorouracil/mitomycin, Principles of Systemic Therapy (ANAL-B)
            - or capecitabine/mitomycin (ANAL-B)
            - or cisplatin/fluorouracil (ANAL-B) (category 2B)
- T1, N0 poorly differentiated, or T2 to T4, N0, or any T, N+, Principles of Surgery (ANAL-A)
    - With or without positive para-aortic lymph nodes
        - Para-aortic nodes that can be included in a radiation field
    - Fluorouracil/mitomycin (ANAL-B) + RT (ANAL-C)
    - or capecitabine/mitomycin (ANAL-B) + RT (ANAL-C)
    - or cisplatin/fluorouracil (ANAL-B) + RT (ANAL-C) (category 2B)
- Metastatic disease
    - First-line Systemic Therapy (ANAL-B 2 of 5)
    - Reevaluate and consider chemo/RT to the primary site (ANAL-B, ANAL-C) with fluorouracil or capecitabine for local control

# Next

- Local excision with adequate margins, observe, go to Follow-up Therapy and Surveillance (ANAL-3)
- Local excision with inadequate margins, after re-excision or local RT, go to Follow-up Therapy and Surveillance (ANAL-3)
- After chemoradiation, go to Follow-up Therapy and Surveillance (ANAL-3)
- Metastatic disease, after first-line systemic therapy, go to Second-line and Subsequent Systemic Therapy (ANAL-B 2 of 5)
