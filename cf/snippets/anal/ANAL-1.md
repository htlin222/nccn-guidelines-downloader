# Source

- NCCN Anal Carcinoma v2.2026, ANAL-1, p7
- Applies to anal canal cancer with biopsy showing squamous cell carcinoma
- Anal canal defined by its functional borders
    - Superior border separating it from the rectum is the palpable upper border of the anal sphincter and puborectalis muscles of the anorectal ring
    - Approximately 3 to 5 cm in length
    - Inferior border starts at the anal verge, the lowermost edge of the sphincter muscles, corresponding to the introitus of the anal orifice
- For melanoma histology, see the NCCN Guidelines for Melanoma: Cutaneous
- For adenocarcinoma, see the NCCN Guidelines for Rectal Cancer
- All recommendations category 2A unless otherwise indicated

# Workup

- Digital rectal examination (DRE)
- Inguinal lymph node evaluation
    - Consider biopsy or fine needle aspiration (FNA) if suspicious nodes
- Chest/abdomen CT plus pelvis CT or MRI
    - CT should be with IV and oral contrast
    - Intravenous contrast is not required for the chest CT
    - Pelvis MRI with contrast
    - If intravenous iodinated contrast material is contraindicated due to significant contrast allergy or renal failure, then MRI examination of the abdomen and pelvis with IV gadolinium-based contrast agent (GBCA) can be obtained in select patients
    - See the American College of Radiology contrast manual
- Consider FDG-positron emission tomography (PET)/CT or FDG-PET/MRI
    - FDG-PET/CT does not replace a diagnostic CT
    - FDG-PET/CT performed skull base to mid-thigh
- Anoscopy
- HIV testing, if HIV status unknown
- Gynecologic exam, including screening for cervical cancer
- Fertility risk discussion/counseling in appropriate patients
- For information on DPYD testing and fluoropyrimidine-associated toxicity, see the NCCN Guidelines for Colon Cancer
- Refer to the NCCN Distress Thermometer and Problem List, which includes social determinants of health, see NCCN Guidelines for Distress Management (DIS-A)

# Clinical stage

- Locoregional disease, with or without positive para-aortic lymph nodes
    - Para-aortic nodes that can be included in a radiation field
    - Principles of Surgery (ANAL-A)
- Metastatic disease

# Primary treatment

- Modifications to cancer treatment should not be made solely based on HIV status, see NCCN Guidelines for Cancer in People with HIV
- Locoregional disease, choose one
    - Fluorouracil/mitomycin plus radiation therapy (RT)
    - Capecitabine/mitomycin plus RT
    - Cisplatin/fluorouracil plus RT (category 2B)
    - Systemic therapy per Principles of Systemic Therapy (ANAL-B)
    - RT per Principles of Radiation Therapy (ANAL-C)
- Metastatic disease
    - First-line systemic therapy (ANAL-B 2 of 5)
    - Reevaluate and consider chemo/RT to the primary site with fluorouracil or capecitabine for local control
        - Systemic therapy per Principles of Systemic Therapy (ANAL-B)
        - RT per Principles of Radiation Therapy (ANAL-C)
