# Who early detection is recommended for (footnote i)

- Adults with a prostate, starting at age 45 years
    - Start at 40 years if risk factors, see risk assessment on PROSD-2
- Through 75 years
- Who might have a life expectancy of 10 years or more
- Who opt to participate in an early detection program

# Testing beyond 75 years (footnote i)

- Individualize the decision to test beyond 75 years
- Include a discussion of the risks and benefits
    - Based on comorbidity status
    - Based on estimated life expectancy
- Test after 75 years of age only in very healthy people with little or no comorbidity
    - Especially if they have never undergone PSA testing
    - Especially if they have a rising PSA
    - Purpose is to detect the small number of aggressive cancers that pose a significant risk if left undetected until signs or symptoms develop
- Widespread testing in this population is not recommended
    - It would substantially increase rates of overdetection

# Transgender and gender-diverse individuals (footnote j)

- Transgender women and other gender-diverse individuals assigned male at birth retain their prostates regardless of gender-affirmation surgery
    - They should therefore undergo shared decision-making regarding age-appropriate prostate cancer early detection
- For those on gender-affirming hormones, such as estrogen, interpret PSA values cautiously
    - Gender-affirming hormones have castrating effects that may drastically decrease PSA levels
    - Reference values from one study of transgender women with no known prostate cancer, median age 60 years, on estrogen for at least 6 consecutive months
        - Median PSA 0.02 ng/mL
        - IQR 0 to 0.2 ng/mL
        - 95th percentile 0.6 ng/mL
        - Nik-Ahd F, et al. JAMA 2024
    - Take this into account when interpreting PSA values in transgender and other gender-diverse patients undergoing gender-affirming care
- Pay special attention to rising PSA levels in this population

# PSA above the age-specific median (footnote k)

- For those aged 40 to 49 years
    - Median PSA values range from 0.5 to 0.7 ng/mL
    - 75th percentile values range from 0.7 to 0.9 ng/mL
- Individuals with a PSA above the median for their age group are at higher risk for prostate cancer and for aggressive prostate cancer
    - The higher above the median, the greater the risk
- Further evaluation (PROSD-3) can be considered for a PSA above the median for the age group
    - Even if it does not meet the threshold of >3 ng/mL

# Very low lifetime risk of metastases (footnote l)

- Individuals 60 years or older with a PSA <1.0 ng/mL have a very low risk of prostate cancer metastases in their lifetime
- Individuals older than 75 years of age with a PSA <3.0 ng/mL have a very low risk of prostate cancer metastases in their lifetime
    - This low risk is especially true for this latter category

# Testing intervals are not unanimous (footnote l)

- The testing interval ranges on PROSD-2 were not a unanimous decision by the Panel
    - Some Panel members believe that 2 to 4 years is too long for individuals aged 40 to 75 years with PSA >1.0 ng/mL
    - The Panel majority supported the intervals as listed
