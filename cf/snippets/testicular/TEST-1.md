# Workup

- History and physical (H&P)
- Testicular ultrasound
- Alpha-fetoprotein (AFP)
    - Mildly elevated, non-rising AFP levels may not indicate presence of germ cell tumor
    - Decisions to treat should not be based solely on AFP values <20 ng/mL
    - More highly elevated AFP levels generally indicate the presence of nonseminomatous tumor elements
- Beta-human chorionic gonadotropin (beta-hCG)
    - Quantitative analysis of beta subunit
    - Consider further workup before initiating treatment for mildly elevated beta-hCG (generally <20 IU/L), since other factors, including hypogonadism and cannabis use, can cause false-positive results
- Lactate dehydrogenase (LDH)
- Chemistry profile
    - Consider measuring baseline levels of gonadal function

# Primary treatment

- Consider abdomen/pelvis CT with contrast, or MRI with and without contrast
- Radical inguinal orchiectomy
    - May consider testis-sparing surgery (TSS), ie, partial orchiectomy, in select patients, see Principles of Surgery (TEST-H)
    - Although rare, when a patient presents with rapidly increasing beta-hCG or AFP and symptoms related to disseminated disease with a testicular mass, chemotherapy can be initiated immediately without waiting for a biopsy diagnosis or performing orchiectomy
    - If chemotherapy was initiated first, orchiectomy should be performed at completion of chemotherapy
- Discuss sperm banking, if clinically indicated
    - For patients wishing to preserve fertility, sperm banking is recommended prior to chemotherapy, radiation therapy (RT), or retroperitoneal lymph node dissection (RPLND)
    - For patients with a single testicle, and patients undergoing bilateral orchiectomy, sperm banking is recommended prior to orchiectomy
- Discuss testicular prosthesis
- For patients with bilateral testicular abnormalities, consider inguinal exploration of the contralateral testis
    - Only if ultrasound shows cryptorchid testis, marked atrophy, or suspicious mass
    - Inguinal exploration is with exposure of testis, with direct observation and partial orchiectomy, see Principles of Surgery (TEST-H)
    - Biopsies are not recommended for microcalcifications

# Pathologic diagnosis

- Pure seminoma
    - Pure seminoma histology and AFP normal
    - May have elevated beta-hCG
- Nonseminomatous germ cell tumor (NSGCT)
    - Includes mixed seminoma/nonseminoma tumors
    - Includes seminoma histology with elevated AFP
