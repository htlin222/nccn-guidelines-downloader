# Stage IA, IB primary treatment

- Discuss sperm banking prior to chemotherapy or radiation treatment
- Surveillance for pT1–pT3 tumors (strongly preferred)
    - Then follow-up for seminoma, Table 1 (TEST-A 1 of 3)
- or Single-agent carboplatin, area under the curve (AUC) = 7 x 1 cycle, or AUC = 7 x 2 cycles
    - Recommend imaging within the 4 weeks prior to the initiation of chemotherapy to confirm staging, even if the scan was done previously (TEST-I)
    - There are limited long-term follow-up data on the toxicity and efficacy of carboplatin
    - A recent population-based study suggested patients with larger tumors, rete testis involvement, or both derive a smaller reduction in relapse rate with 1 cycle of carboplatin than previously reported
    - Then follow-up for seminoma, Table 2 (TEST-A 1 of 3)
- or Radiation therapy (RT)
    - Principles of Radiotherapy for Pure Testicular Seminoma (TEST-C)
    - For stage I seminoma, long-term follow-up studies indicate an increase in late toxicities with radiation treatment
    - Then follow-up for seminoma, Table 2 (TEST-A 1 of 3)

# Stage IS primary treatment

- For further information on stage IS, see Discussion
- Repeat elevated serum tumor marker measurement
    - Mildly elevated, non-rising AFP levels may not indicate presence of germ cell tumor
    - Decisions to treat should not be based solely on AFP values <20 ng/mL
    - More highly elevated AFP levels generally indicate the presence of nonseminomatous tumor elements
    - Consider further workup before initiating treatment for mildly elevated beta-hCG (generally <20 IU/L), since other factors including hypogonadism and cannabis use can cause false-positive results
- Assess with imaging
    - Recommend imaging within the 4 weeks prior to the initiation of chemotherapy to confirm staging, even if the scan was done previously (TEST-I)
    - Chest/abdomen/pelvis (C/A/P) CT with contrast, to scan for evaluable disease
        - Elevated tumor markers increase the risk of disease outside of the retroperitoneum, so systemic therapy should be encouraged (TEST-E)
    - Repeat scrotal ultrasound to rule out a contralateral testis mass, if one has not been conducted in the last 3 months
- Then follow-up for seminoma, Table 2 (TEST-A 1 of 3)

# Recurrence

- Treat according to extent of disease at relapse
    - Patients should not be treated based upon an elevated LDH alone
    - Patients previously on surveillance can be treated with RT or chemotherapy, depending on the extent of disease
    - Patients who received carboplatin or RT should be treated with first-line cisplatin-based chemotherapy (TEST-E)
