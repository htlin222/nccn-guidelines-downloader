- Applies to a suspicious penile lesion
- These Guidelines are for treatment of squamous cell carcinoma of the penis
- All recommendations category 2A unless otherwise indicated

- ___ (age) yo, suspicious penile lesion

- HPV status ___ (hpv)

# Primary evaluation

- History and physical (H&P)
    - Risk factors
        - Balanitis
        - Chronic inflammation
        - Penile trauma
        - Lack of neonatal circumcision
        - Tobacco use
        - Lichen sclerosus
        - Poor hygiene
        - Sexually transmitted disease (eg, human immunodeficiency virus [HIV])
    - Lesion characteristics
        - Diameter ___ (lesion_diameter)
        - Location ___ (lesion_location)
        - Number of lesions ___ (lesion_number)
        - Morphology ___ (morphology)
        - Relationship to other structures ___ (relationship), submucosal, corpora spongiosa, cavernosa, and/or urethra
- Histologic diagnosis
    - Punch biopsy, or
    - Excisional biopsy, or
    - Incisional biopsy
    - Principles of Pathology (PN-F)
- Assess human papillomavirus (HPV) status
- Refer to the NCCN Distress Thermometer and Problem List, which includes social determinants of health, see NCCN Guidelines for Distress Management (DIS-A)

# Primary treatment if clinical diagnosis is penile intraepithelial neoplasia (PeIN) or Ta

- Topical therapy, or
- Wide local excision, or
- Laser therapy (category 2B), or
- Complete glansectomy (category 2B), or
- Mohs surgery in select cases (category 2B)
- Each of these options follows Principles of Penile Organ-Sparing Approaches (PN-A)
