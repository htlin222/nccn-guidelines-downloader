# Source

- NCCN Sarcoma v5.2026, RMS-1, p26
- Applies to patients diagnosed with pure RMS after full slide review
- RMS identified within another histology is not on this pathway, and should be treated as the original histology
- Referral to centers with expertise in the management of pediatric cancers is recommended
- All recommendations are category 2A unless otherwise indicated

    - FDG-PET imaging (CT or MRI) scan may be useful for initial staging
        - Because of the possibility of nodal metastases
        - Because of the appearance of unusual sites of initial metastatic disease in adult patients
    - Principles of Imaging (SARC-A)

# Histology

- Pleomorphic RMS
    - Not to be confused with anaplastic variant in children
- Non-pleomorphic RMS
    - Alveolar
    - Embryonal
    - Spindle cell/sclerosing
        - VGLL2-related fusions
        - Intraosseous
        - MYOD1 mutation
    - Up to 13% of RMS in younger patients may have anaplastic features
        - Not to be confused with the high-grade tumors seen in adults designated as pleomorphic RMS

# Treatment if pleomorphic RMS

- Recommend treating like high-grade soft tissue sarcoma
- Systemic therapy options, see SARC-G

# Treatment if non-pleomorphic RMS

- Referral to institutions with expertise in treating patients with RMS is strongly recommended
- Multidisciplinary evaluation is strongly encouraged
    - Pediatric oncology
    - Medical oncology
    - Surgical oncology
    - Radiation oncology
- Multimodality treatment planning is required
- Risk stratification is required
    - Systemic therapy options for RMS may be different than those used with other soft tissue sarcoma histologies
