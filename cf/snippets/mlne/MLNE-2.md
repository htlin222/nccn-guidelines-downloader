# General diagnostic studies

- History and physical examination
    - Skin exam
    - Palpation of spleen
    - Detail any family history of eosinophilia
    - Detail signs/symptoms of immunodeficiency, to identify rare primary immunodeficiency disorders
    - Rule out secondary (reactive) eosinophilia (MLNE-A)
- Complete blood count (CBC) with differential
- Examination of blood smear
    - Monocytosis
    - Dysplasia
    - Eosinophilia
    - Circulating blasts
- Comprehensive metabolic panel
    - Uric acid
    - Lactate dehydrogenase (LDH)
    - Liver function tests (LFTs)
- Serum tryptase
- Erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR) and/or C-reactive protein (CRP)
- Quantitative serum immunoglobulin (Ig) levels, including IgE
- BM aspirate and biopsy
    - IHC for tryptase/CD117/CD25/CD30/CD2, and/or flow cytometry for CD117/CD25/CD30/CD2 and tryptase
    - Reticulin/collagen stains for fibrosis
- Peripheral blood (PB) assessment for PDGFRA rearrangement
    - By fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) and/or nested quantitative reverse transcriptase polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR)
    - Testing for imatinib-sensitive TK gene fusions by PB is feasible and appropriate in certain clinical circumstances (MLNE-B)
- Confirmatory FISH (PB or BM) if chromosome analysis reveals the following breakpoints
    - 4q12 (PDGFRA)
        - The overwhelming majority of PDGFRA fusions are represented by FIP1L1::PDGFRA, which is cytogenetically occult
        - Requires FISH for the detection of CHIC2 deletion for initial screening
    - 5q31~33 (PDGFRB)
        - In rare cases cryptic PDGFRB rearrangements have been found, and FISH may be used to uncover, not only confirm, PDGFRB rearrangements
    - 8p11~12 (FGFR1)
    - 9p24 (JAK2)
    - 9q34 (ABL1)
    - 13q12 (FLT3)
        - Reverse transcriptase polymerase chain reaction (RT-PCR) may be preferred over NGS for FLT3
- T-cell immunophenotyping flow cytometry (preferred) and/or IHC
- Molecular analysis to confirm T-cell clonality when appropriate
- Myeloid mutation panel (next-generation sequencing [NGS])
    - Reverse transcriptase polymerase chain reaction (RT-PCR) may be preferred over NGS for FLT3
    - Role of NGS in the diagnosis: MLNE-C

# Evaluation of target organ involvement

- Based on clinical presentation requiring engagement of other subspecialists
- Organ-directed biopsy generally needed to confirm tissue eosinophilia
- Consultation with specialized referral services is recommended for the management of relevant target end-organ damage
- In certain circumstances urgent treatment, including the use of high dose corticosteroids, may be indicated to mitigate organ damage
- Chest x-ray
- Electrocardiogram
- Symptom-directed CT/MRI scan of the body
- Cardiac troponin and/or NT-proBNP measurement
    - If elevated, or clinical features of cardiac injury: echocardiogram (ECHO) and/or cardiac MRI
- Lung involvement
    - Pulmonary function tests
    - Bronchoscopy with bronchoalveolar lavage
    - Lung biopsy
- Gastrointestinal involvement
    - Endoscopy with relevant mucosal biopsy
    - IHC for tryptase/CD117/CD25/CD30/CD2, and/or flow cytometry for CD117/CD25/CD30/CD2
- Liver involvement
    - Liver biopsy
- Neuropathy
    - Electromyography
    - Nerve biopsy
- Ear, nose, and throat symptoms
    - Evaluation for sinusitis, nasal polyposis, sensorineural hearing loss, etc
- Cutaneous involvement
    - Skin biopsy
- Eosinophilic fasciitis
    - Deep biopsy that includes fascia
    - MRI
