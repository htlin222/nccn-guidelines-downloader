# Source

- NCCN Myeloid/Lymphoid Neoplasms with Eosinophilia and Tyrosine Kinase Gene Fusions v2.2026, MLNE-1, p7
- Applies once secondary (reactive) eosinophilia has been ruled out (MLNE-A)
- This diagnostic algorithm excludes conditions associated with secondary (reactive) eosinophilia
- It does include eosinophilia associated with non-myeloid malignancies such as T-cell lymphoma, Hodgkin lymphoma, and ALL
- Primary (clonal/neoplastic) eosinophilia is generally an absolute eosinophil count of at least 1.5 x 109/L
- All recommendations category 2A unless otherwise indicated

# Initial evaluation

- Rule out secondary (reactive) eosinophilia (MLNE-A)
- Evaluate and treat by a multidisciplinary team in a specialized center, for all patients
- Assess for clinical situations that may require urgent intervention, recommended for all patients
    - Immediate institution of oral or high-dose IV corticosteroids may be necessary
- Assess for distress
    - Refer to the NCCN Guidelines for Distress Thermometer and Problem List, which includes social determinants of health
    - See NCCN Guidelines for Distress Management (DIS-A)

# Diagnostic tip-offs

- Primary (clonal/neoplastic) eosinophilia may be suggested by one or more of the following
    - Elevated serum tryptase level
    - Abnormal T-cell population
    - Increased blasts, dysplasia, cytogenetic or molecular abnormality, and/or BM fibrosis
    - Splenomegaly and/or lymphadenopathy

# Diagnostic testing

- Exclude the diagnosis of BCR::ABL1-positive CML, PV, ET, PMF, CNL, and BCR::ABL1-negative atypical CML based on WHO criteria
- Screen for TK gene fusions or other cytogenetic abnormality (MLNE-3)
- Immunohistochemistry (IHC) for tryptase/CD117/CD25/CD30/CD2
- And/or flow cytometry for CD117/CD25/CD30/CD2
- Molecular testing for KIT D816V
    - ASO-qPCR or an alternative high-sensitivity method is recommended for KIT D816V mutation testing
    - See NCCN Guidelines for Systemic Mastocytosis
- T-cell immunophenotyping flow cytometry (preferred) and/or IHC, to establish evidence of abnormal T-cell phenotype or T-cell proliferation
    - Molecular analysis to confirm T-cell clonality when appropriate

# Next

- PDGFRA, PDGFRB, FGFR1, JAK2, ABL1, FLT3 rearrangements identified, see Workup (MLNE-2) and Diagnostic Testing Algorithms (MLNE-3)
    - The diagnosis requires a combination of histopathologic, clinical, laboratory, and cytogenetic/molecular analyses
- 1 major plus 1 minor, or at least 3 minor criteria for SM satisfied, SM, see NCCN Guidelines for Systemic Mastocytosis
- Other clonal cytogenetic or molecular abnormality, or excess blasts (at least 5% to less than 20%), CEL, NOS
    - Additional cytogenetic or molecular testing may be required to confirm the differential diagnosis of clonal hematopoiesis of indeterminate potential (CHIP) versus CEL, NOS
- Abnormal T-cell phenotype or T-cell proliferation, lymphocyte-variant of hypereosinophilia (HE)/hypereosinophilic syndrome (HES)
- Eosinophilia without myeloid/lymphoid neoplasm, genetic/molecular abnormality, or abnormal T-cell phenotype or T-cell proliferation
    - With eosinophilia-related organ damage, idiopathic HES
    - Without eosinophilia-related organ damage, idiopathic HE
