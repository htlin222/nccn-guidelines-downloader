# Testing is clinically indicated in the following scenarios

- Individuals with any blood relative with a known P/LP variant in a cancer susceptibility gene
- Individuals who meet the criteria but tested negative with previous limited testing, and are interested in pursuing multigene testing
    - Limited testing, eg, single gene and/or absent deletion duplication analysis
- A P/LP variant identified on tumor genomic testing that has clinical implications if also identified in the germline
- To aid in systemic therapy and surgical decision-making
    - Eg, PARP inhibitors for breast cancer
    - Eg, PARP inhibitors for ovarian cancer, prostate cancer, pancreatic cancer, and metastatic HER2-negative breast cancer
    - Eg, platinum therapy for prostate cancer and pancreatic cancer
    - Eg, risk-reducing surgery
    - See the relevant NCCN Treatment Guidelines for further details
- Individual who meets Li-Fraumeni syndrome (LFS) testing criteria (CRIT-7)
- Individual who meets CS/PHTS testing criteria (CRIT-8)
- Individual who meets Lynch syndrome (LS) criteria, see NCCN Guidelines for Genetic/Familial High-Risk Assessment: Colorectal, Endometrial, and Gastric
- Personal or family history of breast, ovarian, pancreatic, prostate or colorectal cancer, routed below
    - Consideration of the limitations of unknown or limited family structure is indicated, see EVAL-A

# Testing may be considered in the following scenarios

- Requires appropriate pre-test education and access to post-test management
- An individual of Ashkenazi Jewish ancestry without additional risk factors
    - Testing for three founder P/LP variants of BRCA1/2 may be offered as early as age 18–25 years
    - Applies to an individual with one grandparent identified as of Ashkenazi Jewish ancestry, irrespective of cancer history in the family, as part of longitudinal studies
    - Without access to longitudinal research studies, testing may be provided if there is access to pre-test education along with post-test counseling, additional genetic testing if indicated, and high-risk management
    - Testing should not be offered outside of a medical framework or clinical trial
    - Other ancestries besides Ashkenazi also demonstrate founder mutations, see Discussion
- Personal history of serous endometrial cancer
    - A rare subtype of uterine cancer for which there is evolving evidence of an association with BRCA1 P/LP variants
