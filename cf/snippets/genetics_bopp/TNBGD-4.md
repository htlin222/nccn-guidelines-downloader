# Barriers to health care to recognize

- Transgender, non-binary, and gender diverse individuals encounter many challenges to health care
    - Stigmatization
    - Denial of services
    - Discrimination
    - Abuse
    - Possible higher rates of mortality due to lack of access to appropriate preventive care

# Where and by whom the patient should be followed

- Individuals pursuing gender-affirming care should be followed at centers of excellence
    - With access to a multidisciplinary team that understands their unique needs
    - That provides a safe and welcoming environment
- The team should include
    - Surgeons
    - Primary care specialists
    - Oncologists
    - Radiologists
    - Pathologists
    - Endocrinologists
    - Pediatricians
    - Psychologists
    - Genetic counselors
    - Social workers
- All team members should be trained in the appropriate care of the transgender population
- The team should be able to address medical, psychologic, and social care needs

# Gaps acknowledged by the guideline

- Formal education in the care of transgender, non-binary, and gender diverse individuals is needed at every level of the health care system
    - With a particular focus in breast/chest cancer screening
- Research is needed regarding the impact of gender-affirming hormones and puberty-blocking agents
    - How they interact with hereditary susceptibility to cancer syndromes
    - Optimal prevention strategies for these populations
- Most electronic health data do not incorporate gender identity
    - SEER data
    - Census data
    - Even EMRs
    - This hinders collection of health data in these populations
    - This denies appropriate screening invitations to these individuals
- A National Registry on the health outcomes of transgender, non-binary, and gender diverse populations is needed
    - To fill the many gaps in the magnitude and management of risks associated with gender-affirming treatment in the setting of hereditary cancer susceptibilities
- As in all research involving human participants, care must be taken to preserve the privacy and protection of this population
