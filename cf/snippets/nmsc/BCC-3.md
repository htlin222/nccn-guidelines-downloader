# Primary treatment, choose one

- Curettage and electrodesiccation (C&E)
    - Not if the tumor appears to extend beyond the dermis, in which case surgical excision should generally be performed instead
- Shave removal
    - Can be considered for clinically low-risk BCC in a low-risk site, and where additional treatment would pose morbidity or hardship
    - Sharp removal by transverse bowl-shaped slicing to remove epidermal and dermal lesions, without including fat
    - Does not require suture closure
    - Not if the tumor appears to extend beyond the dermis, in which case surgical excision should generally be performed instead
- Standard excision with 4-mm clinical margins and postoperative margin assessment
    - Do not undertake tissue rearrangement (eg, flap reconstruction, extensive undermining) until clear margins are identified
    - Second intention healing, linear repair, or skin graft are acceptable
- Nonsurgical modalities, only for tumors clinically and histologically consistent with superficial BCC, without dermal extension
    - Topical imiquimod (preferred)
        - Cure rates are approximately 10% lower than for surgical treatment modalities
    - Topical calcipotriene/fluorouracil (useful in certain circumstances)
    - Topical fluorouracil (useful in certain circumstances)
        - Cure rates are approximately 10% lower than for surgical treatment modalities
    - Cryotherapy (useful in certain circumstances)
        - Current guidelines for low-risk BCC, including superficial BCC (sBCC), recommend several treatment options including destructive methods such as cryosurgery with or without prior curettage, or curettage and electrodesiccation
    - Photodynamic therapy (useful in certain circumstances)
        - eg, topical aminolevulinic acid (ALA), porfimer sodium (category 2B)
        - Cure rates are approximately 10% lower than for surgical treatment modalities

# Margin result after primary treatment

- Margins ___ (margins)
- Negative margins, no additional treatment on this page
- Positive margins, proceed to additional treatment below

# Additional treatment for positive margins, choose one

- Mohs, or other forms of peripheral and deep en face margin assessment (PDEMA)
    - Mohs surgery should be performed by dermatologic surgeons who have specialized training and experience in this procedure
    - As per other appropriate use criteria
    - PDEMA with permanent section analysis or intraoperative frozen section analysis is an alternative to Mohs
    - See Principles of PDEMA Technique (SCC-G) within the NCCN Guidelines for Squamous Cell Skin Cancer
- Re-excision if clinically feasible
- RT for nonsurgical candidates
    - Per Principles of Radiation Therapy (BCC-C)
    - Determination of the appropriateness of RT should be performed together with a radiation oncologist
- Consultation with radiation oncologist for nonsurgical candidates
