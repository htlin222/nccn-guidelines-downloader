# Source

- NCCN Neuroblastoma v2.2026, NEUROB-6, p12
- Applies to intermediate-risk disease, stage MS, by Risk Classification (NEUROB-2)
- Principles of Systemic Therapy (NEUROB-D)
- Principles of Surgery (NEUROB-E)
- All recommendations category 2A unless otherwise indicated

# Assessment

- ___ (age) old
- Intermediate risk, stage MS (NEUROB-2)
- Evolving hepatomegaly ___ (hepatomegaly)
- Symptomatic ___ (symptom_status)
    - Total symptom score ___ (symptom_score)
    - See Hsu LL, et al. Med Pediatr Oncol 1996;27:521-528 for symptoms; a total score of at least 2 should lead to emergent therapy
- Biologic features
    - Histology ___ (histology_status)
    - DI ___ (di)
    - SCA ___ (sca)
    - Favorable biologic features include FH, DI >1, no SCA
    - If SCA or histology status are not available, consider as unfavorable
- Biopsy ___ (biopsy_status)
    - Safety considerations (coagulopathy, impending organ failure) may preclude biopsy, especially with hepatomegaly leading to respiratory compromise
    - Symptomatic patients should start intermediate-risk treatment emergently prior to biopsy
    - For infants too unstable to undergo biopsy before starting treatment, chemotherapy may be initiated and a biopsy obtained when it is safe to do so
    - Once symptoms have resolved following initial therapy, patients should undergo biopsy and risk assignment should be revised based on available data

# Primary treatment

- Age <3 months with evolving hepatomegaly, or symptomatic, with all favorable biology
    - 2 cycles of chemotherapy (NEUROB-D)
- Symptomatic with unfavorable biology
    - 4 cycles of chemotherapy (NEUROB-D)
- With any chemotherapy
    - Audiologic assessment at diagnosis and again at end of therapy
    - Echocardiogram at diagnosis and again at end of therapy
    - Evaluate all prior sites of disease after 2 cycles if only 2 cycles of chemotherapy are planned
    - If 4 cycles are planned, perform the complete evaluation of all sites of prior disease after 4 cycles
- Symptomatic patients with ongoing organ dysfunction with inadequate response to initial chemotherapy may benefit from radiation therapy (RT)
    - Multidisciplinary discussion is recommended
    - Principles of Radiation Therapy (NEUROB-H)

# Response after 2 cycles

- Primary tumor response historically measured by volume, see NEUROB-D 1 of 13 and Response Assessment (NEUROB-G)
- At least PR for primary tumor, and resolution of symptoms, and resolution of non-liver and non-skin metastatic sites
    - Surveillance (NEUROB-F)
- Less than PR for primary tumor, or persistence of symptoms, or persistence of residual marrow metastatic disease
    - Multidisciplinary discussion of role for additional therapy
    - Then surveillance (NEUROB-F)

# Response after 4 cycles

- Resolution of symptoms, and at least PR for primary tumor, and CR of metastatic sites
    - Surveillance (NEUROB-F)
- Less than PR for primary tumor, or persistence of symptoms, or persistence of residual metastatic disease
    - Multidisciplinary discussion of role for additional therapy
    - Then surveillance (NEUROB-F)

# Multidisciplinary discussion of additional therapy

- Undertake on an iterative basis
- Surveillance should begin once the response or reduction in primary tumor size noted above is achieved, with chemotherapy and/or surgery
- If additional chemotherapy is given, consider 2–4 additional cycles
    - Repeat primary tumor imaging after every 2 cycles
- Consider surgery if chemotherapy has led to SD or minimal improvement in tumor size
- Multidisciplinary discussion is important in decisions surrounding surgery and additional chemotherapy
- Consider biopsy as the tumor may have differentiated and additional treatment may not be required
- If 8 cycles of chemotherapy have been given and the tumor remains unresectable, consider treatment with second-line chemotherapy as per Twist CJ, et al. J Clin Oncol 2019;37:3243-3255

# Next

- Surveillance, see Disease Surveillance/Follow-up (NEUROB-F)
    - If the treatment endpoint has been achieved, obtain MIBG scan as part of the end of therapy evaluation
- Inadequate response, go to multidisciplinary discussion of role for additional therapy, then surveillance (NEUROB-F)
- Once symptoms have resolved after initial therapy, biopsy and revise risk assignment, see Risk Classification (NEUROB-2)
