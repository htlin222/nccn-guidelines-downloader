# Tissue sampling

- Surgical resection should be considered in the setting of localized disease, particularly in the absence of image-defined risk factors (IDRFs)
- When biopsy rather than upfront resection is indicated, open biopsy may be preferred in some clinical scenarios if adequate tissue is not obtained or obtainable by less invasive means
- Multiple core biopsies may provide adequate tissue
    - The need for full histologic and molecular evaluation of specimens must be considered
- Fine-needle aspiration is not recommended
- Review of frozen sections by an experienced pathologist, to determine specimen adequacy
    - Some samples may be necrotic at the time of initial biopsy
- When sufficient tissue is obtained, use of additional tissue for research purposes is encouraged
- See Principles of Pathology (NEUROB-A) for biomarker testing considerations and additional information
- Delay biopsy until after therapy is started and the patient is medically stable, for:
    - Patients <2 months of age with existing or evolving hepatomegaly
    - Infants in whom safety considerations (coagulopathy, impending organ failure) preclude biopsy
    - In these cases, perform the biopsy when it is safe to do so, to obtain tissue for biomarker testing
- Biopsy is not required for patients <6 months of age with L1 adrenal tumor, maximum diameter
    - ≤3.1 cm if solid, or
    - 5 cm if at least 25% cystic component

# Bone marrow

- Bilateral bone marrow aspirates and trephine biopsies
    - In rare cases, marrow may be the only source of diagnostic material
    - In those cases, every attempt should be made to obtain material for the full complement of biomarker testing
    - See Principles of Pathology (NEUROB-A) for biomarker testing considerations and additional information

# Laboratory, essential

- Complete blood count (CBC) with differential
- Comprehensive metabolic panel
- Homovanillic acid (HVA)/vanillylmandelic acid (VMA): ___ (hva_vma)
    - Required for diagnosis if the only diagnostic tissue is bone marrow, or if no biopsy is to be obtained initially

# Laboratory, useful in selected cases

- Prothrombin time (PT)/international normalized ratio (INR), if liver is involved or if there is concern for bleeding
- Lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) and ferritin may be considered
- Pregnancy test, for patients of childbearing potential

# For patients receiving systemic therapy

- Audiogram, if platinum-containing chemotherapy is indicated
- Echocardiogram, if anthracycline-containing chemotherapy is indicated
- Electrocardiogram (ECG), if anthracycline-containing chemotherapy is indicated
- Fertility/fertility preservation, for patients likely to receive extensive alkylator therapy
    - Refer to fertility specialists for discussion of possible options and appropriate timing

# Imaging

- Cross-sectional imaging to evaluate soft tissue disease
    - MRI with/without contrast, or CT with contrast
- MRI of spine with/without contrast, in the setting of paraspinal disease or concerns regarding involvement of nerve roots or spinal cord
- MRI of brain with/without contrast, or CT skull/orbits with contrast, if neurologic symptoms are present or if otherwise clinically indicated
- Iodine-123 (I-123) metaiodobenzylguanidine (MIBG) imaging, to assess for metastatic disease
    - With SPECT or SPECT/CT, where available
- Fluorodeoxyglucose (FDG)-PET should be obtained in patients with MIBG non-avid disease or suspected mixed-avidity disease
    - Not required in patients <6 months of age with L1 adrenal tumor, maximum diameter ≤3.1 cm if solid, or 5 cm if at least 25% cystic component
- See Principles of Imaging (NEUROB-B) for radiology initial workup and additional information

# Paraneoplastic syndromes

- Paraneoplastic syndromes may occur with neuroblastoma
- See NEUROB-J for additional information
