# Tobacco use history and cessation support

- Offer support and resources to help reduce or quit smoking to all individuals who currently use tobacco
- Complete tobacco use history documents both
    - Extent of exposure in pack-years, the number of packs smoked every day multiplied by the number of years
    - Amount of time since smoking cessation in individuals who previously smoked
- Recognize individuals who have previously used tobacco for their commitment, and offer continued support to remain tobacco-free
    - https://www.smokefree.gov

# Risk factors beyond age and smoking history

- Age and smoking history are used for risk assessment, but other potential risk factors may be considered during discussions of benefits and risks
- Occupational exposure
    - Agents identified specifically as carcinogens targeting the lungs: arsenic, asbestos, beryllium, cadmium, chromium, coal smoke, diesel fumes, nickel, silica, soot, uranium
- Radon exposure
    - Documented sustained and substantially elevated radon exposure increases the risk for lung cancer
    - Many state websites have information more specific to local areas, including areas of known elevated radon
- Cancer history
    - Increased risk of developing new primary lung cancer among survivors of lymphoma, breast cancer, head and neck cancer, or other smoking tobacco-related cancers
    - Increased risk among those who have received radiation treatment to the chest
- Family history of lung cancer
- Lung disease history
- Second-hand smoke
    - Exposure to the carcinogens is highly variable, with varying evidence for increased risk after this variable exposure
    - Not independently considered a risk factor sufficient for recommending lung cancer screening
- Race
    - Black and African American individuals with less cigarette smoking exposure have a similar risk for lung cancer as white individuals with more cigarette smoking exposure
    - Consider this increased risk for Black/African Americans in shared decision-making and risk assessment
- Refer to the NCCN Distress Thermometer and Problem List, which includes social determinants of health (DIS-A)

# Risk calculators and shared decision-making

- Consider using risk calculators if possible, because additional candidates at higher risk for lung cancer may be identified for lung screening
    - PLCOm2012 Lung Cancer Risk Calculator, https://www.evidencio.com/models/show/992
    - Tammemagi lung cancer risk calculator is reasonable to consider, to assist in quantifying risk for individuals in this group
        - A 1.3% threshold of lung cancer risk over a 6-year timeframe was considered similar to that of the USPSTF
- Shared decision-making aids may assist in counseling individuals about the risks and benefits of screening
    - http://www.shouldiscreen.com/benefits-and-harms-screening
    - Use of risk models may identify individuals with a lower risk or higher risk within the current recommendations

# Eligibility for curative intent treatment

- Screening with LDCT is appropriate to consider for individuals at higher risk for cancer who are potential candidates for curative-intent treatment
    - Chest x-ray is not recommended for lung cancer screening
- Curative intent treatment includes
    - Surgery
    - Stereotactic ablative radiotherapy (SABR), also known as stereotactic body radiation therapy (SBRT)
    - Ablative image-guided thermal ablation (IGTA) techniques as additional alternatives: radiofrequency ablation, microwave ablation, cryoablation
- SABR or IGTA may be used for individuals with advanced age, and for individuals with cardiac disease or severe COPD who are unable to have surgery
    - These factors themselves do not preclude eligibility for screening
- See also the NCCN Guidelines for Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer

# Upper age limit

- Randomized trial evidence supports screening up to age 77 years
- There is uncertainty about the upper age limit to initiate or to continue screening
- Screening beyond age 77 years can be considered as long as individual functional status and comorbidity allow consideration for curative intent therapy

# Program requirements

- Institutions performing lung cancer screening should use a multidisciplinary approach for nodule management
    - Thoracic radiology
    - Pulmonary medicine
    - Thoracic surgery
    - Some institutions also include medical oncology, radiation oncology, and/or pathology
- All screening and follow-up chest CT scans
    - CT dose index volume (CTDIvol) threshold of ≤3 mGy for an individual of average size
    - Standard-dose CT with IV contrast might be appropriate when evaluating mediastinal abnormalities or lymph nodes (LCS-A)
    - Adjust parameters for individuals of smaller or larger size
    - There should be a systematic process for appropriate follow-up
    - See ACR-STR Practice Parameter for the Performance and Reporting of Lung Cancer Screening Thoracic Computed Tomography (CT)
- Use of a tracking tool, in addition to a navigation process, is recommended
    - Systematically ensures individuals with screen-detected lung nodules complete guideline-concordant nodule management
    - Systematically ensures individuals without significant screen-detected abnormalities continue annual screening if they remain eligible
