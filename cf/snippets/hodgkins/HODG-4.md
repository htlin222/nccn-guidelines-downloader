# Treatment selection

- Obtain multidisciplinary team input prior to final treatment decisions
    - Team includes radiation oncology
    - All patients will benefit from this
- Combined modality therapy (CMT) provides better progression free survival (PFS)/freedom from progression (FFP)
    - No difference in overall survival
- Base the selection of CMT or chemotherapy alone on:
    - Patient age
    - Sex
    - Family history of cancer or cardiac disease
    - Comorbid conditions
    - Sites of involvement, especially within mediastinum or axilla

# Patients assigned female at birth with intact breast tissue

- Chemotherapy alone may be preferred for those <30 years
    - Where recommended breast dose-volume histogram (DVH) constraints are exceeded
    - Or if cardiac constraints cannot be met
    - Or in the presence of high-risk comorbidities
- CMT may be preferred if the doxorubicin dose would exceed 200 mg/m2
    - Only provided that breast and cardiac constraints can be met

# Special considerations for ABVD

- Neutropenia is not a factor for delay of treatment or reduction of dose intensity
- Routine use of growth factors is not recommended
    - Evens AM, et al. Br J Haematol 2007;137:545-552
- Consider PFTs after 4 cycles of ABVD

# Deauville 4-5 treatment responses

- The degree of abnormality of both Deauville 4 and 5 scores is quite variable
    - Clinical judgment is often necessary in such settings
- Disease responding well to chemotherapy but interim FDG-PET/CT shows a Deauville 4 or 5 response
    - It may be appropriate to continue with the same chemotherapy regimen
    - Repeat an FDG-PET/CT after 2 additional cycles
- Post-chemotherapy FDG-PET/CT only focally positive
    - Consolidation RT may be appropriate, especially if a biopsy is not feasible
    - See Principles of Radiation Therapy (HODG-C 2 of 16)
- New lymph node that can be explained by an alternative etiology (eg, inflammation or infection)
    - Use clinical judgement
    - Repeating a short-interval FDG-PET/CT may be preferred
- Residual FDG-uptake that may be related to treatment (eg, immunotherapy)
    - Use clinical judgement
    - Repeating a short-interval FDG-PET/CT may be preferred
- At any point, if there is concern for refractory disease
    - A biopsy is appropriate to inform subsequent therapy
