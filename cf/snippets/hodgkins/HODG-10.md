# Source

- NCCN Hodgkin Lymphoma (Age >=18 years) v2.2026, HODG-10, p27
- Applies to classic Hodgkin lymphoma diagnosed during pregnancy
- CHL is the most common hematologic malignancy diagnosed during pregnancy, as the peak incidence coincides with the reproductive years
- CHL in patients who are pregnant is enriched for the nodular sclerosis subtype
    - Similar clinical presentation, natural history, and prognosis compared to patients who are not pregnant
- The approach below is the NCCN Panel's suggested approach to management by trimester
- All recommendations are category 2A unless otherwise indicated

# Multidisciplinary care

- Multidisciplinary approach, with the goal of maximizing the cure rate for the patient and allowing for delivery of a healthy child
    - Medical oncology
    - High-risk obstetrics
    - Neonatology
- Referral to or consultation with a center with expertise
    - Strongly encouraged at diagnosis
    - Especially important in the setting of relapsed or refractory disease
- Consultation with pharmacy, to ensure supportive medications are appropriate for use in pregnancy

# Radiologic staging during pregnancy

- Single view (posteroanterior [PA]) chest x-ray with abdominal shielding
- Abdominal ultrasound, or MRI without gadolinium
- Avoid FDG-PET imaging
- Avoid CT imaging

# What to avoid during pregnancy

- Chemotherapy in the first trimester, given the high risk of congenital malformations or fetal demise
- Escalated BEACOPP, given the paucity of data
- BV + AVD, given the paucity of data
- RT, given potential risks of teratogenesis, prematurity, cognitive impairment, and childhood malignancy
- Breastfeeding in patients receiving chemotherapy in the post-partum period

# Supportive care

- G-CSF is category C in pregnancy
- Ondansetron is a preferred antiemetic for patients who are pregnant
- Metoclopramide is a preferred antiemetic for patients who are pregnant

# Suggested treatment approach - first trimester

- If asymptomatic or minimally symptomatic
    - Delay treatment with close observation until second or third trimester
- If severe symptoms or organ compromise, options are
    - Consider referral to center with expertise
    - Consider pregnancy termination and urgent treatment
    - Single-agent vinblastine, followed by ABVD after the start of the second trimester

# Suggested treatment approach - second or third trimester

- If asymptomatic or minimally symptomatic
    - Delay treatment with close observation until after delivery
- If severe symptoms or organ compromise
    - Treat with ABVD
        - ABVD can be safely administered in the second and third trimesters with excellent maternal and fetal outcomes
    - Work with high-risk obstetrics to avoid delivery while at nadir

# Next

- First trimester, asymptomatic or minimally symptomatic: observe, reassess in the second or third trimester
- First trimester, severe symptoms or organ compromise: center with expertise, pregnancy termination and urgent treatment, or vinblastine then ABVD from the second trimester
- Second or third trimester, asymptomatic or minimally symptomatic: observe, reassess after delivery
- Second or third trimester, severe symptoms or organ compromise: ABVD, delivery timed off nadir with high-risk obstetrics
