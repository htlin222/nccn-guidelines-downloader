# Source

- NCCN Head and Neck Cancers v2.2026, ETHM-1, p62
- Ethmoid sinus tumors: workup, biopsy, and pathology
- Ethmoid sinus tumors are rare and histopathologically diverse; correct pathologic diagnosis is paramount to treatment decisions
    - Consider referral to a major medical center that specializes in these tumors for confirmation of diagnosis
- All recommendations are category 2A unless otherwise indicated

# Workup

- H&P including a complete head and neck exam
    - Nasal endoscopy as clinically indicated
    - Document and quantify tobacco use history, in pack years smoked
    - Document alcohol use, and counsel
    - Advise every patient who currently smokes to quit
    - Advise those who formerly smoked to remain abstinent from smoking
    - For additional cessation support, refer to the Smoking Cessation and Treatment Resources in the NCCN Guidelines for Smoking Cessation
    - Screen for distress, using the NCCN Distress Thermometer and Problem List, which includes social determinants of health (DIS-A)
- CT with contrast or MRI with and without contrast of skull base and neck (IMG-A)
- HPV testing (may inform etiology)
- Multidisciplinary consultation as clinically indicated

# As clinically indicated

- Chest CT, with or without contrast (IMG-A)
- Consider FDG-PET/CT (IMG-A)
- Dental evaluation (DENT-A)
- Nutrition, speech, and swallowing evaluation/therapy (NUTR-A)
- Smoking cessation counseling
- Fertility/reproductive counseling
    - See fertility and reproductive endocrine considerations in the NCCN Guidelines for Adolescent and Young Adult (AYA) Oncology
- Screening for hepatitis B

# Biopsy

- Biopsy to establish the pathologic diagnosis
    - Image-guided (US or CT) needle biopsy of cystic neck nodes may offer better diagnostic yield than FNA by palpation alone for initial diagnosis in this setting
    - For unresectable or metastatic disease where there is a plan for systemic therapy, a core biopsy would allow for biomarker testing

# Pathology

- Squamous cell carcinoma
- Adenocarcinoma
- Minor salivary gland tumor
    - See the salivary gland algorithm for management after resection (SALI-1)
- Esthesioneuroblastoma
- Undifferentiated carcinoma (sinonasal undifferentiated carcinoma [SNUC], small cell, or sinonasal neuroendocrine carcinoma [SNEC])
- Mucosal melanoma, go to MM-1
- Sarcoma, see NCCN Guidelines for Soft Tissue Sarcoma
- Lymphoma, see NCCN Guidelines for B-Cell Lymphomas and NCCN Guidelines for T-Cell Lymphomas
