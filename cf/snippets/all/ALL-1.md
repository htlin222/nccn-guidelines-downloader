# Diagnosis

- Diagnosis of ALL generally requires demonstration of at least 20% bone marrow lymphoblasts
- Established on hematopathology review of bone marrow aspirate and biopsy materials, which includes:
    - Morphologic assessment of Wright-Giemsa-stained bone marrow aspirate smears
    - Morphologic assessment of hematoxylin and eosin (H&E)-stained core biopsy sections
    - Morphologic assessment of hematoxylin and eosin (H&E)-stained clot sections
    - Comprehensive flow cytometric immunophenotyping
    - Baseline flow cytometric and/or molecular characterization of the leukemic clone
        - Purpose is to facilitate subsequent measurable (minimal) residual disease (MRD) analysis (ALL-F)
    - Conventional analysis of G-banded metaphase chromosomes (karyotype)

# Molecular characterization

- Cytogenetic and molecular prognostic risk stratification for B-cell ALL (B-ALL) (ALL-2)
- Optimal risk stratification and treatment planning require testing marrow or peripheral blood lymphoblasts for specific recurrent genomic abnormalities, using:
    - Interphase fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) testing
        - Include probes capable of detecting the major recurrent genomic abnormalities
    - Reverse transcriptase polymerase chain reaction (RT-PCR) testing for BCR::ABL1 in B-ALL
        - Include determination of transcript size (ie, p190 vs. p210)
    - Multigene panel testing (MGPT) for gene fusions and pathogenic variants is recommended
- Additional optional tests include:
    - Chromosome microarray analysis (CMA)/array comparative genomic hybridization (aCGH), in cases of aneuploidy or inadequate karyotype
