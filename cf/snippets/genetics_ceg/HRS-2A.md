# Assessment

- Criteria for evaluation for a polyposis syndrome on hereditary risk assessment ___ (polyposis_criteria)
- Personal history of an LS-related cancer ___ (ls_cancer)
- Family history, which side of the family ___ (family_side)
- First-degree or second-degree relative with CRC, EC, GC, or another LS-related cancer ___ (relative_history)
- Known MMR deficiency ___ (mmr_deficiency)
- Tumor mutational burden (TMB) ___ (tmb)
- Tumor somatic testing result ___ (somatic_testing)

# Assumption behind these testing criteria

- This assumes criteria for evaluation for a polyposis syndrome on hereditary risk assessment has not been met

# What counts as an LS-related cancer

- Colorectal
- Endometrial
- Gastric
- Ovarian
- Pancreatic
- Urothelial
- Brain, usually glioblastoma
- Biliary tract
- Small intestine
- Sebaceous adenomas, as seen in Muir-Torre syndrome
- Sebaceous carcinomas, as seen in Muir-Torre syndrome
- Keratoacanthomas, as seen in Muir-Torre syndrome

# How family history is counted

- Family history indicates family history on the same side of the family
- The risk to unaffected individuals with a first-degree or second-degree relative with CRC, EC, GC, or another LS-related cancer may not be high enough to warrant testing without other criteria

# Tumor-based testing and MMR results

- See LS-A for details on tumor screening for LS
- Tumor mutational burden (TMB) can be used as a surrogate to some degree for MSI
    - There are causes of increased TMB other than dMMR
- A person with an LS-related cancer but without a known MMR deficiency may still warrant additional genetic evaluation based on personal and family history

# Weighing germline sequencing after a somatic finding

- Careful evaluation of personal and family history of the individual, to determine the yield of germline sequencing

# Genes to include on the multigene panel

- Biallelic MUTYH mutations have been shown to lead to dMMR tumors
- MUTYH should therefore be included in MGPT at a minimum
    - Consider other base-excision repair genes (NTHL1)
    - Consider DNA polymerase genes (POLE and POLD1)
    - These have the potential to also lead to biallelic somatic MMR gene inactivation
    - Morak M, et al. Eur J Hum Genet 2014;22:1334-1337
