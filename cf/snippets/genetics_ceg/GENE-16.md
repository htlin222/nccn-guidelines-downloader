# RPS20 - colon cancer and colorectal phenotype

- Estimated absolute risk: insufficient data to define
- Management
    - Colonoscopy beginning at age 20 y, then repeat every 3 to 5 y
    - If the patient had a hematopoietic cell transplant prior to age 20, begin colonoscopy one year after transplant
    - See NCCN Guidelines for Colorectal Cancer Screening
- Strength of evidence: limited
- Colorectal phenotype: unknown
- Screening interval
    - Data to support the exact screening interval is uncertain
    - Can be further tailored based on family history and shared decision-making

# RPS20 - endometrial cancer

- No evidence of increased risk

# RPS20 - gastric cancer

- No evidence of increased risk

# RPS20 - other cancers and other risks

- Unknown or insufficient evidence
- Four families who meet Amsterdam I criteria have been reported with pathogenic variants in RPS20
    - In one of them all the CRCs were MSS, familial CRC type X
    - In one of the variant-positive Amsterdam I families, two individuals had more than 10 polyps
- One individual with a pathogenic variant in RPS20 had metachronous CRC primaries by age 39
- The earliest CRC diagnosis reported thus far was at age 24
- Relationship to Diamond-Blackfan anemia (DBA)
    - DBA is a rare inherited bone marrow failure syndrome characterized by red blood cell failure, congenital anomalies, poor linear growth, and cancer predisposition, most commonly CRC and osteogenic sarcoma
    - Two unrelated children with DBA, lacking variants in known DBA genes, had de novo novel missense variants in RPS20 that reduce the RPS20 protein level
    - Increased CRC surveillance has been recommended for patients with DBA
    - The link between RPS20 pathogenic variants and DBA is uncertain at present, but individuals with RPS20 pathogenic variants are recommended to follow the DBA CRC surveillance recommendations, given the early ages of CRC in the RPS20 families

# SMAD4 juvenile polyposis syndrome - colon cancer and colorectal phenotype

- Estimated absolute risk: up to 50%
- Management: see Juvenile Polyposis Syndrome (JPS-2)
- Strength of evidence: strong
- Colorectal phenotype
    - 5 or more hamartomatous polyps
    - Sometimes referred to as juvenile polyps or juvenile type hamartomas

# SMAD4 juvenile polyposis syndrome - endometrial cancer

- No evidence of increased risk

# SMAD4 juvenile polyposis syndrome - gastric cancer

- Estimated absolute risk: stomach cancer approximately 10%
- Management: see Juvenile Polyposis Syndrome (JPS-2)

# SMAD4 juvenile polyposis syndrome - other cancers and other risks

- Unknown or insufficient evidence
- Possible increased risk for small intestine cancer, but no management recommendations have been made
- SMAD4 carriers are at increased risk for HHT
    - Screening should begin ideally within the first 6 mo of life
