# Source

- NCCN Genetic/Familial High-Risk Assessment: Colorectal, Endometrial, and Gastric v1.2026, JPS-3, p114
- Adult surveillance for juvenile polyposis syndrome (JPS)
- Due to the rarity of the syndrome and complexities of diagnosing and providing care for individuals with JPS, referral to a specialized team is recommended
- All recommendations category 2A unless otherwise indicated

# Colon

- Initiation age ~18 y
- SMAD4 or BMPR1A PV carriers, lifetime risk up to 50%
    - High-quality colonoscopy every 1–3 y
    - Intervals should be based on polyp size, number, and pathology
- Clinical diagnosis but no PV identified, lifetime risk undefined
    - High-quality colonoscopy every 1–3 y
    - Intervals should be based on polyp size, number, and pathology
    - If no polyps, consider increasing interval to every 5 y
- If polyp burden or polyp-related symptoms (ie, anemia) cannot be controlled endoscopically, or prevent optimal surveillance for cancer, consideration should be given to gastrectomy and/or colectomy

# Stomach

- Initiation age ~18 y
- SMAD4 PV carriers, lifetime risk ~10%, especially if multiple gastric polyps present
- BMPR1A PV carriers, rare
    - In a meta-analysis of 181 patients with BMPR1A, only one patient with gastric cancer was identified
    - While SMAD4 PV carriers often have severe upper GI tract involvement, BMPR1A PV carriers may have a less severe upper GI tract phenotype and may merit lengthened surveillance intervals in the absence of polyps
    - Gastric cancer risk for BMPR1A PV carriers may be lower than for SMAD4 PV carriers
- SMAD4 or BMPR1A PV carriers
    - Upper endoscopy every 1–3 y
    - Intervals should be based on polyp size, number, and pathology
    - Consider extending surveillance interval to every 3 y if EGD does not show any abnormalities
    - Gastrectomy may be indicated in select, rare circumstances
- Clinical diagnosis but no PV identified, lifetime risk undefined
    - Upper endoscopy every 1–3 y
    - Intervals should be based on polyp size, number, and pathology
    - If no polyps, consider increasing interval to every 5 y in conjunction with colonoscopy
    - Gastrectomy may be indicated in select, rare circumstances
- If polyp burden or polyp-related symptoms (ie, anemia) cannot be controlled endoscopically, or prevent optimal surveillance for cancer, consideration should be given to gastrectomy and/or colectomy

# Indications for gastrectomy

- Gastrectomy is indicated in patients with gastric cancer or multi-focal high-grade dysplasia
- Consideration for gastrectomy should be made if any of the following
    - Inability to adequately survey or endoscopically control polyposis
    - Persistent anemia or gastrointestinal bleeding from gastric polyposis or angioectasia
    - Symptoms of gastric outlet obstruction
    - Protein-losing gastropathy
- A referral to a center with expertise is advised

# Small intestine

- All patients with JPS, risk rare and undefined
- No recommendations have been made

# HHT

- SMAD4 PV carriers, 22%
- Initiation at time of diagnosis
- Screen for signs, symptoms, and vascular lesions associated with HHT
