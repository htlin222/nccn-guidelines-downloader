# MUTYH biallelic - colon cancer and colorectal phenotype

- Absolute risk: 70%–90% if polyposis left untreated
- Management: MUTYH-associated polyposis (MAP-2)
- Strength of evidence: strong
- Colorectal phenotype: 10–100 polyps
    - Adenomas and hyperplastic polyps most frequent
    - Serrated, sessile serrated, and mixed polyps less frequent
    - 18% meet criteria for SPS

# MUTYH biallelic - endometrial cancer

- No evidence of increased risk

# MUTYH biallelic - gastric cancer

- Gastric FGP: 11%
- Gastric cancer: no evidence of increased risk

# MUTYH biallelic - other cancer risks

- Absolute risk
    - Duodenal polyposis: 17%–34%
    - Duodenal cancer: 4%
- Management: MUTYH-associated polyposis (MAP-2)

# MUTYH monoallelic - colon cancer and colorectal phenotype

- Absolute risk: no increased risk
- Management
    - General population screening is appropriate for these individuals
    - For probands with a personal or first-degree family history of CRC or polyps not explained by MAP, increased screening as per the relevant guidelines
        - NCCN Guidelines for Colon Cancer
        - NCCN Guidelines for Rectal Cancer
        - NCCN Guidelines for Colorectal Cancer Screening
- Strength of evidence: limited
- Colorectal phenotype: no polyposis

# MUTYH monoallelic - endometrial cancer

- No evidence of increased risk

# MUTYH monoallelic - gastric cancer

- No evidence of increased risk

# MUTYH monoallelic - other cancer risks

- Unknown or insufficient evidence

# MUTYH monoallelic - what not to change

- Limited evidence of increased risk for EC 3%–9% and for gastric cancer, but no changes in management have been made
- Ovarian, bladder, breast, and thyroid cancers have been reported
- Approximately 1%–2% of the general population are monoallelic MUTYH carriers
- A comparison of MUTYH heterozygote prevalence in colorectal, endometrial, and breast cancer cohorts versus controls of European (non-Finnish) descent found no difference in prevalence
    - Suggests no association between colorectal, endometrial, or breast cancer and MUTYH heterozygosity in individuals of European ancestry
- A large meta-analysis of monoallelic MUTYH carriers found only a slight increase in CRC risk (OR, 1.17; 95% CI, 1.01–1.34)
- A study of 125 MUTYH heterozygotes who underwent at least one surveillance colonoscopy identified no CRCs, and the adenoma rate was not high
    - Supports providing care for these patients in the same way as the general population
- Some reports suggest monoallelic MUTYH may be associated with increased risk of gastric, liver, breast, and endometrial cancer, whereas other reports demonstrate no association with breast or endometrial cancer
