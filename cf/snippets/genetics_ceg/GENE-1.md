# APC / familial adenomatous polyposis

- Colon cancer and colorectal phenotype
    - Absolute risk: approaches 100% if polyposis is left untreated
    - Management: Familial Adenomatous Polyposis (FAP-B)
    - Strength of evidence: strong
    - Colorectal phenotype: 100 or more adenomas
- Endometrial cancer
    - No evidence of increased risk
- Gastric cancer
    - Estimated absolute risk: 0.1%–7.1%
    - Management: Familial Adenomatous Polyposis (FAP-B)
- Other risks
    - Other cancers: Familial Adenomatous Polyposis Risk table (FAP-A)
    - Management: Familial Adenomatous Polyposis (FAP-B)
- Comments
    - About half of patients with FAP develop adenomas by 15 years of age, and 95% by age 35 years
    - FAP may also present with gastric FGP/adenomas, duodenal adenomas, CHRPE, osteomas, supernumerary teeth, odontomas, desmoids, and epidermoid cysts

# APC / attenuated familial adenomatous polyposis

- Colon cancer and colorectal phenotype
    - Absolute risk: approaches 70% if polyposis is left untreated
    - Management: Attenuated Familial Adenomatous Polyposis (AFAP-1)
    - Strength of evidence: strong
    - Colorectal phenotype: 10 to fewer than 100 adenomas
- Endometrial cancer
    - No evidence of increased risk
- Gastric cancer
    - Estimated absolute risk: 0.1%–7.1%
    - Management: Familial Adenomatous Polyposis (FAP-B)
- Other risks
    - Other cancers: Attenuated Familial Adenomatous Polyposis (AFAP-1)
    - Management: Attenuated Familial Adenomatous Polyposis (AFAP-1)

# APC I1307K variant

- Footnotes a and b apply to this entry, and are on GENE-18
- Colon cancer and colorectal phenotype
    - Estimated absolute risk: 5%–10%
    - Management, for probands with CRC and this PV
        - See surveillance recommendations for post-CRC resection in the NCCN Guidelines for Colon Cancer and the NCCN Guidelines for Rectal Cancer
    - Management, for probands without a personal history of CRC
        - High-quality colonoscopy screening every 5 years
        - Begin at age 40, or 10 years prior to the age of the first-degree relative's CRC diagnosis
    - Strength of evidence: strong
    - Colorectal phenotype: polyposis usually not observed
- Endometrial cancer
    - No evidence of increased risk
- Gastric cancer
    - No evidence of increased risk
- Other risks
    - Other cancers: unknown or insufficient evidence
- Comments
    - In the Ashkenazi Jewish population in the United States, the APC c.3920T>A (p.I1307K) variant is reported in 11.5% of those diagnosed with CRC and in 7.2% of those not diagnosed with CRC
    - The incidence of CRC in probands and family members is similar for both Ashkenazi Jewish APC I1307K heterozygotes and non-Jewish APC I1307K heterozygotes
    - The same screening recommendations apply to all APC I1307K variant heterozygotes
