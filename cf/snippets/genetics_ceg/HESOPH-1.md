# Criteria for further risk evaluation, esophageal SCC

- Individual with a known hereditary syndrome associated with increased risk of esophageal SCC
    - Referral to a cancer genetics professional is recommended
- Individual with esophageal SCC and personal history or exam findings consistent with a hereditary syndrome associated with increased risk of esophageal SCC, eg, Fanconi anemia, Tylosis
    - Genetic evaluation
    - Consideration of genetic testing
- Whom to test within the family
    - The most efficient strategy to identify a causative gene PV in a family is to test a family member with a relevant cancer
    - If that family member is either unwilling or unavailable for testing, then consider testing of an unaffected at-risk family member
    - Detailed discussion of genetic counseling and testing, see EVAL-A

# Hereditary syndromes associated with increased risk for esophageal SCC

- Tylosis with esophageal cancer (TOC), also called tylosis with non-epidermolytic palmoplantar keratoderma (PPK), and Howel-Evans syndrome
    - Very rare condition with an autosomal dominant pattern of inheritance
    - Caused by germline PVs in the RHBDF2 gene
    - Individuals with germline RHBDF2 PVs have a significantly increased risk for SCC of the esophagus
    - Characterized by palmoplantar keratoderma appearing in early childhood, with hyperkeratosis of palms and soles
- Fanconi anemia (FA)
    - Rare autosomal recessive syndrome
    - Characterized by bone marrow failure
    - Increased risk for cancer development
    - Congenital abnormalities referred to as VACTERL-H in about 60% of individuals
        - Vertebral abnormalities
        - Anal atresia
        - Cardiac defects
        - Tracheoesophageal fistula
        - Esophageal atresia
        - Renal and radial abnormalities
        - Limb abnormalities
        - Hydrocephalus
    - Increased frequency of SCC of the esophagus has been observed
    - The genes involved include FA complementation groups A-W
        - Biallelic PVs in FANCA are the most common cause of FA
        - Biallelic PVs in FANCC and FANCG account for a minority of cases
        - Biallelic PVs in other complementation groups are rare causes of FA: FANCD1 (BRCA2), FANCJ (BRIP1), FANCN (PALB2), FANCO (RAD51C), FANCR (RAD51D), FANCS (BRCA1), and others

# Criteria for further risk evaluation, esophageal adenocarcinoma

- Currently, there is insufficient evidence to provide specific recommendations for testing for potential familial or inherited causes of esophageal adenocarcinoma
- Personalized recommendations can be considered by providers for families with
    - Multiple family members with esophageal adenocarcinoma and/or Barrett's esophagus
    - An early age of onset in family member(s) with esophageal adenocarcinoma and/or Barrett's esophagus
