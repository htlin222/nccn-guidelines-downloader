# Colon cancer and colorectal phenotype

- Estimated absolute risk: >20%
- Strength of evidence: strong
- Colorectal phenotype: 30–100 polyps; adenomas
- Begin high-quality colonoscopy at age 25–30 y
    - Or 2–5 y prior to the earliest CRC in the family, if it is diagnosed before age 25 y
- Repeat every 2–3 y if negative
- If polyps are found, colonoscopy every 1–2 y
    - Consider surgery if the polyp burden becomes unmanageable
- Surgical evaluation if appropriate

# Endometrial cancer

- Absolute risk: increased risk
- Education regarding the importance of prompt reporting of any abnormal uterine bleeding or postmenopausal bleeding
- Evaluation of symptoms with endometrial biopsy
- Transvaginal ultrasound can be considered at the clinician's discretion in postmenopausal patients
    - Has not been shown to be sufficiently sensitive or specific to be recommended

# Gastric cancer

- No evidence of increased risk

# Other risks and other cancers

- Increased risk for duodenal adenomas/cancer and possibly other cancer
- Management for duodenal cancer: baseline upper endoscopy beginning at age 30–35 y
    - Or earlier based on family history
    - See FAP-C for follow-up of duodenoscopic findings
- Information about cancer risk in POLD1 PV carriers is limited by small sample sizes
    - In one study, the cancers with risk greater than that of the general population were colon cancer and EC
    - Limited evidence of increased risk for breast cancer, brain cancers, and possibly other cancers has been reported

# Next

- Gain-of-function P/LP variant in the exonuclease domain, manage as polymerase proofreading-associated polyposis with the colonoscopy and duodenal surveillance above
- Duodenoscopic findings on baseline upper endoscopy, follow FAP-C
- LOF PV, or PV outside the exonuclease domain, consider MDPL syndrome rather than PPAP
