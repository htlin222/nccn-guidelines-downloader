# Source

- NCCN Genetic/Familial High-Risk Assessment: Colorectal, Endometrial, Esophageal, and Gastric v1.2026, HRS-1, p27
- General criteria, applies to deciding whether testing or genetic evaluation is clinically indicated for a hereditary syndrome associated with colorectal, endometrial, esophageal, and/or gastric cancer
- Obtain a comprehensive assessment for hereditary colorectal/endometrial/esophageal/gastric cancers (EVAL-B)
- Genetic counseling/patient education is highly recommended when genetic testing is offered and after results are disclosed
    - A genetic counselor, medical geneticist, oncologist, gastroenterologist, surgeon, oncology nurse, or other health professional with expertise and experience in cancer genetics should be involved early in counseling patients who potentially meet criteria for an inherited syndrome
- Every scenario below is read together with the Principles of Cancer Risk Assessment and Counseling (EVAL-A) and the NCCN Guidelines for Genetic/Familial High-Risk Assessment: Breast, Ovarian, Pancreatic, and Prostate
- Footnotes for this page are on HRS-1A
- All recommendations category 2A unless otherwise indicated

# Testing is clinically indicated in the following scenarios

- Individuals with any blood relative with a known P/LP variant in a cancer susceptibility gene
- Individuals meeting the criteria below but who tested negative with previous limited testing, and are interested in pursuing MGPT
    - Limited testing, eg, single gene and/or absent deletion duplication analysis
- A P/LP variant identified on tumor somatic testing that has clinical implications if also identified in the germline
    - Somatic P/LP variants in several genes with germline implications are common (eg, TP53, STK11, PTEN, APC)
    - Such a somatic finding will rarely be indicative of a need for germline testing unless clinical/family history features suggest the possibility of a germline P/LP variant
- To aid in surgical decision-making and medical management
    - For example, planning extent of colon resection
    - For example, planning type and timing of risk-reducing surgeries
    - See the relevant NCCN Treatment Guidelines for further details
- Individual who meets LS testing criteria (HRS-2), based on any of:
    - Family history of LS-associated PV
    - Personal or family history of LS-related cancer
        - LS-related cancers include colorectal, endometrial, gastric, ovarian, pancreatic, urothelial, brain (usually glioblastoma), biliary tract, and small intestine
        - Also sebaceous adenomas, sebaceous carcinomas, and keratoacanthomas as seen in Muir-Torre syndrome
    - Personal history of mismatch repair deficient (dMMR) tumor
        - Any tumor that is microsatellite instability-high (MSI-H) by polymerase chain reaction (PCR) or next-generation sequencing (NGS)
        - Or any tumor with abnormal/dMMR protein expression on immunohistochemistry (IHC), without concurrent MLH1 promoter hypermethylation or BRAF V600E mutation
- Personal or family history meeting criteria as specified for:
    - Colorectal cancer (CRC) (HRS-2)
    - Endometrial cancer (EC) (HRS-2)
    - Gastric cancer or gastric polyposis (HGAST-1)
    - Esophageal cancer (HESOPH-1)
- Individual who meets colorectal adenomatous polyposis testing criteria (POLYP-1)
    - Personal or family history of polyps is based on cumulative lifetime history of adenomas, hamartomas, and/or serrated polyps/lesions in the proband or a single family member
- Individual who meets clinical criteria for JPS (JPS-1)
- Individual who meets clinical criteria for PJS (PJS-1)
- Individual who meets Li-Fraumeni syndrome (LFS) testing criteria, see NCCN Guidelines for Genetic/Familial High-Risk Assessment: Breast, Ovarian, Pancreatic, and Prostate
- Individual who meets Cowden syndrome (CS)/PTEN hamartoma tumor syndrome (PHTS) testing criteria, see NCCN Guidelines for Genetic/Familial High-Risk Assessment: Breast, Ovarian, Pancreatic, and Prostate

# Genetic evaluation is clinically indicated in the following scenarios

- Genetic evaluation implies review of the criteria specific for the condition
    - It may or may not include additional evaluation by a genetics expert or germline testing
- Individuals with 10 to 19 adenomatous polyps of the colorectum
- Individual who meets clinical criteria for serrated polyposis syndrome (SPS) (SPS-1)
