# No known history of cancer: workup

- Contrast-enhanced CT chest / abdomen / pelvis
- Consider whole body FDG-PET/CT
- Other tests as indicated
- Then decide where to obtain tissue
    - Tumor found outside the CNS is readily accessible
        - Biopsy or resect the tumor found outside the CNS, to confirm the cancer diagnosis
    - No other readily accessible tumor for biopsy
        - Surgery to confirm the diagnosis of CNS metastases (BRAIN-B)
        - Resection for management of mass effect or symptoms
        - Consider resection for treatment of patients with newly diagnosed or stable systemic disease, or reasonable systemic treatment options
        - Biopsy if resection not planned

# Known history of cancer: consider surgery for brain metastases

- Surgery per Principles of Surgery (BRAIN-B)
- Resection for management of mass effect or symptoms
- Resection for treatment of patients with newly diagnosed or stable systemic disease, or reasonable systemic treatment options
- Biopsy if concern exists about the diagnosis of the CNS lesions and resection is not planned
- The decision to resect may depend on
    - The need to establish a histologic diagnosis
    - The size of the lesion
    - Its location
    - Institutional expertise
    - Smaller (<2 cm), deep, asymptomatic lesions may be considered for treatment with SRS
    - Larger (>2 cm), symptomatic lesions may be more appropriate for surgery
