# Additional diagnostic testing, essential

- Adequate immunophenotyping to establish diagnosis
    - Typical immunophenotype: CD5+, CD20+, CD43+, CD23-/+, cyclin D1+, CD10-/+
    - Some cases of MCL may be CD5- or CD23+
    - If the diagnosis is suspected, cyclin D1 staining or FISH for t(11;14) should be done
    - There are rare cases of CCND1- MCL (<5%) with an otherwise typical immunophenotype
- IHC panel
    - CD20, CD3, CD5, cyclin D1, CD10, CD21, CD23, BCL2, BCL6, SOX11, Ki-67
    - Ki-67 proliferation fraction of <30% in lymph nodes is associated with a more favorable prognosis
- With or without flow cytometry, with peripheral blood and/or biopsy specimen
    - kappa/lambda, CD19, CD20, CD5, CD23, CD10, CD200
- TP53 sequencing
    - TP53 mutation has been associated with poor prognosis in patients treated with conventional therapy, including transplant
    - Clinical trial is strongly recommended for these patients
    - TP53 sequencing is preferred
    - In the front-line setting, p53 by IHC can be used as a surrogate, but should be confirmed with sequencing

# Additional diagnostic testing, useful under certain circumstances

- IHC: LEF1 may help distinguish from variant CLL
- IHC: SOX11 or IGHV sequencing may be useful for determination of clinically indolent MCL
    - Most common biomarker for indolent disease: SOX11- with IGHV mutated
    - Typical clinical presentation: leukemic non-nodal CLL-like with splenomegaly, low tumor burden, Ki-67 proliferation fraction <10%
- IHC may also help in diagnosis of CCND1- MCL
- FISH: t(11;14)
- FISH for CCND2 and CCND3 gene fusions may also help in diagnosis of CCND1- MCL

# Workup, essential

- Physical exam
    - Attention to node-bearing areas, including Waldeyer's ring
    - Attention to size of liver and spleen
- Performance status
- B symptoms
- CBC with differential
- Comprehensive metabolic panel
- LDH
- PET/CT scan (preferred), or C/A/P CT with contrast of diagnostic quality, if therapy is planned
- Hepatitis B testing
    - Indicated because of the risk of reactivation with immunotherapy + chemotherapy
    - HBsAg and core antibody for a patient with no risk factors
    - For patients with risk factors or previous history of hepatitis B, add e-antigen (NHODG-B)
    - If positive, check viral load and consider consult with gastroenterologist
- Echocardiogram or MUGA scan, if an anthracycline-based regimen is indicated
- Pregnancy testing in patients of childbearing age, if therapy planned

# Workup, useful under certain circumstances

- Endoscopy/colonoscopy
    - Essential for confirmation of stage I-II disease
- Bone marrow biopsy with or without aspirate
- Neck CT with contrast
- Uric acid
- Beta-2-microglobulin
- Hepatitis C testing
- Lumbar puncture, for blastic variant or central nervous system (CNS) symptoms
- Discuss fertility preservation
    - Options include sperm banking, IVF, or ovarian tissue or oocyte cryopreservation
