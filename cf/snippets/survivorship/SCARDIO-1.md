# Source

- NCCN Survivorship v3.2026, SCARDIO-1, p76
- Focuses specifically on heart failure or cardiomyopathy that may arise from anthracycline therapy
    - Other cancer treatments can result in diverse cardiovascular issues (SCVD-1)
    - Other systemic therapies may also cause cardiomyopathy (eg, HER2-targeted therapies, tyrosine kinase inhibitors, immunotherapy); some of the concepts here may apply to these other cardiomyopathies
- All recommendations category 2A unless otherwise indicated

# Risk factors for cardiomyopathy and heart failure

- History of anthracycline exposure is a risk factor that predisposes survivors to develop cardiomyopathy (SCARDIO-3)
- Structural heart disease
    - Considered stage B heart failure even if no actual symptoms
- Risk for cardiovascular problems varies greatly depending on
    - Type of anthracycline used
    - Cumulative dose received
        - High cumulative anthracycline dose defined as cumulative doxorubicin dose at or ≥250 mg/m2 or equivalent
- Additional cardiovascular risk factors, in combination with anthracycline exposure, increase the risk of developing cardiomyopathy and heart failure
    - Hypertension
    - Dyslipidemia
    - Diabetes
    - Encouraged to address these in coordination with primary care

# Clinical course

- May take years or even decades to manifest
- Signs of cardiac dysfunction can be seen prior to the development of symptoms
- If detected early, may be responsive to cardioprotective medications
    - Prospective studies evaluating these medications are lacking

# Screening emphasis

- For survivors at high risk for CVD, thorough clinical screening for heart failure within one year after completion of anthracycline therapy
