# Source

- NCCN Survivorship v3.2026, SPAIN-4, p116
- Cancer Pain Syndrome: neuropathic pain
- Also see NCCN Guidelines for Adult Cancer Pain
- All recommendations category 2A unless otherwise indicated

# Assessment

- ___ (diagnosis) survivor
- Neuropathic pain features
    - Paresthesias (tingling or prickling)
    - Shooting, "electrical"
    - Numbness
    - Allodynia (pain with non-painful stimulus)

# Treatment: general measures

- Pharmacologic
    - Non-opioid/adjuvant analgesics (see PAIN-E in the NCCN Guidelines for Adult Cancer Pain)
        - Antidepressants: SNRIs (including duloxetine), TCAs, anticonvulsants
            - Duloxetine has the most evidence for treating neuropathic pain
    - Topicals
        - Patches (ie, lidocaine, capsaicin)
        - Creams/gels: Diclofenac topical gel
        - Compounded creams (eg, combinations of lidocaine, baclofen, ketamine, and amitriptyline)
- Non-pharmacologic
    - CBT and psychosocial support (see PAIN-C in the NCCN Guidelines for Adult Cancer Pain)
    - Physical modalities
        - Heat
        - Ice
        - Massage
        - Acupuncture
        - Transcutaneous electrical nerve stimulation (TENS) unit
            - Consider scrambler therapy

# Treatment: moderate or severe pain

- Opioids and dual-action opioid agonist/noradrenaline reuptake inhibitor
    - See PAIN-3, PAIN-4, and PAIN-5 in the NCCN Guidelines for Adult Cancer Pain
    - Principles of Opioid Use in Long-Term Survivors (SPAIN-2)
    - Initiating opioids in cancer survivors should be carefully considered if other interventions are unsuccessful

# Referral

- Consider referral to
    - Pain management services
    - Interventional specialist
    - Physical therapy
    - Physical medicine and rehabilitation
    - Integrative services
    - Palliative care, as appropriate
