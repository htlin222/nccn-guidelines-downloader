    - Pain medications

    - Stimulants
    - Antidepressants
    - Anti-psychotics
    - Sedative/hypnotics
    - Opioids
    - OTC sleep aids
    - Antihistamines

# Footnote d, menopause and vasomotor symptoms

- Sleep disorders such as insomnia, RLS, and sleep apnea are more prevalent in menopausal individuals
- Hormonal changes, vasomotor symptoms, and lifestyle contribute to these sleep disturbance(s)
- For menopausal survivors for whom insomnia is associated with vasomotor symptoms
    - Treatment of vasomotor symptoms can improve sleep, which can in turn help with vasomotor symptoms
    - Best evidence for improving sleep dysfunction related to vasomotor symptoms is use of MHT
    - Other strategies could be considered for patients unable to be on MHT (SHRS-4)

# Footnote e, differential diagnosis of insomnia or excessive sleepiness

- Consider parasomnias or circadian rhythm sleep wake disorders
- Referral to a sleep specialist

# Footnote f, overlap with insomnia symptoms

- Sleep disordered breathing (eg, obstructive sleep apnea), RLS, circadian rhythm sleep wake disorders, and parasomnias may also present with symptoms of insomnia

# Footnote g, RLS terminology

- RLS is also known as Willis-Ekbom disease
