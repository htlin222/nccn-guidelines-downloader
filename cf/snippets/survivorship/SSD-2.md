# Screening for sleep disturbances and sleep disorders

- Screening for sleep disorders should occur at regular intervals for all cancer survivors, especially when there is a change in clinical status or treatment
- Notation of average sleep duration, bedtime and wake times, regularity (duration and timing), and satisfaction is recommended
- As part of the history, patients should be asked about their sleep quality (SSD-1)
- Patients who report disturbed sleep generally describe one or more of four types of problems
    - Insomnia symptoms
    - Excessive daytime sleepiness
    - Complex behaviors or disturbed sensations in sleep
    - Difficulty breathing/snoring
- The International Classification of Sleep Disorders (ICSD-3-TR) lists six major categories of sleep disorders
    - Insomnia disorders
    - Sleep-related breathing disorders
    - Central disorders of hypersomnolence
    - Circadian rhythm sleep-wake disorders
    - Parasomnias
    - Sleep-related movement disorders

# Assessment and treatment

- Diagnosis of most sleep disorders relies on a careful history, is clinically based, and typically does not require diagnostic procedures
- Sleep studies are necessary to establish the diagnosis of
    - Sleep apnea
    - Narcolepsy
    - Some parasomnias
- For some disorders (eg, parasomnias), a specialized in-lab polysomnogram (PSG) is required for diagnosis
- For narcolepsy, the multiple sleep latency test (MSLT) is required to establish the diagnosis
- In addition to in-lab PSG, home-based sleep studies are increasingly being used for diagnosis of sleep apnea
- Most patients with sleep disorders will benefit from sleep hygiene recommendations (SSD-A)
    - Sleep hygiene alone has not been shown to be an effective non-pharmacological approach for treatment of chronic insomnia
    - Cognitive behavior therapy for insomnia (CBT-I) is recommended as first-line treatment for chronic insomnia (SSD-4)
- Pharmacological approaches may be used as adjunctive therapy in sleep disorders, most notably with
    - Insomnia
    - Restless legs
    - Hypersomnia
    - Some parasomnias, such as rapid eye movement (REM) sleep behavior disorder (SSD-4)
- Given the high comorbidity of sleep disturbances with medical, neurologic, and psychiatric disease, clinicians must be aware of the safety profile of sedatives and stimulants and guard against adverse drug interactions

# Management of nocturia

- Nocturia can disrupt sleep, but it may also be a symptom of sleep disorders
- In insomnia, it is unclear whether the awakenings are due to nocturia or vice versa
- Nocturia is a common symptom of sleep apnea, and treatment of sleep apnea has been shown to reduce nocturia
- Sleep disorders, specifically sleep-related breathing disorders, should be considered in the differential diagnosis of patients with nocturia
- Treatments for nocturia include
    - Restricting fluids close to bedtime
    - Treatment of sleep disorders to improve sleep continuity
