# Vaginal dryness

- Non-hormonal treatments
    - Vaginal moisturizers
    - Vaginal gels
    - Hyaluronic acid (category 2B)
    - Oils (category 2B)
    - Recommend as first-line therapy for those with hormone-dependent cancers or other survivors with mild vaginal dryness
- Lubricants for sexual activity
    - Survivors should be cautioned that some lubricants may be irritating to the area of application
- Local estrogen treatment (ie, rings, suppositories, creams) (category 2B)
    - Limited data in breast cancer survivors suggest minimal systemic absorption with rings and suppositories
    - If estrogen-based treatment is warranted, rings and suppositories are preferred over creams for survivors of hormonally sensitive tumors
    - Limited data regarding use of androgen therapy in survivors of hormonally mediated cancers
        - Small studies suggest it may be beneficial and safe
        - More information is needed to assure safety, particularly for breast cancer survivors on AIs
        - Taranto P, et al. Breast Cancer Res 2024;26:133; Laing AJ, et al. Cancer J 2022;28:196-203
- Other topical hormones (ie, testosterone, dehydroepiandrosterone [DHEA])
    - Limited data regarding use of androgen therapy in survivors of hormonally mediated cancers
        - Small studies suggest it may be beneficial and safe
        - More information is needed to assure safety, particularly for breast cancer survivors on AIs
        - Taranto P, et al. Breast Cancer Res 2024;26:133; Laing AJ, et al. Cancer J 2022;28:196-203
- Consider referral to appropriate specialist for management
- For vaginal pain or discomfort, see SSH-2

# Urogenital symptoms

- Vaginal estrogen treatment
    - Limited data regarding use of androgen therapy in survivors of hormonally mediated cancers

        - More information is needed to assure safety, particularly for breast cancer survivors on AIs
        - Taranto P, et al. Breast Cancer Res 2024;26:133; Laing AJ, et al. Cancer J 2022;28:196-203
