# Source

- NCCN Survivorship v3.2026, SIMIN-1, p61
- General Principles of Immunizations
- Applies to cancer survivors, including those with hematologic or solid tumor malignancies and those post transplant
- All recommendations category 2A unless otherwise indicated

# Assessment

- Consider and encourage administration of inactivated vaccines (eg, influenza) or vaccines made of purified antigens (eg, pneumococcus), bacterial components (eg, diphtheria-tetanus-pertussis), or genetically engineered recombinant antigens (eg, hepatitis B [HepB]) in all cancer and transplant survivors
    - In the absence of known harm, administration of inactivated vaccines with the hope of achieving some protection may be worthwhile
    - Usual doses and schedules recommended
- Actively immunocompromised, or residual immune deficits
    - Vaccines may not trigger protective immune responses in actively immunocompromised individuals or in survivors with residual immune deficits
        - Immunocompromised individuals include those with certain inherited immunodeficiencies (eg, chronic granulomatous disease) and those with acquired immunodeficiency from disease or treatment (eg, hematopoietic malignancy, RT, immunosuppressive medications, HIV infection)
    - Live attenuated vaccines (eg, measles, mumps, rubella [MMR]) contraindicated in actively immunosuppressed individuals
        - Proven or theoretical increased risk of prolonged shedding and disease from the live organism present in the vaccine
        - Other live attenuated vaccines might also be contraindicated in survivors' close contacts
    - When other vaccine options exist, prefer them over live attenuated vaccines in survivors (eg, recombinant zoster vaccine [RZV])

# Timing relative to cancer treatment

- Ideally, administer all indicated vaccines before initiation of cancer treatment
    - If possible, at least 2 weeks before cancer treatment
    - Cancer treatment includes chemotherapy, surgery, treatment with immunosuppressive drugs, radiation, and splenectomy
- Inactivated or recombinant vaccines ideally administered 2 to 4 weeks before cancer treatment
- Non-live vaccines such as the inactivated influenza vaccine (IIV) and COVID vaccine can be administered during or after cancer treatment
- Live viral vaccines can be administered
    - At least 4 weeks before cancer treatment, or
    - At least 3 months after cancer chemotherapy
    - Consultation with an infectious disease specialist or physician familiar with vaccination in survivors and/or patients with cancer strongly recommended
    - See vaccines contraindicated or to use with caution in actively immunocompromised survivors and in close contacts/household members (SIMIN-A)
- COVID-19 vaccine recommended as appropriate

# Anti-B-cell antibody therapy

- In survivors who received anti-B-cell antibody therapy, vaccination should be delayed for at least 6 months after chemotherapy and the last dose of such therapy
