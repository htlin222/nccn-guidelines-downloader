# General principles

- Physical activity and exercise recommendations should be tailored to individual survivor's abilities and preferences

# Aerobic activity

- All exercise should be preceded by a light-intensity aerobic warm-up
- Strive for at least 150 minutes of moderate-intensity or 75 minutes of vigorous-intensity activity (or equivalent combination of moderate- and vigorous-intensity) spread out over the course of a week
    - Category 1 for survivors of localized colon cancer who have completed adjuvant chemotherapy
        - In patients receiving adjuvant therapy for colon cancer, health education plus a structured exercise program (behavior-support sessions and supervised physical activity sessions aimed at increasing cardiovascular exercise) resulted in longer disease-free and overall survival compared with health education alone
- If feasible and safe, aim for an ultimate goal of 300 minutes of moderate- or 150 minutes of vigorous-intensity activity (or equivalent combination) per week
- Survivors who are unable to meet the recommended exercise goals on their own should be referred to a structured program that includes behavior change approaches

# Exercise intensity definitions

- Light physical activity: no noticeable change in breathing pattern
- Moderate exercise: can talk, but not sing
- Vigorous exercise: can say a few words without stopping to catch a breath
- Examples of physical activity: SPA-B

# Strength, stretching, and balance

- Engage in two to three sessions per week of strength/resistance training that include major muscle groups (SPA-A)
- Stretch major muscle groups
    - For aerobic exercise sessions, stretching and/or range of motion exercises should be performed after a warm-up and/or cool-down when muscles are warm, to reduce injury and discomfort
    - Stretching and/or range-of-motion exercises of major muscle groups after a warm-up should also be performed at least 2 days per week that exercises on those muscle groups are not performed
- Core exercises and balance training are recommended especially for older survivors and those at risk for falls

# Daily activity and sedentary behavior

- Engage in general physical activity daily (eg, take the stairs, park in the back of parking lot)
    - Physical activity includes exercise, daily routine activities, and recreational activities (SPA-B 1 of 2)
    - See Strategies to Increase Physical Activity (SPA-B 2 of 2)
- Avoid prolonged sedentary behavior (eg, sitting for long periods, prolonged screen-based activities)
    - Schedule movement/activity breaks regularly
    - Stand or move while talking on the phone, using the computer, or watching television
