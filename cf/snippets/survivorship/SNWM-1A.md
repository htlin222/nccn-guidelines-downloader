# Footnote a, radiation to digestive tract

- Survivors who had radiation directed to the digestive tract may benefit from
    - More specialized nutritional counseling
    - Swallow therapy
    - Referrals to gastrointestinal specialists
- Also see certain NCCN treatment guidelines (Treatment by Cancer Type) for additional nutrition recommendations

# Footnotes b-c, patient education resources

- Recommendation for healthy food portion sizes can be found on the AICR New American Plate website
- Encourage the use of healthy recipes from resources such as the American Cancer Society's Find Healthy Recipes website

# Footnote d, processed meat

- Consumption of processed meats is associated with an increased risk of colorectal and gastric cancers

# Footnotes e-f, highly processed foods

- Highly (sometimes referred to as "ultra") processed foods are industrial formulations typically with 5 or more, and usually many, ingredients
    - Examples: soft drinks, sweet or savory packaged snacks, reconstituted meat products (eg, sausage, chicken nuggets), prepared frozen dishes
    - Besides salt, sugar, oils, and fats, ingredients include food substances not commonly used in culinary preparations
        - Hydrolyzed protein
        - Modified starches
        - Hydrogenated or interesterified oils
    - Additives whose purpose is to imitate sensorial qualities of unprocessed or minimally processed foods and their culinary preparations, or to disguise undesirable qualities of the final product
- Consumption of highly processed foods is associated with an increased risk of cancer

# Footnote g, alcohol

- Data on the effect of alcohol consumption on cancer recurrence is mixed
- If alcohol is consumed, lower amounts are associated with lower risk

# Footnotes h-i, dietary fat sources

- These types of fats (plant sources such as olive or canola oil, avocados, seeds and nuts, and fatty fish) should be prioritized over saturated fats, and used in moderation in the context of weight loss strategies
- Examples of fatty fish include mackerel, salmon, herring, and others

# Footnote j, soy

- AICR: soy intake does not increase risk for breast cancer survivors
