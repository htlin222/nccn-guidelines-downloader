# Footnotes e-f, highly processed foods

- Highly (sometimes referred to as "ultra") processed foods are industrial formulations typically with 5 or more, and usually many, ingredients
    - Examples: soft drinks, sweet or savory packaged snacks, reconstituted meat products (eg, sausage, chicken nuggets), prepared frozen dishes
    - Besides salt, sugar, oils, and fats, ingredients include food substances not commonly used in culinary preparations, such as hydrolyzed protein, modified starches, and hydrogenated or interesterified oils
    - Additives whose purpose is to imitate sensorial qualities of unprocessed or minimally processed foods and their culinary preparations, or to disguise undesirable qualities of the final product (Martinez Steele E, et al. BMJ Open 2016;6:e009892)
- Consumption of highly processed foods is associated with an increased risk of cancer (Fiolet T, et al. BMJ 2018;360:k322)

# Footnote r, weight monitoring

- Daily monitoring has been shown to be associated with improved weight loss (Steinberg, et al. J Acad Nur Diet 2015;115:511-518; Zheng Y, et al. Int J Obes [Lond] 2016;40:1392-1396)

# Footnote s, threshold for escalation

- Survivor has negligible weight loss from diet and exercise interventions: strongly consider

# Footnote t, pharmacologic therapy

- The safety and efficacy of these drugs in cancer survivors is unknown
- Lifestyle modifications are preferred over pharmacologic therapy
