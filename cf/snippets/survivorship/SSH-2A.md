# Footnotes

- General
    - Sexual function and management of hormone-related symptoms are important aspects of quality of life for all cancer survivors
    - Recommendations here are mainly based on data in cisgender survivors, but should be followed for gender diverse survivors as applicable, with the involvement of the appropriate health care specialists
- Cardiovascular risk
    - There are reports of an association between CVD and sexual dysfunction in all individuals, although there is less evidence in individuals assigned female at birth compared to those assigned male at birth
    - Screen all cancer survivors with sexual dysfunction for cardiovascular risk factors (SCVD-1)
- Referral to sexual health specialist
    - May come from a range of backgrounds, including primary care, gynecology, urology, oncology, psychology, and/or rehabilitation medicine
    - Refer to an appropriate local resource for further sexual health evaluation whenever possible
- Referral to sex therapist
    - Certified by the AASECT
- Prescription medications
    - Discuss risks/benefits if not contraindicated for cancer type
    - Or refer to appropriate health care provider (eg, sexual health specialist) for prescription and/or treatment as necessary
- Androgen therapy
    - Limited data regarding use in survivors of hormonally mediated cancers
    - Small studies suggest it may be beneficial and safe
    - More information is needed to assure safety, particularly for breast cancer survivors on AIs
        - Taranto P, et al. Breast Cancer Res 2024;26:133
        - Laing AJ, et al. Cancer J 2022;28:196-203
- Bupropion and buspirone
    - May be considered as off-label treatments for hypoactive sexual desire disorder, despite limited safety and efficacy data
- Ospemifene
    - Currently contraindicated in survivors with a history of estrogen-dependent cancers
