# Assessment

- ___ (diagnosis) survivor
- Sleep and wake disturbances are common in the general population, but prevalence is significantly higher in patients with cancer and survivors
    - Ranges from 30% to over 80%
    - Sleep problems can persist for years after completion of cancer treatment

# Importance of sleep health

- Sleep regulates a broad range of brain and systemic functions in cells and tissues
- Sleep and wake cycles are regulated by interaction between sleep homeostatic and circadian processes
    - Their proper alignment determines sleep quality
- Sleep is critical for overall health and quality of life
- Sleep and circadian rhythm disturbances increase the risk for
    - Cardiovascular disorders
    - Metabolic disorders
    - Neurologic disorders
    - Psychiatric disorders
    - Immunologic disorders
    - Certain types of cancer
- Bidirectional relationship: chronic medical and mental health disorders negatively affect sleep quality

# Healthy sleep

- Sleep health is multidimensional: duration, timing, regularity, satisfaction
- Experts recommend at least 7 hours per night for adults
- Keeping a regular sleep schedule, even on weekends, helps maintain a regular sleep-wake cycle
- Negatively affect metabolic, cardiovascular, cognitive, and immune functions, and increase risk for cardiometabolic disease and neurodegeneration
    - Short sleep duration, typically less than 7 hours in adults
    - Later timing of sleep
    - Sleep fragmentation
    - Higher day-to-day variability

# Contributing factors in cancer survivors

- Psychological stress
- Mood disorders
    - Depression
    - Anxiety
- Environmental/behavioral factors
- Side effects of cancer treatment
    - Chronic pain, lymphedema, nocturia, and menopausal symptoms are commonly associated with insomnia
    - Treatment-related iron deficiency and neuropathy can increase vulnerability for restless legs syndrome
    - Menopause-related hormonal changes increase risk for vasomotor symptoms that disturb sleep, insomnia disorders, and obstructive sleep apnea

# Assessment rationale

- Cancer survivors are at increased risk for impaired cognitive, cardiometabolic, immune, and mental health from sleep disruption and sleep disorders
- Improving sleep may improve quality of life, overall health, and resilience
