# Scope of these guidelines

- Cancer survivors include those who are
    - Initiating treatment
    - In ongoing treatment
    - Completed cancer treatment
    - In clinical remission
    - Also see NCCN Guidelines for Supportive Care Table of Contents
- Provide screening, evaluation, and treatment recommendations for common consequences of cancer and cancer treatment
    - Can be used to optimize health and wellness for all survivors
    - Created to assist health care professionals who work with survivors of adult-onset cancer in the post-treatment period
        - Includes both oncology and primary care practices
- Provide a framework for the general survivorship care and management of potential long-term and/or late effects of cancer and its treatment
- Should be used as a supplement to the follow-up recommendations within the disease-specific guidelines
    - See NCCN Guidelines for Treatment by Cancer Type
    - See NCCN Guidelines for Palliative Care, for recommendations regarding metastatic disease
- Together with the appropriate disease-specific guideline, provide a framework for coordination of care between the survivor's health care providers, to ensure needs are appropriately addressed

# Principles of use

- Many post-treatment issues covered in these guidelines are best addressed before cancer treatment begins, so problems can be prevented or minimized
- Not all survivorship issues need to be addressed at every visit
    - Periodic screening assessments and appropriate follow-up care as clinically indicated
- Referral to other health care disciplines/providers or community resources may address several indications or identified issues with one intervention
    - Example: rehabilitation for fatigue, depression, and pain
- Stakeholders should ensure implementation of guideline-concordant survivorship care within their unique health systems
    - Institutions encouraged to determine how to systematically deliver the six key components of survivorship care (SURV-2)
    - Several models of care frame implementation according to provider type(s), clinic type(s), patient risk, and other factors
    - Institutions should provide the resources necessary to plan, deliver, and evaluate their survivorship care program(s)

# Special populations

- Younger populations: also see NCCN Guidelines for Adolescent and Young Adult (AYA) Oncology, and the Children's Oncology Group Long-Term Follow-up Guidelines for Survivors of Childhood, Adolescent, and Young Adult Cancers
- Survivors treated with immunotherapy
    - Ongoing surveillance for immune-mediated toxicities is warranted
    - See NCCN Guidelines for Management of Immune Checkpoint Inhibitor-Related Toxicities
    - See NCCN Guidelines for Management of CAR T-Cell and Lymphocyte Engager-Related Toxicities
