- Recommendations mainly based on data in cisgender survivors, but should be followed for gender diverse survivors as applicable, with the involvement of the appropriate health care specialists

# Symptoms

- Symptoms of menopause, go to SHRS-1
- Vaginal dryness, or other issues related to vaginal health (eg, discomfort, discharge, pain), go to SHRS-5
- Low or lack of desire, libido, or intimacy
- Symptoms of pain with sexual activity
- Problems with orgasm (eg, less intensity, difficulty achieving, pain)
- Global symptoms of distress, anxiety (generalized or about sex), depression, or other psychological concerns

# Treatment options

- Refer to appropriate health care provider to address contributing psychosocial problems
- Discussion of available drugs
    - Discuss risk/benefits of prescription medications if not contraindicated for cancer type, or refer to appropriate health care provider (eg, sexual health specialist) for prescription and/or treatment as necessary
    - Androgens
        - Limited data regarding use of androgen therapy in survivors of hormonally mediated cancers; small studies suggest it may be beneficial and safe; more information needed to assure safety, particularly for breast cancer survivors on AIs
    - Bupropion
        - May be considered as off-label treatment for hypoactive sexual desire disorder, despite limited safety and efficacy data
    - Buspirone
        - May be considered as off-label treatment for hypoactive sexual desire disorder, despite limited safety and efficacy data
    - Flibanserin
    - Bremelanotide
- Topical vaginal therapies (SHRS-5), OTC or prescription
    - Prescription formulations carry the same limited safety data noted for androgen therapy
- Vaginal dilators
- Ospemifene
    - Currently contraindicated in survivors with a history of estrogen-dependent cancers
- DHEA
    - Same limited safety data noted for androgen therapy applies
- Pelvic physical therapy
- Topical anesthetics, OTC or prescription
- Discussion of options including vibrator or clitoral stimulatory device with referral to appropriate specialist
    - Sexual health specialists may come from a range of backgrounds, including primary care, gynecology, urology, oncology, psychology, and/or rehabilitation medicine; refer to an appropriate local resource for further sexual health evaluation when possible

# Follow-up

- Concerns regarding sexual function improved or resolved
    - Re-evaluate at regular intervals
- Ongoing concerns regarding sexual function
    - See SANXDE-1
    - Multiple issues identified
        - Refer to appropriate health care provider for further evaluation, workup, and/or treatment
            - Primary care
            - Gynecology
            - Psychology (may include couples counseling)
            - Sexual health specialist
                - May come from a range of backgrounds, including primary care, gynecology, urology, oncology, psychology, and/or rehabilitation medicine; refer to an appropriate local resource for further sexual health evaluation when possible
            - Sex therapist, if available
                - Referral to a sex therapist certified by the AASECT
