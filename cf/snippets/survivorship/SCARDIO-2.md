# History and physical exam

- Assess for signs and symptoms of heart failure
    - Shortness of breath or chest pain after physical activity or exercise
    - Shortness of breath when laying flat (ie, orthopnea)
    - Waking up at night due to shortness of breath
    - Swelling in the legs
    - Patients with symptoms of heart failure should undergo an ECHO
- Cardiovascular risk factor management
    - Encourage primary care provider involvement in treatment of cardiovascular risk factors
    - Encourage routine follow-up in coordination with primary care provider
- Assess patient's ability to perform routine and desired activities of daily living (ADL)
- Look for signs of volume overload
- Review medications, alcohol use, and other substance use
- Review oncologic history
    - Total cumulative dose of anthracycline
    - Other systemic therapy (eg, anti-HER2, immune checkpoint inhibitor treatment)
    - Chest RT

# Evaluate for presence of heart failure risk factors

- Hypertension
- Dyslipidemia
- Diabetes mellitus
- Family history of cardiomyopathy
- Age >65 years
- Low-normal left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) (50%-54%) at baseline
- History of other cardiovascular comorbidities (ie, atrial fibrillation, known CAD, baseline evidence of structural heart disease)
- Smoking
- Obesity
- Physical inactivity
- Consider use of a CVD risk assessment tool (eg, ASCVD Risk Estimator Plus, PREVENT Risk Calculator)

# Consider assessment of cardiac function with imaging

- Transthoracic echocardiography or cardiac magnetic resonance, in line with best practices, within 1 year after completion of anthracycline therapy, for survivors with
    - High cumulative anthracycline dose (cumulative doxorubicin dose ≥250 mg/m2 or equivalent)
    - Low cumulative anthracycline dose and 1 or more heart failure risk factors
- Referral to cardiologist/cardio-oncologist if there are echocardiographic abnormalities and/or any cardiovascular symptoms or concerns
- For survivors of certain cancer types, longer-term cardiovascular surveillance may be needed, see NCCN Guidelines for Treatment by Cancer Type

# Next

- No evidence of structural heart disease, but symptomatic
    - Workup for other causes of symptoms
    - Referral to other specialties (eg, pulmonology or cardiology)
- No evidence of structural heart disease and asymptomatic, go to Stage A (SCARDIO-3)
- No ECHO performed and asymptomatic, go to Stage A (SCARDIO-3)
- Evidence of structural heart disease (asymptomatic or symptomatic), determine stage of cardiomyopathy, go to SCARDIO-3
    - LV dysfunction
    - LV hypertrophy
    - Valvular disease
    - LV dilatation and/or wall thinning
