# Source

- NCCN Survivorship v3.2026, HL-1, p42
- General Principles of Healthy Lifestyles
- All recommendations category 2A unless otherwise indicated

# Assessment

- Healthy lifestyle habits associated with improved overall health and quality of life in all phases of survivorship, from diagnosis through treatment and follow-up
- For some cancers, a healthy lifestyle associated with a reduced risk of recurrence and death
- Cancer prehabilitation appropriate for many newly diagnosed survivors prior to initiating treatment with surgery or chemotherapy
    - Consider referral to physical therapy or exercise oncology specialists
- Encourage all survivors to set incremental and ultimate goals for diet, physical activity, and weight management

# Minimum lifestyle counseling

- Achieve and maintain a healthy body weight throughout life (SNWM-2)
- Avoid physical inactivity
- Engage in physical activity daily (eg, exercise, take the stairs, park in the back of the parking lot) (SPA-1)
- Maintain a healthy diet high in vegetables, fruits, beans/legumes, whole grains, nuts, and seeds
    - Limit red meats to <18 oz per week
    - Best to avoid processed meat
    - Limit highly processed foods, particularly those high in fats and sugars (SNWM-1)
        - Highly ("ultra") processed foods are industrial formulations with 5 or more, usually many, ingredients (eg, soft drinks, sweet or savory packaged snacks, reconstituted meat products, prepared frozen dishes)
        - Consumption is associated with an increased risk of cancer
    - Limit sugar-sweetened beverages; consume sparingly, if at all
    - Best to avoid alcohol, as any consumption increases risk for several cancers (SNWM-1)
        - Data on the effect of alcohol consumption on cancer recurrence is mixed
        - If alcohol is consumed, lower amounts are associated with lower risk
- Discontinue use of cigarettes, other tobacco products (including hookah), and e-cigarettes (NCCN Guidelines for Smoking Cessation)
    - Avoid secondary exposure to cigarette smoke
- Practice sun safety
    - Utilize a sunscreen with an SPF of at least 30 that protects against UVA and UVB rays and is water resistant
    - Apply sunscreen generously and reapply every 2 hours or after swimming/excessive sweating
    - Consider using physical barriers whenever possible (eg, hats, shirts with sleeves, avoiding direct sun during peak hours)
    - Do not use tanning beds
    - Avoid sunburns
    - Seek shade and wear protective clothing (eg, hats and long-sleeved garments) if outside for prolonged periods of time or during peak direct sun hours
- Strive for sufficient sleep on a regular basis (SSD-1)
    - Adults: 7-9 hours
    - Adolescents: 8-10 hours
    - Older adults: 7-8 hours
- Follow up with PCP regularly
    - Adhere to age-appropriate and treatment-associated health screening, preventive measures (SIMIN-1), and cancer screening recommendations (NCCN Guidelines for Detection, Prevention, and Risk Reduction)
- Obtain nutrients from food sources rather than relying on dietary supplements
    - Routine use of dietary supplements not recommended for the purposes of cancer control (SSUP-1)

# Multidisciplinary approach

- Utilize clinicians trained in lifestyle medicine, physical therapists, occupational therapists, dietitians, social workers, patient navigators, certified exercise professionals, health coaches, and tobacco treatment specialists to:
    - Assess individual and community-level barriers to meeting the healthy lifestyle recommendations
    - Support patients in developing strategies to overcome challenges throughout the continuum of survivorship care, from diagnosis to long-term survivorship
    - Consider specialty referrals to supportive programs offered by medical centers or the community (eg, Livestrong at the YMCA)
