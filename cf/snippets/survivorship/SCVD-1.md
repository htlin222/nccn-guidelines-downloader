# Cardiovascular effects of cancer treatment

- Cancer treatments can result in diverse cardiovascular issues
    - Cardiomyopathy
    - Hypertension
    - Hyperlipidemia
    - Cardiac arrhythmia
    - Myocardial infarction
    - Carotid stenosis, after head and neck or mantle radiation
    - Cerebrovascular accidents
- See NCCN Guidelines for Management of Immune Checkpoint Inhibitor-Related Toxicities
- See NCCN Guidelines for Management of CAR T-Cell and Lymphocyte Engager-Related Toxicities
- Survivors treated with anthracyclines are at increased risk for heart failure (SCARDIO-1)
- Androgen or estrogen deprivation therapy may elevate cardiovascular risk
- Shared risk factors for both cancer and CVD, eg, smoking, poor health behaviors
    - Contribute to development of CVD and structural heart disease or heart failure
    - Counsel regarding shared risk factors to improve cancer- and cardiovascular-related outcomes

# CVD risk estimation

- Most CVDs, such as atherosclerosis, develop over time as a result of well-defined risk factors
    - Hypertension
    - Hyperlipidemia
    - Use of tobacco products
    - Obesity
    - Diabetes
    - Control of these risk factors can decrease the risk of subsequent cardiovascular events
- Tools that help quantify CVD risk in the general population ___ (risk_tool_used)
    - Some of these tools not validated in cancer populations and do not account for cardiotoxic treatments
    - May be considered to help determine appropriate risk reduction strategies
- Personalized approach to individualize CVD risk estimation for cancer survivors
    - Cardiotoxic cancer treatments associated with increased risk for CVD
    - Traditional risk factors
    - Risk enhancers, eg, diabetic neuropathy, ≥10 years duration of type 2 diabetes
- Specific cardio-oncology risk models are being refined to improve identification of survivors at elevated risk for CVD
- Aggressive risk factor management is recommended for those deemed to be at higher risk

# Referral and shared care

- Referral to a cardiologist or cardio-oncologist ___ (referral_indicated), for patients at elevated CVD risk
    - Should be considered at any stage of the cancer journey
- Counsel on any increased risk of CVD based on prior treatment, comorbidity, or CVD risk factors

- Cooperation and shared care with primary care providers and cardiovascular specialists as needed, key to optimizing cardiac and vascular outcomes
