# Increased risk of subsequent primary cancers

- Overall cancer rate in survivors is higher than in the general population
- Increased risk is due to
    - Genetic susceptibilities (eg, hereditary cancer syndromes) and/or family history
    - Shared etiologic exposures
        - Smoking
        - Environmental exposures
        - Health behaviors
        - Human papillomavirus (HPV)
    - Mutagenic effects of cancer treatment
- Subsequent new primary malignant neoplasms
    - May occur years after treatment, when the survivor's oncologist may no longer be involved in the survivor's care
    - May occur at younger ages than in the general population
- Treatment-related subsequent primary cancers vary with the type and intensity of cancer treatment
    - Associated in particular with RT and specific chemotherapeutic agents

# Screening considerations

- Screening for subsequent primary cancers is a shared responsibility between primary and oncology care physicians
- For survivors living with metastatic disease, tailor screening recommendations to the survivor's individualized risk and disease status
    - See NCCN Guidelines for Detection, Prevention, and Risk Reduction Table of Contents
- Use of radiologic studies to screen for recurrent cancer
    - Excess lifetime radiation exposure from CT imaging may be associated with a mildly increased risk of developing a radiation-associated cancer
    - Base use on diagnosis and evidence that early detection of recurrence will improve cancer-related outcomes
    - Recommendations for surveillance imaging modality and frequency: see NCCN Guidelines for Treatment by Cancer Type
