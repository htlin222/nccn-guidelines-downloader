# Source

- NCCN Survivorship v3.2026, SNWM-1, p54
- General Principles of Nutrition
- Footnotes and references: SNWM-1A
- All recommendations category 2A unless otherwise indicated

# Assessment

- ___ (diagnosis) survivor
- Assess dietary pattern
    - Fruits, vegetables, and whole grains intake
    - Red and processed meats intake
    - Alcohol intake
    - Dietary supplements
    - Processed foods or beverages with added fats and/or sugars
- Assess timing of meals and snacking habits, portion size, frequency of eating out, and use of added fats and/or sugars to foods or beverages
- History of radiation directed to the digestive tract
    - May benefit from more specialized nutritional counseling, swallow therapy, and/or referrals to gastrointestinal specialists
    - See NCCN treatment guidelines (Treatment by Cancer Type) for additional nutrition recommendations

# Encourage — general dietary pattern

- Follow a predominantly nutrient-rich plant-based diet, including vegetables, fruit, beans/legumes, whole grains, nuts, and seeds
    - Portion size guidance: American Institute for Cancer Research (AICR) New American Plate website
    - Healthy recipe resources: American Cancer Society "Find Healthy Recipes" website
- Make informed choices about food to ensure variety and adequate nutrient intake

# Limit

- Red meat (beef, pork, lamb): no more than 18 oz (cooked) per week
    - Best to avoid processed meats (ham, hot dogs, deli cuts, bacon, sausage)
        - Associated with increased risk of colorectal and gastric cancers
- Highly processed foods high in fat, starches, or sugars (chips, cookies, candy bars, desserts, processed baked goods, sugary cereals, fried foods)
    - Highly ("ultra") processed foods: industrial formulations typically with 5 or more ingredients not commonly used in culinary preparations
    - Associated with increased risk of cancer
- Sugar-sweetened beverages
- Refined sugars
    - <6 tsp (25 g) for a 2000-calorie daily diet
    - <9 tsp (38 g) for a 3000-calorie daily diet
    - A 12-oz soft drink has about 10 tsp of sugar
- Alcohol
    - Best to avoid alcohol, as any consumption increases risk for several cancers
    - Data on the effect of alcohol consumption on cancer recurrence is mixed
    - If alcohol is consumed, lower levels are associated with lower risk

# Monitor calorie intake

- Self-monitoring of food and beverage intake is an effective strategy for weight management
- Prolonged periods of fasting may impair adequate caloric and nutrient intake

# Further dietary guidance

- Consider referral to a registered dietitian
- AICR "The New American Plate"
    - Vegetables, fruits, and whole grains should comprise two-thirds or more of the volume of food on the plate
    - Protein should comprise one-third or less of plate

# Recommended sources of dietary components

- Fat: plant sources such as olive or canola oil, avocados, seeds and nuts, and fatty fish
    - Prioritize over saturated fats, used in moderation in the context of weight loss strategies
    - Examples of fatty fish: mackerel, salmon, herring
- Carbohydrates: fruits, vegetables, whole grains, and legumes
- Protein: poultry, fish, legumes, low-fat dairy foods, eggs, nuts, and seeds
- Soy foods
    - Moderate consumption (up to 3 servings per day) beneficial in promoting overall health and survival
    - Strongest evidence for prevention of lung cancer and reduction of breast cancer recurrence
