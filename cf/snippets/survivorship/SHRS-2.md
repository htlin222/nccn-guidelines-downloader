# Non-prostate malignancy survivors

- Received RT, chemotherapy, or surgery for non-prostate malignancy
    - Evaluate for biochemical evidence of hypogonadism
        - Testosterone, free and total
        - Luteinizing hormone (LH)
        - Prolactin
    - Treat with testosterone for hormone-related symptoms

# Prostate cancer survivors

- No evidence of recurrent disease
    - May have symptoms of hypogonadism, or prior history of hypogonadism
    - Evaluate for biochemical evidence of hypogonadism
    - When to initiate treatment for low testosterone, or resume treatment for those with pre-existing hypogonadism, is controversial
        - Coordinate with the patient's PCP (ie, surgeon, oncologist, radiation oncologist)

# Androgen deprivation therapy (ADT)

- Main therapeutic approach to metastatic prostate cancer
- May be used as adjuvant or neoadjuvant therapy in the treatment of prostate cancer
    - Individuals assigned male at birth who have breast cancer may also receive ADT, although rare, and recommendations for vasomotor symptoms may differ
- Survivors receiving ADT may experience hormone-related symptoms and sexual dysfunction
    - Should not receive androgens (eg, testosterone)

# ADT-related symptoms and health risks

- Acute kidney injury
- Fatigue
- Anemia
- Gynecomastia
- Arthralgias/myalgias
- Osteoporosis/bone fractures
- CVD
    - May increase cardiovascular morbidity and mortality, notably in the first 6 months of therapy and in individuals with two or more prior cardiovascular events
    - Increase in serum LDL cholesterol, HDL cholesterol, and triglycerides may also be seen
- Sexual dysfunction
    - Loss of libido, loss of nocturnal and morning erections, and varying degrees of erectile dysfunction
- Prolongation of QT/QTc interval
- Sleep disturbance
- Cognitive dysfunction
- Testicle atrophy
- Decreased muscle (sarcopenia) and increased body fat
- Thinning body hair
    - Facial and body hair decrease; some bald individuals may have some regrowth of scalp hair
- Vasomotor symptoms (ie, hot flashes/night sweats)
    - May be associated with nausea and sweating, and may occur during sleep
- Decreased penile size
- Venous thromboembolic disease
- Mood disturbance and depression
- Diabetes mellitus (new onset)
    - Reduced insulin sensitivity
