- BMI ___ (bmi) kg/m2

# Weight goal by BMI

- BMI between 18.5 and 24.9 kg/m2: encourage survivor to achieve and maintain this range, and strive for metabolic health
    - Weight maintenance should be a priority for survivors who have a BMI between 18.5 and 24.9 kg/m2
        - BMI cannot distinguish fat from muscle and therefore has limitations for its use to determine the recommendation for a survivor to lose or gain weight
        - Consider utilizing waist-to-hip ratios and/or body composition measures to individualize weight management recommendations
        - Data are currently limited on the optimal body composition and waist-to-hip ratios for cancer survivors
- Underweight, with or without disease-related malnutrition: intentional weight gain should be a priority (SNWM-4)
    - The focus of weight gain should be on increasing lean muscle mass
- Overweight/obesity: intentional weight loss should be a priority
    - Weight gain after cancer diagnosis and treatment is common
        - May exacerbate risk for functional decline, comorbidity, and possibly cancer recurrence or death
        - May reduce quality of life

# Assessment beyond BMI

- In conjunction with primary care, survivors should be assessed for metabolic health and body composition independently of BMI

# Weight management approach

- Weight management includes a three-pronged approach
    - Caloric management
    - Physical activity
    - Behavior modification
- Providers should discuss strategies and goal setting for weight management and optimal metabolic health, including how to achieve low overall body fat and higher amounts of muscle mass
    - Practice portion control
    - Make informed food choices through routine evaluation of food labels
    - Incorporate physical activity, particularly strength training, to assure optimal lean body mass (SPA-1)
        - Exercise is especially important for preserving muscle mass and preventing longer term weight regain when taking medications that promote weight loss (eg, glucagon-like peptide-1 [GLP-1] agonists)
    - Monitor weight, diet, calories, and physical activity routines (eg, journaling, mobile phone apps)

# Referral

- Referrals to registered dietitians should be considered, especially those who are Certified Specialists in Oncology Nutrition (CSO) and members of the Oncology Nutrition Dietetic Practice Group of the Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics
    - Many hospitals use CSOs, and those in private practice can be accessed via the Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics locator at www.eatright.org
- There is no current evidence to support the use of weight loss supplements in cancer survivors
