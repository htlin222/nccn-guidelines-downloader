- All recommendations category 2A unless otherwise indicated

# Assessment

- ___ (diagnosis) survivor
- Reports: ___ (sleep_complaint)

# Screening

- Ask at regular intervals, especially when there is a change in clinical status or treatment
    - Problems falling asleep, staying asleep, waking up too early, or poor sleep quality?
    - Excessive sleepiness (sleepiness or falling asleep in inappropriate situations, or sleeping more during a 24-hour period than in the past)?
    - Told that snores frequently or stops breathing during sleep?
    - Additional tools for individual intensive screening: PSQI, PROMIS SLEEP, Epworth Sleepiness Scale
- No concerns for sleep disorder/disturbance
    - Re-evaluate at subsequent visits/post therapy
- Concerns for sleep disorder/disturbance
    - Patients may have more than one sleep disorder

# Assessment of contributing factors

- H&P for concerns of sleep disorder/disturbance
- Comorbidities
    - Alcohol and/or substance use disorder
    - Obesity
    - Cardiac dysfunction
    - Respiratory disorders
    - Endocrine dysfunction (eg, hypothyroidism)
    - Anemia
        - Iron and ferritin levels
        - Transferrin saturation %, when indicated
    - Emotional distress: screen for anxiety and depression (SANXDE-1; NCCN Guidelines for Distress Management)
    - Neurologic disorders, including chemotherapy-induced peripheral neuropathy
    - Psychiatric disorders
- Medications
    - Re-evaluate the need for persistent use of sleep aids, pain medications, antiemetics, stimulants, antidepressants, anti-psychotics, sedative/hypnotics, opioids, OTC sleep aids, or antihistamines
- Vasomotor symptoms (SHRS-4 for menopause, SHRS-6 for ADT)
    - More prevalent in menopausal individuals: insomnia, RLS, sleep apnea
    - Hormonal changes, vasomotor symptoms, and lifestyle contribute to these sleep disturbances
    - For menopausal survivors with insomnia associated with vasomotor symptoms, treatment of vasomotor symptoms can improve sleep
    - Best evidence for improving sleep dysfunction related to vasomotor symptoms is use of MHT
    - Other strategies can be considered for patients unable to be on MHT (SHRS-4)
- Review sleep/wake timing and/or sleep log/diary
- Review caffeine intake
- Review history of cancer treatments
- Pain (SPAIN-1)
- Fatigue (SFAT-1)
- Shift work
- Current coping strategies (eg, relaxation techniques, meditation)

# Insomnia symptoms or circadian rhythm disorders

- Insomnia symptoms: difficulty falling asleep, staying asleep, or waking up too early
    - Duration ≥3 months
    - Occurring at least 3 times per week
- Circadian rhythm disorders
- In the differential diagnosis, consider parasomnias or circadian rhythm sleep-wake disorders and referral to a sleep specialist

# Sleep disturbance and/or excessive sleepiness

- Hypersomnias
- Sleep disordered breathing, includes
    - Obstructive sleep apnea (most common)
    - Central sleep apnea
    - May also present with symptoms of insomnia, as can RLS, circadian rhythm sleep-wake disorders, and parasomnias
- RLS
    - Also known as Willis-Ekbom disease
    - May also present with symptoms of insomnia
- In the differential diagnosis, consider parasomnias or circadian rhythm sleep-wake disorders and referral to a sleep specialist
