# At time of cancer diagnosis

- Discuss risks of treatment-induced infertility with all reproductive-aged survivors
- Document in the medical chart
    - Survivor's goals regarding fertility
    - Discussion regarding the risks of treatment-induced infertility

# Prior to initiation of cancer treatment

- Discuss available options for fertility preservation, for those patients wishing to preserve fertility
- Refer to the appropriate specialists, for those patients wishing to preserve fertility
    - Fertility preservation procedures prior to cancer treatment are the most effective way to preserve fertility in cancer survivors
    - Fertility cryopreservation procedures
        - In vitro fertilization (IVF) with oocyte and/or embryo cryopreservation
        - Ovarian tissue cryopreservation
            - Currently only about 40% successful in centers with this expertise
        - Sperm cryopreservation
    - Much greater success with traditional IVF with oocyte or embryo cryopreservation than with ovarian tissue cryopreservation
    - Offer GnRH agonists during chemotherapy to breast cancer survivors, to preserve ovarian function
- Shield reproductive organs during RT, if possible

# After aggressive cancer treatment, without prior fertility preservation

- Ovarian tissue cryopreservation after aggressive cancer treatment can result in viable tissue and live births following transplantation (emerging evidence)
- Testicular sperm extraction and intracytoplasmic sperm injection can help posttreatment survivors with minimal sperm achieve pregnancy

# If cancer treatment changes

- Discuss the impact of treatment on potential infertility again
- Refer to the appropriate specialists

# Once cancer treatment is complete

- Do not assume the survivor is infertile
- Survivors interested in fertility should be assessed by a fertility specialist

# Pregnancy after breast cancer

- Pregnancy is considered safe for most survivors of breast cancer
- The hormonal environment associated with pregnancy is not thought to increase the risk of breast cancer recurrence
- Prior breast cancer treatment does not increase the future risk of congenital malformations
- General recommendations have traditionally been to wait until the survivor is disease free for 2 years before attempting to conceive, whether naturally or via assisted reproductive technologies, because of the higher risk of recurrence within that time

# Additional information

- For additional information regarding fertility preservation for patients with cancer, see NCCN Guidelines for Adolescent and Young Adult (AYA) Oncology
- For additional information regarding fertility preservation for patients with cancer, see NCCN Guidelines for Breast Cancer
