# Source

- NCCN Survivorship v3.2026, SSD-5, p149
- Survivorship: SYMPTOM — Sleep disturbance and/or Excessive sleepiness
- In the differential diagnosis of insomnia or excessive sleepiness symptoms, consider parasomnias or circadian rhythm sleep wake disorders and referral to a sleep specialist
- For other less frequent syndromes, refer to a sleep specialist
- General Sleep Hygiene Measures: SSD-A
- Essential Diagnostic Criteria for Restless Legs Syndrome: SSD-D
- All recommendations category 2A unless otherwise indicated

# Associated with insufficient sleep time

- Testing: sleep log or diary
- Diagnosis: insufficient sleep syndrome
- Treatment
    - Increase time for sleep
    - Sleep hygiene education (SSD-A)

# Associated with observed apneas, snoring

- STOP-Bang Questionnaire may be used to help identify individuals at high risk for obstructive sleep apnea
- Testing
    - Refer to sleep specialist or PCP for further evaluation, or
    - Sleep study
        - In-laboratory polysomnography or home sleep study
        - Survivors with certain medical disorders (ie, cardiac, respiratory, neurologic), or currently on opiates for cancer-related pain, may not be good candidates for home sleep studies
- Diagnosis: sleep disordered breathing
- Treatment
    - Refer to sleep specialist or PCP
        - Most common medical treatment for sleep disordered breathing is continuous positive airway pressure (CPAP)
    - Weight loss (HL-1)
        - Tirzepatide is a GLP-1 agonist FDA approved for sleep apnea in adults with BMI ≥30 kg/m2 and can be considered in cancer survivors
    - Exercise (SPA-1)

# Restless legs syndrome (RLS)

- RLS is also known as Willis-Ekbom disease
- Associated with uncomfortable sensation
- Testing
    - H&P (SSD-D) and evaluate for iron deficiency, or
    - Refer to sleep specialist or PCP for further evaluation
- Diagnosis: RLS
- Treatment — management options
    - Iron replacement as clinically indicated
    - Initial preferred therapy
        - Gabapentin enacarbil
        - Gabapentin
    - Dopamine agonists
    - Opioids
    - Clonazepam
    - Sleep hygiene education (SSD-A)
    - Refer to a sleep specialist or PCP

# Excessive daytime sleepiness not associated with other symptoms

- Associated with
    - Prolonged wakefulness or awakenings
    - Prolonged nocturnal sleep (ie, >9 hours for adults)
    - Cataplexy, frequent short naps, vivid dreams, disrupted sleep, or sleep paralysis
        - Cataplexy: sudden loss of muscle tone, typically triggered by strong emotions, such as laughter or anger
        - Cataplexy is the most specific diagnostic feature of narcolepsy
- Treatment: refer to a sleep specialist
