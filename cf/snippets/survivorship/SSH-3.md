# Source

- NCCN Survivorship v3.2026, SSH-3, p138
- Applies to cisgender male survivors with concerns/issues regarding sexual health
- Sexual function and management of hormone-related symptoms are important aspects of quality of life for all cancer survivors
    - Recommendations here are mainly based on data in cisgender survivors, but should be followed for gender diverse survivors as applicable, with the involvement of the appropriate health care specialists
- All cancer survivors with sexual dysfunction should be screened for cardiovascular risk factors (SCVD-1)
- All recommendations category 2A unless otherwise indicated

# Assessment

- Symptom(s) reported ___ (sexual_symptom)
- Follow-up status ___ (follow_up_status)

# Erectile dysfunction

- Oral phosphodiesterase type 5 (PDE5) inhibitors as needed, if not contraindicated
    - Dosing should be titrated to optimal effect
- Testosterone therapy may be indicated
    - If total morning testosterone ___ (morning_testosterone) <300 ng/dL, repeat second morning total testosterone and free testosterone, LH, and prolactin
    - Use with caution in individuals with breast cancer
    - Contraindicated in metastatic or active prostate cancer
    - Should not be prescribed to those currently trying to conceive
    - Long-term impact on spermatogenesis should be discussed with patients interested in future fertility
- Daily low-dose oral PDE5 inhibitors, if not contraindicated
- Lifestyle modification (HL-1), eg
    - Increased physical activity
    - Smoking cessation
    - Reduction of alcohol consumption
    - Weight loss if obese
- Pelvic physical therapy

# Low or lack of desire, libido, or intimacy

- Refer to appropriate health care provider
- Testosterone therapy may be indicated

# Problems with ejaculation (premature, absent, delayed, or climacturia)

- Testosterone therapy may be indicated
- Psychological evaluation
- SSRIs (ie, paroxetine, sertraline, citalopram, fluoxetine) dosed daily
- Clomipramine dosed on demand
- Pelvic physical therapy
- For climacturia
    - Empty bladder prior to sex
    - Pelvic physical therapy
    - Use of condoms to catch urine

# Problems with orgasm (eg, less intensity, difficulty achieving, pain)

- Testosterone therapy may be indicated
- Vibratory therapy
- PDE5 inhibitors, if not contraindicated
- Cabergoline
- Pelvic physical therapy

# Global symptoms of distress, anxiety (generalized or about sex), depression, or other psychological concerns

- Refer to appropriate health care provider for further evaluation, workup, and/or treatment
    - Primary care
    - Urology
    - Psychology, may include couples counseling
    - Sexual health specialist
        - May come from a range of backgrounds, including primary care, gynecology, urology, oncology, psychology, and/or rehabilitation medicine
        - Refer to an appropriate local resource for further sexual health evaluation when possible
    - Sex therapist, if available
        - Referral to a sex therapist certified by the AASECT

# Next

- Concerns regarding sexual function improved or resolved: re-evaluate at regular intervals
- Ongoing concerns regarding sexual function, or multiple issues identified: go to SANXDE-1
