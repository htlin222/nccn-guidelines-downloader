# Assessment

- Insomnia disorder that is problematic, causing
    - Decreased daytime functioning

    - Distress to patient

# Evaluate for and address comorbid causes

- Other sleep disorders
- Medical
- Neurologic
- Psychiatric (SANXDE-1)
- Pain (SPAIN-1)
- Vasomotor symptoms
    - Related to menopause, see SHRS-4
    - Related to ADT, see SHRS-6
    - Sleep disorders such as insomnia, RLS, and sleep apnea are more prevalent in menopausal individuals
    - Hormonal changes, vasomotor symptoms, and lifestyle contribute to these sleep disturbance(s)
    - For menopausal survivors for whom insomnia is associated with vasomotor symptoms, treatment of vasomotor symptoms can improve sleep, which can in turn help with vasomotor symptoms
    - Best evidence for improving sleep dysfunction related to vasomotor symptoms is for use of MHT
    - Other strategies could be considered for patients unable to be on MHT (SHRS-4)
- Shift work
- Medications that may cause insomnia
- Evaluate environmental causes and sleep hygiene (SSD-A)
- Circadian rhythm sleep wake disorder
    - A chronic or recurrent pattern of sleep-wake rhythm disruption due primarily to
        - An alteration of the circadian timing system, or
        - A misalignment with the sleep-wake schedule required by an individual's physical environment or social/work schedules

# Treatment for insomnia

- CBT-I (preferred)
    - Preferred over pharmacologic interventions as first-line therapy (SSD-B)
- Mindfulness
    - Strategies such as tai chi and mindfulness therapy may be beneficial
- Multicomponent behavioral and cognitive strategies
- Sleep hygiene education as part of a multicomponent approach (SSD-A)
    - Sleep hygiene alone has not been shown to be effective, but should be part of the initial treatment of all survivors with sleep disorders and as a prevention strategy for insomnia disorder
    - Sleep hygiene alone is not the recommended treatment for insomnia, but should be used in conjunction with other treatments such as CBT-I and/or pharmacotherapy
    - Use of general sleep hygiene measures should not delay other interventions or referral to a specialist, especially if quality of life is impacted or if sleep problems (eg, insomnia) are severe
- Pharmacologic intervention, if safe, for
    - Difficulty in falling asleep
    - Difficulty maintaining sleep
    - See SSD-C for principles for choosing an FDA-approved hypnotic
- Refer to sleep specialist or PCP for chronic or refractory symptoms (>=3 months)

# Treatment for circadian rhythm sleep wake disorder

- Sleep hygiene education as part of a multicomponent approach (SSD-A)
- Refer to sleep specialist or PCP
