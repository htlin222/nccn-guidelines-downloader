# Screening

- Inquire at regular intervals about
    - Swelling or feeling of heaviness, fatigue, or fullness
    - Frequency and severity of swelling
    - Swelling, tightness, or uncomfortable sensation that interferes with daily activities
    - Pain/discomfort
    - Range of motion and mobility (ie, bending, stretching, flexibility)
    - Strength
- Perform clinical examination, which may include, but is not limited to
    - Range of motion
    - Muscle performance
    - Circulation
    - Sensation
    - Hemodynamic functioning
    - Functional mobility
- If available, obtain comprehensive objective measurements to identify early signs of lymphedema
    - Tools may include perometry, 3D camera or tape measure/girth measure converted to volumetric measures, or bioimpedance spectroscopy
    - Result: ___ (objective_measurement)
    - In the PREVENT trial, postoperative breast cancer survivors underwent up to 36 months of surveillance; those with subclinical lymphedema treated with a compression intervention had only 16% progression to clinical lymphedema

# Workup if lymphedema is suspected

- Rule out
    - Recurrence of cancer
    - Infection
    - Deep vein thrombosis (DVT) of an extremity
- Refer to a certified lymphedema therapist (if available) for assessments such as
    - Subjective symptoms/signs
    - Limb volume measurement: ___ (limb_volume)
        - Preoperative baseline measurement recommended; misdiagnosis will occur up to 50% of the time in absence of baseline measurements
        - If baseline measurements are not available, emphasis should be on clinical exam and symptoms alongside objective measurements, using the unaffected limb as a reference
    - Clinical examination, which may include, but is not limited to
        - Range of motion
        - Muscle performance
        - Circulation
        - Sensation
        - Hemodynamic monitoring
        - Functional mobility
- Lymphoscintigraphy, if clinically indicated
    - Result: ___ (lymphoscintigraphy_result)
- Assess distress (SANXDE-1)
- Certified lymphedema therapists can be identified using resources from the Lymphology Association of North America (LANA) and the National Lymphedema Network (NLN)
    - NCCN recommends attention to evidence-based practice and specialized training for lymphedema management

# Treatment

- Survivor lymphedema education, including self-care management, skin care, and self-bandage (SLYMPH-A)
- Refer to certified lymphedema therapist (if available) for consideration of
    - Compression
        - Fit for compression garments
            - Compression garments should be prescribed, optimally fitted and measured by a certified lymphedema therapist
        - Review use of garments
        - Pneumatic compression for ongoing home management
        - Review use of multilayered bandage wrapping
    - Progressive resistance training under supervision
        - If a certified therapist is not available, survivors with lymphedema can perform resistance training with a professional trainer who has knowledge of cancer-related physical activity principles
        - Weights should be slowly progressed as tolerated, and lymphedema should be evaluated periodically
        - Aerobic exercise or other forms of physical activity as tolerated (SLYMPH-B)
    - Manual lymphatic drainage
        - If a certified lymphedema therapist is not available, consider referral to appropriate provider for treatment
- Refer to qualified therapist for range-of-motion exercises
    - Trained personnel can include physical and occupational therapists, certified exercise professionals, and physical medicine and rehabilitation clinicians
- Consider early referral to a lymphedema surgeon, in consultation with a certified lymphedema therapist and/or physiatrist specializing in lymphedema
