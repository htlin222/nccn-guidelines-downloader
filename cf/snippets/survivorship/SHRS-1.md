# Menopause status

- Many survivors may experience symptoms whether or not they have ovarian function
- If prior chemotherapy, pelvic radiation exposure, or on tamoxifen
    - Serial estradiol levels may be useful to confirm current menopausal status
- Peri- or premenopausal survivors
    - If amenorrheic and later develop bleeding
        - Serial estradiol levels can be useful to determine return of ovarian function
    - Other markers may provide additional information on ovarian status in survivors with prior chemotherapy or on tamoxifen, but alone are not reliable to ensure menopausal status
        - Follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH)
        - Anti-Mullerian hormone (AMH)
        - Inhibin
    - If amenorrheic and sexually active
        - Counsel on the need for contraception to prevent unintended pregnancy, if they do not meet the definition of menopause and their sexual activity could result in pregnancy
- Menopause is defined as
    - No menses for one year, in the absence of prior chemotherapy or tamoxifen use
    - Or no menses after surgical removal of all ovarian tissue
- In non-cancer populations, primary ovarian insufficiency or early menopause may be associated with specific menopause-related health risks
    - Limited data in cancer survivors

# Menopausal signs and symptoms

- Vasomotor symptoms (ie, hot flashes/night sweats)
- Vaginal dryness
- Urogenital symptoms
- Sexual dysfunction
- Sleep disturbance
    - Sleep disorders such as insomnia, restless legs syndrome (RLS), and sleep apnea are more prevalent in menopausal individuals
    - Hormonal changes, vasomotor symptoms, and lifestyle contribute to these sleep disturbances
    - For general approaches to treatment, refer to the Sleep Disorders algorithm (SSD-1)
    - Treatment of sleep apnea and RLS is the same for menopausal survivors as for other survivors (SSD-1)
    - For menopausal survivors for whom insomnia is associated with vasomotor symptoms, treatment of vasomotor symptoms can improve sleep, which can in turn help with vasomotor symptoms
    - Best evidence for improving sleep dysfunction related to vasomotor symptoms is use of menopausal hormone therapy (MHT)
    - Other agents could be considered for survivors unable to be on MHT (SHRS-4)
- Mood disturbance and depression
- Cognitive dysfunction
- Arthralgias/myalgias
- Fatigue

# Menopause-related health risks

- Osteoporosis/bone fractures
- CVD
- Cognitive change

# Treatment options for vasomotor symptoms (SHRS-4)

- Hormonal therapies
    - Relatively contraindicated in survivors of hormonally mediated cancers
    - Use with caution in those with increased genetic cancer risk
    - Combination estrogen and progestins, for survivors with an intact uterus
    - Or estrogen alone, for survivors without a uterus
- Non-hormonal options (SHRS-B)
    - Prescription alternatives (SHRS-A)
    - OTC options
    - Integrative therapies
- Lifestyle modifications (HL-1)
- Survivors often use herbal supplements for vasomotor symptom management
    - Some supplements may interfere with hormonal cancer treatments
    - Routine use of supplements is not recommended (SSUP-1)
    - Providers should encourage survivors to discuss such therapies prior to use
