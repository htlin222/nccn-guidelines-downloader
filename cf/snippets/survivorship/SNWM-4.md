# Weight gain

- Modification of diet and dietary components should be done on an individual basis
- Manage contributing treatment effects and risk factors as clinically indicated
    - Dental health and risk factors for poor oral intake
    - Swallowing disorder, taste/smell disorders, and GI motility as appropriate
    - Offer smoking cessation assistance as appropriate (NCCN Guidelines for Smoking Cessation)
    - Contributing psychosocial factors (SANXDE-1)
    - Barriers to access of healthy food such as living too far from grocery store, lack of transportation, or lack of resources to acquire and prepare food
- Discuss increasing frequency of feeding and portion size
- Discuss avoiding fluid intake with meals
- Encourage foods that are both high in calories and nutrient-dense (eg, avocados, nuts)
- Consider referral to registered dietitian for individualized counseling
- Monitor weight regularly
- Consider appetite stimulants only after other interventions have been determined ineffective

# Weight maintenance

- Reinforce maintenance of healthy body weight throughout lifetime
- Monitor weight regularly
    - Daily monitoring has been shown to be associated with improved weight loss
- Limit foods that are high in calories, particularly those that are highly processed, with relatively few nutrients such as sugar-sweetened beverages and foods with high amounts of fats and sugars
    - Highly processed foods are industrial formulations typically with ≥5 and usually many ingredients (eg, soft drinks, sweet or savory packaged snacks, reconstituted meat products such as sausage or chicken nuggets, prepared frozen dishes)
    - Consumption of highly processed foods is associated with an increased risk of cancer
- Practice portion control through plate and serving size awareness
- Promote regular physical activity (SPA-1)

# Weight loss

- Manage contributing treatment effects and risk factors as clinically indicated
    - Contributing psychosocial factors, including depression (SANXDE-1)
    - Barriers to access of healthy food such as living too far from grocery store, lack of transportation, or lack of resources to acquire and prepare food
- Monitor weight regularly
    - Daily monitoring has been shown to be associated with improved weight loss
- Recommend weight loss of no more than 2 lb per week
    - No more than 1 lb per week in survivors >64 years
- Limit foods that are high in calories, particularly those that are highly processed, with relatively few nutrients such as sugar-sweetened beverages and foods with high amounts of fats and sugars
    - Highly processed foods are industrial formulations typically with ≥5 and usually many ingredients (eg, soft drinks, sweet or savory packaged snacks, reconstituted meat products such as sausage or chicken nuggets, prepared frozen dishes)
    - Consumption of highly processed foods is associated with an increased risk of cancer
- Substitute high-calorie foods with low-calorie, nutrient-dense foods such as water-rich/low-starch vegetables, broth-based soups, whole grains, fresh fruits for desserts, and beverages such as water, unsweetened tea, and black coffee
- Practice portion control by using smaller plates and restricting intake to one serving
- Promote regular physical activity (SPA-1)
- Focus on a whole food, plant forward diet such as the Mediterranean diet, including a variety of vegetables, fruits, whole grains, lean animal proteins, and plant proteins such as beans, legumes, whole soy foods, nuts, and seeds
    - Recommended portion sizes: see AICR New American Plate resource
- Refer to community-based weight loss resources and PCP
- Refer to registered dietitian or weight management programs for individualized help as needed
    - Strongly consider for survivors with negligible weight loss from diet and exercise interventions
- Consider evaluation for bariatric surgery or pharmacologic therapy as appropriate
    - Safety and efficacy of these drugs in cancer survivors is unknown
    - Lifestyle modifications are preferred over pharmacologic therapy
