# Treatment response assessment

- Imaging of involved sites
    - FDG-PET/CT scan vertex to toes (preferred)
    - Or contrast-enhanced CT/MRI, as indicated by the initial organ of involvement
    - Grade the result against the radiographic response criteria (ADULCH-4A)
    - For LCH without radiographic abnormalities (eg, skin, gastrointestinal tract), use clinical criteria instead
        - Physical exam
        - Endoscopy
- Timing of response assessment
    - After 2–3 cycles of systemic therapy, and at completion
    - After completion of surgical curettage
    - After RT, per Principles of Radiation Therapy (HIST-G)

# Surveillance of patients on observation or in remission

- H&P and labs at least annually, or more frequently as clinically indicated
- Imaging
    - FDG-PET/CT scan vertex to toes (preferred)
    - Or contrast-enhanced CT/MRI, as indicated by the initial organ of involvement
    - No more than every 6 months for up to 2 years post completion of treatment
    - Beyond 2 years, no more than annually
    - For patients who are asymptomatic with a single-site bone lesion, imaging surveillance can potentially end after year 1, with continued tracking of symptoms
- Monitor PFTs every 6–12 months for up to 2 years for pulmonary LCH, with further follow-up as clinically indicated
    - Single-system pulmonary LCH with abnormal PFTs at baseline should undergo follow-up testing to assess response
- Bone marrow evaluation in the presence of unexplained CBC abnormality
    - To rule out hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis, LCH, and/or associated myeloid neoplasm
- For patients on BRAF inhibitors
    - Regular skin examination
    - Echocardiogram, per Principles of Targeted Therapy (HIST-F)
- For patients on MEK inhibitors
    - Regular skin examination
    - Retinal examination
    - Echocardiogram, per Principles of Targeted Therapy (HIST-F)
- Monitor every 1–2 years for pituitary hormone abnormalities
    - Endocrinopathies from pituitary involvement may require ongoing hormone replacement despite attaining a radiographic complete response

# Treatment for relapsed/refractory disease

- Systemic therapy (HIST-D)
- If duration of response >1 year, consider the same regimen
- Otherwise use a regimen not used for first-line therapy
