# Source

- NCCN Histiocytic Neoplasms v2.2026, MHN-1, p27
- Applies to malignant histiocytic neoplasm (MHN), workup / evaluation
- Includes histiocytic sarcoma, Langerhans cell sarcoma, and interdigitating dendritic cell sarcoma
- Covers both primary MHN and secondary MHN
- Refer to the NCCN Distress Thermometer and Problem List, which includes social determinants of health
    - See NCCN Guidelines for Distress Management (DIS-A)
- All recommendations category 2A unless otherwise indicated

- Primary or secondary MHN ___ (mhn_type)
    - Primary MHN refers to de novo disease without prior or concomitant hematologic malignancy
    - Secondary MHN refers to prior or concomitant hematologic malignancy
    - A subset of MHN are clonally related to other hematolymphoid neoplasms (secondary MHN)
    - Secondary MHN may present after, concurrently with, or, rarely, before diagnosis of the clonally related hematolymphoid neoplasm
    - The majority of clonally related hematolymphoid neoplasms recorded to date are lymphomas, predominantly mature B cell lymphomas, but also mature T and B lymphoblastic leukemias/lymphoma

# Essential workup

- History and physical examination (H&P), with attention to common sites of involvement
    - See Principles of Diagnostic Evaluation (HIST-A) for details on H&P
    - Common sites of involvement: lymph nodes
    - Common sites of involvement: bone
    - Common sites of involvement: liver
    - Common sites of involvement: spleen
    - Common sites of involvement: bone marrow
    - Common sites of involvement: CNS
    - Common sites of involvement: lung
    - Common sites of involvement: skin
- Whole-body FDG-PET/CT scan including distal extremities (vertex to toes)
- Laboratory evaluation
    - See Principles of Diagnostic Evaluation (HIST-A) for details on laboratory evaluation
- Tissue biopsy, for tissue biopsy analysis see MHN-1A
    - Pathology should be sent to a center of excellence for confirmation of diagnosis due to the rarity of the disease
    - See Principles of Pathology (HIST-C)

# Useful under certain circumstances, based on symptoms or organ involvement

- CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis with contrast, if additional structural details are needed
- Brain MRI with and without contrast
- Consider bone marrow biopsy
    - For children
    - For adults with unexplained CBC abnormalities
    - For marrow uptake on FDG-PET/CT
    - To assess for MHN involvement, lymphoma, or other concomitant hematolymphoid neoplasm
    - See Principles of Pathology (HIST-C)
    - See NCCN Guidelines for different lymphoma subtypes
- Refer to radiation therapy (RT) based on symptoms
    - See Principles of Radiation Therapy (HIST-G)
- Refer to palliative care based on symptoms
    - See NCCN Guidelines for Palliative Care
- Refer to reproductive endocrinology
    - See NCCN Guidelines for Adolescent and Young Adult (AYA) Oncology
- Refer to psychosocial supportive services upon diagnosis
