# Essential workup

- History and physical examination (H&P), with attention to common sites of involvement
    - See Principles of Diagnostic Evaluation (HIST-A) for details on H&P
    - Long bones: bilateral and symmetric diaphyseal and metaphyseal osteosclerosis with subchondral sparing
    - Orbits: retro-orbital mass with exophthalmos; xanthelasma
    - CNS: pituitary gland, posterior fossa
    - Lungs: interstitial changes
    - Vascular: periaortic infiltrate; pericardium; right atrium
    - Retroperitoneal / perinephric or "hairy kidney" (mesentery)
    - See Characteristic Features of Histiocytic Neoplasms (HIST-B)
- Whole-body FDG-PET/CT scan including distal extremities (vertex to toes)
- Laboratory evaluation
    - See Principles of Diagnostic Evaluation (HIST-A) for details on laboratory evaluation
    - If unexplained complete blood count (CBC) abnormality, consider bone marrow biopsy
        - Assess for hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis, ECD, and/or associated myeloid neoplasm
        - See NCCN Guidelines for Myelodysplastic Syndromes or NCCN Guidelines for Myeloproliferative Neoplasms
- Tissue biopsy, for analysis see ECD-1A
    - See Principles of Pathology (HIST-C)
    - If biopsy is not possible because of location or risk factors, mutational analysis of the peripheral blood is an option
- MRI of the brain with and without contrast

# Useful under certain circumstances, based on symptoms or organ involvement

- CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis with contrast
    - Hairy kidney is highly suggestive of ECD and is present in 50% to 68% of patients
    - Periarterial infiltration involving the thoracic or abdominal aorta and other vessels is present in 56% to 85% of patients with ECD
- CT of the sinuses with contrast
- CT of the chest (high-resolution)
- MRI with and without contrast of the orbit
- MRI with and without contrast of the spine
- MRI with and without contrast of the sella turcica, with or without the pituitary
    - If arginine vasopressin (AVP) deficiency is suspected
    - AVP deficiency was previously known as diabetes insipidus
- Pulmonary function tests (PFTs), especially for suspected pulmonary Erdheim-Chester disease
- Transthoracic echocardiogram (TTE)
- Renal artery ultrasound (US)
- Testicular ultrasound (US)
- Technetium-99m (99mTc) methylene diphosphonate (MDP) bone scintigraphy
