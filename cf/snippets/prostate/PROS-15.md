# Workup for metastases

- Perform physical exam
- Perform imaging for staging
    - Principles of Imaging (PROS-E)
- Perform and/or collect PSA and calculate PSADT
- Estimate life expectancy
    - Principles of Life Expectancy Estimation (PROS-A)
- Perform germline and somatic genetic testing, if not previously done
    - Principles of Genetics and Molecular/Biomarker Analysis (PROS-C)
- Obtain family history
    - Principles of Genetics and Molecular/Biomarker Analysis (PROS-C)
- Assess quality-of-life measures
    - Principles of Quality of Life and Shared Decision-Making (PROS-D)
- ADT alone (PROS-G) or observation are recommended for asymptomatic patients with metastatic disease or M0 CRPC and life expectancy ≤5 years

# Confirm the disease is high volume

- Synchronous or metachronous high-volume metastases
- High-volume disease in this setting is defined based on CHAARTED criteria
    - Presence of visceral metastasis, or
    - ≥4 bone lesions with ≥1 beyond the vertebral bodies and pelvis

# ADT backbone, applies to every regimen below

- ADT is strongly recommended in combination therapy for metastatic castration-sensitive disease
- ADT monotherapy in metastatic castration-sensitive disease is discouraged, unless there are clear contraindications to combination therapy
    - If ADT monotherapy is given, intermittent ADT can be considered to reduce toxicity
- For details on the use of ADT and ARPIs, see Principles of Androgen Deprivation Therapy (PROS-G) and Discussion

# ADT with docetaxel and one of the following

- Preferred
    - Abiraterone (category 1)
    - Darolutamide (category 1)
- Other Recommended
    - Apalutamide (category 2B)
    - Enzalutamide (category 2B)

# ADT with one of the following

- Preferred
    - Abiraterone (category 1)
    - Apalutamide (category 1)
    - Enzalutamide (category 1)
- Other Recommended
    - Darolutamide

# ADT with, useful in certain circumstances

- Niraparib/abiraterone, BRCA2m only
    - Can be used following the completion of docetaxel in patients receiving triplet therapy, or following EBRT to the primary tumor
    - Use of this combination in patients with low-volume disease is controversial, because 78% of the patients in the trial had high-volume disease and there were very few patients with low-volume metachronous disease in particular
    - This regimen adds considerable toxicity, and patients with low-volume disease should be informed that the benefit in this setting is not clear

# ADT with capivasertib/abiraterone

- Capivasertib/abiraterone, in very select patients with PTEN deficiency only (category 2B)
    - FDA-approved for adults with PTEN-deficient mCSPC
    - There is considerable disagreement amongst Panel members regarding the use of this regimen, given the modest rPFS benefit, lack of OS benefit, considerable toxicity, and lack of comparison to triplet therapy (ie, ADT with docetaxel and an ARPI)
    - Many Panel members advocate waiting for more mature follow-up before using this regimen

# Supportive care to consider

- EBRT to sites of bone metastases can be considered if metastases are in weight-bearing bones, or if the patient is symptomatic
- Bone antiresorptive therapy is indicated for elevated fracture risk based upon FRAX in the castration-sensitive setting (PROS-B)

# Monitoring

- Physical examination + PSA every 3–6 mo
- Imaging for symptoms
    - Principles of Imaging (PROS-E)
- Periodic imaging to monitor treatment response
