- NCCN Prostate Cancer v6.2026, PROS-13, p29
- Applies to metachronous oligometastatic CSPC
- Castration-sensitive defines disease in patients who have not been treated with ADT, and those who are not on ADT at the time of progression
    - The NCCN Prostate Cancer Panel uses the term castration-sensitive even when patients have had neoadjuvant, concurrent, or adjuvant ADT as part of RT, provided they have recovered testicular function

# Workup for metastases

- Perform physical exam
- Perform imaging for staging
    - Principles of Imaging (PROS-E)
- Perform and/or collect PSA and calculate PSADT
- Estimate life expectancy
    - Principles of Life Expectancy Estimation (PROS-A)
- Perform germline and somatic genetic testing, if not previously done
    - Principles of Genetics and Molecular/Biomarker Analysis (PROS-C)
- Obtain family history
    - Principles of Genetics and Molecular/Biomarker Analysis (PROS-C)
- Assess quality-of-life measures
    - Principles of Quality of Life and Shared Decision-Making (PROS-D)
- ADT alone (PROS-G) or observation are recommended for asymptomatic patients with metastatic disease or M0 CRPC and life expectancy less than or equal to 5 years

# Treatment

- MDT with or without ADT
    - See Principles of MDT (PROS-M)
    - For details on the use of ADT and ARPIs, see Principles of Androgen Deprivation Therapy (PROS-G) and Discussion
    - ADT is strongly recommended in combination therapy for metastatic castration-sensitive disease
    - The use of ADT monotherapy in metastatic castration-sensitive disease is discouraged, unless there are clear contraindications to combination therapy
        - If ADT monotherapy is given, intermittent ADT can be considered to reduce toxicity (PROS-G)
- Or, for M1 only, treat as metachronous low-volume M1 (see PROS-14)
- EBRT to sites of bone metastases can be considered if metastases are in weight-bearing bones, or if the patient is symptomatic
- Bone antiresorptive therapy is indicated for elevated fracture risk based upon FRAX in the castration-sensitive setting (PROS-B)

# Monitoring

- Physical examination + PSA every 3-6 mo
- Imaging for symptoms
    - Principles of Imaging (PROS-E)
- Consider periodic imaging to monitor treatment response
