# General considerations

- Assess the patient's values and goals in context with the items above (OAO-3)
- Low social support
    - There are data to suggest correlation between low social support and a higher risk for mortality
    - In patients with low levels of social support, consider referral to social work and/or case management, to explore home supports and community resources
- Offer a shared decision-making tool/framework to guide treatment decisions
- Make an integral part of cancer care in older adults
    - Multidisciplinary team management
    - Patient-specific treatment approach with shared decision-making
    - Palliative/supportive care for symptom management
    - See NCCN Guidelines for Supportive Care and NCCN Guidelines for Palliative Care
- Older adult cancer care is complex
    - Use of multidisciplinary care teams can be a strategy for efficient care delivery
    - Testing of different models of care delivery can be a strategy for efficient care delivery
- Observation may be an appropriate approach in particular clinical scenarios, and may align with the patient's preference

# Age-Friendly Health Systems 4Ms

- What Matters: care is aligned with individual values, goals, and preferences
- Mobility: move safely and maintain function
- Medication: treatment is necessary and non-redundant
- Mentation: prevent, identify, treat, and manage dementia, depression, and delirium

# Surgery

- Chronologic age is not the primary consideration for surgical risk
- All older adults undergoing surgery should undergo an assessment for components of frailty
    - Comorbidities
    - Cognition
    - Mobility
    - Functional status
    - Nutrition
- For patients who are at high risk with multimorbidities and complex health conditions, consider multidisciplinary input with geriatric expertise
- American College of Surgeons (ACS) Geriatric Surgery Verification (GSV) Program
    - Provides a framework for hospitals to take an interdisciplinary approach to continuously optimize surgical care for older adults
    - Includes 32 standards to improve surgical care for older adults
    - Emphasis on goals of care and shared decision-making
    - Emphasis on assessment of geriatric-specific vulnerabilities (eg, cognition, mobility)
    - Emphasis on interdisciplinary postoperative care
- ACS National Surgical Quality Improvement Program (NSQIP) Surgical Risk Calculator
    - Includes both geriatric-specific predictors and geriatric-specific outcomes
    - Can be a useful tool for sharing patient-specific predicted outcomes after surgery
    - Facilitates a more informed discussion regarding risks of surgery
- Postoperative delirium
    - Often preventable, and the most common postoperative complication in older adults
    - American Geriatrics Society (AGS) practice guideline covers risk factors, diagnosis and screening, prevention, medical evaluation, and pharmacologic treatment
    - See OAO-F 2 of 4
- The surgical plan can be altered as necessary to consider nonsurgical or less morbid options
