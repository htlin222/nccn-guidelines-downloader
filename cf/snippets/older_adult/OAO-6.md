# Before initiating cancer-specific treatment

- Assessment of the patient's values and goals with regard to cancer diagnosis completed prior to initiation of cancer-specific treatment
- Supportive and palliative care assessment recommended for any older adult with cancer (OAO-2)
- Age should not be the primary consideration for determining if the use of these therapies is appropriate
- Multidisciplinary evaluation performed to determine eligibility
    - Use appropriate tools (OAO-D)

# Immunotherapy

- No evidence that older patients derive less benefit from immunotherapy compared to younger adults
- Closer and more aggressive follow-up for toxicities may be needed
- See disease-specific NCCN Guidelines for Treatment by Cancer Type
- See NCCN Guidelines for the Management of Immune Checkpoint Inhibitor-Related Toxicities

# Targeted therapy

- Similar clinical benefit has been observed with these therapies in older patients compared to younger adults
- Side effects are not as well understood
    - Patients may require greater supervision
    - Consider earlier and more aggressive management of side effects/toxicities
- Oral targeted therapy monitored closely for adherence
- See NCCN Guidelines for Treatment by Cancer Type

# Cellular therapy / T-cell engagers

- Chimeric antigen receptor (CAR) T-cell therapy has been shown to be an effective therapeutic option for older adults, with similar response rates as younger adults
    - Age should not be a contraindication for using these therapies
- Cellular therapies come with a range of toxicities for older adults
    - Older adults, especially those who are frail, may have a higher incidence of neurologic toxicities
    - Close monitoring required
    - See NCCN Guidelines for the Management of CAR T-Cell and Lymphocyte Engager-Related Toxicities
