# Source

- NCCN Older Adult Oncology v1.2026, OAO-7, p14
- Management of common side effects in older adults undergoing cancer-specific treatment
- Assessment of the patient's values and goals with regard to cancer diagnosis should be completed prior to initiation of cancer-specific treatment
- Supportive and palliative care assessment is recommended for any older adult with cancer (OAO-2)
- All recommendations category 2A unless otherwise indicated

# General principles

- An older patient may have less reserve to manage toxicities
    - Complications may be more clinically significant
    - Complications may require more intense surveillance
- Consider that toxicities may have immediate or even long-term effects that are more significant
- Assess for polypharmacy and use of non-prescribed agents, as they can enhance these side effects
    - 2023 American Geriatrics Society Beers Criteria for Potentially Inappropriate Medication Use in Older Adults
    - See OAO-D

# GI-related, diarrhea, constipation, nausea/vomiting

- See NCCN Guidelines for Antiemesis
- See NCCN Guidelines for Palliative Care

# Mucositis

- Early hospitalization is needed for patients with mucositis who also develop dysphagia/diarrhea
- Provide nutritional support
- See NCCN Task Force: Prevention and Management of Mucositis in Cancer Care

# Bone marrow suppression

- See NCCN Guidelines for Hematopoietic Growth Factors

# Infection/immunosuppression

- See NCCN Guidelines for Prevention and Treatment of Cancer-Related Infections
- Assess if up to date on recommended vaccinations
    - See NCCN Guidelines for Survivorship

# Neurotoxicity

- Monitor hearing loss
    - Avoid neurotoxic agents if significant hearing loss is present
- Monitor cerebellar function if treated with high-dose cytarabine
- Monitor for peripheral neuropathy
- Monitor for cognitive dysfunction (OAO-D)
- Consider polypharmacy (OAO-D)
    - 2023 American Geriatrics Society Beers Criteria for Potentially Inappropriate Medication Use in Older Adults

# Falls

- Periodic assessment of history of falls, balance, and gait difficulties is recommended for all patients, as fall risk may change over time (OAO-E)
- Early and preventive use of durable medical equipment is recommended for patients at high risk for falls
- In-home safety evaluations are recommended for patients at high risk for falls

# Cardiac toxicity

- Monitor for symptomatic or asymptomatic congestive heart failure (CHF)
- Caution with use of anthracyclines
    - Consider alternative treatment dosing schedule, or treatment as appropriate per disease site
    - See NCCN Guidelines for Treatment by Cancer Type
- Caution with use of trastuzumab, among patients with normal left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF)
    - Risk factors for CHF include older age
    - Receipt of an anthracycline-based regimen
    - Baseline LVEF of 50%-54%
    - Coronary artery disease
    - Hypertension
    - Weekly trastuzumab administration
    - See SCARDIO-1, SCARDIO-2, and SCARDIO-3 in the NCCN Guidelines for Survivorship
