# Source

- NCCN Older Adult Oncology v1.2026, OAO-2, p9
- Approach to shared decision-making in the older adult prior to cancer-specific treatment
- Assessment of the patient's values and goals with regard to cancer diagnosis should be completed prior to initiation of cancer-specific treatment
- Supportive and palliative care assessment is recommended for any older adult with cancer
- All recommendations category 2A unless otherwise indicated

# Step 1 - Is the patient a candidate for cancer-specific treatment considering the patient's overall life expectancy?

- Life expectancy calculators are available at www.eprognosis.com
    - These calculators determine anticipated life expectancy independent of the cancer
    - They could be utilized in clinical decision-making to weigh whether the cancer is likely to shorten the patient's life expectancy
    - Or whether the patient is likely to become symptomatic from cancer during anticipated life expectancy
- Life Expectancy of General Population (OAO-A)
- No, not a candidate
    - Symptom management/supportive care (NCCN Guidelines for Palliative Care)
- Yes, a candidate
    - Go to step 2

# Step 2 - Does this patient have decision-making capacity?

- Patients must have the ability to:
    - Understand the relevant information about proposed cancer-specific diagnostic tests or treatment options
    - Appreciate their situation, including their underlying values and current medical situation
    - Use reason to make a decision
    - Communicate a consistent choice (Guidance for the Optimization of Communication with Older Adults, OAO-B)
- No, capacity absent
    - Obtain information from:
        - Patient's proxy
        - Advance directive/advance care planning document
        - Living will
        - Health care power of attorney
        - Clinician's documentation
    - These should be obtained for all patients at the time of treatment initiation
    - This approach can be helpful in reassessing the patient's capacity and preferences throughout therapy
    - Consider family/care coordination meeting
    - Communicate with patient's primary care provider
    - Consider consult from social work, psychology, palliative care (NCCN Guidelines for Palliative Care), or ethics committee
- Yes, capacity present
    - Go to step 3

# Step 3 - Assess preferred role, values and goals

- Assess the patient's preferred role in decision-making
    - This can include the patient's preference for their own role, their caregiver's role, and/or their provider's role in shared decision-making
- Assess the patient's values and goals regarding the management of their cancer
- Are the patient's values and goals consistent with wanting cancer-specific treatment?

# Next

- Values and goals not consistent with wanting cancer-specific treatment, symptom management/supportive care (NCCN Guidelines for Palliative Care)
- Values and goals consistent with wanting cancer-specific treatment, go to pre-treatment evaluation (OAO-3)
