# Source

- NCCN Hematopoietic Growth Factors v3.2026, MGF-2, p8
- Applies to prophylactic use of G-CSFs for febrile neutropenia when the overall febrile neutropenia risk of the regimen is intermediate (10%–20%)
- Febrile neutropenia is defined as
    - Single temperature ≥38.3 °C orally, or ≥38.0 °C over 1 hour
    - And neutropenia: <500 neutrophils/mcL, or <1000 neutrophils/mcL and a predicted decline to ≤500 neutrophils/mcL over the next 48 hours
    - See NCCN Guidelines for Prevention and Treatment of Cancer-Related Infections
- All recommendations category 2A unless otherwise indicated

# Patient risk factors

- Prior chemotherapy or radiation therapy
- Persistent neutropenia
- Bone marrow involvement by tumor
- Recent surgery and/or open wounds
- Liver dysfunction, bilirubin >2.0
- Renal dysfunction, creatinine clearance <50
- Age >65 years receiving full chemotherapy dose intensity
- Other possible patient risk factors for febrile neutropenia may include
    - Poor performance status
    - HIV infection, in particular patients with low CD4 counts
- The listed patient risk factors are based on a multivariable risk model using a prospective cohort study of several thousand ambulatory patients with cancer receiving chemotherapy
    - That cohort did not include patients with HIV, acute leukemia, or hematopoietic cell transplant
- Other factors may warrant the use of G-CSFs
    - Chronic immunosuppression in the post-transplant setting, including organ transplant
- If the regimen is low risk rather than intermediate, G-CSFs may still be considered when the patient has ≥2 patient-related risk factors
    - Use of G-CSF in this setting is based on clinical judgment
