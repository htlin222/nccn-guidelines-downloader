# Source

- NCCN Hematopoietic Growth Factors v3.2026, MGF-4, p10
- Applies to patients who present with febrile neutropenia during the current chemotherapy cycle
- Also applies to patients with radiation-induced myelosuppression following a radiologic/nuclear incident
- The decision to use MGFs in the therapeutic setting is controversial, see Discussion for further details
- An FDA-approved biosimilar is an appropriate substitute for any recommended systemic biologic therapy
- For antibiotic therapy recommendations for fever and neutropenia, see NCCN Guidelines for Prevention and Treatment of Cancer-Related Infections
- All recommendations category 2A unless stated otherwise

# Assessment

- ___ (age) yo, receiving ___ (regimen)
- Temperature ___ (temp)
- ANC ___ (anc) /mcL
- Expected duration of neutropenia ___ (expected_neutropenia_days) days
- Prophylactic G-CSF given this cycle: ___ (prophylactic_gcsf)
- Febrile neutropenia present, both criteria met
    - Single temperature of at least 38.3 °C orally, or at least 38.0 °C over 1 hour
    - Neutropenia: <500 neutrophils/mcL, or <1000 neutrophils/mcL with a predicted decline to 500 neutrophils/mcL or less over the next 48 hours
    - See NCCN Guidelines for Prevention and Treatment of Cancer-Related Infections

# Febrile neutropenia in patients receiving or who received prophylactic G-CSFs

- Receiving daily prophylactic filgrastim or tbo-filgrastim
    - Continue G-CSFs
- Received long-lasting prophylactic pegfilgrastim, eflapegrastim-xnst, or efbemalenograstim alfa-vuxw
    - No additional G-CSF
    - No studies have addressed therapeutic use of filgrastim for febrile neutropenia in patients who have already received prophylactic pegfilgrastim
    - Pharmacokinetic data of pegfilgrastim demonstrated high levels during neutropenia and suggest that additional G-CSFs may not be beneficial
    - In patients with prolonged neutropenia, additional G-CSFs may be considered

# Febrile neutropenia in patients who did not receive prophylactic G-CSFs

- Check risk factors / possible indications for an infection-associated complication
    - Sepsis syndrome
    - Age >65 years
    - ANC <100/mcL
    - Neutropenia expected to be >10 days in duration
    - Pneumonia or other clinically documented infections
    - Invasive fungal infection
    - Hospitalization at the time of fever
    - Prior episode of febrile neutropenia
- No risk factors present
    - No therapeutic myeloid growth factors (MGFs)
- Risk factors present
    - Consider therapeutic MGFs

# Therapeutic MGF selection and dosing

- Pegfilgrastim (or biosimilars), eflapegrastim-xnst, and efbemalenograstim alfa-vuxw have only been studied for prophylactic use
- Filgrastim (or biosimilars), tbo-filgrastim, or sargramostim may be used therapeutically with initial dosing, and discontinued at time of neutrophil recovery
    - Filgrastim (or biosimilars) or tbo-filgrastim: daily dose of 5 mcg/kg
    - Sargramostim: used in clinical trials at a dose of 250 mcg/m2 per day
- Continue therapeutic MGFs until post-nadir ANC recovery to normal or near-normal levels by laboratory standards
- See Discussion for further details

# Radiation-induced myelosuppression following a radiologic/nuclear incident

- Hematopoietic acute radiation syndrome (H-ARS)
    - Therapeutic MGFs
    - Options include filgrastim (or biosimilars), tbo-filgrastim, pegfilgrastim (or biosimilars), sargramostim, eflapegrastim-xnst, and efbemalenograstim alfa-vuxw
