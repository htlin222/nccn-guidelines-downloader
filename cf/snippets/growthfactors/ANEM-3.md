# Source

- NCCN Hematopoietic Growth Factors v3.2026, ANEM-3, p33
- Special categories in considering erythropoiesis-stimulating agent (ESA) use
- All recommendations are category 2A unless otherwise indicated

# Assessment

- Hemoglobin ___ (hb)
- Current cancer therapy ___ (therapy)
- Intent of that therapy ___ (treatment_intent)
- Moderate to severe chronic kidney disease ___ (ckd)
- Other identifiable cause of anemia ___ (other_cause)
- Iron storage and availability ___ (iron_status)
- Patient refuses blood transfusion ___ (refuses_transfusion)
- Previous risk factors for thrombosis ___ (thrombosis_risk)

# Categories in which ESAs may be considered

- Cancer and chronic kidney disease (moderate to severe)
    - Consider ESAs by FDA dosing/dosing adjustments
    - The Hb threshold for treatment and dosing with ESAs is different for chemotherapy-induced anemia and chronic kidney disease
    - For more details on the use of ESAs in patients with cancer and chronic kidney disease, see Discussion
- Patient undergoing palliative treatment
    - Consider based on patient preferences, one of
        - ESAs by FDA dosing/dosing adjustments
        - RBC transfusion per AABB Guidelines
        - Clinical trial
    - For comparison of risks and benefits of ESA use versus RBC transfusion, see Discussion
- Remainder of patients with anemia on myelosuppressive chemotherapy without other identifiable cause of anemia
    - Consider ESAs by FDA dosing/dosing adjustments
    - Evaluation of Iron Deficiency (ANEM-4)
    - For comparison of risks and benefits of ESA use versus RBC transfusion, see Discussion
- Select patients who refuse blood transfusions
    - Management of Cancer- and Chemotherapy-Induced Anemia for Patients Who Refuse Blood Transfusions (ANEM-C)

# Applies to every consideration of ESAs on this page

- Optimal response to ESAs depends on adequate iron storage and availability
- Erythropoietic Therapy - Dosing, Titration, and Adverse Effects (ANEM-A)
- Patients with previous risk factors for thrombosis are at higher risk for thrombosis with the use of ESAs
- If considering use of ESAs, evaluate and counsel patients regarding the risk factors for thrombosis
    - History of thromboembolism
    - Known heritable mutation
    - Hypercoagulability
    - Elevated pre-chemotherapy platelet counts
    - Hypertension
    - Steroids
    - Prolonged immobilization
    - Recent surgery
    - Certain therapies for multiple myeloma
    - Hormonal agents
    - NCCN Guidelines for Cancer-Associated Venous Thromboembolic Disease

# Categories in which ESAs are not recommended

- Patients with cancer not receiving therapy
- Patients receiving non-myelosuppressive therapy
- Patients receiving myelosuppressive chemotherapy with curative intent
    - Examples of cancers for which there is therapy with curative intent
        - Early-stage breast cancer
        - Hodgkin lymphoma
        - Non-Hodgkin lymphomas
        - Testicular cancer
        - Early-stage non-small cell lung cancer
        - Small cell lung cancer
    - A few studies suggest that patients with small cell lung cancer on myelosuppressive chemotherapy may not have an increase in mortality when receiving ESAs
- There is not enough evidence to support ESA use in these patient populations; therefore, ESAs are not recommended at this time
    - In the absence of other indications, eg, advanced chronic kidney disease
