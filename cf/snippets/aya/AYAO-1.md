# Definition of the adolescent and young adult oncology population

- AYA cancers are defined by patient age
    - Individuals diagnosed between 15 and 39 years, based on the 2006 National Cancer Institute (NCI) Progress Review Group
    - See https://www.cancer.gov/types/aya/research/ayao-august-2006.pdf
    - Include the full spectrum of malignancies that arise during this developmental stage
    - Emphasis on the unique clinical, psychosocial, biological, and survivorship needs of this population
- Early-onset (EO) cancers are defined by the occurrence of cancer at a younger-than-expected age
    - Typically before 50 years
    - Most often malignancies traditionally seen in older adults, such as colorectal, breast, pancreatic, and gastric cancers
- How the two differ
    - AYA cancers are a patient-centered, life-stage-based category
    - EO cancers are a disease-centered concept, focused on age-inappropriate presentation and underlying epidemiologic and biologic differences
    - The groups overlap, particularly among patients aged 15 to 39 years
- Shared concerns in both groups, do not ignore
    - Delays in diagnosis
    - Lack of clinical trial representation
    - Psychosocial burdens

# Purpose of the NCCN Guidelines for AYA Oncology

- These are supportive care guidelines, not treatment guidelines
- Purpose
    - Identify and increase awareness of unique issues in AYA oncology
    - Identify resources available to the AYA population
    - Include appropriate tabular materials
    - Make recommendations for patient care
- Unique medical and psychosocial concerns identified by panels of experts, including but not limited to
    - Interpersonal relationships
    - Fertility preservation
    - Parenting
    - Education
    - Employment attainment and retention
- The relative importance of these issues varies markedly across the broad age range defined
- These issues should be considered as part of the overall therapeutic plan for the patient

# Referral and site of care

- Many centers have established AYA centers to accommodate the specific needs of patients with cancer in this age group
- Give consideration to referring the patient to an AYA center of excellence if feasible
    - Available literature demonstrates improved outcomes in patients receiving AYA-focused care through a comprehensive and multidisciplinary approach
- Encourage the patient to seek care at centers with established social, clinical, therapeutic, and psychosocial support programs

# Considerations in the treatment decision-making process

- Consider the distinct biology of disease in the AYA population
- Consider other age-related issues, per Perez GK, et al. Am Soc Clin Oncol Educ Book 2020
    - Fertility
    - Long-term side effects
    - Insurance and financial issues
    - Transportation to clinic appointments
    - Child care
    - Psychosocial support
    - Adherence to therapy
- Apply the same considerations during the transition of care from pediatric to adult medical teams
- Practice cultural humility in every interaction, across clinical and psychosocial aspects, throughout the cancer continuum
    - Address stigma as well
- Strongly encourage participation in clinical trials
- Strongly encourage enrollment on tumor banking and biologic protocols
