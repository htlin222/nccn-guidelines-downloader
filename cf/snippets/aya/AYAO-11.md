- NCCN Adolescent and Young Adult (AYA) Oncology v1.2027, AYAO-11, p23
- Survivorship for the AYA cancer survivor
    - An individual is considered a cancer survivor from the time of diagnosis, through the balance of their life
    - Family members, friends, and caregivers are also impacted
- All recommendations category 2A unless otherwise indicated

# Care plan and follow-up

- Develop a "Cancer Treatment Summary and Survivorship Care Plan"
    - See NCCN Guidelines for Survivorship
- Always conduct medical and psychosocial care in the language preferred by the patient/family/caregiver
    - Use certified interpreters
    - Do not rely on family members/caregivers, friends, or non-certified medical staff for interpretation
- Provide a periodic evaluation focusing on history, physical examination, and screening
    - Based on treatment exposures and risk for treatment-related late effects
- Encourage relationship with primary health care provider for routine health issues
- Support end-of-treatment transition to primary care physician (PCP)
    - With clear communication about PCP role and oncology team role
- Provide assistance in establishing care with primary health care provider
- Communicate with the new care team once established
    - The initial care team is responsible for this, given the transient lifestyle of the AYA population (ie, attending college far from home, traveling to different locations to establish careers)
    - Applicable across the care continuum

# Mental health

- Ensure patient is provided continuous access to mental health support
    - Support groups
    - Individual therapy
    - AYA-specific organizations
- Ensure patient is aware of risks for mental health following treatment

# Vaccinations

- Human papillomavirus (HPV) vaccine
    - Recommended for all survivors aged 9-26 years
    - May be given through age 45, however best protection is achieved at earlier ages
    - Discuss HPV vaccination with survivors aged 27-45 years, and determine on an individual basis whether or not vaccination may be of benefit for that individual survivor
- COVID-19 vaccines
    - CDC: Interim Clinical Considerations for Use of COVID-19 Vaccines in the United States
    - CDC: Staying Up to Date with COVID-19 Vaccines
    - Infectious Diseases Society of America: COVID-19
- Annual influenza vaccine
- See NCCN Guidelines for Survivorship for additional guidance on vaccines
- Further details
    - https://www.cdc.gov/acip-recs/hcp/vaccine-specific/index.html
    - https://www.survivorshipguidelines.org
    - https://www.idsociety.org/ID-topics/special-topics/immunization

# Risk reduction counseling

- Counsel regarding striving to meet physical activity guidelines
- Counsel regarding methods to reduce risk of long-term chronic health problems, recurrence, or new tumors
    - Avoiding smoking
    - Increasing level of physical activity
    - Sun protection
- Screen for risky behavior on a regular basis, and provide education on risks when needed
    - Substance use
    - Tobacco/nicotine use
    - Binge drinking/excessive alcohol use
    - Cannabis use
    - Applicable across the care continuum

# Sexual health

- Educate on safe sex practices/contraceptive options if not trying to actively conceive
    - Regardless of infertility risk post treatment
    - Applicable across the care continuum
- Ask about sexual dysfunction on a routine basis, and how it may be impacting interpersonal relationships/self-image
    - Applicable across the care continuum

# Socioeconomic support

- Advocate for appropriate health care coverage by providing resources such as social work
