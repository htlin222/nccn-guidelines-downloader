# Psychosocial support

- Offer psychosocial support and counseling to help alleviate distress
    - See NCCN Guidelines for Distress Management
- If psychosocial support specialists are present, have them meet with the patient soon after diagnosis
    - Includes psychology, child life specialists, or integrative medicine
    - Address the patient's concerns regarding treatment or procedures
    - Provide psychoeducation
    - Assist with coping mechanisms to reduce anxiety related to procedures
- If psychosocial support specialists are not present in the institution, refer to
    - A social worker
    - A mental health provider
    - Community-based resources serving AYA patients
    - Purpose of the referral is to screen for
        - Symptoms of depression
        - Anxiety
        - Suicidal ideation/behaviors
        - Self-injurious behavior

# Neuropsychological assessment

- Discuss with patient/guardian regarding referral for neuropsychological assessment
    - If there are concerns regarding the patient's cognitive function
        - Attention
        - Memory
        - Executive function
    - And/or prior to educational and career transitions, including returning to school/work after treatment

# Treatment adherence

- Facilitate adherence to treatment by considering
    - Flexible treatment dates
    - Flexible consultation times
    - Flexible procedures (evenings/weekends)

# Educational and career services

- Refer for educational and career services to address
    - Training/education
    - Employment
    - Disability disclosure
    - Vocational adjustment training
    - Transition services
        - Social services
        - Vocational counseling
        - Occupational therapy
        - Financial counselors

# Sexual health and fertility

- For all AYA patients, provide counseling around sexual health conversations
- Decision-making regarding the risks of treatment-related fertility impairment
- Discuss options for fertility preservation prior to the start of therapy
- See Fertility, Reproductive Endocrine, and Sexual Health Considerations (AYAO-5)

# Patients with minoritized identities

- Practice cultural humility
    - Remain open to the impact of cultural and identity factors in treatment decision-making
    - See Cultural Humility in Cancer Care
- Offer additional psychosocial support and referrals surrounding stressors, stigma, or rejection related to their sexuality or gender identity
    - See Cancer and Gender Diversity and Young Adult Survivors United
- Ensure record system accurately states patient's pronouns and preferred name
- For transgender youth, consider referral to a specialized gender clinic
    - For psychosocial support and coordination of gender-affirming medical care
    - At the patient's discretion

# Legal and existential support

- Refer patients to legal services (if applicable) for
    - Estate or legacy planning
    - Child custody concerns
    - Other legal issues
- Assess for existential or spiritual distress
    - Refer to chaplain or spiritual counselor for support or intervention
