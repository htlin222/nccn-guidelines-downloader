# Dose schedules

- Offer enrollment in open clinical trials for the patient's specific disease, when available and appropriate
- Supportive care should follow well-established guidelines, such as those available at www.NCCN.org
- Consider whether dose reduction is appropriate
    - Dose reductions are often based on avoiding severe, irreversible organ damage
    - Unless clear clinical data indicate otherwise, assume this patient population has significant long-term survival
    - Unless clear clinical data indicate otherwise, assume that significant end-organ damage may compromise long-term function and quality of life (QOL)
- Establish maximum cumulative dosing parameters
- Monitor cumulative dosing and schedule for certain medications associated with irreversible organ damage and fertility issues when certain lifetime exposure is encountered
    - Specific agents, see AYAO-9A
- When feasible and outcomes are equivalent, consider an outpatient-based therapy regimen

# Toxicities

- Reversible toxicities do not necessarily warrant dose reductions
- For management of treatment-related toxicities, see NCCN Guidelines for Supportive Care, including
    - NCCN Guidelines for Adult Cancer Pain
    - NCCN Guidelines for Antiemesis
    - NCCN Guidelines for Cancer-Related Fatigue
    - NCCN Guidelines for Palliative Care
    - NCCN Guidelines for Prevention and Treatment of Cancer-Related Infections
- For growth factor support, see NCCN Guidelines for Hematopoietic Growth Factors
    - Also for management of cancer- and chemotherapy-induced anemia in those who refuse blood transfusions
- Give the patient a summary of potential therapy-related side effects
- Documentation of consent is recommended
- Ongoing monitoring for treatment-specific acute toxicities is essential
- Screening is recommended for treatment-related toxicities
    - Includes specific core outcomes that should be evaluated according to the initial diagnosis
    - Basis: van Kalsbeek RJ, et al. Nat Med 2023;29:1340-1348
