# Psychosocial factors

- Evaluate for current and past trauma history
    - Adverse childhood experiences (ACEs)
    - Medical-related trauma
    - Abusive relationships
- Developmental and cognitive function
- Communication and information delivery preferences
    - Assess health literacy
- Preferred coping style of patient/family/caregiver
- Evaluate distress and adjustment through screening for depression and anxiety
    - If interest is expressed, provide opportunity for patient to share their cancer story
    - See NCCN Guidelines for Distress Management
- Evaluate for current and past psychiatric symptoms and treatments
    - Anxiety
    - Depression
    - Suicidal thoughts
    - Eating disorders
    - Self-injurious behavior
- Involvement/interruption of school/work
- Living status
    - Alone
    - Spouse/partner
    - Friends/roommates
    - Parents
    - Children
    - Unhoused or nonstable living environment
- Impact of cancer on identity
    - Personal values
    - Self-esteem
    - Relational identity
    - Body image and physical changes
    - Strengths/resilience
    - Future goals
- Impact of cancer on support network
    - Social isolation
    - Lack of will to discuss diagnosis due to feelings of shame or embarrassment
    - Loss of family, caregiver, or friends
- Existential/spiritual beliefs or exploration
- Assess for gender expression, gender identity, chosen name, pronouns, and sexual orientation
- Patient or family/caregiver involvement with criminal justice system or family court system
- Patient preference for information sharing and involvement of certain biological or chosen family members in care, and exclusion of others
