# Collaborative communication

- Promote collaborative communication between AYA patients and:
    - Parents/caregivers
    - Children
    - Spouse/partner
    - Siblings and other family members/caregivers
    - Friends
    - Social network

# Communicate directly with individual patients

- Provide psychoeducation and assistance exploring and documenting advance directive preferences
- Ask for permission to share information with identified family members/caregivers or supports
- Provide developmentally appropriate information about their cancer, treatment options, and potential side effects
    - See Online Resources for AYA Patients and Survivors
    - See NCCN Patient Guidelines for Adolescent and Young Adult Cancer
- If AYA patient is >18 years of age, provide information on and necessary forms that legally allow medical information to be shared with caregivers of patient's choice
- Always conduct medical and psychosocial care in the language preferred by the patient/family/caregiver
    - Use certified interpreters
    - Do not rely on family members/caregivers, friends, or non-certified medical staff for interpretation

# Advance care planning

- Early in the treatment process encourage education and the completion of a medical power of attorney and a living will at age of majority
- Provide access to AYA-specific advanced care planning guides to determine a health care proxy

# Family, caregiver, and partner support

- Provide identified family members/caregivers and partners with information about psychosocial support and behavioral services
    - Increase awareness of and normalize the possible psychosocial issues associated with cancer diagnosis in AYAs, so that family members/caregivers and partners may continue to support the patient

# AYA-specific psychosocial support

- Provide education and access to AYA-specific psychosocial support
    - See Online Resources for AYA Patients and Survivors
    - Parent/caregiver support groups
    - AYA support groups, see Cactus Cancer Society
    - Psychoeducational programs

# Supportive/palliative care and integrative oncology

- Provide education and access to supportive/palliative care and integrative oncology for stress and symptom management
    - Palliative/supportive care for symptom management
    - Integrative oncology including mind-body therapies, acupuncture, tai chi, yoga, music therapy, massage therapy, etc.

# Peer visiting and peer interaction

- Create flexible visiting hours and an environment that will encourage peers to visit AYA patients
    - Ensure staff are trained to recognize normative expectations for AYA interactions with peers

# AYA patients who are parents

- Refer patients to resources that particularly offer support for AYA patients who are parents
    - Identify resources for respite care for AYA patients with young children
    - See Bright Spot Network
    - See Pickles Group
    - See Young Adult Survivors United

# AYA-specific activities

- Provide education and access to AYA-specific activities
    - See Online Resources for AYA Patients and Survivors
    - Inpatient: recreational activities to reduce boredom and foster social connections with peers
    - Outpatient
        - Social networking events and opportunities
        - Camp and retreat programs
        - Peer mentoring
    - Education on peer support: establishing relationships with AYA patients and maintaining previous relationships
