# Source

- NCCN Prevention and Treatment of Cancer-Related Infections v1.2026, INF-7, p13
- Applies to vaccination of patients with cancer
- Assess appropriate timing of vaccination in patients whose disease is unlikely to respond, eg, patients who received anti-B-cell antibodies within 6 months, induction and consolidation chemotherapy for acute leukemia
- For vaccination recommendations in cancer survivors, see SIMIN-1 in the NCCN Guidelines for Survivorship
- All recommendations category 2A unless otherwise indicated

# General

- Live vaccines should NOT be administered during chemotherapy or periods of significant immunosuppression, such as treatment of GVHD
- All household members should be up-to-date with vaccines

# Vaccination recommendations

- Influenza
    - Inactivated or recombinant influenza vaccine annually, for patients with hematologic or solid tumor malignancies
- COVID-19
    - All persons with cancer, or who have been previously treated for cancer, should receive COVID-19 vaccination
    - Consider revaccination every 6 months with the currently available COVID-19 vaccine
    - If an updated vaccine for a novel strain is available, wait at least 2 months after the last COVID-19 vaccination or infection before revaccination
- Pneumococcal
    - Administer to adults who are newly diagnosed with cancer and are pneumococcal vaccine-naive
- Meningococcal, for adults with anatomic or functional asplenia, persistent complement component deficiency, or on complement inhibitor, eg, eculizumab, ravulizumab
    - Meningococcal conjugate vaccine, quadrivalent (MenACWY), 2-dose primary series at least 8 weeks apart
        - 1 booster dose every 5 years if risk remains
    - Serogroup B vaccination for these same patients
        - 1 booster dose 1 year after the primary series
        - Then every 2-3 years if risk remains
    - A combination meningococcal pentavalent (ACWY and B) vaccine is available and can be considered as an alternate regimen
- Human papillomavirus (HPV)
    - Recombinant 3-dose HPV vaccine offered to patients of all sexes up to 26 years of age
    - May be considered in patients up to 45 years of age
- Recombinant zoster vaccine (RZV)
    - Recommended for adult patients aged 50 years and older, and those 18 years and older who are at increased risk for herpes zoster (HZ)
    - Given in 2 doses, at least 2 to 6 months apart
    - For adults who are at risk and 18 years of age and older, a second dose can be given 1-2 months after the first dose if they will benefit from a shorter vaccination schedule
    - For patients who have previously received the live-attenuated zoster vaccine live (ZVL), give RZV at least 2 months after the last ZVL dose
- Tdap (tetanus/diphtheria/pertussis)
    - Given every 10 years
- Respiratory syncytial virus (RSV)
    - Recommended for adults 75 years and older
    - Can be considered for patients 50-74 years of age with moderate or severe immune compromise who may be at increased risk for severe RSV disease
    - One current dose is recommended
    - Administration between August and October is suggested
    - The long-acting RSV monoclonal antibody (mAb) (nirsevimab) is approved for infants under 24 months of age to prevent RSV infection
- Travel and other vaccines
    - ID consult for travel vaccines is recommended
