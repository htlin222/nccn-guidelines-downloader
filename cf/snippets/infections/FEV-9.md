# Source

- NCCN Prevention and Treatment of Cancer-Related Infections v1.2026, FEV-9, p37
- Applies to the initial clinical presentation (day 0) of a patient with possible respiratory infection whose finding is lung infiltrates
- All recommendations category 2A unless otherwise indicated

- Nature of the radiologic appearance ___ (radiologic_appearance)
- Epidemiology ___ (epidemiology)
- Risk factors ___ (risk_factors)
- Patient history ___ (history)
- Clinical presentation ___ (presentation)
- Influenza season in the local area ___ (influenza_season)
- Other diagnoses to consider
    - Pulmonary edema
    - Hemorrhage
    - Drug toxicities
- Assess for health care-acquired pneumonia and/or resistant pathogens

# Evaluation

- The potential diagnoses and useful tests are extensive
- CT scan, to define nature and extent of infiltrates
- Culture of blood
- Culture of sputum, if available
- Consider bronchoalveolar lavage (BAL), especially if no response to initial therapy
- Targeted testing should be considered, based on the nature of the radiologic appearance, epidemiology, risk factors, patient history, and clinical presentation

# Targeted diagnostic testing

- Microbial cell-free DNA testing can be considered as determined by center preference
    - Experience with the use of microbial cell-free DNA testing has been variable
- Blood
    - Aspergillus galactomannan
    - Cryptococcal antigen
    - Coccidioides antigen serology
    - Blastomyces and/or Histoplasma antigen
- Urine
    - Blastomyces and/or Histoplasma antigen
    - Legionella and pneumococcal antigen
- Respiratory/bronchoscopy specimens
    - Rapid immunofluorescent viral antigen tests may be negative for H1N1
    - Aspergillus galactomannan
    - Pneumocystis jiroveci PCR or DFA
    - Mycobacterial PCR
    - Mycoplasma and Chlamydia PCR
    - Nucleic acid amplification test (NAAT) for viral respiratory pathogens, via nasopharyngeal swab or BAL fluid

# Treatment modifications

- All patients with febrile neutropenia should receive broad-spectrum antibiotics (FEV-5)
    - See Antibacterial Agents (FEV-A) for dosing, spectrum, and specific comments/cautions
- Consider adding coverage for atypical bacteria
    - Azithromycin
    - Doxycycline
        - Can be used for Mycoplasma or Chlamydia
        - Not recommended for legionellosis, as some non-pneumophila Legionella species can be resistant to tetracyclines
    - Or fluoroquinolone
    - See General Recommendations for Vaccination in Patients with Cancer: Pneumococcal Vaccination (INF-7)
- Consider adding mold-active antifungal agent, for patients at intermediate to high risk (INF-1)
    - See Antifungal Agents (FEV-B) for dosing, spectrum, and specific comments/cautions
- Consider adding antiviral therapy during influenza season in the local area
    - Antiviral susceptibility of influenza strains is variable and cannot be predicted based on prior influenza outbreaks
    - In cases of seasonal influenza and pandemic strains (eg, H1N1), be familiar with susceptibility patterns and guidelines on appropriate antiviral treatment
    - See Antiviral Agents (FEV-C) for dosing, spectrum, and specific comments/cautions
    - The antivirals are not equal in terms of efficacy, side effects, and resistance
- Consider adding TMP/SMX, if possible Pneumocystis jirovecii etiology
- Consider adding vancomycin or linezolid, if MRSA suspected
- Re-evaluate for ability to de-escalate
