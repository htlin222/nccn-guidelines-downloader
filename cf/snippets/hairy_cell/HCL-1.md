# Diagnosis, essential

- Bone marrow biopsy, with or without aspirate
    - Presence of characteristic hairy cells upon morphologic examination of peripheral blood or bone marrow
    - Characteristic infiltrate with increased reticulin in bone marrow biopsy samples
    - Dry tap is frequent
- Adequate immunophenotyping, essential for establishing the diagnosis and for distinguishing cHCL from HCLv (ICC)/SBLPN (WHO5)
    - IHC or flow cytometry for CD19, CD20, CD5, CD10, CD11c, CD22, CD25, CD103, CD123, cyclin D1, and CD200
    - Typical immunophenotype for cHCL: CD5 negative, CD10 negative, CD11c positive, CD20 positive (bright), CD22 positive, CD25 positive, CD103 positive, CD123 positive, cyclin D1 positive, annexin A1 positive, CD200 positive (bright)
    - Monocytopenia is characteristic of cHCL
    - HCLv (ICC)/SBLPN (WHO5) is characteristically CD25 negative, CD123 negative, annexin A1 negative, and negative for BRAF V600E mutations, which helps distinguish it from cHCL
    - See Use of Immunophenotyping/Molecular Analysis in Differential Diagnosis of Mature B-Cell and NK/T-Cell Neoplasms in the NCCN Guidelines for B-Cell Lymphomas
- IHC or molecular analysis to detect BRAF V600E mutation
    - If testing for BRAF V600E mutation is negative, next-generation sequencing (NGS) or comparable testing for other BRAF and MAP2K1 mutations can be considered
    - HCLv (ICC)/SBLPN (WHO5) and IGHV4-34-mutant HCL often show mutations in MAP2K1

# Diagnosis, useful under certain circumstances

- Molecular analysis to detect IGHV4-34 rearrangement
    - Ten percent of B-cell lymphoproliferative neoplasms with a cHCL phenotype possess IGHV4-34 rearrangements and typically lack BRAF V600E mutations
    - These diseases behave more like HCLv (ICC)/SBLPN (WHO5): they do not respond well to purine analog therapy and generally have a poorer prognosis

# Workup, essential

- History and physical exam with attention to node-bearing areas and the measurement of size of liver and spleen
    - Presence of enlarged spleen and/or liver
    - Presence of peripheral lymphadenopathy (uncommon)
- Performance status
- Peripheral blood smear examination
- Complete blood count (CBC) with differential
- Comprehensive metabolic panel, with particular attention to renal function
- Lactate dehydrogenase (LDH)
- Quantitative immunoglobulins
- Bone marrow biopsy, with or without aspirate
- Hepatitis B and C testing if treatment contemplated
    - Hepatitis B testing is indicated because of the risk of reactivation during treatment, eg, immunotherapy, chemoimmunotherapy, chemotherapy, targeted therapy
    - Tests include hepatitis B surface antigen and core antibody for a patient with no risk factors
    - For patients with risk factors or previous history of hepatitis B, add e-antigen
    - If positive, check viral load and consult with a gastroenterologist
    - See Anti-Infective Prophylaxis and Viral Reactivation in the NCCN Guidelines for CLL/SLL

# Workup, useful under certain circumstances

- CT of chest/abdomen/pelvis with contrast of diagnostic quality
- Pregnancy testing in patients of childbearing age, if systemic therapy planned
- Discussion of fertility preservation
    - Options include sperm banking, semen cryopreservation, in vitro fertilization (IVF), or ovarian tissue or oocyte cryopreservation
