# Diagnosis

- Establish the diagnosis against WHO Criteria for Lymphoplasmacytic Lymphoma and Waldenstrom Macroglobulinemia (WM/LPL-A)
- Lymphoplasmacytic lymphoma (LPL) encompasses IgG, IgA, serum FLC alone, and non-secretory subtypes
    - These make up <5% of all LPLs
    - Treatment of non-IgM LPLs parallels that of IgM-secreting LPLs
    - Non-IgM LPLs are less likely to have hyperviscosity associated with them
    - Non-IgM LPLs are less likely to have autoimmune-related neuropathy
- Differentiate from IgM monoclonal gammopathy of undetermined significance (MGUS)
- Differentiate from IgM multiple myeloma
    - For classification of IgM monoclonal gammopathies, see Table 1 in Treon SP, et al. Semin Hematol 2023;60:97
    - And see MGNS-1 in the NCCN Guidelines for Multiple Myeloma

# Molecular results

- MYD88 wild-type occurs in <10% of patients
    - Do not use MYD88 wild-type to exclude the diagnosis of Waldenstrom macroglobulinemia (WM) if other criteria are met
- Mutations in CXCR4 and TP53 can impact BTK inhibitor (BTKi) response

# Cryoglobulinemia

- Consider testing in patients with suspected cryoglobulinemia
- Cryocrit ___ (cryocrit)
    - If cryocrit is positive, repeat testing of initial serum IgM
    - If cryocrit is positive, obtain all subsequent serum IgM levels under warm conditions

# Amyloidosis

- Rule out amyloidosis in patients presenting with peripheral neuropathy
- Rule out amyloidosis in patients presenting with nephrotic syndrome
- Rule out amyloidosis in patients presenting with unexplained cardiac problems

# Before calling the patient symptomatic

- Confirm symptoms are not related to or caused by comorbidities

# Older adults

- Consider frailty assessment in older adults
    - See NCCN Guidelines for Older Adult Oncology
