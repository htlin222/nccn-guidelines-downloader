# Presentation

- Pain
- Jaundice
- Abnormal LFTs
- Obstruction or abnormality on imaging

# Workup

- Multidisciplinary evaluation
- H&P
- Multiphasic abdomen/pelvis CT/MRI with IV contrast, assess for vascular invasion
    - Principles of Imaging (BIL-A)
    - Imaging evaluation for suspected eCCA is ideally performed prior to biliary decompression, to facilitate accurate local staging for surgical candidacy
- Chest CT, with or without contrast
    - Principles of Imaging (BIL-A)
    - Ideally performed prior to biliary decompression
- Biliary protocol imaging
    - Principles of Imaging (BIL-A)
    - Ideally performed prior to biliary decompression
- Cholangiography
    - MRCP is preferred
    - ERCP/PTC are used more for therapeutic intervention
- Consider CEA testing
    - CEA is a baseline test and should not be done to confirm diagnosis
- Consider CA 19-9 testing
    - CA 19-9 is a baseline test and should not be done to confirm diagnosis
- LFTs
- Consider endoscopic ultrasound (EUS), after surgical consultation
- Consider serum IgG4 testing, to rule out IgG4-related sclerosing cholangitis
    - Patients with IgG4-related cholangiopathy should be referred to an expert center

# Primary treatment, resectable

- Resectable, see Principles of Surgery (EXTRA-A)
- Surgical exploration
    - Surgery may be performed when index of suspicion is high; biopsy is not required
- Consider laparoscopic staging
- Consider preoperative biliary drainage (BIL-A)
- Resectable at exploration
    - Resection, see Principles of Surgery (EXTRA-A)
    - Principles of Pathology (EXTRA-B)
- Unresectable at exploration, see the unresectable pathway below

# Primary treatment, unresectable

- Biliary drainage, if indicated (BIL-A)
    - Consider biliary drainage for patients with jaundice prior to instituting systemic therapy
    - Consider baseline CA 19-9 after biliary decompression
- Biopsy, only after determining transplant status
    - Principles of Pathology (EXTRA-B)
    - Before biopsy, evaluate if the patient is a resection or transplant candidate
    - If the patient is a potential transplant candidate, consider referral to a transplant center before biopsy
    - Transperitoneal and surgical biopsy may be contraindicated in transplant candidates
- Biomarker testing, see Principles of Biomarker Testing (BIL-B)
    - For patients with dMMR/MSI-H tumors, consider germline testing and/or referral to a genetic counselor
    - For patients with a family history suggestive of BRCA1/2 PVs, consider germline testing and/or referral to a genetic counselor
- Consider referral to a transplant center, see Principles of Surgery (EXTRA-A)
    - Unresectable perihilar or hilar CCAs measuring 3 cm or less in radial diameter, with absence of intrahepatic or extrahepatic metastases and without nodal disease, may be considered for liver transplantation
    - Unresectable perihilar or hilar CCAs with primary sclerosing cholangitis may also be considered for liver transplantation
    - Transplantation only at a transplant center with a UNOS-approved protocol for transplantation of CCA

# Options for unresectable disease

- Order does not indicate preference; the choice of treatment modality may depend on extent/location of disease and institutional capabilities
- Systemic therapy, see Principles of Systemic Therapy (BIL-C)
- Clinical trial
- Combination of chemotherapy and chemoradiation (BIL-C)
    - Principles of Radiation Therapy (BIL-D)
    - There are limited clinical trial data to define a standard regimen or definitive benefit; clinical trial participation is encouraged
    - For a list of gemcitabine-based regimens and fluoropyrimidine-based regimens to be used before or after chemoradiation, see Adjuvant Chemotherapy (BIL-C, 1 of 5)
- Chemoradiation (BIL-C)
    - Principles of Radiation Therapy (BIL-D)
    - There are limited clinical trial data to define a standard regimen or definitive benefit; clinical trial participation is encouraged
- Palliative RT, see Principles of Radiation Therapy (BIL-D)
- Best supportive care, see NCCN Guidelines for Palliative Care

# Metastatic disease

- Biliary drainage, if indicated (BIL-A)
    - Consider biliary drainage for patients with jaundice prior to instituting systemic therapy
    - Consider baseline CA 19-9 after biliary decompression
- Biopsy, see Principles of Pathology (EXTRA-B)
- Biomarker testing, see Principles of Biomarker Testing (BIL-B)
    - For patients with dMMR/MSI-H tumors, consider germline testing and/or referral to a genetic counselor
    - For patients with a family history suggestive of BRCA1/2 PVs, consider germline testing and/or referral to a genetic counselor
- Options, order does not indicate preference
    - Systemic therapy, see Principles of Systemic Therapy (BIL-C)
    - Clinical trial
