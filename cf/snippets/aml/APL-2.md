# Source

- NCCN Acute Promyelocytic Leukemia v5.2026, APL-2, p17
- Applies to APL, age ≥18 years, low-risk, treatment induction and consolidation therapy
- See references (APL-7) and APL-B 1 of 6 for details on regimens
- Principles of Supportive Care for APL (APL-A)
- All recommendations category 2A unless otherwise indicated

# Assessment

- ___ (age) yo, low-risk APL
- Treated at ___ (site)
    - For patients being treated at a community center, collaboration with a center with expertise has been shown to reduce induction mortality
- Arsenic available and not contraindicated: ___ (arsenic_available)
- Blood count recovery by day 28
    - Platelet ___ (platelet)
    - Absolute neutrophil count ___ (anc)
- Several groups have published large trials with excellent outcomes; to achieve the expected results, use the regimen consistently through all components
    - Do not mix induction from one trial with consolidation from another
- Early mortality is related to bleeding, differentiation syndrome, or infection
    - Persistent disease is rare

# Induction, preferred regimens

- ATRA + daily arsenic trioxide (category 1)
- or ATRA + intermittent arsenic trioxide (category 1)
- For either regimen
    - Data suggest that lower doses of ATRA (25 mg/m2) in divided doses until clinical remission may be used in children and adolescents
    - Begin prophylaxis with prednisone
        - The optimal duration of steroid prophylaxis is unknown
        - If differentiation syndrome develops, change to dexamethasone
        - See Principles of Supportive Care for APL (APL-A)

# Induction, useful in certain circumstances, if arsenic is not available or contraindicated

- ATRA + idarubicin (category 1)
    - Begin prophylaxis with prednisone; the optimal duration of steroid prophylaxis is unknown
    - If differentiation syndrome develops, change to dexamethasone
- or ATRA + gemtuzumab ozogamicin
- For either regimen
    - Data suggest that lower doses of ATRA (25 mg/m2) in divided doses until clinical remission may be used in children and adolescents

# Response assessment after arsenic trioxide induction

- If blood count recovery by day 28, proceed with consolidation
    - Platelet >100 x 109/L
    - Absolute neutrophil count >1 x 109/L
- BM aspirate and biopsy may be considered to document <5% blasts and no abnormal promyelocytes
    - If no evidence of morphologic disease (<5% blasts and no abnormal promyelocytes), discontinue ATRA and arsenic trioxide to allow for peripheral blood recovery, since arsenic trioxide can be associated with significant myelosuppression
    - If evidence of morphologic disease, continue ATRA and arsenic trioxide and repeat BM 1 week later
    - If the full course of induction treatment is not given, or counts have not recovered by days 28-35, a BM aspirate and biopsy is recommended to document <5% blasts and no abnormal promyelocytes before proceeding with consolidation
- The presence of measurable cytogenetic and molecular markers post-induction does not carry prognostic or therapeutic implications

# Response assessment after idarubicin or gemtuzumab ozogamicin induction

- BM aspirate and biopsy days 28-35 to document <5% blasts and no abnormal promyelocytes before proceeding with consolidation
- The presence of measurable cytogenetic and molecular markers post-induction does not carry prognostic or therapeutic implications
- For regimens using high cumulative doses of cardiotoxic agents, consider reassessing cardiac function prior to each anthracycline/mitoxantrone-containing course

# Consolidation therapy

- Continue consolidation as per protocol (see references and APL-B 1 of 6)
- The presence of measurable cytogenetic and molecular markers post-induction does not carry prognostic or therapeutic implications
- For regimens using high cumulative doses of cardiotoxic agents, consider reassessing cardiac function prior to each anthracycline/mitoxantrone-containing course
